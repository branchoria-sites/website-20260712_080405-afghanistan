## Introduction

Zorats Karer, a prehistoric stone complex near the town of Sisian in southern [Armenia]({{ 'what-do-armenias-ancient-mysteries/' | relative_url }}), is one of the country's most debated archaeological sites. The central question is simple: was it an ancient astronomical observatory, or primarily a ceremonial and funerary landscape that later acquired astronomical interpretations? The dispute matters because the answer would dramatically change how the monument is understood. If it functioned as a sophisticated observatory thousands of years before similar monuments elsewhere, it would transform ideas about the development of early astronomy. If not, it remains an exceptionally important prehistoric cemetery and ritual complex whose significance has sometimes been overshadowed by more spectacular claims.

Current archaeological evidence strongly supports the site's role as a long-used burial and ceremonial landscape. While it remains possible that some stones were aligned with celestial events or that prehistoric communities observed the sky there, the stronger claims that Zorats Karer was the world's oldest observatory have not been accepted by most archaeologists or archaeoastronomers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.incipit.csic.es/en/research_outcomes/publication/book-section/carahunge-a-critical-assessment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: incipit.csic.es">[csic.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">incipit.csic.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carahunge (A critical assessment)...</span></span></span>

## What archaeologists have found at the site

Zorats Karer occupies a windswept plateau roughly 1,770 metres above sea level. The monument consists of more than two hundred basalt standing stones, several stone-built burial chambers, low walls and alignments extending across a broad area. Around eighty of the stones contain carefully drilled circular holes, a feature that has become central to the observatory debate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/unraveling-mystery-armenian-stonehenge-180964207/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine Unraveling the Mystery of the &quot;Armenian Stonehenge</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Magazine Unraveling the Mystery of the &quot;Armenian Stonehenge</span></span></span>

Excavations have produced a more complex picture than early popular descriptions suggested. Rather than revealing a single-purpose monument, archaeologists have identified a landscape that developed over many centuries. Burials from the Bronze Age and Iron Age are firmly documented, and the monument appears to have been reused repeatedly rather than constructed all at once. Excavated tombs, grave goods and multiple burial phases indicate that funerary and ceremonial activity formed an important part of the site's history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.am/en/verified/2019/06/24/11928/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.am">[media.am]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.am</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">People are the media &#124; The Zorats Karer Are Not An Observatory, And The Accusations Of Their Destruction Are Groundless • media.am...</span></span></span>

This layered history is significant because it cautions against treating every stone as part of one original design. Some standing stones may have been repositioned or reused over time, making it much harder to reconstruct their earliest purpose with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/unraveling-mystery-armenian-stonehenge-180964207/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine Unraveling the Mystery of the &quot;Armenian Stonehenge</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Magazine Unraveling the Mystery of the &quot;Armenian Stonehenge</span></span></span>

## How the astronomical theory developed

Interest in possible astronomical alignments began during the 1980s, when Armenian researchers suggested that some of the perforated stones might have been used to observe the rising or setting of celestial bodies. The idea gained international attention through the work of Armenian radio physicist Paris Herouni during the 1990s and early 2000s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carahunge" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Herouni argued that the holes functioned as precision sighting devices aimed at the Sun, Moon and prominent stars. Based on these proposed alignments, he suggested that the monument dated to around the sixth millennium BC or earlier, making it substantially older than Stonehenge. The theory attracted considerable public interest because it appeared to place Armenia among the earliest centres of scientific observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carahunge" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

The comparison with Stonehenge also proved highly effective in popular media. Tourism material frequently described Zorats Karer as the "Armenian Stonehenge", encouraging visitors to view it primarily through the lens of prehistoric astronomy rather than archaeology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/unraveling-mystery-armenian-stonehenge-180964207/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine Unraveling the Mystery of the &quot;Armenian Stonehenge</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Magazine Unraveling the Mystery of the &quot;Armenian Stonehenge</span></span></span>

## Why the observatory claim remains disputed

The disagreement is not about whether ancient people understood the sky. Archaeologists readily acknowledge that many prehistoric societies tracked seasonal changes and incorporated astronomical knowledge into ritual life. The dispute concerns whether Zorats Karer provides convincing evidence of a specialised observatory.

Several problems have been identified by critics.

First, many proposed alignments depend heavily on selecting particular stones while overlooking others. With hundreds of stones and numerous possible sight lines, some apparent alignments can arise simply by chance unless rigorous statistical methods demonstrate otherwise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.incipit.csic.es/en/research_outcomes/publication/book-section/carahunge-a-critical-assessment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: incipit.csic.es">[incipit.csic.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">incipit.csic.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carahunge (A critical assessment)...</span></span></span>

Second, different researchers have assigned the same stone rows to different celestial targets. One alignment has variously been interpreted as marking the summer solstice, a major lunar standstill or the rising of Venus. When a single feature can support several incompatible astronomical interpretations, confidence in any one explanation decreases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.incipit.csic.es/en/research_outcomes/publication/book-section/carahunge-a-critical-assessment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: incipit.csic.es">[incipit.csic.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">incipit.csic.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carahunge (A critical assessment)...</span></span></span>

Third, Herouni proposed that narrow tubes or similar devices were inserted into the drilled holes to produce sufficiently precise observations. However, excavations have found no archaeological evidence that such instruments ever existed at the site. Without them, critics argue that the holes are too wide to provide the level of precision claimed for astronomical measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.incipit.csic.es/en/research_outcomes/publication/book-section/carahunge-a-critical-assessment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: incipit.csic.es">[incipit.csic.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">incipit.csic.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carahunge (A critical assessment)...</span></span></span>

Finally, the chronology itself is disputed. The dates proposed for the observatory are largely inferred from astronomical calculations rather than securely established through excavation. Archaeological evidence instead points to substantial activity during the Bronze and Iron Ages, making the exceptionally early dating uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.incipit.csic.es/en/research_outcomes/publication/book-section/carahunge-a-critical-assessment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: incipit.csic.es">[csic.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">incipit.csic.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carahunge (A critical assessment)...</span></span></span>

## The mystery of the perforated stones

The circular holes remain one of the site's most intriguing features because their original purpose has not been conclusively established.

Several possibilities have been suggested:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* They served as sighting devices for astronomical observation.
* They were used to secure ropes during transport or construction.
* They held wooden fittings or structural elements.
* They had symbolic or ritual functions connected with ceremonies performed at the monument.

</div>

At present, none of these explanations has been demonstrated beyond doubt. Archaeologists therefore regard the holes as an unresolved question rather than proof of an observatory. Their existence is genuinely mysterious, but mystery alone does not establish the astronomical interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.am/en/verified/2019/06/24/11928/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.am">[media.am]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.am</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">People are the media &#124; The Zorats Karer Are Not An Observatory, And The Accusations Of Their Destruction Are Groundless • media.am...</span></span></span>

## Why the debate became larger than archaeology

The controversy reflects more than technical disagreements about prehistoric astronomy. It also illustrates how archaeological discoveries can become part of national identity.

For many supporters, the observatory hypothesis represents evidence that ancient Armenia possessed advanced scientific knowledge long before better-known monuments elsewhere. Because of this wider cultural significance, criticism of the theory has sometimes been interpreted as an attack on Armenian heritage rather than a normal scientific debate. Researchers working at the site have occasionally found themselves responding not only to archaeological questions but also to public campaigns defending the observatory interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.am/en/verified/2019/06/24/11928/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.am">[media.am]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.am</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">People are the media &#124; The Zorats Karer Are Not An Observatory, And The Accusations Of Their Destruction Are Groundless • media.am...</span></span></span>

This has complicated discussion of the evidence. Archaeology advances by testing competing explanations against excavation results, dating evidence and material remains, not by government declarations or popular belief. The scientific status of the observatory hypothesis depends on whether the archaeological record supports it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.am/en/verified/2019/06/24/11928/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.am">[media.am]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.am</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">People are the media &#124; The Zorats Karer Are Not An Observatory, And The Accusations Of Their Destruction Are Groundless • media.am...</span></span></span>

## What is most likely today?

The strongest evidence indicates that Zorats Karer was an important prehistoric ceremonial and funerary complex that evolved over a long period. Monumental burial architecture, repeated use across different eras and associated archaeological finds are securely documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://novoscriptorium.com/2019/03/11/megalithic-complex-zorats-karer-carahunge-in-armenia-a-bronze-age-iron-age-necropolis/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: novoscriptorium.com">[novoscriptorium.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">novoscriptorium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on novoscriptorium.com.</span></span></span>

It remains entirely plausible that the builders paid attention to the Sun, Moon or prominent stars. Many prehistoric societies incorporated astronomical observations into ritual landscapes without constructing dedicated observatories. The available evidence does not rule out astronomical symbolism or occasional observational use.

What has not been demonstrated is the stronger claim that the monument functioned primarily as a precisely engineered observatory dating to the sixth or seventh millennium BC. Critical reviews of the proposed astronomical methods have concluded that the supporting arguments are not sufficiently robust, and mainstream archaeological opinion continues to favour interpreting the site as a complex, multi-phase ceremonial and burial landscape whose exact development is still being investigated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.incipit.csic.es/en/research_outcomes/publication/book-section/carahunge-a-critical-assessment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: incipit.csic.es">[csic.es]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">incipit.csic.es</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carahunge (A critical assessment)...</span></span></span>
