## Introduction

The central mystery of the Thiaroye massacre is no longer whether French colonial troops killed West African soldiers on 1 December 1944. That is well established. The enduring questions are how many men died, where they were buried, and whether official records deliberately obscured the scale of the killings. Eight decades later, historians still cannot produce a definitive death toll because key military documents are missing, contradictory or incomplete, while the location of many victims' graves remains uncertain. These gaps are not merely technical problems. They lie at the heart of debates over colonial responsibility, historical memory and the recognition owed to African soldiers who fought for France during the Second World War.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/76db7190dcbc12349ff0d6ade82309b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The event is shrouded in controversy, with disputes over the number of casualties and the circumstances of the killings. ## The protest and military assault

The soldiers at Thiaroye were former prisoners of war who had returned from Europe expecting payment of wages, bonuses and demobilisation allowances that they believed they had earned through wartime service. The dispute escalated after negotiations failed, and French authorities assembled substantial military forces around the camp before opening fire.

For many years the official French version described the incident as the suppression of a dangerous mutiny. Modern historical research has challenged that interpretation. Surviving records show no comparable French casualties, and historians have found little evidence that the African veterans possessed the level of weaponry implied by the official justification for the assault. The deployment of armoured vehicles and heavily armed troops has led many researchers to argue that the operation was planned as a decisive show of colonial force rather than an improvised response to an immediate threat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/76db7190dcbc12349ff0d6ade82309b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The event is shrouded in controversy, with disputes over the number of casualties and the circumstances of the killings. French President...</span></span></span>

The massacre therefore remains controversial not because the shooting itself is disputed, but because the surviving documentary record does not fully explain who authorised the operation, what orders were given, or whether the violence was intended from the outset.

## Why the death toll remains disputed

The best-known mystery concerns the number of soldiers who died.

French military reports issued shortly after the massacre stated that 35 African soldiers had been killed. Later official documents referred to around 70 deaths. These figures remained the accepted version for decades, reinforcing the image of a limited military action against mutinous troops.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/76db7190dcbc12349ff0d6ade82309b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The event is shrouded in controversy, with disputes over the number of casualties and the circumstances of the killings. French President...</span></span></span>

Many historians now regard those totals as incomplete. Several lines of evidence point towards a substantially higher number:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* contemporary records disagree with one another even within the French military administration;
* no complete roll of everyone present in the camp has survived;
* later witness testimony suggests bodies were removed rapidly after the shooting;
* estimates based on the number of soldiers assembled and the scale of the firing have led some researchers to suggest that several hundred men may have been killed.

</div>

As a result, published estimates now range from the long-standing official figure of 35 to approximately 400 deaths. Most historians agree that the official total understates the scale of the massacre, but there is still no documentary evidence capable of proving a precise alternative figure beyond dispute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/76db7190dcbc12349ff0d6ade82309b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The event is shrouded in controversy, with disputes over the number of casualties and the circumstances of the killings. French President...</span></span></span>

This uncertainty illustrates an important distinction. The massacre itself is an established historical fact. The exact number of victims remains an unresolved historical problem because the surviving evidence is incomplete rather than because historians disagree that the killings occurred.

## Why missing records matter

The uncertainty surrounding the death toll is closely connected to missing or inaccessible archives.

Researchers have long argued that crucial military documents either disappeared, were never preserved, or remain unavailable. Among the records that would help resolve the case are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* complete personnel lists for the camp;
* detailed casualty reports;
* orders issued before the assault;
* burial records identifying where bodies were taken;
* maps showing possible burial sites.

</div>

Without these materials it is impossible to reconcile contradictory accounts with certainty. Some historians believe records were deliberately removed to minimise the political consequences of the massacre. Others argue that normal wartime administrative failures and later archival losses may explain at least part of the documentary gap. Because the missing documents themselves cannot be examined, neither explanation can be proven conclusively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/76db7190dcbc12349ff0d6ade82309b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The event is shrouded in controversy, with disputes over the number of casualties and the circumstances of the killings. French President...</span></span></span>

A diary written by French junior officer Jean Henry, rediscovered in military archives and publicised in 2025, illustrates both the value and the limits of newly found evidence. The diary broadly reflects the contemporary official account and repeats a death toll close to the original military figures. Some historians consider it an authentic eyewitness source whose perspective was shaped by colonial assumptions. Others question whether it can be relied upon without further scrutiny and emphasise that one officer could not have witnessed every stage of the operation or the subsequent handling of the bodies. Rather than settling the debate, the diary has added another piece of evidence to an already contested historical record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lemonde.fr/afrique/article/2025/05/01/senegal-le-troublant-journal-d-un-militaire-francais-temoin-du-massacre-de-thiaroye-en-1944-tire-de-l-oubli_6602072_3212.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lemonde.fr">[Le Monde.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lemonde.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ce jour-là, des dizaines de tirailleurs africains, anciens prisonniers de guerre allemands, furent tués par l’armée française après avoir...</span></span></span>

## Hidden graves and the search for the victims

The mystery of the missing graves has become almost as significant as the dispute over casualty numbers.

Many families never recovered the bodies of relatives killed at Thiaroye. Although a military cemetery exists nearby, numerous graves are anonymous, and historians cannot confidently identify which victims are buried there. Other researchers argue that additional victims may have been placed in unmarked mass graves elsewhere around the former camp.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/76db7190dcbc12349ff0d6ade82309b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The event is shrouded in controversy, with disputes over the number of casualties and the circumstances of the killings. This uncertainty has several consequences:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* descendants often do not know where relatives are buried;
* forensic confirmation of the number of dead has proved impossible;
* the absence of identified graves has reinforced suspicions that bodies were buried hurriedly to conceal the scale of the killings.

</div>

Recent Senegalese initiatives have placed renewed emphasis on archaeological investigation. Researchers have proposed excavations of suspected burial areas, combining historical documents, landscape analysis and forensic methods in the hope of locating additional graves. Such work could clarify both the number of victims and the manner in which bodies were disposed of after the massacre.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/76db7190dcbc12349ff0d6ade82309b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The event is shrouded in controversy, with disputes over the number of casualties and the circumstances of the killings. ## Colonial responsibility and changing historical memory

For much of the twentieth century, official discussion centred on whether the soldiers had mutinied. Today the debate has shifted towards questions of state responsibility, transparency and remembrance.

French leaders have progressively acknowledged that the long-standing official narrative was inadequate. In 2012, President François Hollande referred to the killings as a "bloody repression" and indicated that more than 70 men had probably died. In 2024, President Emmanuel Macron formally described the event as a massacre in correspondence with [Senegal]({{ 'what-still-haunts-senegals-historical/' | relative_url }})'s president, marking another significant change in official language, although without resolving the disputed casualty figures or addressing every historical question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/76db7190dcbc12349ff0d6ade82309b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[apnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The event is shrouded in controversy, with disputes over the number of casualties and the circumstances of the killings. Senegal has likewise increased efforts to investigate the event through historical commissions, public commemorations and plans for archaeological work. ## What remains unresolved

Despite decades of research, several questions remain unanswered:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **How many soldiers were actually killed?** The most credible estimates now extend well beyond the original official total, but no definitive figure has been established.
* **Where are all the victims buried?** Anonymous graves and suspected mass burial sites have never been fully identified or excavated.
* **Were records deliberately concealed?** Missing archives strongly fuel suspicion, but historians cannot prove whether documents were intentionally destroyed, withheld or simply lost.
* **Can archaeology resolve the debate?** Planned excavations may provide new evidence, but whether they can establish an exact death toll after more than eighty years remains uncertain.

</div>

These unresolved questions explain why the Thiaroye massacre continues to occupy a unique place in Senegal's historical memory. The mystery is not about whether the tragedy occurred, but about how many lives were erased from the historical record and whether the missing evidence can ever be recovered.
