## Introduction

The death of Sir Edward Mutesa II, the Kabaka (King) of Buganda and [Uganda]({{ 'what-still-haunts-ugandas-historical/' | relative_url }})'s first president, remains one of the country's most debated historical episodes. On 21 November 1969, three years after fleeing into exile following the constitutional crisis that brought him into conflict with Prime Minister Milton Obote, Mutesa was found dead in his London flat at the age of 45. The official medical conclusion was clear: acute alcohol poisoning. Yet almost from the moment his death became public, supporters questioned whether a political exile who had become a powerful symbol of resistance had really died by his own actions. More than half a century later, the central mystery is not the medical diagnosis itself but whether anyone deliberately caused or hastened his death. The available evidence provides no definitive proof of assassination, but neither has it entirely silenced suspicion in Buganda's historical memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/magazines/people-power/inside-the-dark-history-of-poison-allegations-in-uganda-s-politics-3792558" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the dark history of poison allegations in Uganda’s politics &#124; MonitorApril 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 2022</span></span></span>

## The documented medical account

By the time of his death, Mutesa was living in modest circumstances in Rotherhithe, south-east London. Once a constitutional monarch and head of state, he had spent several years in exile after the 1966 assault on his palace by Ugandan government forces. During exile he wrote his memoir, *The Desecration of My Kingdom*, while remaining a politically significant figure for many Baganda supporters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutesa_II_of_Buganda" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutesa II of Buganda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutesa II of Buganda</span></span></span>

The official investigation concluded that Mutesa died from acute alcohol poisoning. The coroner's inquest, completed in London on 28 November 1969, heard pathological evidence that his blood alcohol concentration greatly exceeded the level generally regarded as potentially fatal. Contemporary reports record a reading of approximately 406 milligrams per 100 millilitres of blood, substantially above the threshold considered capable of causing death. The medical findings themselves have never been overturned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/magazines/people-power/inside-the-dark-history-of-poison-allegations-in-uganda-s-politics-3792558" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the dark history of poison allegations in Uganda’s politics &#124; MonitorApril 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 2022</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. The accepted medical cause of death was alcohol poisoning. The unresolved question concerns how that fatal level of alcohol entered his body and whether he consumed it voluntarily.

## Why assassination claims took hold

Suspicion did not arise in a political vacuum. Mutesa had been driven from Uganda after one of the country's most dramatic constitutional confrontations. His palace had been attacked, the Buganda monarchy stripped of political authority, and he had become an internationally recognised opponent of Obote's government. Many supporters therefore found it difficult to believe that such a prominent exile had died without political involvement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutesa_II_of_Buganda" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutesa II of Buganda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutesa II of Buganda</span></span></span>

Several factors encouraged alternative explanations:

* **His political importance.** Although living abroad, Mutesa remained an influential symbol for Buganda royalists and opponents of Obote.
* **His relatively young age.** Dying at 45 intensified disbelief among supporters.
* **The circumstances of exile.** Years of political persecution made allegations of foul play appear plausible to many contemporaries.
* **Distrust of the Ugandan government.** Given the violence surrounding Uganda's politics during the late 1960s, rumours of assassination found a receptive audience even without direct evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/magazines/people-power/inside-the-dark-history-of-poison-allegations-in-uganda-s-politics-3792558" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the dark history of poison allegations in Uganda’s politics &#124; MonitorApril 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 2022</span></span></span>

The assassination theory therefore emerged as much from political context as from forensic uncertainty.

## What evidence supports the assassination theory?

The most frequently cited point is the recollection of British journalist John Simpson, who interviewed Mutesa in his flat only hours before his death. Simpson later stated that he found him sober, coherent and in good spirits. Learning of the death the following day, he informed police that he considered the sudden official explanation difficult to reconcile with the condition in which he had left Mutesa only hours earlier. Supporters have long argued that this timeline deserved greater investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutesa_II_of_Buganda" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutesa II of Buganda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutesa II of Buganda</span></span></span>

Another recurring claim is that agents loyal to Obote may have forced Mutesa to drink large quantities of vodka or otherwise administered alcohol against his will. This allegation has appeared repeatedly in popular retellings and political discussion.

The difficulty is that no publicly available forensic evidence has ever demonstrated forced administration of alcohol, identified attackers, or linked any Ugandan officials directly to the death. The claim rests largely on inference from political motive rather than verifiable physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutesa_II_of_Buganda" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutesa II of Buganda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutesa II of Buganda</span></span></span>

## What weakens the assassination claim?

The principal weakness is the absence of corroborating evidence.

The coroner's inquest did not report injuries or forensic findings pointing to homicide. Likewise, no authenticated witness has emerged describing an attack inside the flat, nor have official records surfaced demonstrating that foreign agents entered the property or participated in his death. The available documentary record therefore supports the medical diagnosis of alcohol poisoning while remaining silent on any proven criminal intervention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/magazines/people-power/inside-the-dark-history-of-poison-allegations-in-uganda-s-politics-3792558" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the dark history of poison allegations in Uganda’s politics &#124; MonitorApril 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 2022</span></span></span>

Later historical research has also shown how rumours developed among Ugandan exiles. Political scientist A. B. K. Kasozi, in *The Bitter Bread of Exile*, notes that British officials believed rumours of poisoning were circulating almost immediately among Buganda refugees in London. His work does not dismiss every suspicion outright, but neither does it produce documentary proof of assassination. Instead, it illustrates how exile politics, grief and distrust shaped competing narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/magazines/people-power/inside-the-dark-history-of-poison-allegations-in-uganda-s-politics-3792558" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the dark history of poison allegations in Uganda’s politics &#124; MonitorApril 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 2022</span></span></span>

## What can and cannot be proved today?

Several points are well established.

**Well supported by evidence**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Mutesa died in London on 21 November 1969 while living in exile.
* A formal coroner's inquest determined that the medical cause of death was acute alcohol poisoning.
* He had become a major political opponent of Obote following the 1966 constitutional crisis.
* Assassination rumours appeared almost immediately after his death and have persisted for decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/magazines/people-power/inside-the-dark-history-of-poison-allegations-in-uganda-s-politics-3792558" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[monitor.co.ug]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the dark history of poison allegations in Uganda’s politics &#124; MonitorApril 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 2022</span></span></span>

</div>

**Still unproven**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* That Ugandan government agents entered his flat.
* That he was forcibly given alcohol.
* That British investigators suppressed conclusive evidence of murder.
* That any individual can be directly connected to causing his death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/magazines/people-power/inside-the-dark-history-of-poison-allegations-in-uganda-s-politics-3792558" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the dark history of poison allegations in Uganda’s politics &#124; MonitorApril 24, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 24, 2022</span></span></span>

</div>

In other words, motive can be argued more easily than mechanism. Historians recognise that powerful political reasons existed for wishing Mutesa removed from public life, but motive alone cannot establish responsibility.

## Why the mystery still matters

Mutesa's death has become inseparable from the wider history of Uganda's troubled transition from monarchy to republican rule. When Idi Amin overthrew Milton Obote in 1971, Mutesa's remains were returned to Uganda for a state funeral, further elevating him as a symbol of Buganda's suffering and resilience. His reputation evolved from deposed monarch to national martyr in the eyes of many supporters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutesa_II_of_Buganda" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutesa II of Buganda</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutesa II of Buganda</span></span></span>

Because of that symbolic status, debate over his death has never been solely about forensic medicine. It reflects larger questions about political violence, trust in official investigations, and the lasting consequences of Uganda's constitutional crisis. Unlike some historical mysteries, this one does not survive because no explanation exists. It survives because the documented medical explanation has never fully answered the political questions that many Ugandans continue to ask.

The balance of the evidence today favours the official conclusion that Edward Mutesa II died from acute alcohol poisoning. However, the circumstances surrounding his final hours remain incompletely documented, and no evidence decisive enough either to confirm or conclusively eliminate assassination has emerged. That combination of a clear medical finding and an unresolved political context explains why his death remains one of Uganda's most enduring historical controversies.
