## Introduction

The mystery surrounding Salaspils is not whether terrible crimes occurred there, but how those crimes came to be remembered through radically different narratives. During the Nazi occupation of [Latvia]({{ 'latvia/' | relative_url }}), Salaspils was a camp of imprisonment, forced labour, disease and mass suffering. Thousands of people—including political prisoners, civilians from Latvia, Belarus and Russia, Jews forced to build the camp, and many children—passed through it. Yet for decades the camp was also presented as a place where more than 100,000 people were murdered, a figure that later archival research has found to be unsupported. The enduring historical puzzle is therefore not the existence of atrocity, but why the numbers became so fiercely disputed, how Soviet propaganda reshaped public memory, and what modern historians believe can be established from surviving evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salaspilsmemorials.lv/en/salaspils-camp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salaspilsmemorials.lv">[salaspilsmemorials.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salaspilsmemorials.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span></span></span>

## What Was Salaspils?

Salaspils lay about 18 kilometres southeast of Riga. It was established by Nazi German occupation authorities in late 1941 and officially operated as an "extended police prison and labour correction camp" under the Security Police and Security Service (SD), rather than as one of the centrally administered concentration camps run from Berlin. That distinction affected its administration but not the harshness of conditions experienced by prisoners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salaspilsmemorials.lv/en/salaspils-camp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salaspilsmemorials.lv">[salaspilsmemorials.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salaspilsmemorials.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span></span></span>

Construction itself was lethal. Jews deported from Germany, Austria and what is now the Czech Republic were forced to build the camp during the winter of 1941–42 under appalling conditions, and many died before the camp was fully operational. Over the following years Salaspils served several purposes, including imprisonment of political detainees, punishment of alleged labour offenders, detention of members of Baltic military formations convicted by SS courts, and use as a transit camp for civilians deported during anti-partisan operations in Belarus, western Russia and eastern Latvia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salaspilsmemorials.lv/en/salaspils-camp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salaspilsmemorials.lv">[salaspilsmemorials.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salaspilsmemorials.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span></span></span>

Unlike extermination centres created specifically for industrialised mass murder, Salaspils functioned as a complex detention and forced-labour camp. Nevertheless, prisoners died from starvation, disease, exposure, brutal punishment, shootings and the consequences of forced labour. Children suffered especially high mortality from epidemics and malnutrition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salaspilsmemorials.lv/en/salaspils-camp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salaspilsmemorials.lv">[salaspilsmemorials.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salaspilsmemorials.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span></span></span>

## Who Was Imprisoned at Salaspils?

One reason the camp's history became confusing is that its prisoner population changed repeatedly.

Historical research indicates that around 23,000 people passed through the camp over its existence, although not all were held there for the same length of time. The main prisoner groups included:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Latvian political prisoners and resistance suspects.
* People accused of avoiding compulsory labour.
* Jews forced into camp construction during its earliest phase.
* Civilians deported during German anti-partisan campaigns in Belarus and Russia.
* Adults and children from Latgale and neighbouring occupied territories awaiting deportation for forced labour.
* Members of Baltic military formations imprisoned by German authorities for disciplinary offences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salaspilsmemorials.lv/en/salaspils-camp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salaspilsmemorials.lv">[salaspilsmemorials.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salaspilsmemorials.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span></span></span>

</div>

Children occupy a particularly painful place in the camp's history. Many were separated from parents during anti-partisan deportations. Poor sanitation, inadequate food and infectious diseases caused hundreds of child deaths. Excavations after the war uncovered numerous children's graves, confirming severe mortality even though later researchers rejected many sensational claims that circulated during the Soviet period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salaspilsmemorials.lv/en/salaspils-camp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salaspilsmemorials.lv">[salaspilsmemorials.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salaspilsmemorials.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span></span></span>

## Why Did Soviet Accounts Claim More Than 100,000 Deaths?

The greatest historical controversy concerns the reported death toll.

During the Soviet period, official publications, memorial displays and school education frequently stated that more than 100,000 people had been murdered at Salaspils. Some accounts also alleged systematic medical blood extraction from children for German soldiers, presenting the camp as one of the Nazi regime's largest death camps. These claims became deeply embedded in Soviet public memory and were repeated for decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9789633867969" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliovault.org">[bibliovault.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliovault.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beyond Nazi Crimes and Soviet Propaganda: The Salaspils Camp in Latvia, 1941-1944 (9789633867969): Karlis Kangeris, Uldis Neiburgs and Ru...</span></span></span>

Modern historians generally agree that these figures were politically useful to Soviet authorities for several reasons.

First, they reinforced the narrative of the Soviet Union as the liberator of Europe from fascism. Second, they emphasised Nazi crimes while diverting attention from Soviet repression in Latvia. Third, exceptionally large numbers helped portray Latvia primarily as a victim of fascism while also supporting Soviet accusations against alleged Latvian collaborators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=677938" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ceeol.com">[ceeol.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ceeol.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">article detail</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">article detail</span></span></span>

The enormous casualty figure became difficult to challenge because wartime records had been destroyed, many witnesses were dead or dispersed, and independent historical research was impossible inside the Soviet Union for decades. Official numbers therefore acquired political authority long before researchers gained access to German, Latvian and Russian archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/293733162_Rendez-vous_manque_entre_histoire_et_memoire_Le_camp_de_concentration_de_Salaspils_en_Lettonie" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

## What Later Research Can and Cannot Establish

Following Latvia's restored independence, historians re-examined Salaspils using surviving German documents, Soviet investigative files, archaeological evidence and witness testimony.

The broad conclusions are now considerably more precise than they were during the Cold War.

Researchers broadly agree that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Around 23,000 prisoners passed through the camp.
* Approximately 1,000 Jews died during construction in the winter of 1941–42.
* Hundreds of additional prisoners died from disease, starvation, abuse and forced labour.
* Several hundred children died because of hunger, illness and epidemics.
* The total death toll, including those who died during construction, is best estimated at well over 3,000 rather than 100,000.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salaspilsmemorials.lv/en/salaspils-camp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salaspilsmemorials.lv">[salaspilsmemorials.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salaspilsmemorials.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp – Salaspils Memoriāls</span></span></span>

</div>

Even these figures are not mathematically certain.

Some documentation disappeared during the German retreat, while many victims were transferred elsewhere or died after removal from the camp. Historians therefore distinguish carefully between documented deaths inside Salaspils, deaths linked to construction, and broader suffering connected to deportation operations that extended well beyond the camp itself. The uncertainty lies in refining totals, not in questioning whether mass suffering occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9789633867969" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliovault.org">[bibliovault.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliovault.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beyond Nazi Crimes and Soviet Propaganda: The Salaspils Camp in Latvia, 1941-1944 (9789633867969): Karlis Kangeris, Uldis Neiburgs and Ru...</span></span></span>

## Why the Numbers Matter

The dispute is about more than statistics.

For survivors and descendants, reducing inflated figures has sometimes been interpreted as minimising Nazi crimes. Conversely, historians argue that correcting exaggerated numbers strengthens rather than weakens the historical record because it allows documented evidence to replace politically motivated claims. The central challenge is to avoid two opposite distortions: repeating unsupported Soviet-era figures or using their correction to diminish the real brutality of the camp.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9789633867969" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliovault.org">[bibliovault.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliovault.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beyond Nazi Crimes and Soviet Propaganda: The Salaspils Camp in Latvia, 1941-1944 (9789633867969): Karlis Kangeris, Uldis Neiburgs and Ru...</span></span></span>

Recent scholarship stresses that Salaspils was unquestionably a site of severe persecution and many preventable deaths. Rejecting the figure of 100,000 victims does not make the camp benign or ordinary. Instead, it places Salaspils within the documented system of Nazi occupation, forced labour and repression in the Baltic region while distinguishing verified history from later political mythmaking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9789633867969" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliovault.org">[bibliovault.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliovault.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beyond Nazi Crimes and Soviet Propaganda: The Salaspils Camp in Latvia, 1941-1944 (9789633867969): Karlis Kangeris, Uldis Neiburgs and Ru...</span></span></span>

## How Public Memory Continues to Change

The memorial complex at Salaspils, inaugurated during the Soviet era, remains one of Latvia's most important Second World War memorials. However, its interpretation has evolved considerably.

A new permanent exhibition introduced in 2018 was designed to explain both the history of the camp and the history of the memorial itself. Rather than simply replacing one narrative with another, it explicitly addresses how Soviet authorities shaped public understanding of Salaspils and presents evidence drawn from more recent archival research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://salaspilsmemorials.lv/en/exposition/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: salaspilsmemorials.lv">[salaspilsmemorials.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">salaspilsmemorials.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Exposition – Salaspils Memoriāls</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Exposition – Salaspils Memoriāls</span></span></span>

## The Enduring Historical Mystery

Salaspils illustrates an unusual kind of historical mystery. The greatest uncertainty is not hidden graves or unknown perpetrators, but how collective memory itself became distorted.

The core facts are now much clearer than they once were: Salaspils was a Nazi camp where thousands suffered, thousands died and children endured particularly horrific conditions. What remains historically significant is the transformation of those real crimes into a Cold War symbol whose inflated casualty figures obscured rather than clarified the camp's true history. The continuing task for historians is to reconstruct the past as accurately as surviving evidence permits while recognising both the brutality of the camp and the powerful politics that reshaped its memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9789633867969" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bibliovault.org">[bibliovault.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bibliovault.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beyond Nazi Crimes and Soviet Propaganda: The Salaspils Camp in Latvia, 1941-1944 (9789633867969): Karlis Kangeris, Uldis Neiburgs and Ru...</span></span></span>
