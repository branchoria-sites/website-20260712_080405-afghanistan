## Introduction

Is [Paraguay]({{ 'what-still-haunts-paraguays-historical/' | relative_url }})'s lost war treasure real? The short answer is that **small wartime caches almost certainly existed, but the legendary national fortune has never been demonstrated by reliable evidence**. The devastation of the Paraguayan War (1864–1870) forced soldiers, civilians, churches and government officials to move valuables repeatedly as armies advanced. In such conditions, some money, jewellery, silverware and military funds were undoubtedly hidden and, in some cases, never recovered. What remains unproven is the enduring legend that an enormous state treasure belonging to President Francisco Solano López or the Paraguayan government still lies buried somewhere in the country.

The mystery survives because it combines genuine historical events with folklore, incomplete records and generations of unsuccessful treasure hunting. The result is one of Paraguay's best-known historical legends: a story grounded in real wartime chaos but enlarged over time into a national myth.

## Why valuables were hidden during the war

The origins of the treasure stories are entirely plausible. As the war turned against Paraguay, government offices, military units and civilians repeatedly abandoned towns ahead of advancing Allied forces. People had strong reasons to conceal anything of value rather than leave it to looters or occupying armies.

Several circumstances encouraged temporary burial of valuables:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Families hid jewellery, silver objects and coins before fleeing.
* Churches attempted to protect liturgical vessels and other precious items.
* Military columns transported payroll funds, equipment and official property through increasingly dangerous territory.
* Retreating officials occasionally concealed goods with the intention of recovering them after the fighting.

</div>

The occupation and looting of Asunción in early 1869 further strengthened these fears. Contemporary accounts describe widespread searches for hidden wealth, with occupying troops digging through houses and courtyards in the expectation that valuables had been buried before the city's capture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sesquicentenario.gov.py/materiales/el-saqueo-de-asuncion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sesquicentenario.gov.py">[sesquicentenario.gov.py]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sesquicentenario.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Saqueo de Asunción</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sesquicentenario de la Epopeya NacionalSesquicentenario de la Epopeya NacionalJanuary 2, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 2, 2020</span></span></span>

These documented events provide a realistic foundation for later treasure stories. They do not, however, prove the existence of a vast hidden national fortune.

## The legend of the missing war fortune

The best-known version of the mystery claims that President Francisco Solano López ordered a large quantity of gold, silver, jewels or government wealth buried during the final retreat towards Cerro Corá, where he was killed in March 1870.

Different versions place the treasure in different locations. Some associate it with López's companion, Elisa Lynch, alleging that she knew the hiding place. Others describe secret wagons disappearing into forests or mountains before the final battles. Still others speak of military officers burying chests during hurried marches.

The difficulty is that these stories rarely agree on the essentials:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The supposed size of the treasure varies enormously.
* The alleged burial sites change from one account to another.
* No contemporary inventory identifies a missing cache matching the later legends.
* None of the famous treasure maps has been authenticated by professional historians.

</div>

Rather than a single coherent mystery, the "missing war fortune" is better understood as a collection of overlapping stories that grew from genuine wartime losses.

## What treasure hunters claim to have found

Claims of discoveries have appeared almost continuously since the nineteenth century. Some involve isolated finds that are entirely believable. Farmers, builders and landowners have occasionally reported uncovering old coins, jewellery or metal objects dating from the war period.

These discoveries support the idea that **small caches existed**. They do not support claims of a gigantic buried treasury.

More ambitious expeditions have repeatedly searched for the supposed fortune of Solano López. Well-known individuals joined the hunt within years of the war's end, while foreign expeditions later attempted to locate the treasure without success. Historian Alfredo Boccia Romañach notes that these searches repeatedly failed despite widespread publicity and popular fascination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.portalguarani.com/620_alfredo_boccia_romanach/20576_los_tesoros_ocultos_del_mariscal__por_alfredo_boccia_romanach__ano_2005.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: portalguarani.com">[portalguarani.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">portalguarani.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Portal Guaraní</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LOS TESOROS OCULTOS DEL MARISCAL - Por ALFREDO BOCCIA ROMAÑACH - Año 2005 - ALFREDO BOCCIA ROMAÑACH (+)January 1, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2005</span></span></span>

Modern newspaper investigations have reached a similar conclusion. Historians reviewing surviving evidence acknowledge that military convoys probably lost or concealed some valuable cargo during the retreat, but they find no convincing documentation for an immense hidden fortune awaiting discovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lanacion.com.py/estilodevida/2024/08/13/mito-o-realidad-los-tesoros-enterrados-de-la-guerra-contra-la-triple-alianza/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lanacion.com.py">[La Nación]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lanacion.com.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La NaciónLa Nación / Mito o realidad: los tesoros enterrados de la guerra contra la Triple AlianzaAugust 11, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Where folklore overtakes archaeological evidence

The treasure mystery becomes far more elaborate once it enters Paraguayan folklore.

A central tradition is **Plata Yvyguy**, often translated as "buried silver" or "buried treasure". Although the idea has older roots, the Paraguayan War greatly expanded the legend by providing a historical explanation for why valuables might lie underground.

According to popular belief, hidden treasure may reveal itself through supernatural signs such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* mysterious flames appearing above the ground;
* a white or headless dog;
* ghostly figures;
* strange lights visible only to the person destined to find the treasure.

</div>

Some versions even warn that revealing the discovery to others causes the treasure to disappear or brings misfortune to the finder. These stories remain an important part of Paraguayan folklore and are often told independently of any archaeological evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lanacion.com.py/revista-vos/2020/10/21/leyendas-urbanas-que-asustan-historias-que-se-cuentan-en-asuncion/?outputType=amp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lanacion.com.py">[La Nación]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lanacion.com.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La NaciónLa Nación / Leyendas urbanas que asustan, historias que se cuentan en AsunciónOctober 21, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 21, 2020</span></span></span>

Professional archaeology approaches such claims differently. Without documented provenance, excavation records or securely dated finds, legends cannot establish the existence of a historical treasure. Folklore may preserve memories of wartime hiding places, but it cannot by itself identify genuine archaeological sites.

## Why historians remain sceptical

Several historical considerations argue against the idea of an enormous undiscovered national treasure.

First, Paraguay before the war was **not a country overflowing with gold coinage**. Historians note that cash circulation was relatively limited, barter remained common in many transactions, and wealth was often held in practical goods rather than spectacular hoards of precious metals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lanacion.com.py/estilodevida/2024/08/13/mito-o-realidad-los-tesoros-enterrados-de-la-guerra-contra-la-triple-alianza/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lanacion.com.py">[La Nación]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lanacion.com.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La NaciónLa Nación / Mito o realidad: los tesoros enterrados de la guerra contra la Triple AlianzaAugust 11, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2024</span></span></span>

Second, the final months of the war were marked by constant movement. Retreating columns faced pursuit, shortages and confusion. If valuables were buried temporarily, many of those involved either died before returning or were unable to relocate the sites afterwards. That situation could easily explain scattered unrecovered caches without requiring a single massive hidden treasury.

Third, many supposed treasure maps have highly questionable origins. Recent discussion surrounding alleged historical maps has prompted criticism from Paraguay's historical community, which has questioned their authenticity and warned against accepting attractive stories without documentary support.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lanacion.com.py/estilodevida/2024/08/13/mito-o-realidad-los-tesoros-enterrados-de-la-guerra-contra-la-triple-alianza/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lanacion.com.py">[La Nación]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lanacion.com.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La NaciónLa Nación / Mito o realidad: los tesoros enterrados de la guerra contra la Triple AlianzaAugust 11, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2024</span></span></span>

Finally, more than 150 years of searching has produced no verified discovery matching the scale described in the legends.

## Why the mystery endures

The enduring appeal of Paraguay's lost war treasure has less to do with hidden gold than with the trauma of the Paraguayan War itself.

The conflict destroyed communities, scattered families and erased countless personal records. In that setting, stories about buried wealth offered hope that something valuable from the lost world before the war might still survive beneath the ground.

The legend also reflects broader themes that recur throughout Paraguay's historical memory:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the sudden disappearance of people and property during wartime;
* uncertainty created by incomplete records;
* the blending of documented events with oral tradition;
* the belief that history still conceals undiscovered truths.

</div>

For these reasons, the mystery remains culturally significant even though its strongest claims lack convincing evidence.

Today, the most balanced assessment is that **small wartime caches almost certainly existed and some may never have been recovered**. By contrast, the legendary hidden fortune of Francisco Solano López or a vast buried national treasury remains unsupported by reliable documentary, archaeological or archival evidence. The mystery persists because history left genuine gaps, while folklore filled those gaps with stories compelling enough to outlive the events that inspired them.
