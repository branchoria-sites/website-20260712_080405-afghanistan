## Introduction

Has Alexander the Great's lost city in modern [Uzbekistan]({{ 'which-mysteries-still-haunt-uzbekistans/' | relative_url }}) finally been found? Kampyr Tepe is now one of the strongest candidates for the ancient city known as **Alexandria on the Oxus**, a settlement founded during Alexander's campaigns in Central Asia in the late fourth century BC. Archaeological excavations have uncovered a heavily fortified Hellenistic town in exactly the kind of strategic position that ancient writers described: beside a major crossing of the Oxus (today's Amu Darya), on the route linking Bactria with Sogdia. Yet one crucial piece of evidence is still missing. No surviving inscription states the city's ancient name. As a result, Kampyr Tepe remains a remarkable archaeological discovery whose identity is highly persuasive but not universally accepted. The mystery lies not in whether the city existed, but in whether these ruins truly belong to one of Alexander's legendary foundations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://society.uz/edition/13" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: society.uz">[Society of Uzbekistan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">society.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Society of UzbekistanE. V. Rtveladze, Alexandria of Oxiana — Kampyrtepa: a Fortress City on the Banks of the Oxus...</span></span></span>

## Why Kampyr Tepe is a leading candidate

Kampyr Tepe lies about 30 kilometres northwest of Termez in southern Uzbekistan, overlooking what was once the main course of the Amu Darya. Excavations beginning in the 1970s and greatly expanded after 2000 have revealed a carefully planned fortified settlement with occupation beginning in the late fourth century BC, exactly when Alexander conquered Bactria after defeating the Achaemenid Persian Empire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampir_Tepe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampir Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampir Tepe</span></span></span>

Several features make the site unusually convincing.

* **Its date fits Alexander's campaign.** The earliest occupation belongs to the period immediately following the Macedonian conquest, matching the historical window in which Alexander founded or reorganised cities across Central Asia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampir_Tepe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampir Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampir Tepe</span></span></span>
* **Its location is strategically ideal.** The settlement guarded an important crossing of the Oxus, controlling movement between Bactria and the northern territories where Alexander spent years suppressing revolts. Ancient historians repeatedly emphasise the military importance of these river crossings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.livius.org/articles/place/kampyr-tepe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livius.org">[Livius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livius.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampyr Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampyr Tepe - Livius...</span></span></span>
* **Its town plan has Hellenistic characteristics.** Archaeologists have identified defensive walls, gates and urban planning techniques associated with the Greek world, although local Central Asian traditions also remained visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampir_Tepe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampir Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampir Tepe</span></span></span>
* **Its later history makes sense.** Rather than disappearing after Alexander's death, the city continued under the Graeco-Bactrian and Kushan kingdoms, showing how one military colony evolved into a long-lived regional centre.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampir_Tepe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampir Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampir Tepe</span></span></span>

The site's principal excavator, Edvard Rtveladze, argued that the accumulated archaeological, geographical and historical evidence identifies Kampyr Tepe with the city that the geographer Ptolemy later called Alexandria on the Oxus. This conclusion has been influential, especially within Uzbek archaeology, although not every specialist considers the matter settled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://society.uz/edition/13" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: society.uz">[Society of Uzbekistan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">society.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Society of UzbekistanE. V. Rtveladze, Alexandria of Oxiana — Kampyrtepa: a Fortress City on the Banks of the Oxus...</span></span></span>

## Why the identification remains disputed

The mystery persists because archaeology has produced abundant circumstantial evidence but not the decisive proof historians would ideally like.

The main problem is straightforward: **no inscription discovered at Kampyr Tepe actually names the settlement**. Ancient cities often become securely identified only after excavators recover inscriptions mentioning the place by name or documents tying the site directly to known historical events. Kampyr Tepe has yielded coins, buildings, fortifications and artefacts, but nothing that explicitly says "Alexandria on the Oxus."<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampir_Tepe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampir Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampir Tepe</span></span></span>

There are additional reasons for caution.

Ancient writers themselves are not perfectly consistent. Alexander founded, refounded or renamed numerous settlements during his eastern campaigns, and surviving texts sometimes disagree about their locations or even their names. Rivers also shifted course dramatically over the last two thousand years, complicating efforts to match ancient geographical descriptions with modern landscapes. The Amu Darya has repeatedly altered its channel, meaning that today's landscape differs substantially from the one known to Alexander.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.livius.org/articles/place/kampyr-tepe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livius.org">[Livius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livius.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampyr Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampyr Tepe - Livius...</span></span></span>

For these reasons, many historians describe Kampyr Tepe not as a proven identification but as the strongest available hypothesis.

## How rival sites compare with the ruins

For much of the twentieth century, another famous Hellenistic city dominated discussion of Alexandria on the Oxus: **Ai Khanoum**, in present-day Afghanistan.

Ai Khanoum is undeniably one of the greatest Greek cities ever excavated in Central Asia. It possesses monumental Greek architecture, theatres, gymnasia and inscriptions, making it an obvious candidate for one of Alexander's foundations. However, its identification specifically as Alexandria on the Oxus has become less certain over time. Some scholars believe it was founded slightly later or developed under the Seleucid rulers rather than by Alexander himself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.livius.org/articles/place/kampyr-tepe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livius.org">[Livius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livius.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampyr Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampyr Tepe - Livius...</span></span></span>

Compared with Ai Khanoum, Kampyr Tepe offers several advantages for those arguing it represents Alexander's city.


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* It appears to begin closer to the date of Alexander's occupation.
* It directly commands a major Oxus crossing emphasised in ancient military campaigns.
* Its fortification pattern resembles a military colony established during active conquest rather than an already mature Hellenistic metropolis.
* It fits descriptions of a frontier stronghold linking Bactria with the routes into Sogdia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://society.uz/edition/13" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: society.uz">[Society of Uzbekistan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">society.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Society of UzbekistanE. V. Rtveladze, Alexandria of Oxiana — Kampyrtepa: a Fortress City on the Banks of the Oxus...</span></span></span>

</div>

None of these arguments is individually conclusive, but together they explain why many recent researchers regard Kampyr Tepe as a particularly persuasive candidate.

## What evidence could finally settle the name

The debate could end surprisingly quickly if archaeologists uncover the right object.

The most valuable discoveries would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a stone inscription naming the settlement;
* an official dedication mentioning Alexander or Alexandria;
* administrative tablets identifying local officials and the city's name;
* coins carrying an unmistakable city legend from its earliest phase;
* securely dated documents linking the site to descriptions preserved by ancient writers.

</div>

Archaeology occasionally resolves debates through exactly this kind of discovery. Until then, researchers must rely on the cumulative weight of geography, chronology, architecture and historical reconstruction rather than one decisive piece of evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://society.uz/edition/13" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: society.uz">[Society of Uzbekistan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">society.uz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Society of UzbekistanE. V. Rtveladze, Alexandria of Oxiana — Kampyrtepa: a Fortress City on the Banks of the Oxus...</span></span></span>

Continued excavation may also clarify the settlement's earliest occupation layers. Much of the visible architecture belongs to later Kushan rebuilding, while the Hellenistic foundations lie beneath. Every new excavation into those earliest levels has the potential to reveal evidence unavailable to previous generations of archaeologists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampir_Tepe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampir Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampir Tepe</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery still matters

The question is more than an exercise in matching ancient names to ruined walls. Identifying Alexandria on the Oxus would improve historians' understanding of how Alexander secured one of the most difficult regions of his empire after years of rebellion. It would also illuminate the beginnings of the Graeco-Bactrian world, where Greek and Central Asian traditions mixed to create one of antiquity's most distinctive frontier cultures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.livius.org/articles/place/kampyr-tepe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: livius.org">[Livius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">livius.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kampyr Tepe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kampyr Tepe - Livius...</span></span></span>

For Uzbekistan, Kampyr Tepe represents one of the country's most important archaeological sites because it demonstrates the depth of Hellenistic influence south of the Oxus while preserving centuries of later occupation. Even if the ancient name is never proved beyond doubt, the site has transformed historians' understanding of settlement along the Amu Darya during and after Alexander's campaigns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uzbekistan.travel/en/o/kampyrtepa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uzbekistan.travel">[Uzbekistan Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uzbekistan.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel&#124; Uzbekistan Travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Travel&#124; Uzbekistan Travel</span></span></span>

The enduring mystery is therefore unusually well defined. Archaeologists know they have uncovered an authentic Hellenistic fortress city established at exactly the right moment in history. They can reconstruct much of its layout, trace its later development and explain its strategic role. What they still cannot do with complete confidence is answer the simplest question of all: what did its inhabitants call it?
