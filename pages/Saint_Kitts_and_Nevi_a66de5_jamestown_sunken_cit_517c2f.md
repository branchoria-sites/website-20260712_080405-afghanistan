## Introduction

The story of Jamestown on Nevis is often told as one of the Caribbean's own lost cities: a thriving colonial settlement swallowed by the sea after a devastating earthquake and tsunami in 1690, leaving only submerged ruins and ghostly legends behind. It is an appealing tale, but modern historical and archaeological research paints a more nuanced picture. The evidence strongly supports a destructive earthquake and abnormal sea activity in 1690, yet it does not support the popular claim that an entire town vanished beneath the waves in a single catastrophic moment. Instead, Jamestown appears to have declined through a combination of earthquake damage, coastal change and gradual abandonment, with later generations transforming a genuine disaster into one of [Saint Kitts and Nevis]({{ 'saint-kitts-and-nevis/' | relative_url }})'s most enduring historical legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/colonial-landscape-of-the-british-caribbean/jamestown-nevis-fact-fiction-and-fable/1BDAC4CABE0ABC290D8A17AADCC7F686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentJamestown, Nevis: Fact, Fiction, and Fable (Chapter 9) - The Colonial Landscape of the British Car...</span></span></span>

## What Jamestown and Morton's Bay Actually Were

One reason the legend has proved so persistent is that the historical settlement itself was never as straightforward as later accounts suggested.

Seventeenth-century documents refer far more often to **Morton's Bay** than to a town called Jamestown. Contemporary records describe it as an important trading place on Nevis's western coast, but not as a large urban centre. In a 1676 report, Governor William Stapleton described Morton's Bay as one of the island's two principal trading locations, yet noted that it contained relatively few houses because ships anchored elsewhere and collected cargo by boat. This suggests a commercial port and anchorage rather than a densely populated colonial capital.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.southampton.ac.uk/assets/imported/transforms/content-block/UsefulDownloads_Download/4B532860420C4E4EA83B3291B94EBA40/nevis_2002_Theme_two.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southampton.ac.uk">[University of Southampton]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southampton.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University of Southampton CHAPTER TWO: COLONIAL FORTIFICATIONS (THEME TWO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of SouthamptonCHAPTER TWO: COLONIAL FORTIFICATIONS (THEME TWO)December 5, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2025</span></span></span>

Archaeological investigations carried out through the Nevis Heritage Project identified foundations, warehouses, domestic structures and defensive works associated with this coastal settlement. These discoveries confirmed that an active community existed there during the seventeenth century, but they do not match the image of a substantial city stretching along the shoreline. Instead, researchers describe a dispersed settlement whose importance came from trade rather than size.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/colonial-landscape-of-the-british-caribbean/jamestown-nevis-fact-fiction-and-fable/1BDAC4CABE0ABC290D8A17AADCC7F686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentJamestown, Nevis: Fact, Fiction, and Fable (Chapter 9) - The Colonial Landscape of the British Car...</span></span></span>

Researchers have also questioned the traditional story that Jamestown was founded in 1607 by settlers travelling to Virginia. Documentary evidence places the emergence of the settlement much later, with references appearing only from the early 1670s. Some historians argue that the name "Jamestown" itself may have been adopted only after the accession of King James II in 1685, replacing the earlier name of Morton's Bay.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.southampton.ac.uk/assets/imported/transforms/content-block/UsefulDownloads_Download/4B532860420C4E4EA83B3291B94EBA40/nevis_2002_Theme_two.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southampton.ac.uk">[University of Southampton]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southampton.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University of Southampton CHAPTER TWO: COLONIAL FORTIFICATIONS (THEME TWO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of SouthamptonCHAPTER TWO: COLONIAL FORTIFICATIONS (THEME TWO)December 5, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2025</span></span></span>

## What the 1690 Earthquake Evidence Shows

The earthquake itself is not mythical. Contemporary reports describe a severe seismic event striking the Leeward Islands in April 1690.

One eyewitness recalled hearing a deep rumbling from beneath Nevis before violent shaking caused stone and brick buildings in Charlestown to collapse. The sea reportedly withdrew far from the shoreline, leaving fish stranded on exposed sand before surging back repeatedly. These observations closely resemble behaviour now recognised as consistent with tsunami generation following a major earthquake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/colonial-landscape-of-the-british-caribbean/jamestown-nevis-fact-fiction-and-fable/1BDAC4CABE0ABC290D8A17AADCC7F686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentJamestown, Nevis: Fact, Fiction, and Fable (Chapter 9) - The Colonial Landscape of the British Car...</span></span></span>

Archaeological excavations have uncovered evidence compatible with sudden structural damage. At one excavation trench near the former shoreline, investigators found a masonry wall twisted, tilted and partly collapsed towards the sea. While such damage cannot by itself prove tsunami destruction, it is consistent with strong ground movement during the documented earthquake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://den.mercer.edu/040913archeology/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: den.mercer.edu">[The Den]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">den.mercer.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Den Professor on Team to Discover Jamestown</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The DenProfessor on Team to Discover Jamestown - The Den...</span></span></span>

What remains uncertain is the precise scale of the tsunami and how much of the settlement it destroyed. Seventeenth-century documentation is sparse, surviving eyewitness testimony is limited, and later writers often repeated earlier accounts without independent verification. Modern researchers therefore distinguish carefully between the documented earthquake and later embellishments about its consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2011/1133/pdf/ofr2011-1133_212012.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubs.usgs.gov">[U.S. Geological Survey]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubs.usgs.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Geological Survey7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Accounts of Damage from Historical...</span></span></span>

## Did the Town Really Sink Beneath the Sea?

The strongest archaeological conclusion is that the famous image of an entire submerged town is not supported by the available evidence.

Excavations between 2003 and 2006 successfully located the historic settlement. Researchers reconstructed its layout using excavation, historical maps and documentary records. Although they found evidence of destruction by natural forces, they rejected claims that extensive walls or streets now lie intact beneath the sea. The authors of the principal archaeological study explicitly conclude that reports of visible underwater ruins belong to "fiction and fable" rather than demonstrable archaeology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/colonial-landscape-of-the-british-caribbean/jamestown-nevis-fact-fiction-and-fable/1BDAC4CABE0ABC290D8A17AADCC7F686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentJamestown, Nevis: Fact, Fiction, and Fable (Chapter 9) - The Colonial Landscape of the British Car...</span></span></span>

Equally important is the changing coastline. Coastal erosion, sediment movement and later construction altered the landscape substantially after the seventeenth century. Parts of the original shoreline no longer match their historic position, making it easy for later observers to assume that missing remains had disappeared beneath the sea when many had actually been buried, eroded or built over.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/colonial-landscape-of-the-british-caribbean/jamestown-nevis-fact-fiction-and-fable/1BDAC4CABE0ABC290D8A17AADCC7F686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentJamestown, Nevis: Fact, Fiction, and Fable (Chapter 9) - The Colonial Landscape of the British Car...</span></span></span>

## Why the Sunken City Story Endured

The legend survived because several separate traditions reinforced one another over time.

Nineteenth-century writers increasingly described Jamestown in dramatic terms, sometimes presenting the disaster as though an entire colonial town had disappeared instantly beneath the waves. Romantic Victorian travel writing encouraged vivid storytelling, and later guidebooks, tourist literature and local folklore repeated these accounts until they became widely accepted. Archaeologists studying the site's documentary history argue that many familiar details cannot be traced back to seventeenth-century evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.southampton.ac.uk/assets/imported/transforms/content-block/UsefulDownloads_Download/4B532860420C4E4EA83B3291B94EBA40/nevis_2002_Theme_two.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southampton.ac.uk">[University of Southampton]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southampton.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University of Southampton CHAPTER TWO: COLONIAL FORTIFICATIONS (THEME TWO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of SouthamptonCHAPTER TWO: COLONIAL FORTIFICATIONS (THEME TWO)December 5, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2025</span></span></span>

Local folklore added memorable details that transformed history into legend. Stories claimed that church bells could still be heard ringing beneath the sea or that submerged buildings became visible when the water was exceptionally clear. None of these traditions has been supported by archaeological investigation, but they helped keep the story alive across generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.southampton.ac.uk/assets/imported/transforms/content-block/UsefulDownloads_Download/4B532860420C4E4EA83B3291B94EBA40/nevis_2002_Theme_two.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southampton.ac.uk">[University of Southampton]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southampton.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">University of Southampton CHAPTER TWO: COLONIAL FORTIFICATIONS (THEME TWO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of SouthamptonCHAPTER TWO: COLONIAL FORTIFICATIONS (THEME TWO)December 5, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2025</span></span></span>

Natural observations also encouraged belief. After storms, old bricks sometimes appeared on nearby beaches, leading some people to assume they came from submerged buildings. Archaeologists instead suggest that many were probably discarded ballast from seventeenth- and eighteenth-century ships rather than evidence of a drowned town.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Why Jamestown Still Matters

Jamestown remains historically important even without the image of a lost underwater city.

The site provides rare evidence for the development of an early English Caribbean trading settlement and illustrates how colonial communities responded to natural disasters. The combination of documentary research and archaeology has replaced a simple legend with a richer understanding of how settlements evolve, decline and disappear from the visible landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/colonial-landscape-of-the-british-caribbean/jamestown-nevis-fact-fiction-and-fable/1BDAC4CABE0ABC290D8A17AADCC7F686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentJamestown, Nevis: Fact, Fiction, and Fable (Chapter 9) - The Colonial Landscape of the British Car...</span></span></span>

It also serves as an instructive example of how historical memory changes over time. A genuine earthquake became a tale of an entire city swallowed by the sea because damaged landscapes, incomplete records and compelling storytelling encouraged successive generations to fill the gaps. The enduring mystery is therefore not whether Jamestown lies intact beneath the Caribbean, but how a real colonial tragedy was transformed into one of Saint Kitts and Nevis's best-known historical legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/colonial-landscape-of-the-british-caribbean/jamestown-nevis-fact-fiction-and-fable/1BDAC4CABE0ABC290D8A17AADCC7F686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentJamestown, Nevis: Fact, Fiction, and Fable (Chapter 9) - The Colonial Landscape of the British Car...</span></span></span>
