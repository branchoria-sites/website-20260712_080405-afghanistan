## Introduction

The mystery of the English Harbour wreck is no longer centred on whether a large eighteenth-century ship lies beneath Tank Bay in Antigua. Archaeologists have established that a remarkably well-preserved wooden hull survives beneath layers of mud. The remaining puzzle is its precise biography: although the strongest evidence now identifies the vessel as the French-built *Beaumont*, later renamed *Lyon*, important questions remain about how the ship was reused after its capture by the British and exactly why it ended up abandoned and buried in English Harbour.

This is an unusually evidence-rich historical mystery. Rather than relying on folklore, the debate combines archaeology, naval records, historic maps, timber analysis and the physical dimensions of the wreck. Together these strands increasingly point towards one ship, while leaving aspects of its final years unresolved.

## Which ship lies beneath English Harbour?

The wreck lies in Tank Bay at the entrance to English Harbour, close to Nelson's Dockyard. Hidden beneath mud in only a few metres of water, the vessel escaped notice for centuries because only fragments of its upper structure were visible. Modern hydrographic surveys first highlighted an anomaly during work to update navigation charts, and subsequent diving confirmed that it was a substantial wooden ship rather than scattered debris.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.allatsea.net/exciting-discovery-historic-shipwreck-found-in-antigua/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: allatsea.net">[ALL AT SEA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">allatsea.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ALL AT SEAExciting Discovery – Historic Shipwreck Found in Antigua</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ALL AT SEAJuly 27, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

The scale of the remains immediately suggested that this was no ordinary merchant vessel. Archaeologists mapped a hull approximately 40 metres long with heavy framing and extensive surviving timbers. The robust construction indicated a ship built for military or heavily armed commercial service rather than a lightly built coastal trader.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://antiguaobserver.com/experts-a-step-closer-to-formally-identifying-historic-english-harbour-shipwreck/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguaobserver.com">[Antigua Observer Newspaper]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguaobserver.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Antigua Observer NewspaperExperts a step closer to formally identifying historic English Harbour shipwreck - Antigua Observer NewspaperJu...</span></span></span>

English Harbour itself provides an important clue. During the eighteenth century it served as Britain's principal naval base in the eastern Caribbean. Captured enemy vessels, damaged warships and obsolete naval craft were frequently brought there for repair, conversion or disposal. A large unidentified hull in this location therefore naturally invited comparison with surviving Admiralty records.

## How archaeologists discovered the buried hull

Although local divers had long known that large timbers existed in Tank Bay, systematic investigation did not begin until after a hydrographic survey commissioned in 2013 identified a significant underwater anomaly. That discovery prompted collaboration between the [Antigua and Barbuda]({{ 'antigua-and-barbuda/' | relative_url }}) National Parks Authority, the University of the Antilles, maritime archaeologists and later East Carolina University.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.allatsea.net/exciting-discovery-historic-shipwreck-found-in-antigua/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: allatsea.net">[ALL AT SEA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">allatsea.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ALL AT SEAExciting Discovery – Historic Shipwreck Found in Antigua</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ALL AT SEAJuly 27, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2021</span></span></span>

Fieldwork proceeded cautiously because much of the vessel remained sealed beneath protective mud deposits. Archaeologists employed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* side-scan sonar to define the buried structure;
* detailed mapping and photogrammetry to record exposed timbers;
* limited excavation trenches to examine construction details;
* sampling of wood and ballast for laboratory analysis.

</div>

Rather than exposing the entire wreck, researchers focused on gathering enough evidence to establish its identity while preserving the site for future study. The mud has proved to be one of the wreck's greatest protectors, limiting oxygen exposure and slowing the decay of wooden components.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://acuaonline.org/deep-thoughts/news-from-the-caribbean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: acuaonline.org">[acuaonline.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">acuaonline.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News from the Caribbean: Advisory Council on Underwater Archaeology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">News from the Caribbean: Advisory Council on Underwater Archaeology</span></span></span>

The investigations also demonstrated that the site is exceptionally intact by Caribbean standards. Instead of isolated timbers, archaeologists found much of one side of the hull together with a substantial ballast pile, making it one of the largest surviving eighteenth-century wooden shipwrecks in the region.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://antiguanewsroom.com/groundbreaking-research-on-tank-bay-shipwreck-in-english-harbour-nears-completion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguanewsroom.com">[Antigua News Room]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguanewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Antigua News RoomGroundbreaking Research on Tank Bay Shipwreck in English Harbour Nears Completion - Antigua News Room...</span></span></span>

## Why researchers link the wreck to *Beaumont*, later *Lyon*

The identification rests on several independent lines of evidence rather than on a single dramatic discovery.

First, the wreck's dimensions closely match historical descriptions of *Lyon*, a vessel originally launched in 1762 as *Beaumont* for the French East India Company. Built for long-distance trade to the Indian Ocean, she was unusually large and strongly constructed for commercial service because she also carried heavy armament.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.ecu.edu/2022/12/05/researching-hidden-shipwrecks/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.ecu.edu">[News Services]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.ecu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">researching hidden shipwrecks</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">News ServicesRESEARCHING HIDDEN SHIPWRECKS &#124; News Services &#124; ECUDecember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2022</span></span></span>

Second, the ship's documented career fits the historical geography of English Harbour. After the French East India Company was dissolved, *Beaumont* entered other service before being purchased privately and renamed *Lyon*. During the American War of Independence she transported supplies supporting the American revolutionary cause until HMS *Maidstone* captured her off Virginia in 1778. British prize records place the captured vessel in Antigua afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.ecu.edu/2022/12/05/researching-hidden-shipwrecks/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.ecu.edu">[News Services]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.ecu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">researching hidden shipwrecks</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">News ServicesRESEARCHING HIDDEN SHIPWRECKS &#124; News Services &#124; ECUDecember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2022</span></span></span>

Third, historic cartographic evidence is unusually persuasive. Researchers have identified an eighteenth-century map showing a vessel labelled *Lyon* positioned in English Harbour in essentially the same area as the archaeological site. While maps alone cannot identify a wreck, their agreement with the archaeological location strengthens the case considerably.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.ecu.edu/2022/12/05/researching-hidden-shipwrecks/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.ecu.edu">[News Services]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.ecu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">researching hidden shipwrecks</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">News ServicesRESEARCHING HIDDEN SHIPWRECKS &#124; News Services &#124; ECUDecember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 5, 2022</span></span></span>

Finally, laboratory evidence supports a French origin. Analysis of timber identified French oak, consistent with construction in a major French shipyard. Ballast studies also fit expectations for a vessel built and operated from France, although ballast evidence is less definitive because stones were often replaced during a ship's working life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://antiguaobserver.com/national-parks-work-with-local-divers-to-preserve-300-years-of-heritage/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguaobserver.com">[Antigua Observer Newspaper]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguaobserver.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Antigua Observer NewspaperNational Parks work with local divers to preserve 300 years of heritage - Antigua Observer NewspaperJune 20, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2023</span></span></span>

Taken together, these independent strands—construction, dimensions, documentary records, maps and material analysis—have persuaded most investigators that the Tank Bay wreck is indeed the former *Beaumont*, later *Lyon*.

## What remains unknown about its reuse and sinking

The ship's identity has become increasingly convincing, but its final chapter is still incomplete.

One unresolved question concerns how the British employed the captured vessel after 1778. Large prize ships could be sold, converted into transports, used as storage hulks or adapted for dockyard service. Documentary references indicate that *Lyon* remained at English Harbour, but surviving records do not fully describe her later operational role.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://antiguaobserver.com/experts-a-step-closer-to-formally-identifying-historic-english-harbour-shipwreck/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguaobserver.com">[Antigua Observer Newspaper]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguaobserver.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Antigua Observer NewspaperExperts a step closer to formally identifying historic English Harbour shipwreck - Antigua Observer NewspaperJu...</span></span></span>

A second uncertainty is why she came to rest in Tank Bay. Several explanations remain plausible:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Deliberate disposal.** Old wooden ships were often intentionally sunk to create stable wharves, reclaim land or remove obsolete vessels cheaply.
* **Dockyard hulk.** The ship may have spent years stripped of rigging and armament before eventually being abandoned in place.
* **Storm damage or deterioration.** A weakened vessel could have settled naturally after becoming unseaworthy, although evidence for a catastrophic sinking is limited.

</div>

At present, archaeologists favour deliberate abandonment connected with dockyard activity rather than a dramatic naval disaster. However, the available evidence has not yet identified the exact sequence of events leading to the vessel's burial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://antiguanewsroom.com/groundbreaking-research-on-tank-bay-shipwreck-in-english-harbour-nears-completion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguanewsroom.com">[Antigua News Room]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguanewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Antigua News RoomGroundbreaking Research on Tank Bay Shipwreck in English Harbour Nears Completion - Antigua News Room...</span></span></span>

Researchers also continue to investigate alterations made after British capture. If structural modifications can be identified within the hull, they may reveal whether the ship served as a transport, floating warehouse or another specialised dockyard vessel before its final disposal.

## Why the mystery still matters

The importance of the English Harbour wreck extends well beyond identifying a single ship.

If the identification continues to withstand scrutiny, the vessel represents one of the best-preserved archaeological examples of a French East India Company ship ever discovered. Its timbers preserve information about eighteenth-century French shipbuilding that written records alone cannot provide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://antiguanewsroom.com/antigua-celebrates-at-international-archaeology-congress-for-contributions-to-caribbean-heritage/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: antiguanewsroom.com">[Antigua News Room]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">antiguanewsroom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Antigua News RoomAntigua Celebrates at International Archaeology Congress for Contributions to Caribbean Heritage - Antigua News RoomAugu...</span></span></span>

The wreck also connects several major historical stories through one physical object. Its career links French global trade, the Indian Ocean, the American War of Independence, British naval operations in the Caribbean and the development of Antigua's dockyard into one continuous narrative. Few surviving ships illustrate so many interconnected aspects of eighteenth-century Atlantic and imperial history.

For Antigua and Barbuda, the case demonstrates how archaeology can resolve long-standing historical puzzles while still leaving room for genuine uncertainty. The central question—whether the wreck is *Beaumont*/*Lyon*—has moved steadily towards resolution through accumulating evidence. Yet the vessel's final working life and the circumstances of its abandonment remain open historical questions, ensuring that Tank Bay continues to be one of the country's most significant and carefully investigated maritime mysteries.
