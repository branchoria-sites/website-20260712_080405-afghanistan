## Introduction

For decades, many Sierra Leoneans believed they knew what Bai Bureh looked like. His face appeared in museums, on banknotes, in textbooks and in public art. Yet that familiar image was not based on a verified portrait. Instead, it grew from an imaginative reconstruction created when no authentic likeness was thought to exist. Only after the rediscovery of a forgotten 1898 photograph did historians realise that the country's best-known visual representation of one of its greatest resistance leaders was largely inaccurate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierra-leone.org/Bai_Bureh.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierra-leone.org">[Sierra Leone Web]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierra-leone.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone WebSierra Leone Web - Historic Bai Bureh Photo...</span></span></span>

The mystery is therefore not about Bai Bureh's identity or role in [Sierra Leone]({{ 'which-sierra-leone-mysteries-still/' | relative_url }})'s history, but about how an incorrect image became accepted as historical fact. It is also a striking example of how missing archives, colonial records and museum practices can shape national memory for generations.

## The invented public image

When Sierra Leone gained independence, there was no widely known authentic portrait of Bai Bureh. Although sketches made during the Hut Tax War existed, they showed him only in profile and revealed little about his facial appearance. Faced with this gap, the Sierra Leone Museum commissioned a life-sized sculpture in 1963.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1963.19.56/sculpture-of-bai-bureh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[sierraleoneheritage.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sierra Leone Heritage Sculpture of Bai Bureh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Heritage Sculpture of Bai Bureh</span></span></span>

The sculptor, J. D. Marsh, worked from a contemporary newspaper illustration rather than a verified portrait. Because the surviving drawing showed only one side of Bai Bureh's face, Marsh had to invent many features. The finished figure was also dressed in museum clothing chosen to represent a nineteenth-century warrior rather than clothing known to have belonged to Bai Bureh himself. Museum documentation now openly acknowledges that both the facial features and attire were imaginative reconstructions rather than historically authenticated details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1963.19.56/sculpture-of-bai-bureh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[sierraleoneheritage.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sierra Leone Heritage Sculpture of Bai Bureh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Heritage Sculpture of Bai Bureh</span></span></span>

That reconstruction proved remarkably influential. The sculpture became the standard public image of Bai Bureh, reproduced in educational material and eventually adapted for Sierra Leone's Le1,000 banknote introduced in 1993. As the image circulated, many people naturally assumed it was based on an authentic portrait rather than a creative interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1963.19.56/sculpture-of-bai-bureh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[sierraleoneheritage.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sierra Leone Heritage Sculpture of Bai Bureh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Heritage Sculpture of Bai Bureh</span></span></span>

This was not deliberate deception. Rather, it reflected a practical problem common in post-colonial history: when reliable visual records are missing, symbolic representations can gradually replace documented evidence.

## The rediscovered 1898 photograph

The accepted image changed dramatically after a forgotten photograph resurfaced more than a century after it had been taken.

Research by former Peace Corps volunteer Gary Schulze led to the rediscovery of what is widely regarded as the only known photograph of Bai Bureh. The image shows him under British custody in Freetown following the Hut Tax War in 1898. It had originally been taken by Lieutenant Arthur Greer of the West India Regiment but disappeared from public awareness for generations before being located in a private collection. Schulze described the discovery and its history in the *Journal of Sierra Leone Studies*, while the image has since been displayed by the Sierra Leone National Museum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierra-leone.org/Bai_Bureh.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierra-leone.org">[Sierra Leone Web]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierra-leone.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone WebSierra Leone Web - Historic Bai Bureh Photo...</span></span></span>

The photograph immediately altered historians' understanding of Bai Bureh's appearance. Instead of relying on reconstructed features, researchers could compare an actual contemporary image with the long-familiar sculpture. Although the sculpture had captured some general aspects suggested by earlier drawings, many facial details differed significantly from the real photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierra-leone.org/Bai_Bureh.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierra-leone.org">[Sierra Leone Web]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierra-leone.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone WebSierra Leone Web - Historic Bai Bureh Photo...</span></span></span>

The rediscovered image has since inspired new murals, educational displays and revised representations intended to reflect the historical evidence more accurately. One prominent example is the mural unveiled at the Sierra Leone National Museum, based directly on the authenticated photograph rather than the earlier sculpture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tourismsierraleone.com/news/new-bai-bureh-portrait-unveiled/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tourismsierraleone.com">[Sierra Leone Tourism &#124; Explore Freedom]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tourismsierraleone.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Tourism &#124; Explore FreedomNew Bai Bureh Portrait Unveiled &#124; Sierra Leone TourismMay 29, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why authenticity became a debate

The discovery did not end every controversy. Instead, discussion shifted from *who* Bai Bureh was to *which image* should represent him.

Part of the debate concerned ownership of the original photograph after its rediscovery. Questions arose over whether the physical print should remain in private hands or be transferred to Sierra Leone, while public discussion sometimes confused the original nineteenth-century print with later restored reproductions. These disputes concerned custody and provenance rather than the identity of the man shown in the image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/doubts-widen-over-ownership-and-authenticity-of-the-alleged-original-picture-of-bai-bureh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thesierraleonetelegraph.com">[The Sierra Leone Telegraph]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thesierraleonetelegraph.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Sierra Leone TelegraphDoubts widen over ownership and authenticity of the alleged original picture of Bai Bureh – The Sierra Leone Te...</span></span></span>

Historians generally accept the photograph's authenticity because its documented provenance connects it with Lieutenant Arthur Greer and because it matches known contemporary descriptions and earlier profile sketches made while Bai Bureh was in captivity. The debate has therefore focused less on whether the photograph depicts Bai Bureh than on how it should be preserved, displayed and interpreted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierra-leone.org/Bai_Bureh.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierra-leone.org">[Sierra Leone Web]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierra-leone.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone WebSierra Leone Web - Historic Bai Bureh Photo...</span></span></span>

The episode also illustrates the difference between historical authenticity and cultural familiarity. Many Sierra Leoneans had grown up recognising the reconstructed sculpture as Bai Bureh. Even after the genuine photograph became known, the older image retained emotional and symbolic importance because it had already become part of the nation's visual culture.

## What the case reveals about archives

The Bai Bureh portrait mystery demonstrates how fragile historical memory can become when archival evidence disappears.

Several factors contributed to the mistaken image becoming authoritative:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Only limited visual records of Bai Bureh survived the colonial period.
* The surviving published illustration showed only a partial view of his face.
* Museums understandably filled the gap with an artistic reconstruction.
* Later generations reproduced that reconstruction without always explaining its uncertain basis.
* The authentic photograph remained outside public knowledge for more than a century.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sierraleoneheritage.org/item/SLNM.1963.19.56/sculpture-of-bai-bureh" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierraleoneheritage.org">[sierraleoneheritage.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierraleoneheritage.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sierra Leone Heritage Sculpture of Bai Bureh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone Heritage Sculpture of Bai Bureh</span></span></span>

</div>

The rediscovery therefore did more than correct a portrait. It reminded historians that archives are never complete and that accepted historical images sometimes rest on surprisingly thin evidence. A single overlooked photograph was able to reshape the visual identity of one of Sierra Leone's best-known national heroes.

The case also highlights the value of preserving colonial-era photographs, museum collections and private archives. Historical mysteries are not always solved through dramatic archaeological discoveries. Sometimes the crucial evidence survives quietly in an overlooked album or forgotten collection until careful research reconnects it with the past.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sierra-leone.org/Bai_Bureh.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sierra-leone.org">[Sierra Leone Web]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sierra-leone.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sierra Leone WebSierra Leone Web - Historic Bai Bureh Photo...</span></span></span>
