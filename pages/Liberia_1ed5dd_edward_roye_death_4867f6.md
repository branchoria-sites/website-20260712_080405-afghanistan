## Introduction

Edward James Roye, [Liberia]({{ 'which-mysteries-still-haunt-liberias/' | relative_url }})'s fifth president, was deposed during the country's first successful overthrow of a sitting head of state in October 1871. What happened afterwards is one of the most enduring mysteries in Liberian history. It is well established that Roye was imprisoned following his removal from office and that he was dead by February 1872. What has never been firmly established is exactly how he died, where his final moments took place, or whether his death resulted from accident, escape, execution or murder. Contemporary records are sparse, later accounts contradict one another, and many surviving narratives were written by authors with strong political loyalties. More than 150 years later, the evidence still does not support a single definitive explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_James_Roye" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Edward James Roye</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Edward James Roye</span></span></span>

## The Coup, Imprisonment and Final Days

Edward James Roye entered office in January 1870 during a severe financial crisis. His attempt to secure a large British loan quickly became politically explosive. Critics accused his administration of mismanaging the negotiations, while a constitutional dispute over presidential terms and elections deepened divisions between Roye's True Whig supporters and the Republican opposition. By October 1871, unrest in Monrovia had escalated into the first successful presidential overthrow in Liberia's history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Liberian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span></span></span>

Following the coup, Roye was arrested and imprisoned. The broad outline of these events is widely accepted, but the surviving documentation becomes increasingly uncertain after his imprisonment. Some later accounts state that he remained in custody until his death. Others describe an attempted escape shortly after being sentenced for treason in February 1872. Even basic details—including whether he died on 11 or 12 February—vary between sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Liberian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span></span></span>

The uncertainty begins because few contemporary official records describing Roye's final hours have survived. Liberia's nineteenth-century archives are incomplete, and historians have often had to reconstruct events from memoirs, newspapers and later historical works that disagree with one another. The result is an unusually fragmented historical record surrounding one of the country's most important political transitions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archives.iu.edu/catalog/VAD2737" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.iu.edu">[Archives Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.iu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives OnlineThe Svend Holsoe Collection: Edward James Roye Materials, 1811-1986 - Arclight...</span></span></span>

## Drowning, Escape and Murder Accounts

The mystery persists because several competing explanations remain plausible while none is supported by conclusive evidence.

### The drowning account

The most widely repeated version states that Roye escaped from prison and attempted to reach a British vessel anchored in Monrovia harbour. In one telling he tried to swim to the ship and drowned. In another, he travelled by canoe, which overturned before reaching the vessel. These versions appear repeatedly in later historical summaries, yet few identify a contemporary eyewitness or official investigation confirming the events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_James_Roye" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Edward James Roye</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Edward James Roye</span></span></span>

The story is believable in broad outline. Seeking asylum aboard a foreign vessel would have been understandable for a recently deposed president facing execution, and Monrovia's harbour would have been the obvious route of escape. Nevertheless, plausibility is not proof. No surviving official account conclusively verifies the escape attempt or explains exactly how Roye entered the water.

### The murder theory

Another tradition argues that Roye never died accidentally at all. Instead, it suggests that political opponents captured and killed him after his escape or arranged his death while he remained under guard. Some versions describe him being seized after reaching shore, while others imply a deliberate assassination designed to eliminate any possibility of his return to power.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://liberiainfo.dukaw.com/edward-j-roye/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liberiainfo.dukaw.com">[Dukaw:: LiberiaInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liberiainfo.dukaw.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liberia Info Edward J. Roye » Dukaw:: Liberia Info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LiberiaInfoEdward J. Roye » Dukaw:: LiberiaInfo...</span></span></span>

The political context certainly provides a possible motive. Roye remained a deeply divisive figure whose supporters regarded his removal as unconstitutional, while his opponents viewed him as a threat to constitutional government. Yet no contemporary documentary evidence has emerged proving that officials ordered or carried out a political killing. Modern historians therefore treat the murder hypothesis as possible but unproven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Liberian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span></span></span>

### Death in custody

A third possibility is less dramatic but equally difficult to verify: that Roye died while still effectively in custody and that later stories of escape evolved afterwards. Some historical summaries simply record that he died under mysterious circumstances following imprisonment without endorsing any specific narrative. This cautious approach reflects the weakness of the surviving evidence rather than confidence in an alternative explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_James_Roye" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Edward James Roye</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Edward James Roye</span></span></span>

## Why the Evidence Still Falls Short

The central problem is not simply that accounts disagree. It is that the surviving evidence is unusually thin for an event of such national importance.

Several factors contribute to the uncertainty:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Partisan reporting.** Roye's supporters and opponents had strong political reasons to portray his final days differently, making later narratives difficult to separate from political argument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Liberian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span></span></span>
* **Incomplete archives.** Many nineteenth-century Liberian government records are missing, while later conflicts further disrupted the preservation of historical materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archives.iu.edu/catalog/VAD2737" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.iu.edu">[Archives Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.iu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives OnlineThe Svend Holsoe Collection: Edward James Roye Materials, 1811-1986 - Arclight...</span></span></span>
* **Lack of forensic documentation.** No known contemporary official investigation, coroner's report or similarly authoritative record has resolved the circumstances of his death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://liberiapastandpresent.org/EJRoye.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liberiapastandpresent.org">[Liberia Past and Present]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liberiapastandpresent.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liberia Past and Present</span></span></span>
* **Repetition of uncertain stories.** Later publications often repeat earlier accounts without adding new documentary evidence, making it difficult to identify the original source for many widely circulated claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://liberiapastandpresent.org/EJRoye.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liberiapastandpresent.org">[Liberia Past and Present]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liberiapastandpresent.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liberia Past and Present</span></span></span>

</div>

Because these problems reinforce one another, historians cannot confidently reconstruct Roye's final hours from the available record.

## Why Roye's Death Still Matters

Roye's unexplained death is significant not simply because a president died under uncertain circumstances, but because it shapes how Liberia's first constitutional crisis is interpreted.

If he drowned while escaping, his death may represent the tragic end of a failed attempt to avoid execution. If he was deliberately killed, it would cast the first presidential overthrow as an act of political violence extending beyond his removal from office. If he died in custody under unclear circumstances, it would raise different questions about accountability and the rule of law during Liberia's earliest decades as an independent republic.

The mystery has therefore become inseparable from debates over the legitimacy of the 1871 coup itself. Without reliable contemporary documentation, historians can establish the broad sequence of events—deposition, imprisonment and death—but cannot state with confidence exactly how Roye died.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Liberian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1871 Liberian coup d&#x27;état</span></span></span>

## What Can Be Said with Confidence?

Despite the conflicting stories, several conclusions rest on relatively solid historical ground:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Roye was removed from office during Liberia's first successful presidential overthrow in October 1871.
* He was imprisoned after the coup and remained under the control of the new authorities.
* He was dead by February 1872, although even the exact date is disputed.
* Multiple explanations—including accidental drowning, death during an escape attempt and political murder—appear in historical literature.
* No surviving body of evidence decisively proves any one explanation over the others.

</div>
