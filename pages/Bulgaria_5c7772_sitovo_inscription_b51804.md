## Introduction

The Sitovo Inscription is one of [Bulgaria]({{ 'what-still-haunts-bulgarias-historical/' | relative_url }})'s most enduring archaeological puzzles. Carved into a rock shelter near the village of Sitovo in the Rhodope Mountains, the inscription consists of just two short lines of unusual signs that have resisted every convincing attempt at decipherment. Since its discovery in 1928, researchers have argued over almost every aspect of it: its age, the script, the language and even whether it represents a true written text at all. The mystery survives not because scholars lack imagination, but because the evidence is unusually limited. The inscription is too short to establish reliable patterns, it has no accompanying archaeological context that securely dates it, and no comparable bilingual text exists to unlock its meaning. As a result, the Sitovo Inscription remains an intriguing but unresolved piece of Bulgaria's ancient past rather than proof of a lost civilisation or forgotten alphabet.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitovo_inscription" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sitovo inscription</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sitovo inscription</span></span></span>

## What is carved on the rock?

The inscription is cut into a smoothed section of rock near Sitovo, on the northern slopes of the Rhodope Mountains. It occupies two horizontal lines roughly 3.4 metres long, with unusually large carved symbols that stand around 40 centimetres high. The inscription is positioned about two metres above the ground, suggesting that the rock surface may have been prepared deliberately before the signs were engraved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitovo_inscription" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sitovo inscription</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sitovo inscription</span></span></span>

The site was brought to scholarly attention in 1928 after local woodcutters informed the amateur archaeologist Alexander Peev of the unusual markings. Peev documented the inscription and attempted to interest specialists in deciphering it. His own life later became part of Bulgarian history: during the Second World War he was executed for resistance activities unrelated to the inscription, leaving others to continue the investigation. The first formal publication of the text appeared in 1950.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitovo_inscription" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sitovo inscription</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sitovo inscription</span></span></span>

Although visitors often describe the symbols as resembling runes or an unknown alphabet, appearances are deceptive. Some signs resemble characters known from ancient Mediterranean scripts, while others have no obvious parallels. That mixture has encouraged decades of competing interpretations without producing scholarly agreement.

## Why nobody can agree on its date or script

The greatest obstacle is that the inscription cannot be securely dated from its archaeological setting. Unlike inscriptions excavated from buildings, tombs or clearly stratified layers, the Sitovo carving exists on an exposed rock face. Researchers therefore rely largely on comparisons with known scripts rather than independent dating evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitovo_inscription" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sitovo inscription</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sitovo inscription</span></span></span>

Several major interpretations have been proposed over the decades.

* **A Phrygian inscription.** This has attracted considerable academic attention because certain characters resemble letters used in Phrygian inscriptions from Anatolia. Some scholars have suggested a date around the third to first centuries BC and attempted partial readings based on the Phrygian language. However, none of these proposed translations has gained broad acceptance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitovo_inscription" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sitovo inscription</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sitovo inscription</span></span></span>
* **A Thracian text.** Because the Rhodope Mountains formed part of ancient Thrace, others have argued that the inscription records an otherwise unknown Thracian writing tradition. The difficulty is that the Thracian language itself survives only in extremely limited evidence, making verification almost impossible. A reading cannot be tested against an established grammar or substantial vocabulary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0068245400015926/type/journal_article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe archaeology of the Greek uplands: the early iron age site of Tsouka in the Rhodope Mountains &#124;...</span></span></span>
* **A prehistoric or entirely unknown script.** Popular accounts sometimes describe the inscription as evidence for Europe's earliest alphabet or a forgotten civilisation. These claims go far beyond what the evidence allows. Without secure dating or additional inscriptions from the same system, there is no accepted basis for identifying it as a separate prehistoric writing system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vagabond.bg/bulgarias-very-first-alphabet-5008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vagabond.bg">[Vagabond]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vagabond.bg</span><span class="citation-popover-title">BULGARIA&#x27;S VERY FIRST ALPHABET? &#124; VAGABOND</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BULGARIA&#x27;S VERY FIRST ALPHABET? &#124; VAGABOND...</span></span></span>

Other proposals—including Celtic, Gothic, Slavic, Sarmatian and even much later medieval interpretations—have periodically appeared but have not persuaded most specialists because they depend on selective similarities rather than consistent linguistic evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lostinplovdiv.com/en/articles/weekend-idea-one-of-the-most-beautiful-rhodope-villages" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lostinplovdiv.com">[Lost in Plovdiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lostinplovdiv.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lost in Plovdiv Weekend idea: One of the most beautiful Rhodope villages</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lost in Plovdiv Weekend idea: One of the most beautiful Rhodope villages</span></span></span>

## Why short inscriptions resist decipherment

The Sitovo Inscription illustrates a fundamental problem in historical linguistics: undeciphered texts require more than unusual symbols.

Successful decipherments usually depend on one or more of the following:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* long texts with repeated grammatical patterns;
* many inscriptions for comparison;
* knowledge of the underlying language;
* bilingual inscriptions similar to the Rosetta Stone;
* archaeological context that securely establishes date and culture.

</div>

Sitovo provides almost none of these advantages. It consists of only a few dozen signs, offering too little material to distinguish names, grammar or repeated words with confidence. Even the reading direction has been debated, making statistical analysis difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://direct.mit.edu/coli/article/50/2/725/119990/A-Systematic-Review-of-Computational-Approaches-to" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: direct.mit.edu">[MIT Press Direct]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">direct.mit.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIT Press DirectA Systematic Review of Computational Approaches to Deciphering Bronze Age Aegean and Cypriot Scripts &#124; Computational Ling...</span></span></span>

Because there are no confirmed companion inscriptions in the same script, researchers cannot build a corpus large enough to identify reliable patterns. Every proposed decipherment therefore risks becoming circular: the researcher first assumes a language, then interprets the symbols to fit that assumption.

Computational approaches that have helped analyse other undeciphered writing systems also depend on substantial collections of inscriptions. With only a single short text, Sitovo offers too little data for those methods to produce meaningful conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://direct.mit.edu/coli/article/50/2/725/119990/A-Systematic-Review-of-Computational-Approaches-to" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: direct.mit.edu">[MIT Press Direct]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">direct.mit.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIT Press DirectA Systematic Review of Computational Approaches to Deciphering Bronze Age Aegean and Cypriot Scripts &#124; Computational Ling...</span></span></span>

## Separating evidence from legend

The mystery has encouraged increasingly ambitious claims in popular books, television programmes and online discussions. Some presentations describe the inscription as proof that the Thracians possessed an advanced lost alphabet, while others connect it with the much older Vinča symbols or suggest forgotten prehistoric civilisations.

These ideas remain speculative because they lack supporting archaeological evidence. Similar-looking symbols alone cannot establish a relationship between scripts separated by thousands of years or vast geographical distances. Archaeologists instead emphasise that writing systems develop within wider cultural contexts—contexts that have not yet been demonstrated for Sitovo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://direct.mit.edu/coli/article/50/2/725/119990/A-Systematic-Review-of-Computational-Approaches-to" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: direct.mit.edu">[mit.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">direct.mit.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIT Press DirectA Systematic Review of Computational Approaches to Deciphering Bronze Age Aegean and Cypriot Scripts &#124; Computational Ling...</span></span></span>

The surrounding Rhodope landscape certainly contains important prehistoric and ancient sites, including sanctuaries and settlements from different periods. That rich archaeological background makes the inscription worth studying, but it does not automatically explain who carved it or when.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0068245400015926/type/journal_article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe archaeology of the Greek uplands: the early iron age site of Tsouka in the Rhodope Mountains &#124;...</span></span></span>

## Why the Sitovo Inscription still matters

Despite nearly a century of investigation, the Sitovo Inscription remains valuable precisely because it resists easy answers. It highlights the limits of archaeological interpretation when an artefact survives without sufficient context. Rather than representing a failure of scholarship, its undeciphered status reflects the careful standards required before historians accept claims about ancient languages or lost scripts.

Future discoveries could transform understanding of the inscription. Another text using the same signs, a securely dated archaeological context, or new evidence linking it convincingly to an established script would allow researchers to test competing theories much more rigorously. Until then, the Sitovo Inscription stands as one of Bulgaria's most authentic historical mysteries: an undeniably real artefact whose meaning remains beyond secure interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitovo_inscription" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sitovo inscription</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sitovo inscription</span></span></span>
