## Introduction

Bayil Castle, also known as Sabayil Castle, is one of [Azerbaijan]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-azerbaijans/' | relative_url }})'s most intriguing archaeological mysteries because it is both real and only partly visible. Built in the early thirteenth century on a rocky island just off Baku, the fortress disappeared beneath the waters of the Caspian Sea within little more than a lifetime. What remains today is not a lost civilisation or mythical city but a submerged medieval stronghold whose original purpose is still debated. Archaeologists have recovered hundreds of carved stones and inscriptions that reveal who built it and when, yet they have not settled the question of whether Bayil was primarily a military fortress, a royal residence, a harbour installation, or a combination of all three. The castle's disappearance beneath changing sea levels has also complicated efforts to reconstruct its original appearance and function.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unesco.preslib.az/en/page/MyrZIFLBjC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.preslib.az">[preslib.az]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.preslib.az</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UNESCO Azerbaijan Bayil Tower:: Azərbaycan-UNESCO əlaqələri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Azerbaijan Bayil Tower:: Azərbaycan-UNESCO əlaqələri</span></span></span>

## What archaeologists recovered from the water

For centuries Bayil Castle lay beneath the waters of Baku Bay. The Caspian Sea periodically retreated enough for parts of the ruins to become visible, encouraging local traditions about a drowned fortress. Serious archaeological work began in the Soviet period, particularly between 1939 and 1969, when underwater investigations recovered large quantities of dressed stone from the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unesco.preslib.az/en/page/MyrZIFLBjC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.preslib.az">[UNESCO Azerbaijan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.preslib.az</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UNESCO Azerbaijan Bayil Tower:: Azərbaycan-UNESCO əlaqələri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Azerbaijan Bayil Tower:: Azərbaycan-UNESCO əlaqələri</span></span></span>

The most important discoveries were more than 700 carved limestone panels that once decorated the castle's outer walls. These are unusual because they combine decorative reliefs with long inscriptions in Arabic and Persian. Many stones also display animals, birds, mythical creatures and heraldic symbols linked to the Shirvanshah dynasty. Although waves damaged many inscriptions during centuries underwater, enough survives to transform Bayil from legend into a securely dated historical monument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabayil_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikipedia.org">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikipedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sabayil Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sabayil Castle</span></span></span>

The inscriptions provide several crucial pieces of evidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* They identify members of the Shirvanshah ruling family, including Fariburz, son of Garsasb.
* They place construction in the early 1230s, corresponding to Hijri dates 630–632.
* One inscription identifies the architect as Abdul-Majid Masud oglu, who is also associated with the contemporary fortress at Mardakan.
* The recovered stones preserve valuable examples of medieval decorative art from Shirvan that would otherwise have been lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unesco.preslib.az/en/page/MyrZIFLBjC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.preslib.az">[preslib.az]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.preslib.az</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UNESCO Azerbaijan Bayil Tower:: Azərbaycan-UNESCO əlaqələri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO Azerbaijan Bayil Tower:: Azərbaycan-UNESCO əlaqələri</span></span></span>

</div>

Today many of these carved stones are preserved within the Palace of the Shirvanshahs complex in Baku rather than at the underwater site itself, allowing historians to study details impossible to observe beneath the sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabayil_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikipedia.org">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikipedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sabayil Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sabayil Castle</span></span></span>

## Fortress, palace or harbour installation?

The greatest historical uncertainty concerns Bayil Castle's medieval purpose rather than its existence.

The defensive interpretation is the most widely accepted. The elongated enclosure, towers, thick curtain walls and position guarding approaches into Baku Bay fit naturally within the wider coastal defence system developed by the Shirvanshahs. Contemporary fortifications around the Absheron Peninsula suggest a coordinated network protecting both the capital and maritime trade. UNESCO's Tentative List describes Bayil as protecting Baku from attack from the sea while serving the expanding medieval harbour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/fr/listesindicatives/1573/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre The Caspian Shore Defensive Constructions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreThe Caspian Shore Defensive Constructions - UNESCO Centre du patrimoine mondial...</span></span></span>

Other researchers argue that the complex performed additional roles beyond defence. Because it stood beside one of the western Caspian's most important ports, Bayil may have functioned as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a customs station controlling maritime trade;
* a royal residence or seasonal palace;
* a fortified harbour installation;
* a lighthouse and observation point;
* or a combination of military, administrative and commercial functions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/fr/listesindicatives/1573/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre The Caspian Shore Defensive Constructions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreThe Caspian Shore Defensive Constructions - UNESCO Centre du patrimoine mondial...</span></span></span>

</div>

This debate exists because the archaeological evidence supports several possibilities simultaneously. The walls clearly had defensive value, yet the elaborate decorative programme seems unusually rich for a purely military fortification. The inscribed friezes resemble those expected on prestigious royal architecture rather than a simple coastal fort.

Historical references add another layer of uncertainty. The fifteenth-century geographer Abdurrashid al-Bakuvi described two castles associated with medieval Baku—one on the shore and another offshore whose upper parts had already been destroyed. Many historians believe the offshore fortress was Bayil Castle, although the description is too brief to settle its exact role.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabayil_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikipedia.org">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikipedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sabayil Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sabayil Castle</span></span></span>

## Why did the castle disappear?

The disappearance of Bayil Castle is often presented as a sudden catastrophe, but the evidence suggests a more complicated sequence involving both geological events and long-term environmental change.

The strongest explanation begins with a major earthquake around 1306. Contemporary and later historical traditions associate this earthquake with structural collapse at Bayil, after which the fortress became increasingly vulnerable to the fluctuating waters of the Caspian Sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/fr/listesindicatives/1573/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre The Caspian Shore Defensive Constructions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreThe Caspian Shore Defensive Constructions - UNESCO Centre du patrimoine mondial...</span></span></span>

Unlike the open ocean, the Caspian Sea naturally experiences substantial long-term rises and falls in water level. These fluctuations have repeatedly altered coastlines over centuries. Following the earthquake, higher water levels gradually submerged much of the damaged complex rather than burying it beneath sediment in a single event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bp.com/content/dam/bp/country-sites/en_az/azerbaijan/home/pdfs/esias/swap/swap-2d-esia.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bp.com">[bp global]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bp.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">globalbp AECOMShallow Water Absheron Peninsula</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">bp globalbpAECOMShallow Water Absheron Peninsula...</span></span></span>

The sequence is therefore likely to have been:

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Construction during the early 1230s.
2. Use for several decades as part of medieval Baku's coastal system.
3. Severe earthquake damage around 1306.
4. Progressive inundation as Caspian water levels rose.

This combination of earthquake damage and changing sea levels explains why the fortress is sometimes described as "sunken" even though the process probably unfolded over many years rather than a single dramatic moment.

## What remains uncertain

Bayil Castle illustrates how archaeology can answer some questions while leaving others unresolved.

The following points are now well established:

* the castle was built during the reign of the Shirvanshahs in the early thirteenth century;
* it occupied a small island or rocky outcrop just offshore from medieval Baku;
* it suffered destruction associated with the early fourteenth century;
* and large portions of its decorative stonework have been recovered. [UNESCO Azerbaijan](https://unesco.preslib.az/en/page/MyrZIFLBjC)

Several important questions remain open:

* **Its primary function.** Was it principally a fortress, a palace, a harbour installation or a multifunctional complex?
* **Its complete architectural layout.** Only part of the structure has been investigated, while submerged remains remain difficult to document.
* **Its relationship with Baku's urban defences.** Claims of tunnels connecting the castle to the old city continue to attract public interest, but archaeological evidence remains incomplete and does not yet demonstrate the full extent or purpose of any underground passages. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabayil_Castle)

These uncertainties arise from incomplete preservation rather than from mysterious disappearance. Much of the evidence simply remains underwater or has been damaged by centuries of wave action.

## Why Bayil Castle still matters

Bayil Castle has become known as the "Atlantis of the Caspian", but the comparison can be misleading. Its significance lies not in mythical stories but in the unusual combination of archaeology, geology and medieval history concentrated in a single site. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabayil_Castle)

The recovered inscriptions have greatly expanded knowledge of the Shirvanshah dynasty, medieval architecture and artistic traditions in Azerbaijan. At the same time, the submerged remains remind historians that the Caspian coastline has never been fixed. Earthquakes, changing sea levels and coastal erosion have repeatedly reshaped the landscape around Baku, leaving monuments that appear and disappear across generations.

For that reason, Bayil Castle remains one of Azerbaijan's most compelling historical puzzles—not because its existence is doubtful, but because the surviving evidence continues to support several plausible interpretations of the role this remarkable fortress once played in medieval Baku.
