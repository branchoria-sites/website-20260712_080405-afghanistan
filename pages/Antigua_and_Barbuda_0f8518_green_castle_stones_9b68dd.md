## Introduction

Green Castle Hill is one of Antigua’s most intriguing archaeological landscapes because it combines two facts that are both well supported by the evidence: Indigenous people unquestionably occupied the hill before European colonisation, yet it remains uncertain whether they deliberately erected or rearranged the striking stone formations that have earned the site the nickname “the Stonehenge of the Caribbean”. The mystery lies not in whether people used the hill—they did—but in whether the largest standing stones are human-made monuments or remarkable products of Antigua’s volcanic geology. The available archaeological and geological evidence suggests a more nuanced picture than either extreme: the hill is almost certainly a natural volcanic formation that was later incorporated into an important Indigenous ceremonial or observation site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[Archaeology Antigua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

## What archaeology proves about Indigenous occupation

Archaeologists have little doubt that Green Castle Hill was a significant pre-Columbian location. Excavations have recovered pottery fragments, shell remains, stone-working debris (lithic flakes), animal bones and other evidence of a Ceramic Age settlement, demonstrating that Indigenous communities occupied or repeatedly visited the hill centuries before European arrival.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[Archaeology Antigua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

The hill's position helps explain its attraction. Rising above Antigua's central plain, it offers extensive views across the west coast and neighbouring hills. Such elevated locations frequently served practical purposes, including observation, gathering and ritual activity, throughout the Caribbean.

What archaeology does **not** prove is equally important. None of the excavated material conclusively demonstrates that the largest upright rocks were quarried elsewhere, transported, or intentionally erected. Archaeological association alone cannot establish that every prominent stone is an engineered monument. This distinction lies at the heart of the debate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[Archaeology Antigua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

## Why some researchers see a ceremonial landscape

The site's unusual appearance has inspired interpretations for more than a century. Some investigators have suggested that particular rocks resemble standing stones, altars or seats, while others have argued that the arrangement of certain formations could have helped mark celestial events such as solstices or the seasonal movement of stars. The commanding view from the summit makes such proposals understandable, since many Indigenous societies incorporated astronomical observation into ritual life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[Archaeology Antigua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

Recent heritage presentations note that astronomical surveys have identified possible alignments worthy of further investigation. These findings support the idea that people may have selected the hill because of its natural features and adapted parts of the landscape for ceremonial use. They do not, however, demonstrate that the entire stone complex was constructed in the same way as famous megalithic monuments elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[Archaeology Antigua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

The strongest ceremonial interpretation therefore rests on several cumulative observations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* confirmed Indigenous occupation;
* an elevated location with exceptional visibility;
* unusual stone formations that could have served as landmarks;
* possible astronomical alignments that deserve continued study.

</div>

None of these points independently proves large-scale stone construction, but together they suggest the hill was culturally significant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[Archaeology Antigua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

## Why geology may explain the largest formations

The principal alternative explanation comes from geology rather than archaeology.

Green Castle Hill sits on Antigua's oldest volcanic rocks, near the boundary between the island's volcanic highlands and its younger limestone plains. The prominent "megaliths" consist of resistant volcanic rocks that survived while softer surrounding material gradually weathered away over millions of years. This process can naturally leave isolated pillars, angular blocks and apparently balanced boulders that resemble deliberately erected monuments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.internationalparks.org/antigua-and-barbuda/Green%20Castle%20Hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalparks.org">[International Parks]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalparks.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Parks Green Castle Hill, Antigua and Barbuda (National Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International ParksGreen Castle Hill, Antigua and Barbuda (National Park) - Ratings &amp; Guide...</span></span></span>

Geological reconstructions suggest that ancient volcanic activity created masses of andesite, dacite and volcanic tuff. Later erosion exposed these harder rocks while removing less resistant material, producing the striking hilltop landscape seen today. Similar weathering processes elsewhere in volcanic regions have created natural stone pillars that appear surprisingly artificial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.internationalparks.org/antigua-and-barbuda/Green%20Castle%20Hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalparks.org">[International Parks]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalparks.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Parks Green Castle Hill, Antigua and Barbuda (National Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International ParksGreen Castle Hill, Antigua and Barbuda (National Park) - Ratings &amp; Guide...</span></span></span>

This explanation also accounts for an important archaeological observation: the site lacks many of the unmistakable engineering features expected at a deliberately built megalithic monument, such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* extensive quarrying scars;
* transport routes for enormous stones;
* carefully prepared foundation sockets;
* consistent shaping of stone surfaces;
* a clearly planned architectural layout.

</div>

Their absence does not rule out limited human modification, but it weakens the argument for construction of the entire complex.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.internationalparks.org/antigua-and-barbuda/Green%20Castle%20Hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalparks.org">[International Parks]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalparks.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Parks Green Castle Hill, Antigua and Barbuda (National Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International ParksGreen Castle Hill, Antigua and Barbuda (National Park) - Ratings &amp; Guide...</span></span></span>

## Comparing Green Castle Hill with Stonehenge

The comparison with Stonehenge is useful mainly as a visual analogy rather than an archaeological one.

Stonehenge is unquestionably an engineered monument. Archaeological excavation has documented quarry sites, transport routes, prepared foundations and deliberate construction using stones brought from considerable distances. Its layout forms an organised architectural design.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016787897800016" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ScienceDirect Stonehenge: geology and prehistory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Stonehenge: geology and prehistory - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery persists

Several factors ensure that debate continues.

First, archaeological excavation has sampled only portions of the site, leaving room for future discoveries that could strengthen or weaken competing interpretations.

Second, natural landscapes can acquire profound ceremonial meaning without requiring large-scale engineering. A sacred place may be culturally important precisely because its unusual geology already appears extraordinary.

Third, many earlier descriptions of Green Castle Hill mixed careful observation with speculation. Claims about sacrificial altars, astronomical observatories or elaborate stone monuments have often circulated more widely than the evidence supporting them, making it difficult to separate documented findings from later embellishment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[archaeologyantigua.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

Finally, the hill's dramatic appearance naturally encourages visitors to search for intentional patterns. Human beings are adept at recognising alignments and symbolic shapes even where geological processes may have produced them.

## What is the most likely explanation?

Current evidence supports three conclusions with different degrees of confidence.

The first is well established: Green Castle Hill was an important Indigenous archaeological site occupied during the Ceramic Age. Artefacts leave little room for doubt about sustained human use.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[Archaeology Antigua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

The second is plausible but not proven: some natural rock formations may have been incorporated into ceremonial practices or used as observation points. Possible astronomical relationships remain an active subject of discussion but have not reached scholarly consensus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archaeologyantigua.org/index.php/green-castle-hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archaeologyantigua.org">[Archaeology Antigua]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archaeologyantigua.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archaeology Antigua Green Castle Hill</span></span></span>

The third—that the entire collection of standing stones was intentionally erected as a Caribbean equivalent of Stonehenge—currently lacks convincing archaeological support. Geological evidence provides a simpler explanation for the origin of the largest formations, while archaeology explains why those formations became culturally important to the people who later occupied the hill.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.internationalparks.org/antigua-and-barbuda/Green%20Castle%20Hill" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalparks.org">[internationalparks.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalparks.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Parks Green Castle Hill, Antigua and Barbuda (National Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International ParksGreen Castle Hill, Antigua and Barbuda (National Park) - Ratings &amp; Guide...</span></span></span>

Rather than diminishing Green Castle Hill's importance, this interpretation makes the site more historically interesting. It represents a meeting point between Antigua's ancient volcanic landscape and the spiritual lives of its earliest known inhabitants—a place where natural geology and human history became intertwined, leaving behind one of the country's most enduring archaeological mysteries.
