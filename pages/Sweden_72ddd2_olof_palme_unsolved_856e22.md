## Introduction

The assassination of Swedish Prime Minister Olof Palme on the night of 28 February 1986 remains one of Europe's most consequential unsolved political murders. Nearly four decades of investigation produced hundreds of thousands of pages of evidence, thousands of interviews and numerous suspects, yet no proof capable of supporting a criminal conviction. That distinction is central to understanding the mystery. The enduring question is not simply who may have fired the fatal shots, but why one of the largest murder investigations in modern history failed to establish guilt beyond reasonable doubt. The case became a test of policing, forensic evidence, witness reliability and public confidence in the justice system. Even after prosecutors identified a probable suspect in 2020, later official review concluded that the available evidence was insufficient to identify him as the killer, leaving the murder officially unsolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

## The shooting and the first investigative failures

Shortly before midnight, Olof Palme and his wife Lisbet were walking home from a cinema through central Stockholm without bodyguards. A gunman approached from behind on Sveavägen, fired two shots, mortally wounding the prime minister and injuring his wife, then escaped up Tunnelgatan before police established an effective perimeter. No murder weapon was recovered, and the killer vanished into the city.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

The immediate response has long been criticised as one of the decisive reasons the case became so difficult to solve. Officers arriving at the scene struggled to coordinate communications, witness management and pursuit of the fleeing suspect. The crime scene was not secured as rigorously as would later become standard practice, meaning that potentially valuable evidence was lost or contaminated. Conflicting witness statements were collected under pressure, while the absence of CCTV coverage and modern forensic techniques left investigators heavily dependent on human recollection rather than physical proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

Those early shortcomings shaped every later stage of the inquiry. As memories faded and witnesses revised or contradicted earlier accounts, investigators increasingly relied on circumstantial evidence instead of direct forensic links.

## Why proving the case became so difficult

The Palme investigation illustrates the difference between suspicion and proof.

Several fundamental obstacles never disappeared:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The murder weapon was never recovered or conclusively identified.
* No usable DNA or fingerprint evidence linked a suspect to the shooting.
* Witnesses gave differing descriptions of the gunman's appearance, clothing and escape.
* The event occurred in a busy public street at night, creating confusion about timings and movements.
* Investigators repeatedly shifted attention between competing theories, meaning potentially valuable opportunities were lost before they could be tested thoroughly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

</div>

As the investigation expanded, it accumulated an enormous archive rather than a coherent evidential chain. New leads often generated fresh speculation but rarely produced evidence capable of surviving judicial scrutiny.

## Suspects, witnesses and competing theories

Over the decades investigators examined numerous explanations, each with some supporting circumstances but none supported by conclusive proof.

**Christer Pettersson**

Pettersson, a violent offender with alcohol and drug problems, became the best-known early suspect. He was convicted in 1989 after Lisbet Palme identified him in an identity parade. However, [Sweden]({{ 'sweden/' | relative_url }})'s Court of Appeal unanimously acquitted him because the prosecution lacked corroborating evidence. No murder weapon was linked to him, no convincing motive was established and the identification procedure itself attracted criticism. Later attempts to reopen the case against him also failed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

**The PKK investigation**

Investigators devoted considerable effort to examining possible involvement by the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK). The organisation had previously attracted Swedish security attention, but despite extensive investigation prosecutors found no evidence proving responsibility for the assassination. The theory is now generally regarded as one of several investigative avenues rather than a demonstrated solution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

**Police and right-wing conspiracy theories**

Allegations that members of Swedish police or anti-Palme extremists organised the murder have circulated for decades. These theories gained attention because Palme had strong political opponents and because some witnesses reported unusual behaviour around the crime scene. However, no investigation has produced evidence establishing an organised domestic conspiracy capable of meeting criminal standards of proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

**Foreign involvement**

Various claims have linked the murder to apartheid-era South Africa, foreign intelligence services or international political networks. Palme's outspoken criticism of apartheid and his international diplomacy made such theories appear plausible to some observers. Yet despite decades of inquiry, no documentary or forensic evidence has conclusively connected any foreign government or intelligence agency to the shooting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why the official case against Stig Engström collapsed

In June 2020 Swedish prosecutors closed the investigation while naming Stig Engström—known as the "Skandia Man" because he worked in a nearby office—as the person they believed most likely killed Palme. The decision attracted worldwide attention because it appeared to offer the closest thing to an official solution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://via.tt.se/pressmeddelande/3278448/decision-in-the-investigation-into-the-murder-of-former-swedish-prime-minister-olof-palme" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: via.tt.se">[Via TT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">via.tt.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2020</span></span></span>

The case against Engström rested largely on circumstantial considerations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* he was present close to the crime scene;
* his clothing resembled descriptions given by some witnesses;
* his own accounts of events changed over time;
* prosecutors believed his statements were inconsistent with other witness testimony;
* investigators argued he had been overlooked too quickly during the original investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/jun/10/olof-palme-murder-swedish-prosecutors-reveal-conclusions" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

</div>

What the prosecution did **not** possess was equally significant. There was:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no murder weapon connected to Engström;
* no forensic evidence linking him to the shooting;
* no eyewitness who identified him as the gunman;
* no documented confession;
* no evidence that would have allowed the case to be tested in court because Engström had died in 2000.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://via.tt.se/pressmeddelande/3278448/decision-in-the-investigation-into-the-murder-of-former-swedish-prime-minister-olof-palme" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: via.tt.se">[Via TT]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">via.tt.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2020</span></span></span>

</div>

The controversy deepened in December 2025, when Sweden's Prosecution Authority reviewed the closure decision. Senior prosecutors concluded that although the investigation should remain closed, the evidence was **not sufficient** to identify Engström as the perpetrator. The review therefore rejected the earlier implication that his guilt had been established while also concluding that no realistic prospect existed of obtaining evidence capable of supporting a future prosecution. The investigation remained closed because further inquiry was unlikely to produce proof, not because the mystery had been solved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aklagare.se/globalassets/dokument/ovriga-dokument/amr-8123-25-18dec-2025-translation-of-a-decision-in-a-review-case.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aklagare.se">[aklagare.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aklagare.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">amr 8123 25 18dec 2025 translation of a decision in a review case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ÅklagarmyndighetenTranslation of a Decision in a Review Case18 Dec 2025 — On 10 June 2020, Chief Prosecutor Krister Petersson decided to...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 2020</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery still matters

The Palme assassination remains important because it demonstrates how investigative failures at the beginning of a case can shape its outcome permanently. Once physical evidence disappears and witness memories diverge, even decades of determined work may never recover what was lost.

The investigation also transformed Swedish policing. The shortcomings exposed by the case contributed to changes in homicide investigation, crime-scene management and coordination between police and prosecutors. More broadly, the murder challenged Sweden's image as an unusually secure society where political violence seemed almost unimaginable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://polisen.se/om-polisen/polisens-arbete/palmeutredningen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: polisen.se">[polisen.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">polisen.se</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Palmeutredningen &#124; Polismyndigheten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 9, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2026</span></span></span>

For historians, the central mystery is therefore twofold. One question concerns the identity of the gunman. The other concerns why a modern criminal investigation, supported by enormous resources and sustained over nearly forty years, could never assemble the kind of evidence required to establish the truth in court. Until new physical evidence emerges, the assassination of Olof Palme remains defined less by the abundance of theories than by the enduring absence of proof.
