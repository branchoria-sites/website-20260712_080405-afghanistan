## Introduction

Four cases show the range clearly. The burial place of former president Kārlis Ulmanis has never been found. The extraordinary testimony of a seventeenth-century “werewolf” survives, but historians still debate what he believed and what cultural tradition lay behind it. The history of the [Salaspils]({{ 'salaspils/' | relative_url }}) camp was obscured by both Nazi secrecy and Soviet exaggeration. The case of aviator Herberts Cukurs remains contested because strong historical accusations were never tested in a completed criminal trial. These are genuine puzzles, but they are not blank spaces in which every theory is equally credible.

## Why Latvia has so many fractured histories

Latvia’s geography placed it between competing powers for centuries. Parts of the present country were governed at different times by German crusading orders, the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, Sweden and the Russian Empire. Independent from 1918, Latvia was occupied by the Soviet Union in June 1940, invaded by Nazi Germany in 1941 and reoccupied by Soviet forces in 1944–45. Independence was restored in 1991.

These changes did more than replace governments. Archives were removed, reorganised or destroyed; witnesses were killed, deported or scattered abroad; and successive regimes promoted sharply different accounts of the recent past. Soviet authorities concealed their own repression while sometimes exaggerating or reshaping evidence of Nazi crimes for political purposes. Émigré communities preserved information unavailable inside Soviet Latvia, but distance and incomplete access to records created other gaps.

The result is a landscape of uneven evidence. Some mysteries concern missing physical proof, such as a body or burial site. Others concern interpretation: a document survives, but its meaning is uncertain. In still others, the central facts are known but have become entangled in political arguments over national responsibility, occupation and remembrance.

## Where was Kārlis Ulmanis buried?

Kārlis Ulmanis was one of the founders of independent Latvia and its first prime minister. In 1934 he carried out an authoritarian coup, dissolved parliament and eventually assumed the presidency. His political legacy is therefore divided: he is remembered both as a central figure in Latvian state-building and as the leader who ended the country’s parliamentary democracy.

After Soviet forces occupied Latvia in June 1940, Ulmanis remained in office briefly under intense pressure. He was removed, detained and transported into the Soviet Union. Following Germany’s invasion of the USSR, Soviet authorities transferred him through a succession of places of confinement. He died in September 1942 in a prison at Krasnovodsk, now Türkmenbaşy in Turkmenistan. Latvia’s presidential office and specialist historical accounts agree on the essentials of his arrest, deportation and death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.president.lv/en/karlis-ulmanis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: president.lv">[President of Latvia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">president.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">He received the basic education in Bērmuiža Elementary School.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">President of LatviaKārlis Ulmanis &#124; Valsts prezidenta kanceleja16 Mar 2021 — Kārlis Ulmanis was born on 4 September 1877 in Pikšas farmst...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1877</span></span></span>

What remains unknown is the location of his grave. Prison burials were often poorly marked, recorded only in internal files or lost during later redevelopment. For years, researchers and members of the Ulmanis family tried to identify the burial site. A memorial was established in Turkmenistan, but it did not mark a confirmed grave. In 2017, Ulmanis’s grand-nephew, former Latvian president Guntis Ulmanis, said that the long search had been abandoned because the site was effectively impossible to establish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eng.lsm.lv/article/culture/history/search-for-grave-of-latvias-pre-war-prime-minister-called-off.a248937/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eng.lsm.lv">[LSM.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eng.lsm.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">lv Search for grave of Latvia&#x27;s pre-war prime minister called off</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">for grave of Latvia&#x27;s pre-war prime minister called offSeptember 4, 2017 — The search for the grave site of Karlis Ulmanis, the last prim...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 4, 2017</span></span></span>

Claims have occasionally placed the grave elsewhere, including Georgia, but no persuasive documentary or forensic evidence has confirmed them. The most plausible explanation is not an elaborate concealment operation directed specifically at Ulmanis. It is the more ordinary brutality of the Soviet prison system: a sick political prisoner died far from home and was buried in a grave whose identifying information did not survive.

The missing grave matters because Ulmanis occupies such an unsettled place in Latvian memory. A confirmed burial could provide a physical focus for commemoration, but it would not resolve the argument over his rule. His fate as a Soviet prisoner and his earlier destruction of democratic government are both parts of the same history.

## What did Latvia’s “werewolf” really believe?

In 1691 and 1692, a court in Swedish-ruled Livonia questioned an elderly man known as Thiess of Kaltenbrun. The hearings took place in an area now within Latvia. Thiess was initially connected to an investigation into a church robbery, but the judges had heard local reports that he was a werewolf. Rather than deny the allegation, he accepted it and offered an account unlike the standard demonological stories familiar to educated European judges.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://press.uchicago.edu/sites/thiess/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: press.uchicago.edu">[University of Chicago Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">press.uchicago.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of Chicago PressTrial transcript from Old Thiess, a Livonian WerewolfThis page has the trial transcript comprising chapter one...</span></span></span>

Thiess said that werewolves were not servants of the Devil. They were opponents of witches and protectors of the community. At certain times they supposedly travelled to Hell, fought the Devil’s forces and recovered grain, livestock and other prosperity stolen from the earth. He presented himself, in effect, as one of the “hounds of God”.

The transcript is authentic, and its survival makes the case more than a piece of later folklore. Yet it does not tell historians exactly what stood behind Thiess’s testimony. Several interpretations remain possible.

**A surviving folk belief.** Thiess may have been describing a local tradition in which visionary healers or magical specialists defended crops and animals against supernatural harm. His known activities as a healer and user of charms support the idea that he occupied some recognised role in village life.

**A personal synthesis.** His story may have combined fragments of folklore, Lutheran language, memories of earlier accusations and his own inventions. Oral beliefs rarely form a single, consistent system, particularly when recorded through the questions of hostile officials.

**A courtroom performance.** Thiess may have deliberately turned a dangerous accusation into a claim of public service. By describing himself as an enemy of the Devil, he challenged the judges’ expectation that a self-confessed werewolf must also be a Satanist.

Historian Carlo Ginzburg connected the testimony with wider European traditions in which visionary figures claimed to fight witches for the fertility of the land. Other scholars have warned that similarities across Europe do not prove the existence of one ancient, unified belief system. The surviving record permits comparison, but not certainty about the remote origins of Thiess’s ideas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiess_of_Kaltenbrun" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Thiess of Kaltenbrun</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thiess of Kaltenbrun</span></span></span>

The judges ultimately focused less on literal transformation than on Thiess’s religious nonconformity and magical practices. He admitted that he did not participate properly in church life and described charms that did not fit official Lutheran teaching. He was sentenced to flogging and banishment rather than executed as a supernatural monster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://emotionsblog.history.qmul.ac.uk/2013/03/an-account-of-the-trial-of-a-livonian-werewolf-in-jurgensburg-in-1692/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: emotionsblog.history.qmul.ac.uk">[emotionsblog.history.qmul.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">emotionsblog.history.qmul.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">an account of the trial of a livonian werewolf in jurgensburg in 1692</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an account of the trial of a livonian werewolf in jurgensburg in 1692</span></span></span>

The real mystery is therefore not whether an elderly Latvian physically became a wolf. It is what social and spiritual world can be glimpsed through testimony written down by officials who did not share it. The case survives at the boundary between personal belief, community folklore and courtroom strategy.

## Why are the Salaspils figures still disputed?

The Salaspils camp, established south-east of Riga during the Nazi occupation, held political prisoners, forced labourers, children and other civilians. Jewish prisoners deported from German-controlled Europe were used in its construction under lethal conditions. Thousands of people passed through the complex, and many died from hunger, illness, exposure, mistreatment and execution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de/exhibitions/a-dual-legacy-reviewing-the-new-exhibition-at-salaspils" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de">[cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cultures-of-history.uni-jena.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-jena.de.</span></span></span>

There is no serious historical doubt that Salaspils was a place of imprisonment, forced labour and mass suffering. The dispute concerns its classification, prisoner totals and number of deaths.

After the war, Soviet accounts sometimes claimed that more than 100,000 people had been killed there. Salaspils was presented as an extermination centre on a scale that surviving documentation does not support. Later research using German records, prisoner evidence, burial information and demographic reconstruction produced much lower estimates. A major Latvian study concluded that roughly 23,000 people passed through Salaspils in its different phases and that about 2,000 died there, although exact totals remain difficult to establish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eng.lsm.lv/article/culture/history/new-book-attempts-real-history-of-holocaust-camp.a166107/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eng.lsm.lv">[LSM.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eng.lsm.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">lv New book attempts real history of Holocaust camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">lv New book attempts real history of Holocaust camp</span></span></span>

Correcting the Soviet figure does not diminish the crime. It separates documented victims from a politically useful statistic. The inflated number emerged partly from the Soviet Extraordinary State Commission’s wider method of estimating Nazi losses and partly from the memorial culture built around the camp. Later representations placed particular emphasis on imprisoned children, whose suffering was real but was also surrounded by stories that were sometimes repeated without adequate evidence.

Salaspils illustrates how propaganda can make a historical crime harder, rather than easier, to understand. When an exaggerated figure is disproved, denialists may try to imply that the entire history of the camp was invented. That conclusion is false. Surviving evidence establishes forced labour, severe deprivation, child deaths and the lethal treatment of Jewish construction workers. The uncertainty concerns precise scale and administrative category, not whether the victims suffered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaspils_camp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Salaspils camp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Salaspils camp</span></span></span>

The site’s post-war memorial, opened during the Soviet period, adds another layer. Its monumental sculpture and soundscape became one of Latvia’s best-known places of remembrance, but the original presentation reflected Soviet narratives. A later exhibition sought to incorporate research unavailable or politically unusable when the memorial was created. The continuing debate is therefore as much about how states construct memory as about counting the dead.

## Why was Herberts Cukurs never tried?

Before the Second World War, Herberts Cukurs was celebrated as a Latvian aviator known for long-distance flights. During the Nazi occupation he became associated with the Arajs Commando, a Latvian auxiliary unit that participated in the murder of Jews and other civilians. Holocaust survivors and historical researchers connected him to violent acts in Riga, including operations against Jewish prisoners.

Cukurs escaped to South America after the war. In 1965, Israeli intelligence agents lured him from Brazil to Uruguay and killed him. Israel did not put him on trial, and no Latvian or international court delivered a verdict on the evidence against him. The assassination ensured that the historical case would forever be separated from the procedural certainty that a trial might have provided.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.timesofisrael.com/how-the-mossad-hunted-the-butcher-of-riga-who-murdered-up-to-30000-jews/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofisrael.com">[The Times of Israel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofisrael.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Times of Israel How the Mossad hunted the &#x27;Butcher of Riga,&#x27; who</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Times of Israel How the Mossad hunted the &#x27;Butcher of Riga,&#x27; who</span></span></span>

This distinction has become the centre of the controversy. A person can be strongly implicated by historical evidence even though criminal liability was never proved in court. Conversely, the absence of a conviction does not amount to exoneration, especially when the suspect was killed before proceedings could take place.

Supporters of Cukurs have emphasised his pre-war achievements and inconsistencies in some witness statements. Certain nationalist retellings have treated him as a victim of Soviet or Israeli defamation. Holocaust institutions and many historians argue that the wider body of testimony and documentation places him in a senior operational role within a unit responsible for mass murder. Yad Vashem stated in 2025 that extensive evidence connected him to persecution and killings and condemned a Latvian decision to close the criminal investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yadvashem.org/press-release/22-april-2025-14-58.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yadvashem.org">[Yad Vashem]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yadvashem.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on yadvashem.org.</span></span></span>

The legal story did not end there. In April 2025, Latvian prosecutors closed proceedings on the stated ground that the available material was insufficient to establish a prosecutable offence to the required legal standard. After criticism and an internal review, the Prosecutor General’s Office reopened the investigation in May 2025. Its announcement stressed that the case concerned possible participation in the mass destruction of Jewish civilians during the German occupation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yadvashem.org/press-release/22-april-2025-14-58.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yadvashem.org">[yadvashem.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yadvashem.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on yadvashem.org.</span></span></span>

The unresolved question is not whether the Arajs Commando committed atrocities; that is amply documented. Nor is it simply whether Cukurs was present in occupied Riga. The narrower issue is what can be proved about his individual actions to modern criminal-law standards after the destruction of records, death of witnesses and passage of more than eight decades.

The case also exposes a difficult problem in public memory. A national hero’s earlier achievements do not cancel evidence of later crimes. At the same time, an intelligence-service execution is not a substitute for a transparent trial. Both propositions can be true.

## How to judge Latvia’s mystery stories

Latvia’s past has attracted legends about buried wartime treasure, secret tunnels, vanished documents and hidden mass graves. Some may preserve a distorted memory of real events, but they should not be treated alike. The strongest cases can be separated from modern mythmaking by asking a few basic questions.

**Does a contemporary record survive?** The Thiess case is unusually valuable because a court transcript exists. It does not prove his supernatural claims, but it proves that he made them and shows how the judges responded.

**Is the unknown fact narrow or total?** In the Ulmanis case, his imprisonment and death are documented; the missing element is his precise burial place. Describing his entire fate as unknown would be misleading.

**Are competing numbers based on identifiable methods?** Salaspils estimates can be assessed by comparing prisoner records, transport documents, testimony and burial evidence. A large figure repeated on a monument is not automatically more reliable than a smaller estimate derived from named sources.

**Has historical evidence been confused with a court verdict?** Cukurs was never convicted because he was never tried. That legal fact neither proves innocence nor permits every accusation to be accepted without scrutiny.

**Who benefited from a particular version?** Nazi officials concealed crimes. Soviet authorities publicised Nazi atrocities while manipulating some details and suppressing Soviet crimes. Later political movements have sometimes selected evidence according to present-day identity disputes.

These tests do not remove uncertainty. They make it possible to identify what kind of uncertainty is involved.

## What remains genuinely unresolved

The strongest explanation for Latvia’s enduring enigmas is usually not a grand conspiracy but damaged evidence. Ulmanis’s grave may have disappeared into an unmarked Soviet prison cemetery. Thiess’s inner world can no longer be reconstructed beyond the language recorded by his interrogators. Salaspils can be described accurately, but no surviving archive is complete enough to produce a perfectly final victim count. Cukurs’s conduct can be investigated historically, yet the lost opportunity for a contemporary trial cannot be recreated.

New evidence could still change parts of these assessments. An overlooked prison register might narrow the search for Ulmanis. Comparative research could reveal more about Livonian magical beliefs. Digitised German, Soviet or private archives might refine prisoner lists or establish individual wartime actions. Non-invasive archaeological methods, including ground-penetrating radar, are already helping researchers examine suspected Holocaust-era burial areas in Latvia without disturbing human remains unnecessarily.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382390242_Using_Geophysics_to_Locate_Holocaust_Era_Mass_Graves_in_Jewish_Cemeteries_Examples_from_Latvia_and_Lithuania" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Yet some questions will probably remain open because the people who could answer them are dead and the institutions responsible either destroyed their records or never created reliable ones. Latvia’s historical mysteries are therefore most revealing when treated not as invitations to fantasy, but as evidence of how occupation, terror and political memory can leave a country with facts that are known, truths that are contested and absences that no later investigation can fully repair.
