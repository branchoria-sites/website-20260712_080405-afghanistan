## Introduction

Who ordered the murder of Orlando Martínez? That question remains one of the [Dominican Republic]({{ 'dominican-republic/' | relative_url }})'s most enduring political mysteries. The gunmen and several organisers were eventually identified, prosecuted and convicted for the assassination of the prominent journalist in 1975. Yet the central issue has never been conclusively resolved: who gave the ultimate order, and how far up the chain of command did responsibility extend?



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Dominican_Republic_dba665_orlando_martinez_bla_cbb442-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Blank Page illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Dominican_Republic_dba665_orlando_martinez_bla_cbb442-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Dominican_Republic_dba665_orlando_martinez_bla_cbb442-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The mystery deepened in 1988 when former President Joaquín Balaguer published *Memorias de un cortesano de la Era de Trujillo*. Instead of revealing what he claimed to know about the crime, he inserted a deliberately blank page, promising that its contents would be released after his death by a trusted friend. That disclosure never came. The empty page has since become one of the most powerful symbols of political silence and unresolved accountability in modern Dominican history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://open.upress.virginia.edu/read/idle-talk-deadly-talk/section/59001ab2-4934-4475-9a54-2d655390736a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: open.upress.virginia.edu">[open.upress.virginia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">open.upress.virginia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ress Books...</span></span></span>

## The assassination and the men later convicted

Orlando Martínez Howley was one of the Dominican Republic's best-known investigative journalists and political commentators. As editor of the magazine *Ahora* and a columnist for *El Nacional*, he became an increasingly outspoken critic of corruption, military influence and the government of President Joaquín Balaguer during the period known as the "Twelve Years" (1966–1978).

On 17 March 1975, Martínez was ambushed and shot while driving in Santo Domingo. The killing immediately attracted suspicion because he had received threats connected with his reporting and political criticism. For many years, however, the investigation made little progress, reinforcing widespread beliefs that powerful figures were protecting those responsible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cpj.org/2001/03/attacks-on-the-press-2000-dominican-republic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cpj.org">[Committee to Protect Journalists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cpj.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">attacks on the press 2000 dominican republic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Committee to Protect JournalistsAttacks on the Press 2000: Dominican Republic - Committee to Protect JournalistsMarch 19, 2001...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2001</span></span></span>

The case was revived decades later after renewed judicial efforts in the late 1990s. In 2000, retired General Joaquín Pou Castro, former Air Force officer Mariano Cabrera Durán and Luis Emilio de la Rosa Beras received 30-year prison sentences after admitting involvement in the murder. Other suspects also faced prosecution in related proceedings. The convictions represented one of the Dominican Republic's most significant attempts to hold members of the security establishment accountable for a politically motivated killing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/usdos/2001/25873" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: refworld.org">[Refworld]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">refworld.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of State Country Report on Human Rights Practices 2000 - Dominican Republic &#124; RefworldFebruary 26, 2001...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 26, 2001</span></span></span>

Yet even these convictions did not answer the question that mattered most. The courts established participation in the assassination but did not definitively identify the person who authorised it. Witnesses alleged that senior officials possessed prior knowledge or were involved, but no judicial finding established responsibility at the highest political level. Balaguer himself was summoned but did not testify, citing poor health.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/usdos/2001/25873" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: refworld.org">[Refworld]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">refworld.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of State Country Report on Human Rights Practices 2000 - Dominican Republic &#124; RefworldFebruary 26, 2001...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 26, 2001</span></span></span>

## Balaguer's blank page and its competing meanings

The mystery acquired an extraordinary new dimension in 1988.

In *Memorias de un cortesano de la Era de Trujillo*, Balaguer devoted a chapter to Orlando Martínez. Instead of naming whoever he believed had ordered the murder, he printed a page containing only a photograph and a short statement. He wrote that the page would remain silent for many years but would one day "speak", explaining that he had entrusted the missing text to a friend who was expected to outlive him and publish it after his death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://open.upress.virginia.edu/read/idle-talk-deadly-talk/section/59001ab2-4934-4475-9a54-2d655390736a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: open.upress.virginia.edu">[open.upress.virginia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">open.upress.virginia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ress Books...</span></span></span>

That promised revelation never appeared after Balaguer died in 2002.

The unexplained blank page has generated several competing interpretations.

* **A genuine but unfulfilled promise.** Some supporters argued that Balaguer truly intended the information to emerge later but that the unnamed custodian either died, chose not to publish it or never possessed a completed document.
* **A political manoeuvre.** Critics contend that the blank page allowed Balaguer to suggest he knew the truth while avoiding any statement that might expose him or provoke legal or political consequences.
* **A symbolic act of controlled silence.** Historians and literary scholars often view the page as illustrating how authoritarian governments could simultaneously hint at hidden truths while ensuring they remained inaccessible. Rather than solving the mystery, the blank page became part of it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://open.upress.virginia.edu/read/idle-talk-deadly-talk/section/59001ab2-4934-4475-9a54-2d655390736a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: open.upress.virginia.edu">[open.upress.virginia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">open.upress.virginia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ress Books...</span></span></span>

Because neither the alleged manuscript nor the identity of the trusted friend has ever been verified publicly, there is no evidence that the promised disclosure ever existed beyond Balaguer's own claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://listindiario.com/la-republica/2010/07/14/150426/nadie-ha-llenado-pagina-en-blanco-de-balaguer.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: listindiario.com">[Listín Diario]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">listindiario.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Listín Diario Nadie ha llenado página en blanco de Balaguer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Listín DiarioNadie ha llenado página en blanco de BalaguerJuly 14, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 14, 2010</span></span></span>

## Why the chain of command remains disputed

The enduring mystery is not primarily about who pulled the trigger. It concerns political responsibility.

Several factors explain why the question remains unresolved.

First, much of the available evidence concerns operational participants rather than the origin of the order. Criminal convictions established involvement by specific individuals, but the documentary record identifying who authorised the assassination has never emerged publicly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/usdos/2001/25873" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: refworld.org">[Refworld]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">refworld.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of State Country Report on Human Rights Practices 2000 - Dominican Republic &#124; RefworldFebruary 26, 2001...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 26, 2001</span></span></span>

Second, many potential witnesses died before the case reached court. The long delay between the 1975 murder and the major prosecutions meant memories faded, records disappeared and opportunities for cross-examination were lost.

Third, the political environment of the 1970s discouraged independent investigation. Human rights organisations, journalists and later prosecutors argued that fear and institutional protection delayed accountability for decades, making reconstruction of the decision-making process far more difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cpj.org/2001/03/attacks-on-the-press-2000-dominican-republic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cpj.org">[Committee to Protect Journalists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cpj.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">attacks on the press 2000 dominican republic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Committee to Protect JournalistsAttacks on the Press 2000: Dominican Republic - Committee to Protect JournalistsMarch 19, 2001...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 19, 2001</span></span></span>

Finally, Balaguer's own actions complicated historical interpretation. By claiming privileged knowledge while refusing to disclose it, he simultaneously encouraged belief that the answer existed and prevented historians from evaluating whatever evidence he may have possessed. His blank page became a historical source that withholds information rather than providing it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://open.upress.virginia.edu/read/idle-talk-deadly-talk/section/59001ab2-4934-4475-9a54-2d655390736a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: open.upress.virginia.edu">[open.upress.virginia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">open.upress.virginia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ress Books...</span></span></span>

## Why the blank page still matters

The significance of Balaguer's blank page extends beyond one murder investigation. It has become a broader metaphor for how authoritarian periods leave incomplete historical records.

Writers, historians and literary critics have repeatedly used the episode to illustrate the persistence of official silence after political violence. The blank page has appeared in discussions of Dominican memory, transitional justice and literature as an image of deliberate omission rather than accidental loss. Instead of resolving the past, it reminds readers how power can shape what later generations are able to know.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://open.upress.virginia.edu/read/idle-talk-deadly-talk/section/59001ab2-4934-4475-9a54-2d655390736a" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: open.upress.virginia.edu">[open.upress.virginia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">open.upress.virginia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ress Books...</span></span></span>

For many Dominicans, the mystery therefore lies in two connected questions. One concerns the identity of whoever ordered Orlando Martínez's assassination. The other concerns why a former president publicly suggested he knew the answer, promised to reveal it after his death, and ultimately left only an empty page that has remained blank for decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://listindiario.com/la-republica/2010/07/14/150426/nadie-ha-llenado-pagina-en-blanco-de-balaguer.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: listindiario.com">[Listín Diario]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">listindiario.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Listín Diario Nadie ha llenado página en blanco de Balaguer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Listín DiarioNadie ha llenado página en blanco de BalaguerJuly 14, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 14, 2010</span></span></span>
