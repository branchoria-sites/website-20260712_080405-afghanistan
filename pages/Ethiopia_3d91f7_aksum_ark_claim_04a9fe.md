## Introduction

The claim that the biblical Ark of the Covenant is preserved in the northern Ethiopian city of Aksum is one of the world's most enduring historical and religious mysteries. According to the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, the Ark has been kept for centuries in a guarded chapel beside the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion, where only a single appointed guardian monk is permitted to see it. Because no independent scholar, archaeologist or scientific team has been allowed to examine the object, the claim cannot be confirmed or disproved by historical methods.

That tension between deeply held religious belief and the absence of verifiable evidence is what makes the Aksum Ark such an enduring mystery. The story has shaped Ethiopian religious identity, royal tradition and international fascination, yet the available historical evidence remains incomplete and often much later than the events it describes.

## Is the Ark Really Hidden in Aksum?

The object said to be the Ark is believed to be housed in a small chapel within the compound of the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion in Aksum. The current chapel was built in the twentieth century near older churches that have long been associated with [Ethiopia]({{ 'what-lies-behind-ethiopias-greatest/' | relative_url }})'s Christian monarchy.

According to church tradition:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The Ark arrived in Ethiopia in ancient times.
* It has remained under continuous priestly protection ever since.
* Only one monk, known as the Guardian of the Ark, may enter the chapel and care for it.
* When the guardian dies, his successor is appointed before the previous guardian's death and assumes lifelong responsibility for the relic.

</div>

Visitors, clergy and even Ethiopia's highest church officials are not permitted to inspect the object. This restriction is not presented as a security measure alone but as part of its sacred status. The result is unusual among famous historical artefacts: the central piece of evidence is inaccessible to independent examination.

## What the Ethiopian Orthodox Church Teaches

The Ethiopian Orthodox Church does not treat the Ark tradition as a legend or symbolic story. It teaches that the biblical Ark brought by Moses was eventually carried to Ethiopia.

The traditional narrative, preserved most fully in the medieval work known as the *Kebra Nagast* ("The Glory of Kings"), tells the following story:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The Queen of Sheba visited King Solomon in Jerusalem.
* Their son, Menelik, later travelled to Jerusalem.
* During his return journey, the Ark of the Covenant accompanied him to Ethiopia through divine providence.
* A substitute was left behind in the Temple, meaning Jerusalem no longer possessed the original Ark.

</div>

Within Ethiopian tradition, this story explains far more than the location of a sacred object. It establishes Ethiopia as a chosen Christian kingdom and historically reinforced the legitimacy of the Solomonic dynasty, whose rulers claimed descent from Solomon through Menelik.

The Ark therefore occupies both a religious and historical role. It is revered not simply because it is believed to be ancient, but because it symbolises God's covenant with His people and Ethiopia's place within sacred history.

## What Historical Sources Actually Say

The historical evidence is considerably more complex than the religious tradition.

The Hebrew Bible describes the Queen of Sheba visiting Solomon but does not mention a son named Menelik or the removal of the Ark from Jerusalem. Likewise, it contains no account of the Ark travelling to Ethiopia.

The *Kebra Nagast*, which provides the fullest Ethiopian version of the story, was compiled in the fourteenth century—well over two thousand years after the period traditionally associated with Solomon. Although it draws upon older traditions, historians cannot treat it as direct contemporary evidence for events in the tenth century BC.

Ancient inscriptions from Aksum also do not mention the arrival of the Ark. Archaeological discoveries have revealed a powerful Christian kingdom with extensive links across the Red Sea and eastern Mediterranean, but they have not produced evidence demonstrating that the biblical Ark was transferred there.

For historians, this creates a significant chronological gap. The central narrative appears in medieval sources rather than contemporary records, making it difficult to reconstruct how and when the tradition first developed.

## Why the Claim Cannot Be Verified

Unlike many disputed historical objects, the question cannot currently be settled through scientific investigation because the object itself is inaccessible.

Several factors prevent independent verification.

**No direct examination.** No recognised archaeological or museum study has been allowed to inspect the object inside the chapel.

**No published scientific testing.** There has been no radiocarbon dating, material analysis, photography or high-resolution documentation available for scholarly review.

**Restricted access.** The church's rules prevent outside experts from evaluating the artefact's age, materials or construction.

Because of these restrictions, every historical assessment reaches the same basic conclusion: there is insufficient publicly available evidence to authenticate the object as the biblical Ark.

This uncertainty should not be confused with evidence against the claim. Historians simply lack the information required to reach a definitive conclusion.

## A Famous but Controversial Eyewitness Claim

One frequently discussed episode involves the British Ethiopian scholar Edward Ullendorff.

During the British military presence in Ethiopia in 1941, Ullendorff later stated that he had seen an object claimed to be the Ark. He argued that it resembled a medieval Ethiopian ceremonial chest rather than the biblical Ark described in the Old Testament.

His account has attracted considerable attention because very few outsiders have claimed any access at all.

However, historians also recognise important limitations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no photographs or scientific documentation accompanied the claim;
* the Ethiopian Orthodox Church has rejected his description;
* it remains uncertain whether the object he saw was the same one now identified as the Ark.

</div>

Consequently, Ullendorff's recollection is an important historical testimony but not decisive evidence either for or against the traditional claim.

## Why Ethiopian Tabots Matter

A common misunderstanding is that every Ethiopian church claims to possess the Ark of the Covenant.

In fact, Ethiopian Orthodox churches possess **tabots**—small consecrated altar tablets that symbolically represent the Ark rather than being the Ark itself. Every church requires a tabot for its consecration, and these objects are treated with exceptional reverence. They are normally hidden within the sanctuary and only carried publicly during major religious festivals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1868" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: britishmuseum.org">[British Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">British Museumtabot &#124; British Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">British Museumtabot &#124; British Museum</span></span></span>

## Why the Mystery Endures

The Aksum Ark remains compelling precisely because it cannot be resolved by ordinary historical methods.

If the chapel were opened for scientific examination, researchers could potentially determine:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the object's approximate age;
* whether its materials fit an ancient Near Eastern origin;
* whether it is a medieval Ethiopian construction;
* whether it is something else entirely.

</div>

At present, none of those questions can be answered.

For the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, however, such testing is beside the point. The Ark is not regarded primarily as an archaeological artefact but as a living sacred object whose holiness outweighs scholarly curiosity.

This difference in purpose explains why the debate has lasted for generations. Historians seek evidence that can be independently tested, while the church's authority rests on religious tradition and continuous custodianship rather than public inspection.

## The Most Balanced Historical Assessment

The strongest evidence-supported conclusion is necessarily limited.

It is well established that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the Ethiopian Orthodox Church sincerely teaches that the biblical Ark is preserved in Aksum;
* the tradition has shaped Ethiopian religious life and royal ideology for many centuries;
* access to the object is restricted to a single guardian monk;
* no independent examination has authenticated the object.

</div>

What remains uncertain is whether the artefact in the chapel is the biblical Ark itself, an ancient ceremonial object of another kind, a medieval sacred chest, or an object whose true history is now impossible to reconstruct.

That unresolved gap between enduring faith and inaccessible evidence is what makes the Ark of the Covenant claim in Aksum one of Ethiopia's most remarkable and persistent historical mysteries.

      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};

      } catch (err) {}

    if (localeMarkets.exact && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.exact) >= 0) return localeMarkets.exact;
    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;

    return available[0] || '';
  }
