## Introduction

Were the Vazimba [Madagascar]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-madagascars/' | relative_url }})'s first people? The honest answer is that nobody knows with certainty. The name **Vazimba** appears throughout Malagasy oral traditions as the island's earliest inhabitants, especially in the central highlands, but the figure remembered today is part history, part ancestry and part sacred tradition. Rather than representing a single, clearly identifiable ethnic group, the Vazimba are increasingly understood by many historians and archaeologists as a cultural memory of early communities who lived in Madagascar before the rise of later kingdoms, particularly the Merina. Over centuries, those memories became intertwined with royal origin stories, ancestor worship and beliefs about sacred landscapes. The mystery lies not in whether the Vazimba existed, but in how much of the tradition preserves genuine historical events and how much reflects later political and religious storytelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/fr/vazimba-peuplement-malgache" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archeologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

## What oral traditions say about the Vazimba

Across Madagascar, especially in the central highlands, oral traditions describe the Vazimba as the earliest occupants of forests, marshes and river valleys. Unlike later kingdoms, they are rarely portrayed as builders of large states. Instead, they are remembered as people deeply connected with the land and water, whose descendants or spirits continued to inhabit sacred places long after their political independence disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/history-in-africa/article/abs/tompontany-and-the-tompondrano-in-the-history-of-central-and-western-madagascar/9E22A656CFD23704B98AEA7C9715CC3B" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe tompon-tany and the tompon-drano in the History of Central and Western Madagascar* &#124; History i...</span></span></span>

Different regions preserve different versions of the tradition. Some accounts portray the Vazimba as ordinary human communities who were gradually displaced or absorbed. Others describe them as unusually small people living in forests or wetlands, while still others present them as powerful ancestral spirits rather than historical humans. These conflicting descriptions suggest that the tradition evolved over many centuries instead of preserving a single eyewitness memory. Nineteenth-century collectors of Malagasy folklore recorded stories in which the Vazimba already occupied an ambiguous position between human ancestors and supernatural beings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AThe_Folk-Lore_Journal_Volume_1_1883.djvu/323" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:The Folk Lore Journal Volume 1 1883.djvu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page:The Folk-Lore Journal Volume 1 1883.djvu/323 - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

Many Malagasy communities continue to associate particular lakes, marshes, caves and tombs with Vazimba ancestors. Such places are often treated with respect because disturbing them is believed to risk misfortune. These beliefs are part of wider Malagasy traditions of honouring ancestors and recognising certain landscapes as spiritually significant rather than simply archaeological sites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291119013_Vazimba_et_esprits_helo_la_profondeur_chronologique" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Vazimba et esprits helo: la profondeur chronologique</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Vazimba et esprits helo: la profondeur chronologique</span></span></span>

## Conquest, intermarriage and cultural memory

One of the most influential traditions concerns the expansion of the Merina kingdoms in Madagascar's central highlands between roughly the fifteenth and seventeenth centuries. Royal chronicles describe Merina rulers, especially Andriamanelo, defeating or replacing Vazimba rulers and establishing the political foundations of Imerina. These stories became central to Merina ideas of state formation and royal legitimacy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?DB=p&amp;RID=239995031" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AfricaBib &#124; Les &#x27;sagaies volantes&#x27; d&#x27;Andriamanelo et les &#x27;sagaies à pointes d&#x27;argile&#x27; des Vazimba: un problème de critique de la...</span></span></span>

Modern historians, however, approach these accounts cautiously. Oral traditions often compress generations of gradual change into dramatic stories of conquest. Rather than a straightforward military replacement of one people by another, archaeological evidence increasingly points towards prolonged coexistence, intermarriage and cultural exchange between populations occupying the highlands. The transition appears to have been more complex than the later royal narratives suggested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/fr/vazimba-peuplement-malgache" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archeologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because royal histories were not neutral records. They were also political documents explaining why one dynasty ruled another's territory. Presenting the Vazimba as earlier occupants overcome by later kings helped legitimise Merina authority while simultaneously preserving the memory of those earlier inhabitants as respected ancestors rather than simply defeated enemies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?DB=p&amp;RID=239995031" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AfricaBib &#124; Les &#x27;sagaies volantes&#x27; d&#x27;Andriamanelo et les &#x27;sagaies à pointes d&#x27;argile&#x27; des Vazimba: un problème de critique de la...</span></span></span>

Some traditions even remember marriages between incoming elites and Vazimba families. Whether every such account reflects actual events cannot be demonstrated, but they fit a broader historical pattern in which expanding societies often strengthened political legitimacy by claiming descent from earlier local populations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/fr/vazimba-peuplement-malgache" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archeologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

## Where folklore ends and historical evidence begins

Archaeology has complicated older assumptions that the Vazimba were purely mythical. Excavations in Madagascar's highlands have uncovered settlements, agricultural systems and burial practices predating or overlapping the rise of the Merina kingdom. These discoveries do not prove the identity of their inhabitants, but they demonstrate that organised communities occupied the region before later state formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/fr/vazimba-peuplement-malgache" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archeologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

Current archaeological research therefore asks more specific questions than earlier scholarship. Instead of asking whether the Vazimba "really existed", researchers investigate how early highland communities developed, how populations mixed over time, and whether material remains can be linked with traditions preserved in oral history. Recent multidisciplinary projects combine excavation, ancient DNA, funerary archaeology and landscape studies to examine these questions without assuming that every legend corresponds directly to a single archaeological culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/fr/vazimba-peuplement-malgache" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archeologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

There is also little evidence supporting some popular modern claims. Stories portraying the Vazimba as a race of mysterious dwarfs, supernatural beings or survivors of a forgotten civilisation attract attention online but lack convincing archaeological or genetic support. Such descriptions are better understood as products of folklore and later storytelling than as reliable historical testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291119013_Vazimba_et_esprits_helo_la_profondeur_chronologique" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Vazimba et esprits helo: la profondeur chronologique</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Vazimba et esprits helo: la profondeur chronologique</span></span></span>

Likewise, there is no scholarly consensus that the Vazimba represented a completely separate population unrelated to today's Malagasy. Many researchers instead consider them likely to represent earlier waves of settlement that became absorbed into later Malagasy societies, making their descendants part of the island's present population rather than a vanished people.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/fr/vazimba-peuplement-malgache" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archeologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why the Vazimba remain historically important

The enduring fascination of the Vazimba comes from the way they sit between archaeology and memory. They are neither simply mythical spirits nor securely identifiable historical actors. Instead, they occupy the difficult space where oral tradition preserves echoes of real social change while reshaping those events through centuries of ritual, politics and storytelling.

Their story also illustrates how history is remembered differently from how it is experienced. A gradual process of migration, coexistence, alliance and cultural mixing may later become a dramatic tale of conquest and replacement. For historians of Madagascar, the Vazimba therefore remain less a solved historical identity than an ongoing investigation into how societies remember their origins. The Vazimba continue to represent one of Madagascar's most compelling examples of the boundary between documented history, cultural memory and sacred tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/fr/vazimba-peuplement-malgache" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archeologie.culture.gouv.fr">[Archeologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archeologie.culture.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>
