## Introduction

The disappearances of Mona Blades in 1975 and Kirsa Jensen in 1983 remain two of [New Zealand]({{ 'which-new-zealand-mysteries-still-defy/' | relative_url }})'s best-known unsolved missing-person cases. Although they occurred eight years apart and under very different circumstances, both involve young women who vanished during ordinary journeys, prompted extensive searches, generated significant witness evidence, and ultimately left investigators without the decisive physical evidence needed to establish exactly what happened. In neither case have human remains been recovered, no one has been convicted, and key witness accounts have remained difficult to verify. Their enduring significance lies not in sensational theories but in the contrast between the large amount of investigative effort and the small amount of conclusive evidence that survived.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nzhistory.govt.nz/mona-blades-vanishes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nzhistory.govt.nz">[NZHistory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nzhistory.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NZHistory Mona Blades vanishes &#124; NZ History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mona Blades vanishes &#124; NZ HistoryMay 31, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 31, 2023</span></span></span>

## The last confirmed movements

### Mona Blades

On Saturday 31 May 1975, 18-year-old Mona Blades left Hamilton intending to hitchhike to her parents' home in Hastings, where she planned to surprise her family at her nephew's first birthday celebration. She was carrying a birthday present and expected to arrive the same day. Because the visit was intended as a surprise, several days passed before her disappearance became apparent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nzhistory.govt.nz/mona-blades-vanishes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nzhistory.govt.nz">[NZHistory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nzhistory.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NZHistory Mona Blades vanishes &#124; NZ History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mona Blades vanishes &#124; NZ HistoryMay 31, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 31, 2023</span></span></span>

The best-known early witness reported seeing Mona enter an orange Datsun station wagon. Later that morning, a fencing contractor travelling along Matea Road, off the Napier–Taupō highway, saw what appeared to be the same vehicle parked on a rural side road. He recalled a young woman matching Mona's description sitting in the rear of the car with a middle-aged male driver. When he passed again shortly afterwards, the vehicle was empty. This became the defining sighting around which much of the original investigation was built.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nzhistory.govt.nz/mona-blades-vanishes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nzhistory.govt.nz">[NZHistory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nzhistory.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NZHistory Mona Blades vanishes &#124; NZ History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mona Blades vanishes &#124; NZ HistoryMay 31, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 31, 2023</span></span></span>

Modern police reviews have questioned whether this famous sighting represents the whole story. Detectives later identified several witnesses placing Mona in Taupō later than originally believed, including people who said they spoke with her directly and others who remembered seeing her near the Spa Hotel. Those later accounts raised the possibility that she remained in Taupō for some time and may have left with people she already knew rather than the occupants of the orange Datsun. Police have never established which sequence is correct.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/stolenwanted/coldcase/2020/mona-elizabeth-blades-1975" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Mona Elizabeth Blades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand PoliceMona Elizabeth Blades - 1975 &#124; New Zealand Police...</span></span></span>

### Kirsa Jensen

Fourteen-year-old Kirsa Jensen disappeared on 1 September 1983 after collecting her horse, Commodore, for a routine afternoon ride near Awatoto, south of Napier. Unlike Mona Blades, whose journey depended on strangers, Kirsa was following a familiar local route that she had ridden many times.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/news/ten-one-magazine/remembering-kirsa-%E2%80%93-40-years" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Remembering Kirsa – 40 years on &#124; New Zealand Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand Police Remembering Kirsa – 40 years on &#124; New Zealand Police</span></span></span>

Her last confirmed movements placed her near a disused Second World War gun emplacement close to the mouth of the Tūtaekuri River. Witnesses saw her with her horse during the afternoon, and one account described her leading rather than riding the animal. When she failed to return home, her family began searching almost immediately before police mounted a major operation. Commodore was later found wandering without his rider, making it clear that something unusual had interrupted what should have been a routine ride.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/news/ten-one-magazine/remembering-kirsa-%E2%80%93-40-years" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Remembering Kirsa – 40 years on &#124; New Zealand Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand Police Remembering Kirsa – 40 years on &#124; New Zealand Police</span></span></span>

## Witnesses, searches and missing evidence

Both investigations produced numerous witness statements but very little forensic evidence capable of proving a sequence of events.

In Mona Blades' case, police conducted one of New Zealand's largest missing-person investigations of its time. Hundreds of officers searched roughly 200 kilometres of highway and followed thousands of leads. Investigators examined hundreds of orange Datsun station wagons and interviewed more than 500 potential suspects or persons of interest over the years. Despite thousands of search hours, neither Mona's body nor any confirmed personal belongings were ever recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/stolenwanted/coldcase/2020/mona-elizabeth-blades-1975" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Mona Elizabeth Blades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand PoliceMona Elizabeth Blades - 1975 &#124; New Zealand Police...</span></span></span>

The later cold-case review significantly altered investigators' emphasis. Detectives concluded that the original public photograph of Mona may not have reflected her appearance at the time of her disappearance, potentially affecting witness recognition. They also gave greater weight to sightings placing her in Taupō and appealed for information about a red Toyota station wagon, suggesting that early assumptions about the orange Datsun might have diverted attention from other important leads. Even so, no reconstruction has been proved conclusively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/stolenwanted/coldcase/2020/mona-elizabeth-blades-1975" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Mona Elizabeth Blades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand PoliceMona Elizabeth Blades - 1975 &#124; New Zealand Police...</span></span></span>

Kirsa Jensen's disappearance generated a different type of evidence. Witnesses reported seeing her near the gun emplacement, some recalling an unidentified man and a white utility vehicle in the vicinity. Commodore had apparently been tied to the concrete structure before breaking free, and blood was reportedly found on or near the emplacement. Investigators explored whether Kirsa had suffered a fall, been assaulted there, or been removed from the scene by another person. None of those possibilities could be established beyond doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rnz.co.nz/national/programmes/afternoons/audio/2018758273/crimes-nz-the-unsolved-disappearance-of-kirsa-jensen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rnz.co.nz">[rnz.co.nz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rnz.co.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">RNZCrimes NZ: The unsolved disappearance of Kirsa Jensen &#124; RNZ</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RNZCrimes NZ: The unsolved disappearance of Kirsa Jensen &#124; RNZ</span></span></span>

Searches covered beaches, nearby rivers, surrounding farmland and coastal areas. Volunteers joined police in extensive ground operations, yet no trace of Kirsa herself was discovered. As in Mona Blades' case, the absence of recoverable remains or decisive forensic evidence prevented investigators from testing competing explanations against physical proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/news/ten-one-magazine/remembering-kirsa-%E2%80%93-40-years" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Remembering Kirsa – 40 years on &#124; New Zealand Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand Police Remembering Kirsa – 40 years on &#124; New Zealand Police</span></span></span>

## Why neither disappearance has been solved

The principal obstacle in both cases is not a lack of theories but a lack of evidence capable of distinguishing between them.

For Mona Blades, investigators broadly believe she was the victim of homicide, yet several fundamental questions remain unanswered:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether the famous orange Datsun sighting genuinely involved Mona;
* whether later Taupō sightings are more reliable;
* who, if anyone, she willingly travelled with after arriving in Taupō; and
* where any crime may actually have occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/stolenwanted/coldcase/2020/mona-elizabeth-blades-1975" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Mona Elizabeth Blades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand PoliceMona Elizabeth Blades - 1975 &#124; New Zealand Police...</span></span></span>

</div>

The passage of time has compounded those uncertainties. Witness memories inevitably faded, many potential suspects died or became elderly, and any physical evidence that might once have existed was either never located or has deteriorated beyond useful forensic examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/stolenwanted/coldcase/2020/mona-elizabeth-blades-1975" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Mona Elizabeth Blades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand PoliceMona Elizabeth Blades - 1975 &#124; New Zealand Police...</span></span></span>

Kirsa Jensen's case presents similar investigative problems despite occurring in a far more confined area. Detectives have long regarded foul play as the most likely explanation, but they have never been able to identify a suspect against whom sufficient admissible evidence exists. ## Why the cases still matter

These disappearances occupy an important place in New Zealand's public memory because they illustrate how even large, determined investigations can fail when crucial evidence disappears at the outset.

Mona Blades' case has become closely associated with changing public attitudes towards hitchhiking. During the 1970s it was a common means of travel for young New Zealanders, but her disappearance became one of several widely remembered cases that altered perceptions of its risks. Police continue to treat the investigation as an open homicide inquiry and periodically appeal for new information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nzhistory.govt.nz/mona-blades-vanishes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nzhistory.govt.nz">[NZHistory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nzhistory.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NZHistory Mona Blades vanishes &#124; NZ History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mona Blades vanishes &#124; NZ HistoryMay 31, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 31, 2023</span></span></span>

Kirsa Jensen's disappearance remains equally significant because it occurred in daylight, close to populated areas, and involved a routine horse ride rather than an obviously hazardous situation. The case has continued to receive official attention, including police commemorations marking the fortieth anniversary of her disappearance and renewed public appeals for information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.police.govt.nz/news/ten-one-magazine/remembering-kirsa-%E2%80%93-40-years" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: police.govt.nz">[New Zealand Police]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">police.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Zealand Police Remembering Kirsa – 40 years on &#124; New Zealand Police</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Zealand Police Remembering Kirsa – 40 years on &#124; New Zealand Police</span></span></span>
