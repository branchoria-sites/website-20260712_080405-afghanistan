## Introduction

Great [Zimbabwe]({{ 'which-mysteries-still-haunt-zimbabwes/' | relative_url }}) is one of Africa's greatest archaeological achievements, yet some of its most famous features still resist straightforward explanation. There is no serious scholarly dispute that the city was built by the ancestors of today's Shona-speaking communities and flourished as the political and economic heart of a powerful kingdom between roughly the twelfth and fifteenth centuries. The enduring mysteries lie elsewhere: why were certain monuments built in such unusual forms, what did they symbolise to the people who used them, and why did this prosperous centre gradually lose its political dominance? Archaeology has answered some questions with confidence while leaving others open because the city produced no surviving written records and much valuable evidence was destroyed by early colonial excavators. As a result, the debate today is not about mythical foreign builders, but about interpreting a remarkable African city through the physical evidence that remains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Great Zimbabwe National Monument</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreGreat Zimbabwe National Monument - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

## What archaeologists know about the city

Modern archaeology presents Great Zimbabwe as a royal capital, religious centre and trading hub linked to the wider Indian Ocean world. Imported Chinese porcelain, glass beads from Asia and other prestige goods demonstrate participation in extensive commercial networks, while the massive dry-stone architecture reflects an organised society capable of mobilising skilled labour on a remarkable scale. The surviving complex is usually divided into the Hill Complex, the Great Enclosure and the Valley Ruins, each appearing to have served different social and political functions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smarthistory.org/great-zimbabwe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smarthistory.org">[Smarthistory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smarthistory.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">– Great Zimbabwe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smarthistory – Great Zimbabwe...</span></span></span>

What remains uncertain is exactly how power operated within the city. Archaeologists broadly agree that the walls were not primarily military fortifications. Instead, the arrangement of narrow passages, enclosed courtyards and carefully controlled entrances suggests that architecture regulated movement and expressed political authority. Some researchers argue that different rulers shifted their residence between the Hill Complex and the Great Enclosure over time, while others believe these spaces simultaneously served different ceremonial and administrative purposes. Without contemporary written records, these interpretations must be reconstructed from architecture, artefacts and comparison with later Shona traditions rather than direct testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Great Zimbabwe National Monument</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreGreat Zimbabwe National Monument - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

The site also suffered irreversible damage before modern archaeological methods were introduced. Treasure hunting, uncontrolled digging and colonial-era excavations removed valuable occupation layers that could have preserved evidence about chronology, ritual activity and everyday life. Those losses permanently narrowed the questions that archaeology can now answer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/great-zimbabwe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: education.nationalgeographic.org">[National Geographic Education]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">education.nationalgeographic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Geographic Education Great Zimbabwe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Geographic EducationGreat ZimbabweOctober 19, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2023</span></span></span>

## The Conical Tower and the soapstone birds

Among Great Zimbabwe's monuments, none has inspired more debate than the Conical Tower inside the Great Enclosure. Rising as a solid stone structure with no doorway or internal chamber, it was clearly not designed as a practical building. Its purpose remains one of the site's most enduring mysteries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Great Zimbabwe National Monument</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreGreat Zimbabwe National Monument - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

Several interpretations remain plausible:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A symbol of royal authority.** Many archaeologists view the tower as a public statement of political power rather than a functional structure.
* **A representation of agricultural wealth.** A long-standing interpretation compares its shape with traditional grain storage bins, suggesting it symbolised a ruler's responsibility to provide food security. This does not mean it actually functioned as a granary.
* **A fertility or cosmological monument.** Some scholars see the tower's form and location within ceremonial space as evidence that it carried religious or symbolic meaning connected with prosperity and continuity.

</div>

None of these explanations has been conclusively demonstrated because no inscriptions or contemporary descriptions explain the monument's purpose. The tower therefore remains a carefully argued archaeological interpretation rather than a solved puzzle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://smarthistory.org/great-zimbabwe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smarthistory.org">[Smarthistory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smarthistory.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">– Great Zimbabwe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smarthistory – Great Zimbabwe...</span></span></span>

The famous soapstone birds present a similar challenge. At least eight carved birds once stood on tall stone pillars, most of them associated with the Hill Complex. Their distinctive mixture of bird and human characteristics has generated decades of discussion. Some researchers identify them as birds of prey symbolising royal authority, while others connect them with ancestral spirits or religious intermediaries. Because the sculptures were removed from their original positions during the colonial era and some were damaged or dispersed, reconstructing their precise archaeological context has proved difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arkeologi.bokorder.se/en-US/article/3626/the-soapstone-birds-of-great-zimbabwe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arkeologi.bokorder.se">[bokorder.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arkeologi.bokorder.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bokorder.se.</span></span></span>

Although their original meaning remains uncertain, the birds have acquired enormous modern significance. They became national symbols after Zimbabwe's independence and appear on the country's flag and coat of arms, illustrating how an ancient archaeological mystery has become part of modern national identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/great-zimbabwe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: education.nationalgeographic.org">[National Geographic Education]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">education.nationalgeographic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Geographic Education Great Zimbabwe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Geographic EducationGreat ZimbabweOctober 19, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why the centre lost its power

The decline of Great Zimbabwe is another question without a single accepted answer. Archaeologists increasingly reject the idea of a sudden catastrophic collapse. Instead, evidence suggests a gradual redistribution of political and economic power during the fifteenth century, with influence shifting towards emerging centres such as Khami and the Mutapa state.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thecivilizationarchive.com/civilization/great-zimbabwe/chapter/4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecivilizationarchive.com">[The Civilization Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecivilizationarchive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Civilization Archive Decline</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Civilization ArchiveDecline - Great Zimbabwe &#124; The Civilization Archive...</span></span></span>

Several explanations probably interacted rather than acting independently.

**Changing trade networks.** Great Zimbabwe's prosperity depended heavily on controlling regional trade in gold, ivory and other valuable goods connected to Indian Ocean commerce. As trade routes evolved and rival political centres gained influence, the city's strategic importance appears to have diminished. Imported luxury goods become less common in later archaeological layers, consistent with declining international connections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thecivilizationarchive.com/civilization/great-zimbabwe/chapter/4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecivilizationarchive.com">[The Civilization Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecivilizationarchive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Civilization Archive Decline</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Civilization ArchiveDecline - Great Zimbabwe &#124; The Civilization Archive...</span></span></span>

**Environmental pressure.** Some researchers argue that population growth placed increasing strain on local resources through grazing, farming and wood consumption. Environmental studies have suggested that deforestation, overgrazing or declining agricultural productivity may have reduced the area's capacity to support a large urban population. However, climate alone is not widely accepted as a complete explanation, particularly because Great Zimbabwe occupied a comparatively favourable rainfall zone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thecivilizationarchive.com/civilization/great-zimbabwe/chapter/4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecivilizationarchive.com">[thecivilizationarchive.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecivilizationarchive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Civilization Archive Decline</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Civilization ArchiveDecline - Great Zimbabwe &#124; The Civilization Archive...</span></span></span>

**Political reorganisation.** Rather than representing failure, abandonment may reflect the changing geography of power. As new dynasties expanded into regions with better access to resources or trade, political authority could have shifted naturally to new capitals without requiring a dramatic crisis. Archaeological evidence increasingly supports continuity between Great Zimbabwe and successor states rather than abrupt societal collapse.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thecivilizationarchive.com/civilization/great-zimbabwe/chapter/4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecivilizationarchive.com">[The Civilization Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecivilizationarchive.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Civilization Archive Decline</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Civilization ArchiveDecline - Great Zimbabwe &#124; The Civilization Archive...</span></span></span>

Shona oral traditions add another layer to the debate. Some traditions refer to shortages of salt, which scholars have interpreted literally by some and symbolically by others—as a possible reflection of wider resource depletion or changing trade relationships. Such traditions are valuable historical evidence but cannot be treated as straightforward factual accounts of events centuries earlier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Zimbabwe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Great Zimbabwe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Great Zimbabwe</span></span></span>

## Why these mysteries remain unresolved

The unanswered questions surrounding Great Zimbabwe are not simply the result of missing written records. Several factors continue to limit certainty.

First, archaeology can identify buildings, artefacts and environmental change, but symbolism is inherently more difficult to reconstruct. A monument like the Conical Tower may have carried political, religious and social meanings simultaneously.

Second, the destruction of archaeological layers during nineteenth- and early twentieth-century digging removed evidence that might have clarified chronology, ritual practices and building sequences.

Third, oral traditions, ethnographic comparison and archaeological evidence each illuminate different aspects of the past but rarely provide identical answers. Historians therefore combine multiple forms of evidence rather than relying on any single source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/great-zimbabwe/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: education.nationalgeographic.org">[nationalgeographic.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">education.nationalgeographic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Geographic Education Great Zimbabwe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Geographic EducationGreat ZimbabweOctober 19, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2023</span></span></span>

## What Great Zimbabwe still conceals

Great Zimbabwe is no longer mysterious because of doubts about who built it. That question has been answered by decades of archaeological research. The genuine mysteries concern meaning rather than origin: why its monuments took their distinctive forms, how political authority was expressed through architecture, and why one of southern Africa's greatest medieval capitals gradually gave way to new centres of power.

These unanswered questions continue to attract research because they illuminate far more than a single archaeological site. They reveal how complex African states organised authority without written chronicles, how symbolism can survive long after its original explanation is forgotten, and how prosperous cities may transform through gradual economic and political change rather than sudden collapse. Great Zimbabwe remains remarkable precisely because its stone walls preserve enough evidence to answer many questions while leaving some of the most important ones still open.
