## Introduction

Some cases are genuine puzzles with several plausible explanations. Others are better understood as documented crimes whose precise details were concealed. The distinction matters. Calling every disappearance a “mystery” can obscure responsibility, while treating oral tradition as literal fact can turn rich cultural memory into misleading pseudo-history. The strongest evidence shows that Uganda’s enigmas survive mainly where records were suppressed, physical evidence disappeared or history and legend became inseparable.

## The disappeared under Idi Amin

The abductions of the 1970s form Uganda’s gravest unresolved historical record. After Idi Amin seized power in January 1971, military and intelligence bodies detained opponents, officials, soldiers, professionals and civilians, many of whom were never seen again. A contemporary investigation by the International Commission of Jurists identified military intelligence as one of the organisations responsible for arrests and disappearances and described a climate in which witnesses demanded anonymity because they feared reprisals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.icj.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Uganda-violations-of-human-rights-thematic-report-1974-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icj.org">[International Commission of Jurists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icj.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Commission of Jurists</span></span></span>

These cases are not mysterious in the sense that no one can imagine what happened. The general pattern of state violence is well established. What remains unknown in individual cases is who issued particular orders, where victims were killed and where their remains were taken.

### Benedicto Kiwanuka’s missing body

Benedicto Kiwanuka, Uganda’s first prime minister and first Ugandan chief justice, was abducted from the High Court in Kampala on 21 September 1972. Accounts agree that armed security personnel seized him after he had made judicial decisions displeasing to the regime. He was never publicly produced again, and no authenticated remains have been recovered. Uganda’s Judiciary acknowledges that contradictory accounts emerged about how and where he was killed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://judiciary.go.ug/files/publications/JudiciaryInsiderIssue11webversion.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: judiciary.go.ug">[Judiciary Uganda]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">judiciary.go.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Judiciary UgandaRemembering Benedicto KiwanukaSeptember 19, 2018 — 3 Sept 2018 — Kiwanuka&#x27;s appointment in 1971 as first Ugandan Chief Ju...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 19, 2018</span></span></span>

The historical uncertainty concerns the final hours, not whether Kiwanuka simply wandered away. Reports have variously placed his death at Makindye military prison or another security location. In May 1974, Amin ordered what was described as a full inquiry into disappearances including Kiwanuka’s, but contemporary observers criticised the government’s repeated failure to investigate violence meaningfully.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.icj.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Uganda-violations-of-human-rights-thematic-report-1974-eng.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icj.org">[International Commission of Jurists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icj.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Commission of Jurists</span></span></span>

Kiwanuka’s case became a national symbol because the victim was the country’s highest judicial officer. His seizure from a place associated with law demonstrated that rank and legal procedure offered no protection. The absence of a body has also prevented the ordinary rituals of burial and final confirmation, leaving his family and the public with an unresolved physical question inside an otherwise well-documented history of repression.

### Francis Xavier Ruhesi and less famous victims

The disappearance of engineer Francis Xavier Ruhesi illustrates how many important cases remained outside international attention. Ruhesi, one of Uganda’s early indigenous chartered civil engineers, was reportedly abducted on 25 September 1972 and never seen again. His family has continued to seek information about his fate more than half a century later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/special-reports/ruhesi-the-engineer-who-vanished-without-a-trace-5403510" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Monitor Ruhesi: The engineer who vanished without a trace</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">abducted on September 25, 1972. Such cases complicate the popular memory of the Amin period. Public discussion tends to concentrate on prominent politicians, foreign victims and spectacular incidents, but the archive of disappearance also includes civil servants, soldiers, businesspeople and professionals. Researchers studying family memory describe this condition as “ambiguous loss”: relatives know that a loved one was probably killed but lack a body, a reliable account or an acknowledged grave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/isagsq/article/5/1/ksaf017/8087928" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicAffective Archives of the Disappeared &#124; Global Studies Quarterlyby E Baines · 2025 · Cited by 4 — In this article, we conside...</span></span></span>

A photographic collection rediscovered in the Uganda Broadcasting Corporation’s stores has added a different kind of evidence. Thousands of government images from the Amin era were digitised, and a selection was exhibited at the Uganda Museum in 2019. The pictures illuminate how the regime represented itself, but they do not provide a straightforward catalogue of its victims. They show both the value and the limits of archives created by the same state accused of concealing violence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/unseen-archive-of-idi-amin-making-history-in-a-tight-corner/A3FFA73CD324320B6ED55DEA8C730A8A" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentThe Unseen Archive of Idi Amin: Making History in a Tight Corner &#124; Comparative Studies in Society...</span></span></span>

## Did Edward Mutesa II die naturally?

Sir Edward Mutesa II, Kabaka of Buganda and Uganda’s first president, died in London on 21 November 1969. He had been living in exile since the 1966 confrontation in which Prime Minister Milton Obote suspended the constitution and government forces attacked the royal palace.

The accepted medical account is that Mutesa died from acute alcohol poisoning. That conclusion has never fully displaced suspicions among his supporters that agents connected to Obote forced alcohol or poison upon him. The political background made such suspicions understandable: Mutesa had been overthrown, driven abroad and remained an important symbol of Buganda’s opposition to Obote.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/oi/authority.20110803100219365" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oxfordreference.com">[Oxford Reference]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oxfordreference.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oxford ReferenceMutesa IIHe wrote his autobiography, Desecration of my Kingdom (1967), in exile in London. He died of alcohol poisoning...</span></span></span>

The assassination theory, however, lacks publicly demonstrated forensic proof. Later retellings often repeat detailed claims about an agent, a woman visitor or alcohol being administered by force, but these versions differ and are seldom supported by contemporary documentation. One frequently cited complication is that journalist John Simpson reportedly met Mutesa shortly before his death and remembered him as sober and in good spirits. That observation raises a question about the speed and circumstances of the fatal intoxication, but it does not by itself establish murder.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Simpson_%28journalist%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">John Simpson (journalist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">John Simpson (journalist</span></span></span>

The most defensible conclusion is therefore limited. Alcohol poisoning is the documented cause of death; assassination remains an enduring political suspicion rather than a proven finding. The case persists because the death occurred in exile, without trusted witnesses, after a bitter constitutional struggle. It became part of a larger story about the destruction of Buganda’s political autonomy rather than merely a disputed medical event.

## Who built Bigo bya Mugyenyi?

Bigo bya Mugyenyi is one of Uganda’s most impressive archaeological puzzles. Near the Katonga River in western Uganda, extensive ditches and banks enclose a landscape of roughly ten square kilometres. UNESCO’s description dates the earthworks broadly to between the fourteenth and sixteenth centuries and records their association in oral tradition with the legendary Bacwezi rulers Ndahura and Wamala.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/911/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Bigo bya Mugyenyi (Archaeological Earthworks</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World Heritage Centre Bigo bya Mugyenyi (Archaeological Earthworks</span></span></span>

The site is sometimes promoted as the capital or fortress of a vanished Bacwezi empire. Archaeology supports a more cautious account. Excavations recovered pottery, iron objects and evidence of substantial organised settlement, but no inscription identifies the builders, no royal chronicle fixes their names, and material remains cannot confirm that legendary figures personally constructed the works.

The function of the ditches is also debated. Early interpretations treated them primarily as military defences. Later scholars questioned whether such a long boundary could have been effectively guarded and proposed several overlapping purposes: controlling cattle, protecting cultivation from large animals, marking authority, directing movement and separating an elite centre from the surrounding landscape. The earthworks may have served practical and symbolic functions at the same time.

The real historical interest lies in the meeting of archaeology and oral tradition. Bacwezi stories preserve cultural memories of rulership, cattle-keeping and political change in the Great Lakes region. They should not be dismissed as meaningless folklore, but neither can every supernatural or dynastic detail be converted directly into archaeological fact. Bigo shows how a real medieval centre can accumulate stories that explain its ruins long after its builders’ identities have been lost.

## What started the Kasubi Tombs fire?

On the night of 16 March 2010, fire destroyed the main mausoleum at the Tombs of the Kings of Buganda at Kasubi in Kampala. The building, a major work of architecture made largely from wood, reeds, thatch and other organic materials, contained the burial places of four Buganda kings and stood at the centre of a living spiritual landscape. UNESCO placed the property on its List of World Heritage in Danger after the disaster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2607" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on unesco.org.</span></span></span>

Suspicion spread almost immediately. Relations between Uganda’s central government and the Buganda Kingdom were tense, and some mourners assumed that the blaze must have been deliberate. Other possibilities included an electrical fault, negligence or a fire connected to activity around the site. Public anger intensified when security forces confronted crowds during a presidential visit.

A government commission began hearings in December 2010 and delivered its report in March 2011. The report was not made public, despite legal efforts and repeated demands for its release. People who followed the inquiry recalled testimony pointing towards both possible arson and negligence, but without the report it is impossible for the public to evaluate what evidence the commission accepted or rejected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/insight/kasubi-tombs-unsolved-mystery-11-years-after-the-fire-3418600" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: monitor.co.ug">[Monitor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">monitor.co.ug</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Monitor Kasubi Tombs: Unsolved mystery 11 years after the fire &#124; Monitor</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Monitor Kasubi Tombs: Unsolved mystery 11 years after the fire &#124; Monitor</span></span></span>

Reconstruction eventually restored the principal structure, and UNESCO removed Kasubi from the danger list in 2023 after assessing the conservation work. Restoration resolved the architectural emergency but not the historical question of causation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1022/documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Tombs of Buganda Kings at Kasubi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">World Heritage Centre Tombs of Buganda Kings at Kasubi</span></span></span>

The missing report is now part of the mystery itself. Even a finding of accidental fire would require evidence about ignition, maintenance and emergency response. Continued secrecy leaves space for political interpretations that cannot be conclusively proved or disproved. Kasubi demonstrates how withholding an official inquiry can prolong suspicion long after physical damage has been repaired.

## Where is Sophia Koetsier?

On 28 October 2015, 21-year-old Dutch medical graduate Sophia Koetsier disappeared at Murchison Falls National Park. She had completed an internship in Kampala and was travelling with two companions and a Ugandan guide. After arriving at accommodation near the Nile in the early evening, she walked away and did not return. Searches by Ugandan and Dutch investigators failed to locate her.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.peterrdevries.nl/cases/sophia-koetsier/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: peterrdevries.nl">[Peter R. de Vries Foundation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">peterrdevries.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Peter R. de Vries Foundation Sophia Koetsier</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Peter R. de Vries Foundation Sophia Koetsier</span></span></span>

The main explanations are an accident in difficult terrain, an encounter with wildlife, entry into the river, self-directed disappearance or criminal involvement. None has been supported by enough physical evidence to settle the case. Items believed to be connected with Koetsier were reportedly found during searches, but they did not reveal a confirmed route or cause of death.

Investigators later considered the possibility of a crime, and legal proceedings were brought against the tour operator in connection with the case. In September 2025, Uganda’s Director of Public Prosecutions withdrew those proceedings. The withdrawal did not explain Koetsier’s fate or amount to proof of any alternative theory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.findsophia.org/2020/05/07/the-fight-continues/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: findsophia.org">[Find Sophia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">findsophia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Find Sophia The fight continues</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Find Sophia The fight continues</span></span></span>

The case is especially vulnerable to internet embellishment. Retellings sometimes present an animal attack, drowning or murder as though it were established. The available evidence supports none of these as a final answer. The responsible description is narrower: Koetsier vanished in a large national park, searches found no conclusive trace, and both accidental and criminal scenarios remain open.

## Why Uganda’s mysteries endure

Uganda’s major historical enigmas survive for different reasons, but several patterns recur.

**Missing physical evidence:** In the Amin-era cases, bodies and burial sites were concealed. At Bigo, the builders left no readable written record. In Koetsier’s disappearance, searches produced no confirmed remains.

**Restricted or incomplete archives:** The unreleased Kasubi inquiry report prevents scrutiny of official findings. Records from periods of political terror were often absent, deliberately misleading or created by institutions implicated in abuses.

**Political distrust:** Mutesa’s disputed death and the Kasubi fire cannot be separated from conflict between Buganda and the central state. Where institutions are not trusted, even an official explanation may fail to create public agreement.

**The power of oral memory:** Stories preserve experiences excluded from formal records, especially those of families whose relatives disappeared. Yet oral accounts can also change over time, combine separate incidents or become attached to archaeological sites whose original history is unknown.

The best way to approach these cases is neither to accept every rumour nor to dismiss uncertainty. Evidence should be ranked. Contemporary records, archaeology, forensic findings and independently corroborated testimony carry more weight than late anonymous stories. Official claims also require scrutiny, particularly when made by governments accused of concealing the event in question.

Uganda’s unresolved past matters because these mysteries concern more than curiosity. They shape demands for burial, accountability, access to archives and honest public memory. In several cases, the missing answer is not an exotic secret. It is a report that has not been released, a grave that has not been found or a witness whose testimony was never safely recorded.
