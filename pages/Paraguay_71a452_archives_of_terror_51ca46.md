## Introduction

The discovery of [Paraguay]({{ 'what-still-haunts-paraguays-historical/' | relative_url }})'s **Archives of Terror** on 22 December 1992 transformed one of South America's darkest historical mysteries. Before the files were uncovered, survivors and families had long described arbitrary arrests, torture, disappearances and cross-border persecution under the dictatorship of Alfredo Stroessner (1954–1989), but many officials dismissed or minimised these accounts. The archive changed that. Hidden inside a police building near Asunción were hundreds of thousands of secret documents recording surveillance, imprisonment, torture and cooperation with neighbouring dictatorships through Operation Condor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/archives-terror?hub=1081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Terror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives of Terror - Memory of the World...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_archives_of_terror_51ca46-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Terror Archives illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_archives_of_terror_51ca46-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Paraguay_71a452_archives_of_terror_51ca46-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Yet the archive did not solve every mystery. It proved that repression was systematic rather than accidental, but it did not reveal the fate of every disappeared person, identify every burial site or establish the responsibility of every official involved. Today, the Archives of Terror stand both as one of the world's most important documentary records of political repression and as evidence of how historical truth can remain incomplete even after secret files come to light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/archives-terror?hub=1081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Terror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives of Terror - Memory of the World...</span></span></span>

## How the archive was discovered

The discovery was not the result of a planned government investigation. It emerged from a search by former political prisoner and human rights activist **Martín Almada**, who was seeking official records relating to his own detention and torture. Working with Judge **Agustín Fernández**, Almada followed information suggesting that police files had been hidden rather than destroyed after the dictatorship ended.

On 22 December 1992 they entered a police warehouse in Lambaré, outside Asunción, and uncovered an enormous cache of documents that had remained largely untouched since the Stroessner era. The collection included arrest records, interrogation reports, surveillance files, photographs, fingerprint cards, correspondence and intelligence communications exchanged with security agencies across South America.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cultura.gov.py/2011/05/el-descubrimiento-del-archivo-del-terror-en-1992/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cultura.gov.py">[cultura.gov.py]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cultura.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-title">el descubrimiento del archivo del terror en 1992</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Secretaria Nacional de Cultura ParaguayEl descubrimiento del Archivo del Terror en 1992&#124;Archívo Marã jejuhu 1992-pe &#124; Secretaria Nacional...</span></span></span>

The discovery mattered because it replaced rumours with original state records. Instead of relying solely on testimony, investigators could now compare survivors' accounts with the dictatorship's own paperwork.

## What did the documents actually prove?

The files demonstrated that repression in Paraguay operated through an organised bureaucracy rather than isolated abuses. Officials systematically collected information on suspected opponents, labour organisers, students, clergy, journalists and political parties.

The archive documents several recurring practices:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* long-term surveillance of perceived political opponents;
* arbitrary detention without due process;
* interrogation under torture;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/AMR45/027/1977/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: amnesty.org">[amnesty.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">amnesty.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Deaths Under Torture and the Disappearance of Political Prisoners in Paraguay</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Deaths Under Torture and the Disappearance of Political Prisoners in Paraguay</span></span></span>
* extensive intelligence files on individuals and organisations;
* exchanges of information with neighbouring military governments;
* coordinated pursuit of exiles who believed they had escaped persecution by crossing national borders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/archives-terror?hub=1081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Terror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives of Terror - Memory of the World...</span></span></span>

</div>

Perhaps the archive's greatest historical importance lies in showing how ordinary administrative paperwork enabled extraordinary repression. Typed reports, filing systems, photographs and coded communications reveal that violence was supported by meticulous record-keeping rather than chaotic improvisation.

## How the files exposed Operation Condor

One of the archive's most internationally significant discoveries concerned **Operation Condor**, the secret security alliance linking the military governments of Paraguay, Argentina, Chile, Uruguay, Brazil and Bolivia during the 1970s.

Before 1992, researchers already suspected extensive cooperation between these regimes. The Paraguayan documents supplied direct documentary evidence showing exchanges of intelligence, requests for arrests, prisoner transfers and coordination across national frontiers. They demonstrated that political refugees who escaped one dictatorship could still be tracked through a regional intelligence network.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pj.gov.py/operacion-condor/pages/introduccion.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pj.gov.py">[Poder Judicial]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pj.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Poder Judicial Introducción</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Poder JudicialIntroducción - Archivos del Operativo Cóndor &#124; Museo de la Justicia...</span></span></span>

The records became important evidence in later judicial investigations across Latin America and Europe, including cases examining crimes committed during Operation Condor. Rather than proving every individual crime, they established the existence and functioning of the wider machinery that made many disappearances possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_of_Terror" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Terror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives of Terror</span></span></span>

## Which disappearances remain unresolved?

The archive solved many questions about how repression operated, but it did not answer the most painful question for many families: **what happened to those who never returned?**

Many files end with an arrest or transfer but contain no final record explaining whether the prisoner was released, executed or secretly buried. In other cases, documents indicate cross-border movements without revealing where victims ultimately died.

Major unresolved questions include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the precise burial places of many disappeared prisoners;
* the complete identities of everyone who participated in illegal detention and torture;
* the full number of people disappeared during the Stroessner dictatorship;
* whether some records were removed or destroyed before the archive was discovered;
* the identities of victims whose remains have never been recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/a9af496773e3157201e05220277d2bdd" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In Peru, Colombia, and Paraguay, families continue to search for lost relatives, often without official justice. </div>

These gaps illustrate an important point about historical archives. Even exceptionally rich documentary collections rarely preserve every stage of a crime. Secret police often documented arrests more carefully than killings, and some evidence was almost certainly destroyed before 1992.

## Why the archive does not tell the whole story

Although often described as complete proof of dictatorship-era repression, the Archives of Terror are better understood as an incomplete but extraordinarily powerful body of evidence.

Several factors limit what historians can reconstruct:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* not every police office deposited records in the same location;
* some files were probably destroyed during the dictatorship or after Stroessner's fall in 1989;
* intelligence agencies sometimes avoided creating written records for especially sensitive operations;
* some disappearances occurred outside Paraguay after victims were transferred through Operation Condor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pj.gov.py/operacion-condor/pages/introduccion.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pj.gov.py">[Poder Judicial]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pj.gov.py</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Poder Judicial Introducción</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Poder JudicialIntroducción - Archivos del Operativo Cóndor &#124; Museo de la Justicia...</span></span></span>

</div>

For this reason, investigators continue to combine archival research with witness testimony, forensic archaeology and judicial investigations. The archive often provides the starting point rather than the final answer.

## What has the archive meant for truth and justice?

The documents became central to Paraguay's efforts to confront the dictatorship. They supported the work of the **Truth and Justice Commission**, established in 2003, which used the archive alongside other documentary collections to identify thousands of victims of detention, torture, exile, execution and disappearance. The commission concluded that many additional victims had never appeared in the police records, highlighting both the archive's importance and its limitations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_of_Terror" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Terror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives of Terror</span></span></span>

The archive also encouraged broader recognition that Paraguay's experience formed part of a regional system of repression rather than an isolated national story. This shifted international understanding of Operation Condor from a largely suspected intelligence network to a documented historical reality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/archives-terror?hub=1081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Terror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives of Terror - Memory of the World...</span></span></span>

UNESCO recognised the collection by adding it to the **Memory of the World Register** in 2009, acknowledging its global significance as documentary evidence of state repression and transnational political violence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/archives-terror?hub=1081" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Terror</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives of Terror - Memory of the World...</span></span></span>

## Why Paraguay's disappeared remain a historical mystery

The central mystery today is no longer whether systematic repression occurred. The archive settled that question decisively.

Instead, the unresolved issues concern individual human stories. Hundreds of families still do not know exactly where relatives died, where they were buried or who carried out the final stages of their disappearance.
