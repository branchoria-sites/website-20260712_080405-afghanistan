## Introduction

Few literary forgeries have had as much influence on a nation's identity as the Manuscripts of Dvůr Králové and Zelená Hora. Presented in 1817 and 1818 as long-lost medieval Czech epics, they appeared at exactly the moment when the Czech National Revival was searching for proof that the Czech language and people possessed an ancient, heroic literary tradition. For decades they were celebrated as priceless medieval treasures, inspiring poets, artists, composers and politicians. Yet growing scholarly criticism eventually demonstrated that they were almost certainly sophisticated nineteenth-century creations rather than genuine medieval texts. Their story remains fascinating not because the mystery of their authenticity is still evenly balanced, but because forged documents helped shape a real national culture whose influence outlasted their exposure.

## Why the manuscripts seemed so convincing

The first manuscript was announced in September 1817, when librarian and philologist Václav Hanka claimed to have discovered an old parchment codex in the tower of a church at Dvůr Králové. Soon afterwards a second manuscript, supposedly found at Zelená Hora Castle, surfaced anonymously before entering the collections of the newly founded National Museum. Together they appeared to preserve heroic poems, battles, legends and legal traditions dating from the early Middle Ages.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rukopisy-rkz.cz/rkz/english/index.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rukopisy-rkz.cz">[rukopisy-rkz.cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rukopisy-rkz.cz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manuscript of Dvůr Králové and Manuscript of Zelená Hora</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manuscript of Dvůr Králové and Manuscript of Zelená Hora</span></span></span>

Their arrival was extraordinarily timely. During the early nineteenth century, Czech intellectuals were attempting to revive a language and literature that had been overshadowed for centuries within the Habsburg monarchy. Many European nations pointed to medieval epics such as the *Nibelungenlied* or *Beowulf* as evidence of an ancient literary culture. The Czech lands lacked an equivalent celebrated heroic corpus. The new manuscripts seemed to fill that gap perfectly, offering apparent proof that medieval Czech poetry had once stood alongside Europe's greatest traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/cornell-scholarship-online/book/56587" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicThe Czech Manuscripts: Forgery, Translation, and National Myth &#124; Cornell Scholarship Online &#124; Oxford AcademicOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span>

Several factors made the manuscripts persuasive:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* They were written on old-looking parchment rather than modern paper.
* Their language resembled archaic Czech closely enough to convince many contemporary scholars.
* Their themes celebrated legendary heroes, military courage and ancient justice in ways that matched Romantic-era expectations about the medieval past.
* They appeared before modern scientific methods for testing manuscripts had been developed.

</div>

The poems quickly entered public life. They were translated into other European languages, cited as historical evidence, and became symbols of Czech cultural independence. British writer Sir John Bowring helped spread their reputation abroad through translations and literary surveys, giving them an international audience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rukopisy-rkz.cz/rkz/english/index.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rukopisy-rkz.cz">[rukopisy-rkz.cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rukopisy-rkz.cz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Manuscript of Dvůr Králové and Manuscript of Zelená Hora</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Manuscript of Dvůr Králové and Manuscript of Zelená Hora</span></span></span>

## How scholars challenged their authenticity

Suspicion emerged surprisingly early. Some linguists noticed grammatical forms and vocabulary that seemed inconsistent with genuinely medieval Czech. Historians also questioned whether the manuscripts reflected Romantic nationalism more than medieval society.

The debate intensified throughout the nineteenth century. One of its most influential critics was Tomáš Garrigue [Masaryk]({{ 'masaryk/' | relative_url }}), who argued that national pride should not depend upon doubtful documents. His insistence on rigorous historical criticism proved deeply controversial because many people regarded questioning the manuscripts as unpatriotic rather than scholarly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/cornell-scholarship-online/book/56587" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicThe Czech Manuscripts: Forgery, Translation, and National Myth &#124; Cornell Scholarship Online &#124; Oxford AcademicOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span>

As historical methods improved, the evidence against authenticity accumulated from several directions:

* **Linguistics:** The language contained combinations of forms unlikely to have existed together in the claimed medieval period.
* **Literary style:** The poems reflected Romantic literary ideals more typical of the early nineteenth century than the Middle Ages.
* **Historical inconsistencies:** Some descriptions appeared influenced by modern historical knowledge rather than genuine medieval perspectives.
* **Material examination:** Scientific studies of the manuscripts and their inks increasingly supported the conclusion that they had undergone extensive manipulation and did not provide convincing evidence for a medieval origin. Modern non-destructive techniques such as Raman spectroscopy, X-ray fluorescence and infrared imaging have been used primarily to document their physical condition after earlier testing rather than to revive arguments for authenticity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publikace.nm.cz/en/periodicals/amnphl/63-1-2/the-effect-of-previous-material-research-into-the-manuscript-of-dvur-kralove-and-the-manuscript-of-zelena-hora-on-their-current-state" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publikace.nm.cz">[Národní muzeum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publikace.nm.cz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Národní muzeumThe Effect of Previous Material Research into the Manuscript of Dvůr Králové and the Manuscript of Zelená Hora on their Cur...</span></span></span>

Today, the overwhelming scholarly consensus is that both manuscripts are nineteenth-century literary forgeries, probably associated with Václav Hanka and likely involving other collaborators, although the precise division of responsibility remains uncertain. Exactly who composed each section, and how the deception was organised, has never been established beyond reasonable doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/cornell-scholarship-online/book/56587" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicThe Czech Manuscripts: Forgery, Translation, and National Myth &#124; Cornell Scholarship Online &#124; Oxford AcademicOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span>

## What the forgeries changed in Czech culture

The most remarkable aspect of the story is that exposure as forgeries did not erase their cultural impact.

During the decades when they were accepted as genuine, the manuscripts helped transform how Czechs imagined their own past. They encouraged the belief that the Czech nation possessed a deep literary and heroic tradition equal to those of neighbouring peoples. This confidence supported wider efforts to revive Czech language, literature and education during the National Revival.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/cornell-scholarship-online/book/56587" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicThe Czech Manuscripts: Forgery, Translation, and National Myth &#124; Cornell Scholarship Online &#124; Oxford AcademicOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span>

Their influence reached far beyond literary scholarship:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Writers drew inspiration from their heroic narratives.
* Artists illustrated scenes based upon the supposed medieval poems.
* Composers incorporated their themes into musical works celebrating Czech history.
* Teachers presented them as authentic monuments of the national past for much of the nineteenth century.

</div>

Even after scholars rejected the manuscripts, many people continued to admire them as works of literature. Their emotional power did not disappear simply because their historical claims failed.

The controversy also reshaped Czech intellectual life. It demonstrated that patriotism and historical scholarship could come into conflict, forcing academics to decide whether loyalty to national ideals should outweigh critical evidence. The eventual victory of documentary and linguistic analysis became an important milestone in the professionalisation of Czech historical research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/cornell-scholarship-online/book/56587" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academic.oup.com">[OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academic.oup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">OUP AcademicThe Czech Manuscripts: Forgery, Translation, and National Myth &#124; Cornell Scholarship Online &#124; Oxford AcademicOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery still matters

The real mystery today is no longer whether the manuscripts are authentic. Most specialists consider that question effectively settled. Instead, the enduring puzzle is how forged texts acquired such extraordinary authority and why they continued to inspire generations even after their exposure.

Several lessons explain their lasting significance.

First, successful forgeries often exploit genuine cultural needs. The manuscripts succeeded because they answered a widespread desire for evidence of an ancient Czech literary heritage rather than because they were technically flawless.

Second, authenticity and cultural influence are not always the same thing. Although historically unreliable, the manuscripts genuinely shaped literature, art and national identity. Their effects became historical facts in their own right.

Finally, the affair illustrates the self-correcting nature of scholarship. Decades of debate involving philologists, historians, chemists and conservators gradually replaced patriotic enthusiasm with evidence-based conclusions. The manuscripts remain preserved in the collections of the National Museum not as medieval masterpieces, but as remarkable witnesses to one of Europe's most consequential literary deceptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nm.cz/en/national-museum-library/collection-of-manuscripts-and-early-prints" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nm.cz">[Národní muzeum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nm.cz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Národní muzeum Collection of manuscripts and early prints</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Národní muzeumCollection of manuscripts and early prints - National museum...</span></span></span>
