## Introduction

The mystery of [Nauru]({{ 'what-happened-in-naurus-lost-wartime/' | relative_url }})’s five Australians is no longer whether they were killed. Post-war investigations established that the Japanese occupation authorities deliberately executed the men on 25–26 March 1943. The enduring mystery is what happened afterwards. Conflicting witness testimony, deliberate efforts to conceal the crime and the disappearance of the original burial place mean that historians still cannot say with confidence exactly how the executions were carried out or where the victims were first buried. Although memorials now commemorate them, the location of their original grave remains uncertain, making this one of Nauru’s most persistent wartime historical puzzles.

The victims were Lieutenant-Colonel Frederick Royden Chalmers, the Australian administrator of Nauru, Dr Bernard H. Quinn, Dr William H. Shugg, engineer Frank Harmer and overseer William H. Doyle. They had remained on the island after the Japanese occupation began in August 1942 and were held under detention until their deaths.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Royden_Chalmers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Frederick Royden Chalmers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Frederick Royden Chalmers</span></span></span>

## How the air-raid account was disproved

For many years, the Japanese explanation for the deaths centred on an American bombing raid. The first United States attack on Nauru took place on 25 March 1943 against the Japanese-built airfield. Early reports suggested that the Australians had been killed because they had been caught in the raid or had died as an unavoidable consequence of the attack.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/ESD19450920.2.84" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: paperspast.natlib.govt.nz">[Papers Past]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">paperspast.natlib.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Papers PastPapers Past &#124; Newspapers &#124; Evening Star &#124; 20 September 1945 &#124; NAURU ATROCITIESSeptember 20, 1945...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 1945</span></span></span>

That explanation collapsed once Allied investigators returned to Nauru after Japan's surrender. Interviews with Nauruan witnesses, surviving Japanese personnel and captured officers demonstrated that the five men had survived the bombing. Instead, they were removed from detention and executed under orders from Lieutenant Hiromi Nakayama shortly afterwards. Australian investigations concluded that the bombing merely provided the occasion for the killings rather than their cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C2602799" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: awm.gov.au">[Australian War Memorial]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">awm.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Australian War MemorialAWM54 1010/9 - War Crimes and Trials - Investigation of Atrocities &#124; Australian War Memorial...</span></span></span>

The sequence matters because it transformed the case from an incident of war into a documented war crime. Nakayama was later tried by an Australian military court, convicted of ordering the murders and executed in 1946. His conviction settled the question of responsibility even though many details of the crime remained disputed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/world-war-ii-war-crimes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naa.gov.au">[NAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NAAWorld War 2 war crimes &#124; naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NAAWorld War 2 war crimes &#124; naa.gov.au</span></span></span>

## Conflicting testimony about the executions

Although the executions themselves were legally established, eyewitness evidence differed on crucial details.

One of the earliest post-war accounts came from a Nauruan witness who reported seeing Dr Quinn beheaded with a sword after the prisoners had been assembled before dawn. According to this testimony, the remaining men were also executed and their bodies removed by truck before their clothing was burned to conceal evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/HBHETR19450926.2.63.3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: paperspast.natlib.govt.nz">[Papers Past]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">paperspast.natlib.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Papers PastPapers Past &#124; Newspapers &#124; Hawke&#x27;s Bay Herald-Tribune &#124; 26 September 1945 &#124; Japanese Beheaded Officials On NauruSeptember 26...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1945</span></span></span>

Other testimony, however, described a more complicated sequence. Some accounts suggested that not every victim was killed by beheading. Later evidence referred variously to shooting, bayoneting and beheading, raising the possibility that different methods were used during the same execution or that witnesses observed only part of the event. Modern historians therefore avoid stating with certainty that all five men were beheaded, even though that description became widely repeated in newspaper reports immediately after the war.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/ESD19450920.2.84" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: paperspast.natlib.govt.nz">[Papers Past]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">paperspast.natlib.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Papers PastPapers Past &#124; Newspapers &#124; Evening Star &#124; 20 September 1945 &#124; NAURU ATROCITIESSeptember 20, 1945...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 20, 1945</span></span></span>

Several factors explain these contradictions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* No neutral observer witnessed the entire execution.
* Japanese personnel involved gave inconsistent statements after the war.
* Nauruan witnesses often saw only fragments of the events.
* The occupation authorities deliberately destroyed physical evidence immediately afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/HBHETR19450926.2.63.3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: paperspast.natlib.govt.nz">[Papers Past]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">paperspast.natlib.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Papers PastPapers Past &#124; Newspapers &#124; Hawke&#x27;s Bay Herald-Tribune &#124; 26 September 1945 &#124; Japanese Beheaded Officials On NauruSeptember 26...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1945</span></span></span>

</div>

As a result, historians can confidently reconstruct the broad outline of the crime but not every detail of its final moments.

## Why the burial place disappeared

The greatest remaining mystery concerns the first burial site.

Witnesses agreed that the bodies were buried soon after the executions, apparently near the coast, but descriptions of the exact location differed. Japanese personnel made conscious efforts to erase evidence, reportedly burning clothing and concealing the graves. By the time Australian forces returned in 1945, the landscape had changed through military construction, wartime damage and natural coastal processes, making the original burial place difficult to identify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/HBHETR19450926.2.63.3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: paperspast.natlib.govt.nz">[Papers Past]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">paperspast.natlib.govt.nz</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Papers PastPapers Past &#124; Newspapers &#124; Hawke&#x27;s Bay Herald-Tribune &#124; 26 September 1945 &#124; Japanese Beheaded Officials On NauruSeptember 26...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1945</span></span></span>

Investigators searched for physical evidence while collecting testimony, but no universally accepted original grave was identified. Later commemorations honour the victims, yet the precise location where they were first buried during the occupation has never been established with certainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C2602799" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: awm.gov.au">[Australian War Memorial]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">awm.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Australian War MemorialAWM54 1010/9 - War Crimes and Trials - Investigation of Atrocities &#124; Australian War Memorial...</span></span></span>

Unlike many wartime graves that were carefully recorded after liberation, this case lacked reliable documentation because the perpetrators had every incentive to destroy records and conceal the crime before surrender.

## The search for the lost burial site

Australian war crimes investigators devoted considerable effort to reconstructing the murders. Their files include witness statements, interviews, maps and investigative reports relating specifically to the killings of Chalmers and his companions. These records enabled prosecutors to prove the murders in court, but they did not resolve the question of the burial site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C2602799" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: awm.gov.au">[Australian War Memorial]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">awm.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Australian War MemorialAWM54 1010/9 - War Crimes and Trials - Investigation of Atrocities &#124; Australian War Memorial...</span></span></span>

The surviving evidence has several limitations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Witness memories were recorded more than two years after the executions.
* Many Japanese records had been destroyed before surrender.
* Coastal landmarks described in testimony had altered.
* Some witnesses referred to places using local names that were difficult for investigators to match with military maps.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C2602799" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: awm.gov.au">[Australian War Memorial]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">awm.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Australian War MemorialAWM54 1010/9 - War Crimes and Trials - Investigation of Atrocities &#124; Australian War Memorial...</span></span></span>

</div>

Because no definitive archaeological investigation has identified the original grave, the burial place remains one of the few significant unanswered questions surrounding the case.

## Why the mystery still matters

The mystery of the five Australians is unusual because the central crime is no longer in doubt. Australian military investigations and the subsequent conviction of Lieutenant Hiromi Nakayama established that the executions were deliberate wartime murders rather than accidental deaths during an air raid.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/world-war-ii-war-crimes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naa.gov.au">[NAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NAAWorld War 2 war crimes &#124; naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NAAWorld War 2 war crimes &#124; naa.gov.au</span></span></span>

What remains unresolved is narrower but historically important: the precise method by which each victim died, the exact sequence of the executions and, above all, where the bodies were first buried before the Japanese concealed the evidence. Those missing details illustrate a wider problem in Nauru's wartime history. Even where responsibility can be established, destroyed records, contradictory testimony and vanished physical evidence leave enduring gaps that cannot easily be filled.
