## Introduction

The mystery of Teonimenu centres on a striking question: can an oral tradition preserve the memory of a real island that disappeared beneath the sea? Unlike legendary lost continents such as Atlantis, the story of Teonimenu is tied to a specific place in the [Solomon Islands]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-the-solomon/' | relative_url }}), a known zone of intense seismic activity, and to communities that still identify themselves as descendants of its survivors. While no archaeological excavation has yet proved that an inhabited island once stood there, geological evidence and local traditions point in the same direction. Today, Teonimenu is regarded not as proof of a vanished civilisation but as one of the strongest Pacific examples of how oral history may preserve the memory of an ancient natural disaster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-05-08/the-long-lost-island-still-visible-from-space/103740538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News The story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific - ABC NewsMay 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2024</span></span></span>

## The island remembered between Ulawa and Olu Malau

According to traditions preserved on Ulawa, Owaraha and neighbouring islands in what is now Makira-Ulawa Province, Teonimenu (also recorded as Teonimanu) once lay between Ulawa and the Olu Malau, or Three Sisters, islands. The island is consistently described as inhabited, culturally connected to nearby communities, and destroyed suddenly rather than gradually. Survivors are said to have escaped by canoe and settled elsewhere, with several families maintaining traditions that they descend from these refugees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-05-08/the-long-lost-island-still-visible-from-space/103740538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News The story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific - ABC NewsMay 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2024</span></span></span>

The best-known version of the story explains the catastrophe through a moral narrative. A married woman left her husband for another man and returned to Teonimenu. Seeking revenge, the abandoned husband acquired a supernatural "wave curse", traditionally said to unleash eight enormous waves. One after another, these waves overwhelmed the island until it disappeared beneath the sea.

From a historical perspective, the supernatural explanation cannot be tested. What has attracted researchers is that beneath the legendary framework lie details that remain remarkably consistent across different communities:

* Teonimenu was a real inhabited island rather than an imaginary land.
* Its destruction was sudden.
* Giant waves accompanied the disaster.
* Refugees reached neighbouring islands.
* The island occupied a location that local people could still identify generations later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-05-08/the-long-lost-island-still-visible-from-space/103740538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News The story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific - ABC NewsMay 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2024</span></span></span>

These recurring geographical details are more significant than the legendary elements because they offer information that can be compared with modern geological evidence.

## What Lark Shoal and the seabed reveal

The strongest physical clue is Lark Shoal, a shallow submerged feature located almost exactly where oral traditions place Teonimenu. Satellite imagery and nautical data show a submerged ridge lying only a few metres below the surface in places, suggesting that land once stood considerably higher than it does today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-05-08/the-long-lost-island-still-visible-from-space/103740538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News The story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific - ABC NewsMay 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2024</span></span></span>

Geographer Patrick Nunn and collaborators argue that this ridge occupies one of the most unstable tectonic settings in the Solomon Islands. It lies close to the Cape Johnson Trench, where the Pacific Plate is forced beneath the Australian Plate. Such plate boundaries generate powerful earthquakes capable of triggering enormous submarine landslides.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/4023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repository.usp.ac.fj">[repository.usp.ac.fj]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repository.usp.ac.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USP Electronic Research Repository...</span></span></span>

The proposed mechanism is straightforward:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A major earthquake destabilised the steep underwater ridge supporting the island.
2. Part of the ridge collapsed into deep water as a massive submarine landslide.
3. The island rapidly subsided or slid beneath sea level.
4. The collapse displaced seawater, generating destructive tsunami waves that struck both the sinking island and surrounding coasts.

</div>

This sequence would closely match the oral traditions, which describe both the disappearance of the island and repeated overwhelming waves. It also explains why descendants remembered both events as inseparable parts of a single catastrophe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/4023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repository.usp.ac.fj">[usp.ac.fj]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repository.usp.ac.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USP Electronic Research Repository...</span></span></span>

Researchers have also suggested that Teonimenu may not have been a single isolated island but part of a small cluster of islands or elevated ridges, some of which may also have disappeared during the same geological event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://patricknunn.org/writing/books/vanished-islands-and-hidden-continents-of-the-pacific/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: patricknunn.org">[patricknunn.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">patricknunn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vanished Islands and Hidden Continents of the Pacific – Patrick Nunn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vanished Islands and Hidden Continents of the Pacific – Patrick Nunn</span></span></span>

## Why scientists take the tradition seriously

For much of the twentieth century, many scholars regarded stories of vanished islands as folklore without historical value. That attitude has changed as studies from across the Pacific have shown that oral traditions sometimes preserve surprisingly accurate memories of volcanic eruptions, earthquakes, tsunamis and changing coastlines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2017-12-08/geomythology-loch-ness-monster-and-other-folk-tales-conversation/9240878" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsWhat geomythology can tell us about the Loch Ness monster and other folk tales - ABC NewsDecember 8, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2017</span></span></span>

Teonimenu has become a prominent example of this field, sometimes called geomythology—the study of how traditional stories may encode memories of real geological events.

Several aspects strengthen the case:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The tradition identifies a precise location rather than a mythical distant land.
* Independent communities preserve similar accounts.
* Descendant groups continue to identify with the lost island.
* Geological mapping identifies an unstable submerged ridge exactly where the stories place Teonimenu.
* The proposed mechanism is consistent with known tectonic processes in the Solomon Islands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/4023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repository.usp.ac.fj">[usp.ac.fj]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repository.usp.ac.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USP Electronic Research Repository...</span></span></span>

</div>

Rather than treating oral tradition as literal history, researchers view it as cultural memory: factual observations preserved within symbolic stories that naturally changed over generations.

## Legend, geological inference and the missing proof

Despite the compelling alignment between tradition and geology, important uncertainties remain.

No underwater archaeological investigation has yet recovered settlement remains from Lark Shoal. There are no securely dated buildings, artefacts or human remains demonstrating exactly when the island existed or when it disappeared. Without such evidence, researchers cannot establish its precise size, population or the exact year of its destruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/4023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repository.usp.ac.fj">[repository.usp.ac.fj]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repository.usp.ac.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USP Electronic Research Repository...</span></span></span>

The estimated date also remains approximate. Based on oral traditions and early European exploration, Patrick Nunn has suggested that the disappearance probably occurred sometime after the arrival of the Spanish expedition led by Álvaro de Mendaña in the sixteenth century but before British exploration in the eighteenth century, placing the event broadly several centuries ago rather than in deep prehistory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-05-08/the-long-lost-island-still-visible-from-space/103740538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News The story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific - ABC NewsMay 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2024</span></span></span>

Alternative possibilities also remain open. Instead of one catastrophic collapse, repeated earthquakes, gradual subsidence and tsunami damage may together have rendered the island uninhabitable before it finally disappeared beneath the sea. Current evidence cannot distinguish confidently between these scenarios.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/4023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repository.usp.ac.fj">[repository.usp.ac.fj]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repository.usp.ac.fj</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USP Electronic Research Repository...</span></span></span>

## Why Teonimenu still matters

The enduring significance of Teonimenu lies less in proving every detail of the legend than in demonstrating how different forms of evidence can complement one another.

For historians, the story highlights the limitations of relying solely on written records in regions where literate documentation arrived comparatively late. For geologists, it suggests that oral traditions may point towards ancient earthquakes and landslides that left few written traces. For descendant communities, the tradition remains part of family identity rather than merely an old tale.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-05-08/the-long-lost-island-still-visible-from-space/103740538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News The story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific - ABC NewsMay 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2024</span></span></span>

Teonimenu therefore occupies a distinctive place among the historical mysteries of the Solomon Islands. It is neither simply folklore nor an established geological fact. Instead, it represents a rare convergence of remembered geography, inherited community traditions and modern earth science. The missing archaeological proof keeps the question open, but the evidence available today makes it increasingly plausible that the legend preserves the memory of a genuine island lost to one of the Pacific's most powerful natural processes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-05-08/the-long-lost-island-still-visible-from-space/103740538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[abc.net.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News The story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe story of Teonimenu, a lost island of the Pacific - ABC NewsMay 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2024</span></span></span>
