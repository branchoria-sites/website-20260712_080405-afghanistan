## Introduction

Has the lost city of Tu'am been found? The strongest candidate so far is the archaeological landscape on Al Sinniyah Island in the Emirate of Umm Al Quwain. Over the past few years, excavations have revealed a surprisingly large late antique settlement, an early Christian monastery, extensive evidence of pearl fishing and long-distance trade, all dating to the period when medieval Arabic writers described Tu'am as a prosperous Gulf centre. Although no inscription has yet been found naming the settlement, many archaeologists now regard Al Sinniyah as the best-supported identification. The mystery has therefore changed: instead of asking where Tu'am might have been, researchers are now asking what evidence would be needed to prove that this remarkable pearling town really was the forgotten city recorded in historical texts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2024/06/15/ancient-site-found-in-uae-may-be-sixth-century-lost-city/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The NationalAncient site found in UAE may be sixth-century lost city &#124; The NationalJune 15, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2024</span></span></span>

## What historical sources say about Tu'am

Tu'am appears in early Islamic geographical and historical traditions as an important settlement on the Gulf coast before the rise of later ports such as Julfar. Medieval writers associated it with high-quality pearls and described it as the principal centre of a wider region. Although the surviving descriptions were written centuries after Tu'am's peak, they preserve traditions that almost certainly reach back into the pre-Islamic period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/opinion/comment/2024/06/21/a-curiously-named-lost-city-has-deep-significance-for-the-uae/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thenationalnews.com.</span></span></span>

One reason the city's location became a mystery is that the historical texts provide only broad geographical clues rather than precise coordinates. Coastlines changed, settlements were abandoned, and later towns inherited regional importance. As a result, historians proposed several possible locations over the years without finding archaeological evidence that matched the written descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/opinion/comment/2024/06/21/a-curiously-named-lost-city-has-deep-significance-for-the-uae/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thenationalnews.com.</span></span></span>

The question has therefore never been whether Tu'am existed, but where it stood and whether any surviving archaeological site could convincingly match the historical record.

## Why Al Sinniyah fits the evidence

Recent excavations have transformed Al Sinniyah from an interesting island site into the leading candidate for lost Tu'am.

Several discoveries reinforce one another rather than standing alone:

* **An unexpectedly large settlement.** Archaeologists have uncovered a densely built town covering roughly 12 hectares, with stone houses arranged along narrow lanes. The scale is far greater than would be expected for a small monastic support village.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2024/06/15/ancient-site-found-in-uae-may-be-sixth-century-lost-city/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The NationalAncient site found in UAE may be sixth-century lost city &#124; The NationalJune 15, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2024</span></span></span>
* **Extensive pearling evidence.** Large shell middens, pearls, diving equipment and other remains show that pearl fishing was a major economic activity rather than a minor occupation. Historical sources likewise describe Tu'am as famous for its pearls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://culturalmap.gov.ae/en/facilities/pearling-town-on-siniya-island" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturalmap.gov.ae">[Cultural Map]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturalmap.gov.ae</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cultural Map National Cultural Map &#124; Facilities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cultural Map National Cultural Map &#124; Facilities</span></span></span>
* **A flourishing late antique community.** The settlement dates mainly between the fourth and sixth centuries AD, precisely the period when Tu'am is believed to have been an important regional centre before its decline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2024/06/15/ancient-site-found-in-uae-may-be-sixth-century-lost-city/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The NationalAncient site found in UAE may be sixth-century lost city &#124; The NationalJune 15, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2024</span></span></span>
* **International trade links.** Imported storage jars from Mesopotamia, including examples with Aramaic inscriptions, demonstrate participation in wider commercial networks stretching across the Gulf. Fish remains, imported pottery and other finds suggest regular maritime exchange with Iraq, Iran and India.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://culturalmap.gov.ae/en/facilities/pearling-town-on-siniya-island" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturalmap.gov.ae">[Cultural Map]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturalmap.gov.ae</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cultural Map National Cultural Map &#124; Facilities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cultural Map National Cultural Map &#124; Facilities</span></span></span>
* **A nearby Christian monastery.** The discovery of one of the Gulf's oldest known Christian monasteries shows that the settlement was not an isolated fishing camp but part of a substantial and diverse community before Islam.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://culturalmap.gov.ae/en/facilities/pearling-town-on-siniya-island" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturalmap.gov.ae">[Cultural Map]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturalmap.gov.ae</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cultural Map National Cultural Map &#124; Facilities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cultural Map National Cultural Map &#124; Facilities</span></span></span>

Taken together, these discoveries produce a picture remarkably similar to the prosperous coastal centre described in historical accounts.

## Why archaeologists remain cautious

Despite the excitement surrounding the discovery, specialists generally avoid saying that the mystery has been solved.

The main reason is straightforward: no excavated inscription has yet been found identifying the settlement as Tu'am. Archaeology can establish the size, date, economy and importance of the town, but assigning its historical name requires additional evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/opinion/comment/2024/06/21/a-curiously-named-lost-city-has-deep-significance-for-the-uae/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thenationalnews.com.</span></span></span>

There are also broader historical uncertainties. Medieval authors sometimes used place names for both a town and a wider district, making it possible that Tu'am referred to an entire region rather than a single urban settlement. Coastal landscapes have also changed significantly over fifteen centuries, complicating attempts to match written descriptions with modern geography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/opinion/comment/2024/06/21/a-curiously-named-lost-city-has-deep-significance-for-the-uae/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thenationalnews.com.</span></span></span>

Even so, many archaeologists argue that the absence of any other comparably large settlement of the correct period along this stretch of the Emirati coast makes Al Sinniyah the strongest candidate currently known.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/opinion/comment/2024/06/21/a-curiously-named-lost-city-has-deep-significance-for-the-uae/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thenationalnews.com.</span></span></span>

## What would prove the identification?

The case for identifying Al Sinniyah with Tu'am is already substantial, but it remains an informed hypothesis rather than an established fact.

Evidence that could settle the question would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* An inscription, seal or official document explicitly naming Tu'am.
* Administrative records linking the settlement with the historical name.
* Additional archaeological evidence confirming that the town served as the political and commercial centre described in medieval sources.
* Further mapping showing that the excavated settlement represents the complete urban landscape rather than only one district of a larger complex.

</div>

Modern archaeological techniques are helping to advance this work. Satellite imagery, synthetic aperture radar and ground-penetrating radar have revealed buried structures extending beyond the excavated areas, suggesting that the settlement was larger and denser than first recognised. These methods provide new targets for excavation without disturbing the site unnecessarily.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1569843226003262" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reconstructing history: 3D high-resolution GPR analysis based on SAR discoveries in the ancient Siniyah Island of Umm Al-Quw...</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery matters

Whether or not future discoveries finally confirm the name Tu'am, Al Sinniyah has already reshaped understanding of the early history of the [United Arab Emirates]({{ 'united-arab-emirates/' | relative_url }}).

The site demonstrates that the Gulf coast supported a substantial urban community centuries before the Islamic period, combining pearling, international commerce and religious diversity. Rather than an isolated fishing village, it appears to have been one of the most significant settlements yet discovered on the ancient Emirati coast.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://culturalmap.gov.ae/en/facilities/pearling-town-on-siniya-island" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturalmap.gov.ae">[Cultural Map]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturalmap.gov.ae</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cultural Map National Cultural Map &#124; Facilities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cultural Map National Cultural Map &#124; Facilities</span></span></span>

For that reason, the mystery is no longer centred on whether Al Sinniyah was an important pearl town—that is firmly supported by archaeological evidence. The remaining question is historical identity: whether this remarkable settlement can ultimately be shown beyond reasonable doubt to be the lost city remembered in the historical record as Tu'am.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thenationalnews.com/opinion/comment/2024/06/21/a-curiously-named-lost-city-has-deep-significance-for-the-uae/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thenationalnews.com">[The National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thenationalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thenationalnews.com.</span></span></span>
