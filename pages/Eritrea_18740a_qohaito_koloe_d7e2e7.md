## Introduction

Was Qohaito really the ancient city of Koloe? The short answer is that it is a plausible and widely discussed identification, but it has not been conclusively proved. Ancient Greek and Roman writers described Koloe as an inland trading centre between the Red Sea port of [Adulis]({{ 'adulis/' | relative_url }}) and the kingdom of Aksum. Qohaito, a spectacular archaeological landscape on a high plateau in southern [Eritrea]({{ 'what-secrets-still-survive-in-eritrea/' | relative_url }}), occupies exactly the kind of strategic position that such a place would require. Yet despite decades of archaeological work, no inscription has been found that explicitly identifies the ruins as Koloe. As a result, the question remains one of Eritrea's most important archaeological debates rather than a settled historical fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://navigating-the-periplus.github.io/locations/koloe.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navigating-the-periplus.github.io">[navigating-the-periplus.github.io]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navigating-the-periplus.github.io</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">By providing detailed information on each site and its location, as well as original maps, this website creates digital experiences of th...</span></span></span>

## What survives on the Qohaito plateau

Qohaito is far more than a single ruined settlement. It is an extensive archaeological landscape containing monumental stone buildings, temples, tombs, ancient roads, rock art, agricultural terraces, dams and reservoirs spread across a large plateau overlooking the eastern escarpment of Eritrea. Most visible remains belong to the period between roughly the second and seventh centuries AD, when the Aksumite kingdom dominated much of the northern Horn of Africa.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5600/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Qoahito Cultural Landscape</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreQoahito Cultural Landscape - UNESCO World Heritage CentreMarch 2, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 2, 2011</span></span></span>

The engineering works are particularly striking. A substantial stone dam and sophisticated water-management system demonstrate that the inhabitants invested heavily in controlling seasonal rainfall. Together with evidence of farming and local industry, these features suggest that Qohaito was not merely a stop on a caravan route but a permanent and prosperous settlement capable of supporting a sizeable population.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea/Notes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Periplus of the Erythraean Sea/Notes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Periplus of the Erythraean Sea/Notes - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

Archaeological surveys also show that Qohaito formed the centre of a broader landscape of villages, cultivated land and smaller settlements rather than an isolated city. This wider pattern has become increasingly important because it suggests an organised regional economy linked to long-distance trade.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5600/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Qoahito Cultural Landscape</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreQoahito Cultural Landscape - UNESCO World Heritage CentreMarch 2, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 2, 2011</span></span></span>

## Why scholars link Qohaito with Koloe

The case for identifying Qohaito with Koloe rests on several independent lines of evidence rather than on a single decisive discovery.

The strongest clue comes from the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*, a merchant's handbook written during the first century AD. It describes Koloe as an inland market reached after roughly three days' journey from Adulis. It was said to be the first major collection point for ivory before caravans continued to the Aksumite capital. This description fits the role expected of a settlement positioned between the Red Sea coast and the Ethiopian Highlands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://navigating-the-periplus.github.io/locations/koloe.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navigating-the-periplus.github.io">[navigating-the-periplus.github.io]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navigating-the-periplus.github.io</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">By providing detailed information on each site and its location, as well as original maps, this website creates digital experiences of th...</span></span></span>

Qohaito occupies precisely such a strategic location. It lies on the natural corridor linking Adulis with Aksum, making it an obvious place for merchants to regroup, exchange goods and organise caravans moving inland or towards the coast. Archaeologists have long regarded this geographical position as one of the strongest arguments in favour of the identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/298069292_Cultural_Interaction_between_Ancient_Abyssinia_and_India_Archaeological_Sources_from_1st_to_7th_century_CE" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Cultural Interaction between Ancient Abyssinia and India: Archaeological Sources from 1st to 7th century CEJanuary 1, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2016</span></span></span>

The site's scale also supports the idea. Rather than representing a minor village, Qohaito contains monumental architecture and evidence of substantial investment in infrastructure. Such remains are compatible with an important commercial centre serving regional trade networks extending across the Red Sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/298069292_Cultural_Interaction_between_Ancient_Abyssinia_and_India_Archaeological_Sources_from_1st_to_7th_century_CE" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Cultural Interaction between Ancient Abyssinia and India: Archaeological Sources from 1st to 7th century CEJanuary 1, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2016</span></span></span>

Modern digital reconstructions of the *Periplus* likewise treat modern Qohaito as the leading candidate for Koloe, reflecting the current balance of scholarly opinion even while acknowledging that certainty remains elusive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://navigating-the-periplus.github.io/locations/koloe.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: navigating-the-periplus.github.io">[navigating-the-periplus.github.io]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">navigating-the-periplus.github.io</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">By providing detailed information on each site and its location, as well as original maps, this website creates digital experiences of th...</span></span></span>

## Why the identification remains unproven

Despite these impressive similarities, archaeologists have not reached unanimous agreement.

The greatest problem is the absence of direct epigraphic evidence. No inscription discovered at Qohaito actually names the settlement as Koloe. In ancient archaeology, such inscriptions often provide the decisive proof needed to identify a city beyond reasonable doubt. Without one, the argument relies on matching geography, chronology and archaeological interpretation rather than explicit identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sahoarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/The-Archaeological-Site-and-Cultural-Landscape-of-Qohaito.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sahoarchive.org">[Saho Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sahoarchive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Saho Archive The Archaeological Site and Cultural Landscape of Qohaito, Eritrea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saho Archive The Archaeological Site and Cultural Landscape of Qohaito, Eritrea</span></span></span>

Some researchers have also questioned whether the settlement pattern matches what ancient writers described. Parts of Qohaito consist of dispersed remains spread across a large plateau rather than a densely built urban centre. Critics argue that the commercial city described in the *Periplus* might instead have been located elsewhere, perhaps at Tokonda or Matara, both of which have also been proposed as candidates for Koloe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sahoarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/The-Archaeological-Site-and-Cultural-Landscape-of-Qohaito.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sahoarchive.org">[Saho Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sahoarchive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Saho Archive The Archaeological Site and Cultural Landscape of Qohaito, Eritrea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saho Archive The Archaeological Site and Cultural Landscape of Qohaito, Eritrea</span></span></span>

Another point of debate concerns the caravan route itself. The ancient journey from Adulis is described in terms of travel time rather than measured distance. Modern scholars disagree over which valleys and passes merchants actually followed, and different reconstructed routes can favour different archaeological sites. Research on ancient topography has suggested that if Qohaito was Koloe, caravans probably travelled through the Wadi Komaile rather than another commonly proposed route.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jorient1962/49/2/49_2_133/_article/-char/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstage.jst.go.jp">[J-STAGE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstage.jst.go.jp</span><span class="citation-popover-title">J-STAGEGabaza, Adulis, Koloe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">J-STAGEGabaza, Adulis, Koloe</span></span></span>

Finally, the ancient texts themselves provide only brief descriptions. The *Periplus* was written as a practical trading guide rather than a geographical survey, leaving many details open to interpretation. Even Ptolemy's later geographical references are not sufficiently precise to settle the question on their own.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://periplus.arch.uoa.gr/texts_en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: periplus.arch.uoa.gr">[Periplus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">periplus.arch.uoa.gr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uoa.gr.</span></span></span>

## What future discoveries could settle the mystery

Several kinds of evidence could transform the debate.

A bilingual inscription naming the settlement would be the strongest possible proof. Such finds have resolved similar identification debates elsewhere in the ancient world, but none has yet appeared at Qohaito.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sahoarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/The-Archaeological-Site-and-Cultural-Landscape-of-Qohaito.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sahoarchive.org">[Saho Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sahoarchive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Saho Archive The Archaeological Site and Cultural Landscape of Qohaito, Eritrea</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saho Archive The Archaeological Site and Cultural Landscape of Qohaito, Eritrea</span></span></span>

Further excavation could also clarify the settlement's true size and function. Much of Qohaito remains unexcavated, and archaeologists continue to investigate residential areas, industrial activity and connections with neighbouring sites. ## The most balanced conclusion

Today, Qohaito remains the leading candidate for the ancient city of Koloe, largely because its location, chronology and archaeological importance correspond remarkably well with the descriptions preserved in the *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea*. Nevertheless, the identification is still a scholarly hypothesis rather than an established fact.

The mystery persists because the available evidence is persuasive but incomplete. Geography, trade routes and monumental remains all point in the same direction, yet archaeology has not produced the one discovery—a securely dated inscription naming Koloe—that would end the debate.
