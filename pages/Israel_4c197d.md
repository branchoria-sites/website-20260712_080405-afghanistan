## Introduction

The [Yemenite children]({{ 'missing-children-cf4765/' | relative_url }}) affair exposes the human cost of chaotic mass immigration and unequal state institutions. ## The children who never came home

Between the late 1940s and the mid-1950s, hundreds of families—mostly Jewish immigrants from Yemen, but also families from other Middle Eastern and Balkan communities—reported that babies or young children had disappeared after being taken into hospitals, clinics or children’s homes. Parents commonly recalled being told that a child had died, sometimes without being shown a body, a grave or convincing paperwork. Years later, some families received military or administrative notices addressed to children they had been told were dead, deepening suspicion that at least some had survived.

The affair emerged during a period of immense strain. Israel’s population was rapidly expanding, immigrant camps were overcrowded, infant mortality was high and medical record-keeping was often poor. Many newly arrived parents spoke little or no Hebrew and faced officials who could be dismissive or paternalistic. Those conditions make accidental separation, misidentification and undocumented burial plausible. They also explain why official assurances were often not trusted.

Three official investigations examined the allegations. The earliest inquiry reviewed hundreds of cases and concluded that most of the children had died, while leaving a smaller number unresolved. The later state commission, which reported in 2001 after years of work, similarly rejected the claim of an organised government programme to abduct children. It found documentary evidence of death in most of the cases it examined, located a handful of people alive and failed to determine the fate of several dozen children. It allowed that isolated informal adoptions might have occurred, but found no evidence of a centrally directed operation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemenite_Children_Affair" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Yemenite Children Affair</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Yemenite Children Affair</span></span></span>

That did not settle the matter. Many families argued that the commissions gave too much weight to institutional records produced by the very authorities they distrusted. A death certificate can show what an office recorded, but not necessarily whether relatives were properly informed, whether the identity was correct or where a child was buried. The survival of incomplete files and contradictory documents means the official conclusion—no proven nationwide kidnapping scheme—does not resolve every individual case.

The opening of a large body of inquiry material to the public in 2016 was therefore significant. The Israel State Archives now provides access to collections from the investigative committees, encompassing a vast quantity of testimony and administrative documentation. Yet archival access has produced two opposing effects: it has supplied evidence that many children died, while also revealing the disorganisation, poor communication and institutional arrogance that made families’ suspicions understandable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://catalog.archives.gov.il/site/publication/fileandocr/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: catalog.archives.gov.il">[catalog.archives.gov.il]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">catalog.archives.gov.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">אוסף מלחמת יום הכיפורים, אוסף...Read more...</span></span></span>

A 2024 State Comptroller audit added another concern by examining the preservation of records from the state commission. The continued attention to missing or inadequately managed material shows why the controversy cannot be reduced to a choice between “everything was an abduction” and “nothing improper happened”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.mevaker.gov.il/sites/DigitalLibrary/Documents/2024/2024.05/2024-05-001-EN.docx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.mevaker.gov.il">[Mevaker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.mevaker.gov.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives – Preservation of Archival Material of the State Commission of Inquiry into the Disappearance of Children of Yemeni Immigrants...</span></span></span>

The most defensible assessment is that mass, centrally organised kidnapping has not been demonstrated. It is also clear that many families were denied basic dignity: reliable explanations, access to bodies or graves, and records they could trust. Individual cases remain unresolved, and the state’s failures in care and documentation became part of the mystery itself.

## Who killed Haim Arlosoroff?

On the night of 16 June 1933, Haim Arlosoroff, a leading figure in the Labour Zionist movement and head of the Jewish Agency’s political department, was walking with his wife along the Tel Aviv shore. Two men approached. One shone a light towards the couple; the other shot Arlosoroff, who died soon afterwards.

The killing took place at a moment of bitter conflict within the Jewish community under the British Mandate. Arlosoroff had recently helped negotiate an agreement that enabled some German Jews to leave Nazi Germany while transferring part of their property through the purchase of German goods. Supporters regarded the arrangement as a rescue mechanism. Opponents, particularly on the Revisionist right, denounced dealings with the Nazi government. The political atmosphere made a partisan assassination seem immediately plausible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.timesofisrael.com/an-early-zionists-unsolved-murder-fuels-a-mystery-novel-ripped-from-the-archives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofisrael.com">[timesofisrael.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofisrael.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">At 34,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Times of IsraelAn early Zionist&#x27;s unsolved murder fuels a mystery novel...February 22, 2023 — 22 Feb 2023 — On a balmy summer evenin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2023</span></span></span>

Three Revisionist activists were accused. One, Avraham Stavsky, was convicted largely on the identification made by Arlosoroff’s widow, but the conviction was overturned on appeal because the law required supporting evidence beyond a single eyewitness. The courtroom outcome did not establish who committed the murder; it merely showed that the prosecution had not met the required evidential standard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Haim_Arlosoroff" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assassination of Haim Arlosoroff</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assassination of Haim Arlosoroff</span></span></span>

Competing explanations multiplied. Some continued to believe that extremist political opponents were responsible. Others proposed that the killers were ordinary criminals, that the shooting arose from a sexual assault or attempted robbery, or that foreign agents had intervened. More elaborate accounts invoked Nazi or Soviet involvement. Most such theories depend on motive, ideological association or retrospective speculation rather than decisive physical evidence.

In 1982, the Israeli government appointed a judicial commission to reconsider the case. Its 1985 report cleared the Revisionist suspects examined by the panel, but it could not identify the killers or determine conclusively whether the murder was political. That result was unusually revealing: a state inquiry could rule out a politically explosive accusation without supplying a replacement solution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/744068" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[jstor.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on jstor.org.</span></span></span>

The Arlosoroff affair remains important because the dispute became almost as historically influential as the crime. For Labour and Revisionist movements, the murder served as evidence of the other side’s supposed moral danger. Decades later, arguments over guilt still reflected struggles over the legitimacy of Israel’s political traditions. The mystery survives not because every theory is equally credible, but because weak forensic evidence left a vacuum that politics quickly filled.

## Was the Copper Scroll a genuine treasure map?

Among the Dead Sea Scrolls discovered near Qumran, the Copper Scroll is the great anomaly. Found in Cave 3 in 1952, it was not written on parchment or papyrus but engraved on thin sheets of copper. Corrosion made it impossible to unroll safely, so conservators eventually cut it into sections to expose the writing.

Its contents are equally strange. Rather than scripture, commentary or community rules, the text lists dozens of locations where enormous quantities of gold, silver, vessels and other valuables were supposedly hidden. The directions refer to channels, tombs, cisterns, ruins and landmarks in Jerusalem and the Judean wilderness. The Israel Antiquities Authority describes it as a list of possible treasure deposits, perhaps connected with the Temple, while acknowledging the extraordinary scale of the claimed caches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deadseascrolls.org.il">[The Dead Sea Scrolls]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deadseascrolls.org.il</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on deadseascrolls.org.il.</span></span></span>

No accepted discovery has ever matched one of the entries. That failure has produced several main explanations.

**A real inventory of hidden wealth.** The plain, repetitive language resembles an administrative record more than a literary fantasy. Some scholars therefore think the text recorded genuine deposits concealed during a crisis, perhaps before or during the Jewish revolts against Rome.

**Temple treasure.** A popular theory identifies the riches with valuables removed from the Jerusalem Temple before its destruction in AD 70. The difficulty is that the amounts may be implausibly large, and the text never clearly states that the property belonged to the Temple.

**Community funds or accumulated offerings.** The deposits might represent wealth controlled by a religious group rather than the entire Temple treasury. This would explain why the list was hidden near Qumran, although the relationship between the scroll and the settlement remains debated.

**A symbolic or fictional catalogue.** Some researchers argue that the treasure never existed. The locations may have had a literary, ritual or legendary function, or the text may have preserved an exaggerated tradition rather than a usable set of directions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dornsife.usc.edu/wsrp/copper-scroll/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dornsife.usc.edu">[USC Dornsife]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dornsife.usc.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dornsife Copper Scroll</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dornsife Copper Scroll</span></span></span>

Even if the list was genuine, identifying its sites would be extremely difficult. Place names may have changed, measurements can be interpreted in different ways, and ancient landmarks may have vanished. Looting over two millennia could also have removed any caches long before modern archaeology began.

The Copper Scroll is therefore best understood as an authentic ancient document with an uncertain relationship to real treasure. Its metal, language and catalogue-like structure distinguish it from the other Dead Sea Scrolls. What remains unproved is the dramatic part: that the listed wealth survived, that it can still be located or that it represented the treasure of the Jerusalem Temple.

## Why did the submarine Dakar sink?

The Israeli submarine Dakar disappeared in January 1968 while travelling from Britain to Israel with 69 crew members aboard. A former Royal Navy vessel, it had been purchased and refitted before beginning its final voyage across the Mediterranean. Its last routine transmission was received shortly after midnight on 25 January. When further contact failed, ships and aircraft from several countries joined the search.

The disappearance generated immediate uncertainty. Without a wreck, investigators could not tell whether Dakar had suffered a mechanical failure, a collision, human error or hostile action. The Cold War setting encouraged suspicions involving Egypt or the Soviet Union, although Israeli naval officials publicly regarded technical or operational failure as more likely. Declassified discussions later showed that officials had privately considered several possibilities, including accidental Soviet involvement, but had no firm evidence for it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Dakar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">INS Dakar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">INS Dakar</span></span></span>

For more than three decades, searches concentrated partly on areas near Egypt and Cyprus. The geography of the loss remained uncertain because an emergency buoy from the submarine later washed ashore far from the wreck, misleading some interpretations of its final position.

In 1999, a joint Israeli-American search located Dakar at a depth of about 3,000 metres between Cyprus and Crete. The discovery settled where the submarine had gone down and ruled against many location-based theories. Surveys showed severe structural damage consistent with the vessel descending below the depth its hull could withstand. Parts of the stern and conning tower had separated from the main wreck.

Yet the initiating event remains unknown. The physical evidence suggests a rapid, uncontrolled descent followed by catastrophic pressure failure. It does not clearly reveal why control was lost. A technical defect, an error during diving or an accidental collision remain more plausible than an enemy attack, particularly because there is no accepted evidence of an explosion or combat damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Dakar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">INS Dakar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">INS Dakar</span></span></span>

Dakar demonstrates how a mystery can be partly solved. The wreck’s discovery ended speculation about its broad location, but not the precise chain of events aboard the submarine. The distinction matters: finding a lost object is not always the same as explaining its loss.

## Where is Eli Cohen buried?

Eli Cohen, an Israeli intelligence officer operating under a false identity, penetrated senior Syrian political and military circles in the early 1960s. Syrian counter-intelligence arrested him in January 1965 after detecting clandestine radio transmissions. He was tried and publicly hanged in Damascus on 18 May 1965.

Cohen’s remains were not returned to his family. Reports over the years have placed his burial in different parts of Damascus and have suggested that Syrian authorities moved the body to prevent an Israeli recovery operation. These claims are difficult to verify. Repeated diplomatic approaches, intelligence efforts and public appeals by his widow failed to produce a confirmed grave.

In May 2025, Israel announced that the Mossad had obtained approximately 2,500 items from the official Syrian archive relating to Cohen. The recovered material included interrogation records, photographs, recordings, false documents, handwritten letters and belongings seized after his arrest. It was a major archival discovery, offering a more detailed record of his capture and treatment. It did not, however, include his remains or publicly establish their location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.il/en/pages/official-syrian-archive-on-eli-cohen-brought-to-israel-18-may-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gov.il">[Government of Israel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gov.il</span><span class="citation-popover-title">official syrian archive on eli cohen brought to israel 18 may 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">official syrian archive on eli cohen brought to israel 18 may 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 2025</span></span></span>

An unverified media report in October 2025 claimed that Cohen’s remains might soon be transferred to Israel. His widow said at the time that the Mossad was not aware of such a development, and no confirmed return followed from the report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.timesofisrael.com/spy-eli-cohens-remains-to-be-transferred-to-israel-soon-from-syria-report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesofisrael.com">[The Times of Israel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesofisrael.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Times of Israel Spy Eli Cohen&#x27;s remains to be transferred to Israel soon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Times of Israel Spy Eli Cohen&#x27;s remains to be transferred to Israel soon</span></span></span>

The mystery is therefore narrower than the legends surrounding Cohen’s espionage career. There is no serious doubt about who arrested or executed him. The unresolved question is what happened to his body after burial and whether surviving Syrian records can identify its later location. The recovery of the intelligence archive shows that material once assumed inaccessible can reappear, but it also underlines the remaining gap: Israel recovered the documentary record of Cohen’s final months, not Cohen himself.

## What these mysteries reveal about Israel

These cases endure for different reasons, but several patterns recur.

First, missing evidence creates room for public memory to outrun proof. In the [Arlosoroff murder]({{ 'arlosoroff-murder/' | relative_url }}), inadequate forensic evidence allowed political narratives to harden. In the Dakar case, the missing wreck encouraged hostile-action theories until its discovery narrowed the possibilities.

Second, official records do not automatically create trust. The Yemenite children investigations produced extensive documentation, yet many families continued to doubt institutions that had originally failed to communicate with them. An archive can answer factual questions while also documenting why disbelief arose.

Third, partial discoveries can sharpen a mystery rather than end it. Locating Dakar revealed the physical manner of its destruction but not the initiating cause. Recovering Eli Cohen’s Syrian archive illuminated his imprisonment without locating his grave. Reading the Copper Scroll established what it says but not whether its treasure existed.

Finally, the most responsible approach is to separate levels of certainty. It is established that families lost children amid grave administrative failures; it is not established that the state operated a nationwide abduction programme. It is established that Arlosoroff was murdered by two assailants; their identities remain unknown. The Copper Scroll is genuine; its treasure is unverified. Dakar suffered a fatal deep-sea catastrophe; the original trigger cannot be reconstructed with confidence. Cohen was executed and buried in Syria; the present location of his remains is still unconfirmed.

That disciplined distinction between fact, inference and legend does not make the mysteries less compelling. It explains why they remain unresolved—and why each continues to illuminate a different fault line in Israel’s ancient and modern history.

    return encodeURIComponent(String(value || '').replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim()).replace(/%20/g, '+');
  }
  function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket];
    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
