## Introduction

The Chase Vault near Christ Church Parish Church, close to Oistins in [Barbados]({{ 'which-barbados-mysteries-are-fact/' | relative_url }}), is one of the Caribbean's most famous historical mysteries. According to the traditional story, every time the sealed family vault was opened between 1812 and 1820, the heavy lead-lined coffins inside had been thrown into disorder despite there being no obvious sign of forced entry. The tale has been repeated for almost two centuries as evidence of an unexplained or even supernatural event.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Barbados_93409a_chase_vault_moving_c_d3a5e3-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Chase Vault illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Barbados_93409a_chase_vault_moving_c_d3a5e3-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Barbados_93409a_chase_vault_moving_c_d3a5e3-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The difficulty is that the strongest versions of the story are not supported by equally strong contemporary evidence. Historians can trace how the narrative evolved, but many of its most dramatic details first appeared years after the alleged events and often contradict one another. Today the mystery lies as much in how the legend developed as in whether the coffins ever moved at all.

## What witnesses were said to have found

The accepted version of the story follows a dramatic sequence of events. The Chase family vault, built of coral stone with a heavy marble entrance slab, was opened repeatedly for burials during the early nineteenth century. On each occasion, those present supposedly discovered that the coffins had been disturbed.

According to later accounts:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* In 1812, when Colonel Thomas Chase was buried, the coffins of his daughters were allegedly found displaced.
* Similar disturbances were reported when the vault was reopened for later burials in 1816 and again around 1819 or 1820.
* The lead coffins were described as lying against walls, overturned, or stacked in apparently impossible positions.
* The entrance slab was said to have remained sealed.

</div>

The most famous episode concerns the alleged investigation by Governor Lord Combermere. In the traditional narrative, the coffins were carefully rearranged, fine sand was spread across the floor to reveal any intruder's footprints, and the entrance was sealed with official marks. When the vault was next opened, the seals were supposedly intact, the sand undisturbed, yet the coffins had once again shifted dramatically. The bodies were then removed permanently and the vault abandoned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

These details have become so familiar that many readers assume they come from official records. In fact, they largely come from much later retellings.

## How the documentary record changed

The greatest puzzle for historians is not simply whether the coffins moved but how the story itself evolved.

The earliest widely known published account appeared in James Edward Alexander's *Transatlantic Sketches* in 1833, more than a decade after the final alleged disturbance. Alexander did not identify contemporary documentary sources for the narrative, yet his version became the foundation for many later retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Vault" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Chase Vault</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Chase Vault</span></span></span>

During the nineteenth century the story expanded through several authors, including Sir Robert Schomburgk, Reuben Percy and others. Rather than becoming more consistent, the accounts diverged. Different versions disagree about:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* exactly which burials took place and when;
* how many coffins were present;
* the order in which disturbances occurred;
* who witnessed each opening;
* whether Lord Combermere personally supervised the investigation;
* who ordered the sand to be spread on the floor.

</div>

These inconsistencies matter because they suggest the narrative was still being shaped long after the supposed events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

Another important figure is the Reverend Thomas H. Orderson, rector of Christ Church. Many later writers traced the story back to accounts associated with him, yet surviving versions attributed to Orderson differ from one another. Instead of one stable eyewitness narrative, historians find several overlapping traditions with changing details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

## Why historians remain cautious

Researchers have spent more than a century searching for contemporary confirmation.

The principal problems include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Missing official documentation.** No surviving government report describes Lord Combermere's famous investigation.
* **No secure eyewitness manuscript.** A detailed account has long been attributed to Nathan Lucas, then Chief Justice of Barbados, but the original document has never been securely identified.
* **Silence in expected records.** Scholars have noted the absence of clear reports in contemporary Barbadian newspapers and parish documents that would be expected if such spectacular events had repeatedly occurred.

</div>

This absence does not prove the events never happened. Colonial records have been lost, damaged and dispersed over time. Nevertheless, historians generally regard the lack of contemporary evidence as the strongest reason for treating the story cautiously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

The mystery therefore differs from many supposedly paranormal cases. Instead of possessing numerous conflicting eyewitness statements from the time, it relies largely on stories written years later.

## Flooding, interference and legend-building

If at least some disturbance occurred, several natural explanations have been suggested.

### Flooding

Floodwater is probably the oldest physical explanation. Barbados experiences intense tropical rainfall and hurricanes, and a flooded vault could theoretically float coffins before leaving them in different positions as the water receded.

However, the traditional account creates problems for this explanation by claiming:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no obvious signs of flooding were seen;
* fine sand supposedly showed no disturbance;
* some lighter coffins were reportedly less affected than heavy lead-lined ones.

</div>

Modern investigators have also questioned whether the vault's elevation and construction make repeated flooding sufficiently convincing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

### Human interference

Another possibility is deliberate disturbance.

The vault may not always have been as securely sealed as later accounts imply. Family members, workmen, grave robbers or vandals could potentially have entered between burials. Since many descriptions of the seals and sand appear only in later versions, historians cannot determine with confidence whether these precautions were actually used or later added to strengthen the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

### Earth movement and other physical causes

Earthquakes, subsidence and decomposition gases have all been proposed.

None has gained broad acceptance. Significant earth movement capable of violently rearranging multiple heavy lead coffins would likely have affected surrounding structures, while gas pressure is generally considered incapable of producing the dramatic positions described in the legend. These explanations remain speculative rather than demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

### An expanding legend

Many historians now argue that the strongest explanation is not a single physical mechanism but gradual legend-building.

As the story passed through successive writers, additional dramatic features appeared: official investigations, undisturbed seals, carefully spread sand, impossible coffin positions and increasingly elaborate dialogue. Rather than one fixed historical report, the Chase Vault became a polished supernatural narrative that grew with each retelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

## Why the Chase Vault still matters

The Chase Vault remains important because it illustrates how history, folklore and incomplete archives can become intertwined.

Unlike a straightforward ghost story, it presents a genuine historical puzzle. A real burial vault exists. Real members of the Chase family lived and died. Yet the best-known version of events rests on sources written years afterwards that frequently disagree with one another.

For that reason, the central mystery today is twofold. One question asks whether anything unusual ever happened inside the vault between 1812 and 1820. The other asks how a story with such uncertain documentary foundations became one of the world's best-known haunted burial legends.

The evidence available today supports a cautious conclusion: some unusual local tradition may have existed, but the famous account of repeatedly moving coffins, official seals and impossible disturbances cannot be accepted as established historical fact. The enduring mystery lies less in proving the supernatural than in understanding how memory, storytelling and fragmentary records transformed a local burial vault into one of Barbados's most enduring historical legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2020/05/reopening-the-chase-vault-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer Reopening the Chase Vault Mystery &#124; Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>
