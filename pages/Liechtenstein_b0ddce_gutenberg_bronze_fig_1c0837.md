## Introduction

The Gutenberg Bronzes are among the most intriguing prehistoric discoveries ever made in [Liechtenstein]({{ 'which-liechtenstein-mysteries-still-defy/' | relative_url }}). Unearthed in 1932 on the slope below Gutenberg Castle in Balzers, the small collection of Iron Age bronze human and animal figures almost certainly formed part of a religious offering rather than an ordinary settlement or burial. Archaeologists broadly agree that they were deposited as votive gifts—objects deliberately dedicated to a supernatural power—but the identities of the worshippers, the deities they honoured and the ceremonies they performed remain unknown. That uncertainty is what makes the find one of Liechtenstein's most enduring archaeological mysteries: the artefacts survive, yet the beliefs that gave them meaning have largely vanished.

## The 1932 discovery below Gutenberg Castle

The bronze figures were discovered during excavations on the south-eastern slope of the Gutenberg hill beneath the medieval castle. Rather than lying in their original position, they were found in redeposited or disturbed archaeological layers. This detail has proved crucial because it means the bronzes had already been displaced before modern archaeologists encountered them, making it impossible to reconstruct their precise original arrangement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

Originally the deposit consisted of ten solid-cast bronze statuettes dating to the Late Iron Age, roughly between 500 and 100 BC. Nine survive today. The collection is remarkable because it combines several different themes within a single deposit:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Human warrior figures, including the famous "Mars of Gutenberg".
* Fertility-related human figures.
* Animal figures, notably a stag and a wild boar.

</div>

The figures vary noticeably in artistic style and workmanship. Rather than being produced together as a single set, they appear to have been made in different workshops over an extended period before eventually being deposited together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

Their location is equally significant. Gutenberg hill had already been occupied repeatedly from prehistoric times, long before the medieval castle was built, suggesting that the hill possessed ritual importance across many centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.balzers.li/Portal/UserFiles/files/Service/94_experience_Balzers.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: balzers.li">[Gemeinde Balzers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">balzers.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gemeinde Balzersexperience Balzers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 27, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 27, 2019</span></span></span>

## Why archaeologists favour a votive explanation

Although nobody witnessed the ceremonies that produced the deposit, several strands of evidence point towards ritual use rather than domestic activity.

The strongest clue is the surrounding archaeological context. The bronzes were associated with compact deposits of ash and animal bones, features that archaeologists commonly interpret as the remains of repeated burnt offerings. Together they suggest the existence of a nearby cremation or fire-sacrifice site rather than an ordinary habitation area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

The figures themselves also support this interpretation.

The warrior statuettes wear distinctive helmets of a type known from the Raetian Alpine region, while their artistic style also reflects broader Celtic influences. Rather than belonging exclusively to one culture, the collection appears to come from a frontier zone where Celtic and Raetian traditions overlapped and interacted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

Researchers generally group the figures into three symbolic themes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **War**, represented by armed male figures.
* **Fertility**, represented by human figures emphasising reproduction or prosperity.
* **Animals**, especially the stag and boar, both creatures with symbolic importance in Iron Age Europe.

</div>

Such diversity makes sense if different individuals dedicated offerings for different hopes or concerns—perhaps seeking victory, protection, fertility or thanksgiving—over many years rather than during a single ceremony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

## What disturbed layers prevent us from knowing

The mystery begins where the archaeological evidence ends.

Because the bronzes were recovered from disturbed deposits instead of their original resting place, several important questions can no longer be answered with confidence.

First, archaeologists cannot determine exactly where the original sanctuary stood. The evidence strongly suggests that a ritual area existed nearby, but the precise location of the altar, sacred enclosure or fire pit has been lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

Second, the original arrangement of the figures has disappeared. If the statuettes had been placed facing a particular direction, grouped by type or positioned around a shrine, that information was destroyed before excavation.

Third, there is no accompanying written evidence. The Gutenberg bronzes pre-date Roman rule in the region, leaving no inscriptions naming the gods, the donors or the purpose of the offerings. Unlike later temples with dedicatory texts, these objects speak only through archaeology.

Finally, the collection probably accumulated over time. Since the figures differ in style, quality and manufacture, they were unlikely to have been deposited simultaneously. Instead, generations of worshippers may have added offerings at the same sacred place, gradually creating the assemblage found in 1932.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

## Whose rituals were these?

The cultural setting adds another layer of uncertainty.

During the Late Iron Age, the Rhine valley around modern Balzers lay in a meeting zone between Celtic peoples to the west and the Raetian communities of the Alpine region. The artistic details of the bronzes reflect both traditions, making it difficult to assign the deposit to a single ethnic or political group.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

This mixed character has discouraged attempts to identify specific deities. Earlier writers sometimes labelled one warrior figure the "Mars of Gutenberg", borrowing the name of the Roman god of war because the figure resembles later martial imagery. Modern archaeologists treat that title simply as a convenient nickname rather than evidence that the worshippers honoured the Roman Mars centuries before Roman control reached the area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Gutenberger_Votivstatuetten" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Gutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconGutenberger Votivstatuetten – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

Instead, scholars generally conclude that the bronzes belonged to a local Iron Age religious tradition that absorbed influences from neighbouring cultures without leaving written records of its beliefs.

## Why the Gutenberg Bronzes still matter

The Gutenberg bronzes are not mysterious because their existence is doubted. Their authenticity, age and archaeological importance are well established. The mystery lies in the missing human story behind them.

Archaeologists can identify the bronzes as deliberate ritual offerings with considerable confidence. They can place them within the Late Iron Age and recognise influences from both Celtic and Raetian traditions. They can even infer that sacrifice by fire formed part of the surrounding ritual landscape. Yet they cannot identify the names of the gods invoked, reconstruct the ceremonies performed or explain exactly why these particular figures were chosen.

That combination of strong physical evidence and permanently incomplete interpretation makes the Gutenberg bronzes one of Liechtenstein's most compelling archaeological enigmas. Rather than inviting sensational theories, they remind us that even when remarkable artefacts survive, the beliefs and stories that once gave them meaning can disappear almost completely, leaving only carefully reasoned archaeological clues behind.
