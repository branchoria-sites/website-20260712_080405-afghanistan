## Introduction

Why did everyone leave Tetepare Island? No single answer has ever been accepted. Tetepare, now the largest uninhabited island in the South Pacific, supported thriving communities until the nineteenth century, when its inhabitants dispersed to neighbouring islands. Archaeological remains and descendants' oral traditions agree that the island was once densely occupied, but they differ over exactly why it was abandoned. Rather than pointing to one dramatic event, the available evidence suggests a combination of disease, warfare, insecurity and social disruption. That uncertainty is what makes Tetepare one of the [Solomon Islands]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-the-solomon/' | relative_url }})' most enduring historical puzzles: the island's former settlements are real, its descendants are known, but the decisive cause of the exodus remains unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/why-is-tetepare-the-south-pacifics-largest-uninhabited-island-180987184/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[smithsonianmag.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineWhy Is Tetepare the South Pacific’s Largest Uninhabited Island?August 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

## What remains of Tetepare's former settlements?

The mystery begins with the landscape itself. Although modern visitors encounter dense rainforest and abundant wildlife, the island contains extensive traces of earlier occupation. Former village sites, garden terraces, shell middens, sacred places, defensive structures and burial areas survive beneath the forest canopy, demonstrating that Tetepare was not an untouched wilderness before its abandonment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/why-is-tetepare-the-south-pacifics-largest-uninhabited-island-180987184/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineWhy Is Tetepare the South Pacific’s Largest Uninhabited Island?August 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

Descendants of the island's former inhabitants, now living mainly on Rendova, New Georgia and nearby islands, have preserved knowledge of ancestral places and family connections. Their traditions describe Tetepare's people as culturally distinct, speaking their own language and maintaining customs different from neighbouring communities. Although the language has disappeared, these family histories have helped identify settlement locations and explain why particular places remain culturally significant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/why-is-tetepare-the-south-pacifics-largest-uninhabited-island-180987184/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[smithsonianmag.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineWhy Is Tetepare the South Pacific’s Largest Uninhabited Island?August 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

Dating the abandonment precisely is difficult because written records are scarce. Most accounts place the main exodus around the middle of the nineteenth century, roughly the 1850s or 1860s, before sustained colonial administration reached the region.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tetepare.org/the-history-of-tetepare/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tetepare.org">[Tetepare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tetepare.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The History of Tetepare</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The History of Tetepare - Tetepare...</span></span></span>

## Disease, warfare and internal division

The strongest modern interpretation is not that one catastrophe emptied the island overnight, but that several pressures combined until remaining became impossible.

One widely repeated explanation is epidemic disease. Descendants recount a devastating sickness that spread through villages, killing so many people that survivors could no longer maintain their communities. Some later interpretations suggest the illness may have been an introduced infectious disease, perhaps associated with increased contact with whalers or other outsiders, although no contemporary medical records survive to identify it with certainty. Because the stories were preserved orally, historians cannot determine whether they describe a single epidemic or several outbreaks remembered together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tetepare.org/the-history-of-tetepare/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tetepare.org">[Tetepare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tetepare.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The History of Tetepare</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The History of Tetepare - Tetepare...</span></span></span>

A second explanation centres on warfare and headhunting. During the nineteenth century, much of the western Solomon Islands experienced frequent raiding between rival communities. Oral traditions describe Tetepare's inhabitants living under constant threat from headhunting expeditions. Such attacks disrupted agriculture, trade and everyday security. Even if raids alone did not force permanent abandonment, they may have made recovery from disease or population decline impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/why-is-tetepare-the-south-pacifics-largest-uninhabited-island-180987184/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineWhy Is Tetepare the South Pacific’s Largest Uninhabited Island?August 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

Some traditions also describe conflict within Tetepare itself. These accounts vary considerably and are less consistent than those concerning disease or raiding. Rather than depicting a civil war that clearly emptied the island, they suggest divisions among communities that weakened collective resistance to outside pressures. Because these traditions differ between descendant families, historians generally treat them as possible contributing factors rather than established causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tetepare.org/the-history-of-tetepare/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tetepare.org">[Tetepare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tetepare.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The History of Tetepare</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The History of Tetepare - Tetepare...</span></span></span>

Folklore preserves yet another explanation: a curse or supernatural force, sometimes associated with a sea devil. Such accounts remain culturally important because they reflect how communities understood misfortune before modern medicine or written history. For historical reconstruction, however, researchers generally interpret these stories symbolically rather than literally, recognising that they may encode memories of disease, fear or social collapse.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/why-is-tetepare-the-south-pacifics-largest-uninhabited-island-180987184/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineWhy Is Tetepare the South Pacific’s Largest Uninhabited Island?August 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why no single explanation solves the mystery

The evidence for Tetepare's abandonment comes from different kinds of sources that answer different questions. Archaeology confirms that substantial communities once existed and later disappeared. Oral history preserves memories of why descendants believed their ancestors left. Historical records from the period are sparse and mostly written by outsiders after the crucial events had already occurred. None of these sources alone provides a complete explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/why-is-tetepare-the-south-pacifics-largest-uninhabited-island-180987184/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[smithsonianmag.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian MagazineWhy Is Tetepare the South Pacific’s Largest Uninhabited Island?August 25, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 25, 2025</span></span></span>

This is why most researchers avoid choosing one exclusive cause. A sequence of events is more plausible than a single dramatic trigger. Disease could have reduced the population, making villages more vulnerable to raids. Persistent warfare could then have encouraged survivors to seek safety among relatives on neighbouring islands. Social fragmentation may have accelerated a migration that had already begun. Such interacting pressures fit broader archaeological understanding that settlement abandonment is often the product of multiple reinforcing factors rather than one isolated event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S095937801100121X" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[sciencedirect.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Settlement abandonment in the context of global environmental change - ScienceDirectDecember 1, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2011</span></span></span>

The continuing uncertainty is not simply the result of missing evidence. It also reflects the nature of nineteenth-century island societies, where history was transmitted primarily through oral tradition and where tropical environments rapidly erase fragile archaeological remains.

## How abandonment shaped conservation and customary ownership

Ironically, the disappearance of Tetepare's permanent population helped preserve one of the Solomon Islands' richest natural environments. Without sustained agriculture, logging or modern settlement, much of the island returned to rainforest, allowing native wildlife and coastal ecosystems to flourish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5414" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Marovo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreMarovo - Tetepare Complex - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

The island was never forgotten by its people, however. Descendants retained customary ownership despite living elsewhere and continued to visit for hunting, fishing and ceremonial purposes. In recent decades they formed the Tetepare Descendants' Association, which manages the island as a large community-led conservation area while protecting archaeological sites and ancestral places. This arrangement demonstrates that an uninhabited island is not an ownerless one: cultural ties survived even after permanent settlement ended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5414" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Marovo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreMarovo - Tetepare Complex - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

Tetepare therefore occupies a distinctive place in the history of the Solomon Islands. Its abandoned settlements are not evidence of a vanished civilisation but of a living community whose descendants still remember the island. The central mystery is not whether people once lived there—they unquestionably did—but why several powerful pressures converged strongly enough that an entire island community chose, or was compelled, to leave its ancestral home.
