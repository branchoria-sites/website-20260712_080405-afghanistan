## Introduction

[Tunisia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-tunisias/' | relative_url }})'s most important political assassinations are not mysteries because nobody knows who pulled the trigger. In several of the country's defining cases, investigators have identified gunmen, militant networks or operational cells. The deeper historical question is whether those attackers acted on their own initiative or as the final link in a longer chain of command involving intelligence services, political organisations, colonial authorities or hidden security structures. That distinction has shaped public debate for decades because responsibility for planning and authorising a killing carries far greater political consequences than identifying the immediate perpetrators.

Two periods dominate this debate. The first concerns the assassination of trade union leader Farhat Hached in 1952 during French colonial rule. The second concerns the murders of opposition politicians Chokri Belaid and Mohamed Brahmi in 2013 after Tunisia's revolution. In both eras, investigations uncovered significant evidence about operational networks while leaving enduring disputes over who ultimately gave the orders.

## Farhat Hached and the Red Hand

Farhat Hached, founder of Tunisia's national trade union movement, was assassinated on 5 December 1952 near Tunis. His murder quickly became one of the defining events of the struggle for independence because Hached had become an influential international advocate for ending French colonial rule.

For decades, responsibility was publicly attributed to an organisation known as the "Red Hand" (La Main Rouge), presented as a shadowy anti-nationalist group. The central mystery, however, was whether the Red Hand existed as an independent underground organisation or merely served as a cover name for operations directed by French state agencies.

Evidence released over time has shifted the historical consensus. French archival material, together with statements made after the declassification of official documents, indicates that Hached had been placed under close surveillance before his assassination and that French security and intelligence officials discussed options for neutralising him. Historians generally conclude that the Red Hand functioned less as an autonomous organisation than as a convenient label masking covert state activity, although the precise administrative chain through which the assassination was authorised remains incompletely documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.gnet.tn/archives/actualites-nationales/tunisie-letat-francais-a-assassine-farhat-hached/id-menu-958.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.gnet.tn">[Gnet news]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.gnet.tn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gnet newsTunisie: &quot;LEtat franais a assassin Farhat Hached&quot; &#124; Actualits NationalesNovember 29, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 29, 2013</span></span></span>

The remaining uncertainty is therefore narrower than many popular accounts suggest. The debate is no longer chiefly about whether French authorities bore responsibility. Instead, historians continue to examine which intelligence services, military officers and civilian officials approved the operation, how far decisions travelled through the colonial hierarchy, and whether written authorisations were deliberately avoided or later destroyed.

## Secret networks and colonial responsibility

The Hached case illustrates a recurring feature of covert political violence: operational secrecy often depends on separating planners from those who carry out an attack.

Several characteristics continue to complicate reconstruction of the command chain:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **Layered responsibility.** Surveillance teams, operational commandos and political decision-makers may all belong to different organisations.
* **Use of cover organisations.** Groups such as the Red Hand provided plausible deniability by obscuring whether violence originated from unofficial militants or state services.
* **Fragmentary archives.** Intelligence files were never designed to provide transparent historical records. Some remain classified, while others appear incomplete or were destroyed.
* **Political interpretation.** Newly released documents have clarified aspects of planning but have not answered every question about ministerial approval or individual accountability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.gnet.tn/archives/actualites-nationales/tunisie-letat-francais-a-assassine-farhat-hached/id-menu-958.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.gnet.tn">[gnet.tn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.gnet.tn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gnet newsTunisie: &quot;LEtat franais a assassin Farhat Hached&quot; &#124; Actualits NationalesNovember 29, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 29, 2013</span></span></span>

</div>

Because of these factors, the Hached assassination has evolved from a simple question of "who killed him?" into a broader investigation of how colonial governments organised covert operations while maintaining public deniability.

## Why the 2013 assassinations remain disputed

The assassinations of opposition leaders Chokri Belaid in February 2013 and Mohamed Brahmi in July 2013 reopened similar questions under very different political circumstances.

The immediate investigation established several important facts. Both men were killed using the same firearm, and Tunisian authorities linked the operational cell to extremist militants associated with Ansar al-Sharia and identified figures such as Boubaker Hakim as key suspects in organising the attacks. These findings substantially reduced uncertainty about the operational side of the murders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.sky.com/story/tunisian-leaders-assassinated-with-same-gun-10439121" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.sky.com">[sky.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.sky.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Tunisian Leaders Assassinated With Same Gun &#124; World News &#124; Sky News</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky NewsTunisian Leaders Assassinated With Same Gun &#124; World News &#124; Sky NewsJuly 26, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 26, 2013</span></span></span>

The unresolved controversy instead concerns whether the militant cell acted entirely independently or benefited from protection, assistance or deliberate obstruction by other actors. Over the following years, lawyers representing the victims' families alleged that evidence pointed towards a clandestine "secret apparatus" connected to the political movement Ennahda and accused parts of the judiciary of suppressing evidence. Ennahda has consistently denied these allegations, describing them as politically motivated. Investigations and legal proceedings have continued for years, but many of the most serious claims remain disputed rather than judicially established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nawaat.org/2021/10/14/belaid-brahmis-assassinations-frozen-investigations-warm-up/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nawaat.org">[Nawaat]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nawaat.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Belaid &amp; Brahmis Assassinations: Frozen Investigations Warm Up Nawaat</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belaid &amp; Brahmis Assassinations: Frozen Investigations Warm Up NawaatOctober 14, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 14, 2021</span></span></span>

This distinction is crucial. There is considerably stronger evidence regarding the identities of several militants involved in planning and carrying out the killings than there is regarding claims that a hidden political command structure ordered them.

## Why archives still leave command chains unclear

The persistence of these controversies reflects the way covert political violence is organised rather than simply failures of historical research.

In both the colonial and post-revolutionary periods, investigators encountered similar obstacles:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Intelligence records that were incomplete, classified or unavailable.
* Rival institutions holding different pieces of evidence.
* Competing political interests affecting how investigations were conducted.
* Long delays that allowed documents, witnesses and forensic opportunities to disappear.
* Public allegations that exceeded what courts had formally established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nawaat.org/2022/03/11/belaid-and-brahmi-assassinations-autopsy-of-an-ill-stricken-justice-system/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nawaat.org">[Nawaat]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nawaat.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belaid and Brahmi Assassinations: autopsy of an ill-stricken justice system Nawaat...</span></span></span>

</div>

The resulting gap between operational evidence and political responsibility has encouraged competing narratives. Some observers argue that hidden sponsors almost certainly existed because sophisticated assassinations rarely occur without organisational backing. Others caution that suspicion alone cannot substitute for documentary proof and that complex terrorist networks do not necessarily require direction from political leaders or intelligence agencies.

## What the strongest evidence supports

Across Tunisia's major political assassination cases, the evidence supports different levels of confidence.

The strongest historical consensus concerns Farhat Hached. Modern archival evidence points towards responsibility within the French colonial state, even though historians continue to debate the precise chain of authorisation and individual accountability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.gnet.tn/archives/actualites-nationales/tunisie-letat-francais-a-assassine-farhat-hached/id-menu-958.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.gnet.tn">[Gnet news]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.gnet.tn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gnet newsTunisie: &quot;LEtat franais a assassin Farhat Hached&quot; &#124; Actualits NationalesNovember 29, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 29, 2013</span></span></span>

The 2013 assassinations remain more contested. Investigations have identified extremist networks involved in carrying out the murders, but allegations concerning hidden political command structures, secret organisations and deliberate institutional cover-ups continue to divide investigators, lawyers and political actors. Some court proceedings have addressed related allegations involving concealed networks and documents, yet this has not produced universal agreement about who, if anyone, ultimately ordered the killings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nawaat.org/2022/03/11/belaid-and-brahmi-assassinations-autopsy-of-an-ill-stricken-justice-system/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nawaat.org">[Nawaat]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nawaat.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belaid and Brahmi Assassinations: autopsy of an ill-stricken justice system Nawaat...</span></span></span>

For historians, the enduring mystery is therefore not simply identifying assassins. It is determining where responsibility ended: with the gunmen themselves, with militant organisers, or with higher authorities who may have remained deliberately invisible. That unresolved question continues to shape Tunisia's understanding of both its colonial past and its democratic transition.
