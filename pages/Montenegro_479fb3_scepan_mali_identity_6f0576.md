## Introduction

Who was Šćepan Mali? More than 250 years after his death, historians still cannot answer that basic question with confidence. What is firmly established is that an unknown man appeared in [Montenegro]({{ 'which-mysteries-still-shape-montenegros/' | relative_url }}) in 1766, became the country's effective ruler by exploiting rumours that he was the deposed Russian emperor Peter III, and governed until his assassination in 1773. The remarkable twist is that, even after Russian representatives proved he was not Peter III, many Montenegrins continued to support him because of his political ability rather than his false identity. The enduring mystery is therefore not whether he was the Russian tsar—he was not—but where he really came from, what his original name was, and why he embarked on one of Europe's most successful political impostures.

## How the Peter III Rumour Took Hold

Šćepan Mali emerged at a moment of uncertainty. Montenegro had weak central authority, rival clans frequently fought one another, and Russia enjoyed enormous prestige among the Orthodox population. Peter III, the Russian emperor deposed in 1762, had already become the subject of survival rumours across Europe, making the story believable to many people who had never seen him.

The evidence suggests that Šćepan did not openly proclaim himself to be Peter III. Instead, he allowed others to reach that conclusion through implication, carefully chosen remarks and calculated silence. This ambiguity was politically effective. Those eager to believe could do so without him making a direct claim that could easily be disproved.

His acceptance cannot be explained by gullibility alone. Once in power, he reduced blood feuds, strengthened judicial authority, imposed greater order among the clans and attempted to centralise government. These practical successes meant that his legitimacy increasingly rested on performance rather than the imperial rumour itself. Even after a Russian mission publicly established that he was not the murdered emperor, he remained Montenegro's ruler because many leading figures judged him more capable than the available alternatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/vakanuvis/article/1336135" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dergipark.org.tr">[DergiPark]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dergipark.org.tr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Karadağ’da Küçük Stepan (Sahte Rus Çarı III. ## What the Surviving Accounts Reveal

The greatest obstacle to identifying Šćepan is the contradictory nature of the surviving evidence. No contemporary document securely records his birth name, family or place of origin before his appearance in Montenegro.

Instead, historians are left with scattered diplomatic reports, later memoirs and interrogation accounts that often contradict one another. During questioning by Russian officials, he reportedly offered inconsistent explanations about his background. At different times he was said to have claimed origins in Ioannina, while elsewhere he was described as a Dalmatian using the surname Rajčević. Such inconsistencies are usually interpreted as deliberate attempts to conceal his past rather than honest confusion.

These contradictions create several problems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* There is no continuous documentary trail linking him to any identifiable individual before 1766.
* Many accounts were written by foreign observers with limited local knowledge.
* Later narratives were shaped by political agendas, folklore and admiration for his rule.
* Some details first appeared decades after his death, making them difficult to verify independently.

</div>

Modern historians therefore distinguish carefully between documented contemporary evidence and later traditions that cannot be tested.

An important recent development has been the publication of a large documentary collection bringing together 441 surviving documents from 1767 to 1773, including previously unpublished archival material from Venetian, Russian and regional collections. ## The Leading Identity Theories

Because the documentary record is incomplete, several competing explanations remain under discussion. None commands universal acceptance.

### An educated Balkan adventurer

Many historians regard this as the most plausible general explanation. Under this view, Šćepan was an unusually intelligent and well-travelled man from the western Balkans who possessed sufficient education, linguistic ability and political experience to exploit Montenegro's circumstances. His administrative reforms and diplomatic awareness suggest someone with far greater experience than an ordinary wanderer.

This theory explains his competence but cannot identify him by name.

### Jovan Stefanović Baljević

One of the best-known modern proposals identifies Šćepan with Jovan Stefanović Baljević, a Montenegrin who studied abroad, entered Russian service and possessed the education and international experience that would fit the profile.

Supporters point to Baljević's documented connections with Russia and the uncertainties surrounding parts of his later biography. They argue that these gaps leave open the possibility that he reinvented himself.

The evidence, however, remains circumstantial. No surviving document directly equates Baljević with Šćepan Mali, and the identification has never achieved scholarly consensus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://istorijskizapisi.me/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Raspopovic-Nekoliko-cirilskih-dokumenata-o-Scepanu-Malom.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: istorijskizapisi.me">[istorijskizapisi.me]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">istorijskizapisi.me</span><span class="citation-popover-title">УДК: 94(497.16)”17”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">УДК: 94(497.16)”17”</span></span></span>

### A foreign agent

Some historians have explored whether Šćepan may have been encouraged or supported by an outside power seeking influence in Montenegro.

Candidates have included Russia, Venice or even other regional interests. Yet no convincing archival evidence demonstrates that he acted as a controlled agent for any government. His policies frequently irritated several neighbouring powers simultaneously, making the theory difficult to sustain.

### A calculated self-invention

Another interpretation is that there was never an earlier identity waiting to be rediscovered. Instead, Šćepan deliberately erased his previous life, adopting a new persona whenever circumstances required.

This reading fits his contradictory statements during interrogation and his refusal to provide consistent biographical details. If correct, the surviving documentary silence may not be accidental but the result of deliberate concealment.

## Why the Mystery Remains Unsolved

Unlike many historical mysteries, the problem is not a lack of interest but the nature of the surviving evidence.

Several factors combine to frustrate identification:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Few personal records:** Ordinary individuals in the eighteenth-century Balkans often left little documentary trace before entering public life.
* **Intentional deception:** Šćepan had every incentive to obscure his origins while maintaining political authority.
* **Fragmented archives:** Relevant records are dispersed across Montenegro, Venice, Russia, Austria and other former imperial collections, with inevitable losses over time.
* **Conflicting testimony:** Witnesses repeated rumours, assumptions and hearsay alongside genuine observations.

</div>

Recent archival work has enlarged the documentary base rather than settled the debate. Instead of revealing one decisive answer, it has demonstrated how many contemporary observers themselves disagreed about who Šćepan really was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nb-cg.me/en/events/893-proceedings-scepan-mali-in-documents-1767-1773-presented-to-the-audience-in-cetinje" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nb-cg.me">[Nacionalna biblioteka Crne Gore]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nb-cg.me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nacionalna biblioteka Crne GoreNacionalna biblioteka Crne Gore - Đurđe Crnojević -June 3, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 3, 2022</span></span></span>

## Beyond the Imposture: Why His Identity Still Matters

Šćepan Mali's unknown identity matters because it changes how historians interpret one of Montenegro's most unusual political episodes.

If he can eventually be linked to a known individual, historians would gain valuable insight into his education, political connections and possible long-term objectives. That could clarify whether his rise resulted primarily from personal ambition, foreign influence or exceptional political skill.

Without that identification, attention shifts from biography to performance. The remarkable historical fact is that a stranger with no verifiable pedigree united a divided society, introduced meaningful administrative reforms and retained authority even after the central deception collapsed. His success demonstrates that, in eighteenth-century Montenegro, effective leadership ultimately proved more persuasive than dynastic legitimacy.

The mystery therefore survives not because of fantasies about a hidden emperor but because the documentary record ends where the most important personal questions begin.
