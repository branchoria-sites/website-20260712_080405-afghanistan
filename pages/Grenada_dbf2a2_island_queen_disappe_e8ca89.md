## Introduction

The disappearance of the *Island Queen* is one of [Grenada]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-grenadas/' | relative_url }})'s most enduring maritime mysteries. On 5 August 1944, during the final year of the Second World War, the auxiliary schooner left St George's for St Vincent carrying 67 passengers and crew. Another schooner, the *Providence Mark*, made the same journey and arrived safely. The *Island Queen* never did. No confirmed wreckage, bodies or identifiable debris were recovered, leaving generations of families without answers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nowgrenada.com/2019/08/island-queen-disappeared-without-a-trace-75-years-ago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nowgrenada.com">[NOW Grenada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nowgrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NOW Grenada Island Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW Grenada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOW GrenadaIsland Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW GrenadaAugust 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 5, 2019</span></span></span>

More than eighty years later, historians agree on the basic facts but not on the cause. Official investigations at the time suggested an onboard fire, while later writers have proposed wartime attack, collision with a drifting naval mine, structural failure or an encounter with the submarine volcano Kick-'em-Jenny. None of these explanations has been proved conclusively, making the *Island Queen* not simply a tragic loss of life but one of Grenada's genuine unresolved historical mysteries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.searchlight.vc/special-features/2008/09/19/schooner-disappears-with-67-on-board/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: searchlight.vc">[searchlight.vc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">searchlight.vc</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Schooner disappears with 67 on board</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 19, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 19, 2008</span></span></span>

## The final voyage from Grenada to St Vincent

The voyage was intended as a holiday excursion over the Emancipation weekend. Two schooners departed St George's harbour: the *Island Queen*, commanded by Captain Chicra Salhab, and the *Providence Mark*. Many younger passengers deliberately chose the *Island Queen*, which had a reputation as the livelier vessel. Among those aboard were school pupils, young professionals and people travelling to attend a wedding in St Vincent. The loss therefore affected numerous prominent Grenadian and Vincentian families simultaneously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nowgrenada.com/2019/08/island-queen-disappeared-without-a-trace-75-years-ago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nowgrenada.com">[nowgrenada.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nowgrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NOW Grenada Island Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW Grenada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOW GrenadaIsland Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW GrenadaAugust 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 5, 2019</span></span></span>

Witnesses aboard the *Providence Mark* later recalled that the two vessels travelled on similar courses during the early part of the journey before gradually separating. One of the last reported sightings placed the *Island Queen* some distance offshore near Duquesne Bay in northern Grenada during the evening. As darkness fell, her mast light disappeared from view. By the following morning, the *Providence Mark* had reached St Vincent, where passengers initially assumed the faster schooner would soon arrive. It never did.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uwiseismic.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Kick-em-Jenny.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uwiseismic.com">[UWI Seismic Research Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uwiseismic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UWI Seismic Research Centre Jan M. Searches involving aircraft and vessels continued for days and weeks, yet failed to locate either the schooner or its occupants. Reports of isolated clothing or floating objects were never verified as belonging to the vessel, and no confirmed wreck site has ever been identified. This complete absence of physical evidence has shaped every later theory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nowgrenada.com/2019/08/island-queen-disappeared-without-a-trace-75-years-ago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nowgrenada.com">[NOW Grenada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nowgrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NOW Grenada Island Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW Grenada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOW GrenadaIsland Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW GrenadaAugust 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 5, 2019</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery has proved so difficult to solve

Unlike many shipwrecks, the *Island Queen* left investigators with remarkably little physical evidence. No confirmed hull remains have been found, and there were no survivors to describe the vessel's final moments. Wartime conditions also complicated record-keeping. The Caribbean was an active theatre of naval operations during the early years of the war, and although the danger from German submarines had diminished by mid-1944, military secrecy and incomplete archives have encouraged speculation ever since.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.familysearch.org/search/catalog/4369240" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: familysearch.org">[FamilySearch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">familysearch.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FamilySearch Catalog: Grenada in wartime: the tragic loss of the Island Queen and other memories of World War II/ Beverley A...</span></span></span>

Historian Beverley A. Steele, whose research gathered oral testimony from relatives and witnesses decades later, concluded that many stories developed because families struggled to accept that an entire vessel could disappear so completely. She also noted that surviving official documentation relating to the inquiry is sparse, making it difficult to test competing explanations against contemporary evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nowgrenada.com/2019/08/island-queen-disappeared-without-a-trace-75-years-ago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nowgrenada.com">[NOW Grenada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nowgrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NOW Grenada Island Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW Grenada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOW GrenadaIsland Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW GrenadaAugust 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 5, 2019</span></span></span>

## Submarine, fire and foundering theories

Several explanations have been proposed over the decades, but each encounters important evidential problems.

**Onboard fire**

The official inquiry reportedly concluded that a fire was the most likely cause. Witnesses in Carriacou were said to have observed a plume of smoke at sea around the time the schooner should have been passing nearby. Fire was a recognised hazard aboard wooden vessels carrying fuel and cargo. However, critics note that a fire severe enough to destroy the vessel would normally be expected to leave floating debris or provide opportunities for some passengers to escape. The lack of confirmed wreckage has therefore left many unconvinced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-grenada-won-world-war-ii-shaun-pascal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: linkedin.com">[LinkedIn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">linkedin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">how grenada won world war ii shaun pascal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Grenada Won World War IIMay 19, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 19, 2021</span></span></span>

**German submarine attack**

Because the disappearance occurred during the Second World War, rumours quickly spread that a German U-boat had torpedoed the schooner. German submarines had indeed operated extensively in the Caribbean earlier in the war, attacking merchant shipping and oil tankers. Yet historians have found no German operational records confirming an attack on the *Island Queen*, and by August 1944 U-boat activity in the eastern Caribbean had fallen sharply. No documentary evidence has emerged linking any submarine to the loss.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.familysearch.org/search/catalog/4369240" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: familysearch.org">[FamilySearch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">familysearch.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FamilySearch Catalog: Grenada in wartime: the tragic loss of the Island Queen and other memories of World War II/ Beverley A...</span></span></span>

**Friendly fire**

A variation on the wartime theory suggests the vessel may have been mistaken for an enemy target by Allied forces, possibly because it reportedly carried a German-built engine. This idea has occasionally been regarded as more plausible than deliberate German action, but no Allied naval records have been discovered that document such an incident. As with the submarine theory, it remains speculative rather than evidence-based.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nowgrenada.com/2019/08/island-queen-disappeared-without-a-trace-75-years-ago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nowgrenada.com">[NOW Grenada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nowgrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NOW Grenada Island Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW Grenada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOW GrenadaIsland Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW GrenadaAugust 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 5, 2019</span></span></span>

**Collision with a drifting mine**

Another possibility is that the schooner struck a floating naval mine that had broken free from wartime defensive minefields elsewhere in the Caribbean. Supporters point to the later discovery of drifting wartime mines in the region, including one that washed ashore on Carriacou in 1945 with fatal consequences. An explosion might explain the absence of an intact wreck. Even so, no mine fragments or corroborating naval records have tied this explanation specifically to the *Island Queen*.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.searchlight.vc/special-features/2008/09/19/schooner-disappears-with-67-on-board/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: searchlight.vc">[searchlight.vc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">searchlight.vc</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Schooner disappears with 67 on board</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">September 19, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 19, 2008</span></span></span>

**Ordinary foundering**

Some have suggested that the vessel simply capsized or sank because of stability problems, heavy weather or lack of ballast. Researchers have generally regarded these explanations as weak because weather reports do not indicate exceptional conditions, and ordinary sinkings often leave floating timber, cargo or survivors. The complete disappearance remains difficult to reconcile with a routine foundering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nowgrenada.com/2019/08/island-queen-disappeared-without-a-trace-75-years-ago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nowgrenada.com">[NOW Grenada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nowgrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NOW Grenada Island Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW Grenada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOW GrenadaIsland Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW GrenadaAugust 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 5, 2019</span></span></span>

## Could Kick-'em-Jenny have caused the loss?

The most distinctive modern explanation links the disappearance to Kick-'em-Jenny, the active submarine volcano north of Grenada.

Volcanologist Jan Lindsay and colleagues at the University of the West Indies have argued that witness testimony places the *Island Queen* close to the volcano's location when it was last seen. Kick-'em-Jenny had erupted in 1943, and researchers believe it may have continued releasing volcanic gases during 1944 even without an obvious surface eruption.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uwiseismic.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Kick-em-Jenny.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uwiseismic.com">[UWI Seismic Research Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uwiseismic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UWI Seismic Research Centre Jan M. Lindsay and John B. Shepherd Seismic Researc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UWI Seismic Research CentreJan M. Lindsay and John B. ShepherdSeismic ResearcMarch 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

The proposed mechanism is scientifically plausible in principle. Large volumes of volcanic gas rising through seawater can reduce the water's density, decreasing buoyancy for vessels directly above the gas plume. If a wooden schooner encountered such an area unexpectedly, it could lose support rapidly and sink. Because gas releases may leave little floating debris, supporters argue that this hypothesis helps explain why virtually nothing was recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uwiseismic.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Kick-em-Jenny.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uwiseismic.com">[UWI Seismic Research Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uwiseismic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UWI Seismic Research Centre Jan M. ## What remains unresolved

The strongest established facts are straightforward:

* The *Island Queen* departed Grenada on 5 August 1944 with 67 people aboard.
* It was last observed during the voyage while the *Providence Mark* completed the same route safely.
* No confirmed survivors, wreckage or identified wreck site have ever been found.
* No surviving wartime records conclusively support a submarine attack, friendly fire or mine strike.
* No geological or archaeological evidence has confirmed the Kick-'em-Jenny hypothesis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nowgrenada.com/2019/08/island-queen-disappeared-without-a-trace-75-years-ago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nowgrenada.com">[nowgrenada.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nowgrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NOW Grenada Island Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW Grenada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOW GrenadaIsland Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW GrenadaAugust 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 5, 2019</span></span></span>

Because the central physical evidence—the vessel itself—has never been recovered, every explanation relies on inference rather than direct proof. Unless the wreck is eventually located and examined, it is unlikely that historians will be able to move beyond probabilities.

## Why the disappearance still matters

The *Island Queen* occupies a distinctive place in Grenada's historical memory because it combined wartime uncertainty with profound personal loss. Entire families, schools and communities were affected, and many victims have no known grave. Historians such as Beverley A. Steele have argued that the absence of a memorial or physical resting place has contributed to the tragedy's enduring emotional impact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nowgrenada.com/2019/08/island-queen-disappeared-without-a-trace-75-years-ago/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nowgrenada.com">[NOW Grenada]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nowgrenada.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NOW Grenada Island Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW Grenada</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOW GrenadaIsland Queen disappeared without a trace 75 years ago &#124; NOW GrenadaAugust 5, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 5, 2019</span></span></span>

Today the mystery sits alongside Grenada's other unresolved historical questions as an example of how incomplete records, vanished physical evidence and competing interpretations can leave an event permanently open to debate.
