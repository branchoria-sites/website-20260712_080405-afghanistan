## Introduction

The Angolares are a distinct community from the southern part of São Tomé whose origins remain one of the country's most enduring historical debates. For generations, many people have repeated a dramatic story that the Angolares descended from survivors of a slave ship wrecked near Sete Pedras on the island's south-east coast. Yet modern historical, linguistic and genetic research increasingly suggests that this famous tale is unlikely to explain the community's beginnings on its own. Instead, the strongest evidence points towards a population formed largely by escaped enslaved people who established independent settlements beyond Portuguese control, later developing a unique identity through isolation and adaptation. The mystery is therefore not simply whether a shipwreck occurred, but how oral tradition, colonial records and modern science fit together to explain the emergence of one of São Tomé and Príncipe's most distinctive peoples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8229774/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2021</span></span></span>

## Did the Sete Pedras shipwreck really happen?

The best-known explanation for the Angolares' origins tells of a slave ship that wrecked on the south-eastern coast of São Tomé during the sixteenth century. According to the tradition, enslaved Africans swam ashore, escaped into the forests and mountains, and founded a free community that eventually became the Angolares.

The story remains deeply rooted in local memory and has become part of the islands' cultural heritage. It offers a powerful explanation for why the Angolares maintained a separate identity from other Creole communities on São Tomé and why many of their ancestors were believed to have come from the region of present-day Angola.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://novaresearch.unl.pt/en/publications/do-ref%C3%BAgio-nos-picos-da-ilha-de-s%C3%A3o-tom%C3%A9-%C3%A0-absor%C3%A7%C3%A3o-colonial-a-qu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: novaresearch.unl.pt">[Universidade NOVA de Lisboa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">novaresearch.unl.pt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universidade NOVA de LisboaDo refúgio nos picos da ilha de São Tomé à absorção colonial: a questão dos Angolares - Universidade NOVA de L...</span></span></span>

The historical difficulty is that no contemporary Portuguese document records such a wreck. Researchers have searched sixteenth-century records without finding direct evidence that a slave ship carrying the ancestors of the Angolares was lost at Sete Pedras. The earliest written version of the story only appears in a manuscript from the early eighteenth century, well over a century after the supposed event. That long gap makes it impossible to verify the account as literal history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apics-online.info/surveys/36" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apics-online.info">[APiCS Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apics-online.info</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">APiCS OnlineAPiCS Online - Survey chapter: Angolar...</span></span></span>

As a result, historians increasingly treat the shipwreck as an important oral tradition rather than an established historical fact.

## Marronage offers a stronger historical explanation

An alternative explanation has gained increasing support among historians over the past several decades. Rather than descending primarily from one shipwreck, the Angolares may have emerged through **marronage**—the process by which enslaved people escaped plantations and created independent communities.

This interpretation fits what is already known about sixteenth- and seventeenth-century São Tomé. Sugar plantations depended on enslaved labour, yet the island's mountainous interior, dense rainforest and difficult terrain provided opportunities for escape. Colonial records describe repeated flights from plantations and frequent conflicts between Portuguese settlers and armed communities living beyond colonial control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://novaresearch.unl.pt/en/publications/do-ref%C3%BAgio-nos-picos-da-ilha-de-s%C3%A3o-tom%C3%A9-%C3%A0-absor%C3%A7%C3%A3o-colonial-a-qu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: novaresearch.unl.pt">[unl.pt]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">novaresearch.unl.pt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universidade NOVA de LisboaDo refúgio nos picos da ilha de São Tomé à absorção colonial: a questão dos Angolares - Universidade NOVA de L...</span></span></span>

Rather than appearing suddenly after a single dramatic event, the Angolares could therefore have formed gradually as escaped enslaved people:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* established hidden settlements in the southern mountains;
* absorbed later escapees from different plantations;
* developed their own political leadership;
* maintained relative independence for generations.

</div>

This model also explains why Portuguese authorities struggled to eliminate these communities despite repeated military campaigns. The Angolares occupied terrain that strongly favoured defensive resistance and knew the landscape far better than colonial forces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://novaresearch.unl.pt/en/publications/do-ref%C3%BAgio-nos-picos-da-ilha-de-s%C3%A3o-tom%C3%A9-%C3%A0-absor%C3%A7%C3%A3o-colonial-a-qu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: novaresearch.unl.pt">[Universidade NOVA de Lisboa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">novaresearch.unl.pt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universidade NOVA de LisboaDo refúgio nos picos da ilha de São Tomé à absorção colonial: a questão dos Angolares - Universidade NOVA de L...</span></span></span>

## What archaeology and language may reveal

Direct archaeological evidence remains limited because relatively little excavation has been carried out in the areas traditionally associated with early Angolar settlements. Tropical conditions, heavy vegetation and centuries of later occupation have also made preserving and locating early sites difficult. Consequently, archaeology has not yet produced decisive proof for either the shipwreck story or the marronage model.

Language, however, provides important clues.

The Angolar language is a Portuguese-based Creole that differs noticeably from Santome, the larger Creole language spoken elsewhere on the island. Linguists argue that Angolar probably developed from an early form of Santome that was carried into isolated maroon communities by escaped enslaved people. Over time, prolonged isolation and continued influence from speakers originating in west-central Africa produced a distinct language rather than one imported directly from a single shipload of captives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=P00033647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AfricaBib &#124; Linguistic, Historical, and Ethnographic Evidence on the Formation of the Angolares, a Maroon-Descendent Community i...</span></span></span>

This linguistic pattern is easier to reconcile with a growing maroon population than with a single isolated shipwreck. If the community had originated almost entirely from one wrecked vessel, researchers might expect a different linguistic history than the one reconstructed from comparative Creole studies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=P00033647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AfricaBib &#124; Linguistic, Historical, and Ethnographic Evidence on the Formation of the Angolares, a Maroon-Descendent Community i...</span></span></span>

## Genetics adds another piece to the puzzle

Recent genomic research has added a new line of evidence.

Modern DNA studies show that the Angolares are genetically distinct from other populations on São Tomé, reflecting centuries of relative isolation. At the same time, they share broadly similar African ancestral components with other island communities, rather than displaying the genetic signature expected from descent from one exceptionally isolated group of shipwreck survivors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8229774/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2021</span></span></span>

Researchers instead propose a more complex process of ethnogenesis—the formation of a new people—in which:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* escaped enslaved people established maroon communities;
* leadership may have been dominated by one or several men originating from Angola;
* isolation strengthened a separate cultural identity;
* later generations became an endogamous community with distinctive traditions and language.

</div>

In this interpretation, an Angolan connection remains real, but it reflects leadership, migration and cultural influence rather than requiring a single catastrophic shipwreck.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8229774/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 28, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 28, 2021</span></span></span>

## The evidence gaps around Angolares
The debate continues because each type of evidence has limitations.

Oral tradition preserves memories that written colonial archives often ignored or deliberately omitted. Conversely, oral histories may condense generations of gradual change into a single memorable founding event. Portuguese records are incomplete and naturally focus on colonial officials rather than communities living outside their authority. Archaeology has not yet uncovered decisive early settlement evidence, while genetics can reconstruct broad population history but cannot identify one specific historical event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://novaresearch.unl.pt/en/publications/do-ref%C3%BAgio-nos-picos-da-ilha-de-s%C3%A3o-tom%C3%A9-%C3%A0-absor%C3%A7%C3%A3o-colonial-a-qu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: novaresearch.unl.pt">[unl.pt]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">novaresearch.unl.pt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universidade NOVA de LisboaDo refúgio nos picos da ilha de São Tomé à absorção colonial: a questão dos Angolares - Universidade NOVA de L...</span></span></span>

Most specialists therefore no longer frame the question as a simple choice between "shipwreck" and "escape". It is entirely possible that the famous legend preserves a distant memory of maritime arrivals while the Angolares' enduring identity emerged mainly through centuries of marronage, resistance and community-building in the island's interior. ## Why the debate matters

The question of Angolar origins is more than an academic puzzle. It shapes how São Tomé and Príncipe understands slavery, resistance and national identity.

If the Angolares primarily descend from escaped enslaved people, they represent one of the Atlantic world's longest-lasting maroon societies—a community that successfully resisted plantation slavery for centuries before eventual incorporation into colonial society. If elements of the shipwreck tradition also preserve historical memory, they illustrate how communities transformed traumatic experiences into shared identity across generations.

Either way, the Angolares stand as evidence that the history of São Tomé cannot be understood solely through plantation records. Their origins remind historians that some of the most important chapters of the Atlantic slave system were written not only in colonial archives but also in oral traditions, isolated landscapes and the enduring survival of communities that refused to remain under colonial control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://novaresearch.unl.pt/en/publications/do-ref%C3%BAgio-nos-picos-da-ilha-de-s%C3%A3o-tom%C3%A9-%C3%A0-absor%C3%A7%C3%A3o-colonial-a-qu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: novaresearch.unl.pt">[unl.pt]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">novaresearch.unl.pt</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universidade NOVA de LisboaDo refúgio nos picos da ilha de São Tomé à absorção colonial: a questão dos Angolares - Universidade NOVA de L...</span></span></span>
