## Introduction

The first reports from Timbuktu in January 2013 suggested a cultural catastrophe: militants retreating from the city had burned its famous manuscript collections. Those headlines were only partly true. Thousands of priceless manuscripts were indeed destroyed, stolen or damaged, but the much larger mystery is why the loss turned out to be far smaller than feared. The answer lies in an extraordinary rescue operation carried out in secret by librarians, manuscript-owning families, drivers, boatmen and ordinary residents during the 2012–13 occupation. By the time the fires were lit, most of the manuscripts that the militants hoped to destroy had already disappeared from the city, hidden in metal trunks and smuggled south over many months. The survival of this written heritage has become one of Mali's most remarkable modern historical stories, although the exact number of manuscripts saved, lost and still at risk remains debated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/damage-timbuktus-cultural-heritage-worse-first-estimated-reports-unesco-mission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimatedJune 7, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 7, 2013</span></span></span>

## Were Timbuktu's manuscripts really destroyed?

The short answer is both yes and no.

When Islamist fighters abandoned Timbuktu in January 2013 ahead of advancing French and Malian forces, they set fire to parts of the Ahmed Baba Institute, one of the city's best-known manuscript centres. Around 4,200 manuscripts held there were burned or disappeared during the attack. The destruction was real, and UNESCO later confirmed that the damage to the city's wider cultural heritage had initially been underestimated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/damage-timbuktus-cultural-heritage-worse-first-estimated-reports-unesco-mission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimatedJune 7, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 7, 2013</span></span></span>

What proved misleading were the first claims that Timbuktu's manuscript heritage as a whole had been wiped out. For generations, the city's manuscripts had not been concentrated in a single state library. Instead, hundreds of thousands of documents were dispersed among dozens of private family libraries, where they had often been preserved for centuries. This decentralised tradition unexpectedly became an advantage during the occupation because there was no single collection for the occupiers to seize.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/timbuktu/manuscripts.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csmc.uni-hamburg.de">[CSMCR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csmc.uni-hamburg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-hamburg.de.</span></span></span>

## What was burned, stolen or damaged?

The losses were significant even if they were far smaller than first feared.

Among the best-established losses were:

* Approximately 4,200 manuscripts at the Ahmed Baba Institute that were burned or otherwise lost during the retreat of Islamist forces.
* Damage to conservation facilities and research infrastructure, making future preservation more difficult.
* Theft of some manuscripts and cultural objects whose whereabouts remain uncertain.
* Subsequent deterioration of rescued manuscripts caused by hurried transport and storage in Bamako's much more humid climate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/damage-timbuktus-cultural-heritage-worse-first-estimated-reports-unesco-mission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[unesco.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimatedJune 7, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 7, 2013</span></span></span>

The manuscripts themselves covered far more than religion. Many dealt with astronomy, mathematics, medicine, law, commerce, poetry and local history, demonstrating that medieval West Africa produced a broad written scholarly tradition. Losing even a few thousand therefore represented the disappearance of unique historical evidence rather than duplicate copies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/timbuktu/manuscripts.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csmc.uni-hamburg.de">[CSMCR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csmc.uni-hamburg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-hamburg.de.</span></span></span>

## How residents smuggled collections out of Timbuktu

The rescue was not a single dramatic escape but an organised operation lasting roughly eight months.

The central organiser was librarian and manuscript specialist Abdel Kader Haidara, working through the local organisation SAVAMA-DCI together with numerous family librarians and volunteers. Once occupation began, manuscripts were quietly removed from visible libraries and hidden inside ordinary metal trunks that resembled household storage boxes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/140421-haidara-timbuktu-manuscripts-mali-library-conservation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationalgeographic.com.</span></span></span>

Moving them required constant improvisation. Couriers used donkey carts to reach discreet loading points, travelled by canoe along the Niger River when roads became dangerous, and transported trunks in small vehicles rather than conspicuous convoys. Routes were frequently changed to avoid attracting attention at checkpoints, and many participants accepted considerable personal risk if discovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/timbuktu/manuscripts.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csmc.uni-hamburg.de">[CSMCR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csmc.uni-hamburg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-hamburg.de.</span></span></span>

The rescue also depended on ordinary residents rather than professional archivists alone. Families hid manuscripts in private homes, trusted neighbours temporarily stored collections, drivers transported sealed trunks without advertising their contents, and local library custodians coordinated movements between dozens of collections. The operation's success came from distributing responsibility widely enough that no single arrest or discovery could expose the entire network.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/140421-haidara-timbuktu-manuscripts-mali-library-conservation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[nationalgeographic.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationalgeographic.com.</span></span></span>

By early 2013, the overwhelming majority of manuscripts targeted for evacuation had already reached Bamako or other safer locations before militants burned the Ahmed Baba Institute. The fire therefore destroyed mainly the manuscripts that remained there rather than the city's entire documentary heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/140421-haidara-timbuktu-manuscripts-mali-library-conservation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationalgeographic.com.</span></span></span>

## Why different reports give very different totals

One reason this episode still confuses readers is that several different numbers are all correct within different contexts.

The main figures commonly encountered include:

* **Around 4,200 manuscripts lost** – referring largely to the Ahmed Baba Institute manuscripts burned or stolen during the militants' retreat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/damage-timbuktus-cultural-heritage-worse-first-estimated-reports-unesco-mission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimatedJune 7, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 7, 2013</span></span></span>
* **Around 285,000 manuscripts evacuated** – an estimate used by preservation specialists describing the organised rescue operation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/timbuktu/manuscripts.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csmc.uni-hamburg.de">[CSMCR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csmc.uni-hamburg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-hamburg.de.</span></span></span>
* **Around 300,000 manuscripts moved to safety** – UNESCO's estimate for manuscripts exfiltrated from Timbuktu.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/damage-timbuktus-cultural-heritage-worse-first-estimated-reports-unesco-mission" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Damage to Timbuktu’s cultural heritage worse than first estimatedJune 7, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 7, 2013</span></span></span>
* **Around 350,000 manuscripts saved** – a broader figure appearing in some later accounts that includes multiple collections and estimates of the city's total manuscript heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/aussenpolitik/laenderinformationen/mali-node/130225-handschriften-253738" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: auswaertiges-amt.de">[Auswärtiges Amt]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">auswaertiges-amt.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Auswärtiges Amt Rescuing cultural treasures in Mali</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Auswärtiges AmtRescuing cultural treasures in Mali - Federal Foreign Office...</span></span></span>

These numbers differ because authors are counting different things. Some refer only to the Ahmed Baba Institute. Others include dozens of family libraries. Some count individual manuscript volumes, while others estimate the total holdings of Timbuktu's manuscript tradition. In addition, many collections were privately owned, meaning no single complete catalogue existed before the occupation. As a result, precise totals remain difficult to verify even after the rescue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/timbuktu/manuscripts.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csmc.uni-hamburg.de">[CSMCR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csmc.uni-hamburg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-hamburg.de.</span></span></span>

## Why the rescue still matters

The survival of Timbuktu's manuscripts changed how the 2012–13 crisis is remembered. Instead of becoming solely a story of cultural destruction, it also became one of organised civilian resistance through preservation.

The manuscripts continue to require conservation because many were moved rapidly into environments less suitable than the dry Sahara. Digitisation and restoration projects remain important to reduce future risks from conflict, theft and environmental damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/timbuktu/manuscripts.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csmc.uni-hamburg.de">[uni-hamburg.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csmc.uni-hamburg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uni-hamburg.de.</span></span></span>

The deeper historical lesson is that Timbuktu's manuscript tradition survived not because it was protected by a single grand library, but because generations of local families had preserved collections in dispersed private libraries. That long-standing pattern, developed centuries before the occupation, unexpectedly became the reason that most of one of Africa's greatest written archives escaped destruction.
