## Introduction

[Liechtenstein]({{ 'which-liechtenstein-mysteries-still-defy/' | relative_url }})'s witch persecutions are not mysterious because historians doubt that they happened. The mystery lies in how much of the story can never be fully reconstructed. Between the late sixteenth century and the early 1680s, the territories of Vaduz and Schellenberg experienced repeated waves of witch trials that were among the most intense in Europe relative to their small population. Yet crucial court records were later removed, destroyed or lost, leaving historians unable to establish a complete list of victims or an exact death toll. At the same time, one remarkable survivor, Maria Eberle, challenged the legality of the prosecutions and helped bring the final trials to an end. Her case illuminates both the brutality of the persecutions and the reasons why the surviving archive is so incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

## The known waves of persecution and execution

The surviving evidence shows that witch hunting in the territories that later became Liechtenstein occurred in several distinct phases rather than as one continuous campaign.

The first securely documented prosecutions took place in Vaduz in 1597, 1598 and 1600. These trials resulted in at least eleven known executions by burning. Whether similar prosecutions occurred immediately before or after these years remains uncertain because the documentary record is incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

A much larger outbreak followed during the middle of the seventeenth century, particularly between 1648 and 1651. Historical research indicates that this period alone may have claimed around one hundred lives, making it one of the deadliest episodes in the region's history. Precise figures remain impossible because many records no longer survive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

The final and best-documented campaign came in 1679 and 1680. Contemporary records allow historians to identify approximately 45 executions during these two years. Unlike the common stereotype that witch hunts targeted only women, this final phase involved many male victims. Around three-quarters of those executed in 1679 were men, while women again formed the majority in 1680, reflecting changing patterns of accusation rather than a simple gender narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

Relative to the small population of Vaduz and Schellenberg, these persecutions ranked among the most severe in Europe. Historians argue that local government structures played an important role. The territories lacked some of the administrative checks found in larger neighbouring states, making it easier for local officials to pursue prosecutions with limited outside oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

## Maria Eberle's escape and challenge to the trials

The turning point came through the actions of Maria Eberle of Planken, one of the few individuals whose personal story can be reconstructed in detail.

Her family had already suffered heavily. Both her grandfather and an aunt had previously been executed for alleged witchcraft, leaving the family under lasting suspicion. In November 1680, Maria herself was arrested, imprisoned in Vaduz Castle and subjected to torture. Under torture she confessed, as many accused people did, and legal advisers recommended that she be executed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Eberle_%28Eberlin%29%2C_Maria" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Eberle (Eberlin), Maria – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein Lexicon Eberle (Eberlin), Maria – Historisches Lexikon</span></span></span>

Instead, she carried out one of the most extraordinary escapes associated with any Central European witch trial. According to surviving accounts, she crawled through an opening in a stove, reached the roof space of the castle, removed roof tiles and descended outside using knotted bed linen before fleeing across the border to Feldkirch. There she obtained the assistance of a notary and formally protested the legality of the proceedings against her.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.historisches-lexikon.li/maria-eberle-und-das-ende-der-hexenverfolgungen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.historisches-lexikon.li">[Historisches Lexikon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on historisches-lexikon.li.</span></span></span>

Her challenge did not stop there. Together with four other refugees and the priest Valentin von Kriss, she appealed directly to Emperor Leopold I. The imperial authorities responded by appointing an investigation into the conduct of the trials. The emperor halted further proceedings in 1681, and in 1685 the judgments from the 1679–1680 prosecutions were declared invalid. Maria's confiscated property was ordered to be returned after her death sentence was overturned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.historisches-lexikon.li/maria-eberle-und-das-ende-der-hexenverfolgungen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.historisches-lexikon.li">[historisches-lexikon.li]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on historisches-lexikon.li.</span></span></span>

Although many earlier victims could not be saved, Maria Eberle's intervention is widely regarded as the decisive event that ended organised witch prosecutions in Vaduz and Schellenberg.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Eberle_%28Eberlin%29%2C_Maria" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Eberle (Eberlin), Maria – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein Lexicon Eberle (Eberlin), Maria – Historisches Lexikon</span></span></span>

## Why pages disappeared and death totals remain uncertain

The greatest historical puzzle is not whether the trials occurred but why the documentary record became so fragmentary.

Researchers have established that pages were deliberately removed from official court books and that additional trial documents disappeared over time. This means historians possess enough material to identify major waves of persecution and many individual victims, but not enough to reconstruct every prosecution or produce an exact total of those executed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

Several explanations have been proposed for the missing records.


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Deliberate removal after the trials ended.** Officials or later administrators may have wished to erase evidence of judicial abuses once imperial investigators declared the prosecutions unlawful.
* **Loss through normal archival history.** Court registers from the seventeenth century often suffered damage, neglect or dispersal over centuries of administrative change.
* **Selective survival.** Some files remained because they were connected to appeals, property disputes or imperial investigations, while routine proceedings vanished.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

</div>

No surviving evidence proves a single coordinated effort to destroy every record. Instead, historians see a combination of deliberate removal and ordinary archival loss, making it impossible to distinguish confidently between all missing documents and intentional destruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

This uncertainty explains why published estimates differ. Around 45 executions during 1679–1680 are relatively well documented, while broader estimates for all the persecutions range to roughly 200 documented death sentences across the various waves. The exact total, however, cannot now be established because portions of the archive have disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

## Why the missing records still matter

The vanished files affect more than statistics. Every missing page represents people whose identities, accusations or final days may never be recovered.

For historians, the incomplete archive makes it difficult to answer basic questions. Were some villages targeted more heavily than others? How many accusations collapsed before reaching execution? Did economic motives, including the confiscation of property from convicted people, play a larger role than surviving documents reveal? Existing evidence suggests that confiscated assets formed part of the financial background to the later prosecutions, but the missing records prevent a complete assessment of how consistently this influenced decision-making.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>

The surviving material also demonstrates how fragile historical memory can be. Without Maria Eberle's successful appeal and the imperial investigation it triggered, even more documentation might have disappeared. Ironically, the legal challenge that helped end the persecutions also created many of the records that now allow historians to reconstruct their final phase.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Eberle_%28Eberlin%29%2C_Maria" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Eberle (Eberlin), Maria – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein Lexicon Eberle (Eberlin), Maria – Historisches Lexikon</span></span></span>

Today, the witch trials stand as one of Liechtenstein's most important archival mysteries. The broad outline is well established: repeated persecutions, widespread use of torture, unlawful convictions and eventual imperial intervention. What remains unresolved is the full human cost. Because key records vanished, the exact number of victims and the complete story of many of those accused are likely to remain permanently beyond recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historisches-lexikon.li/Hexenverfolgung" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historisches-lexikon.li">[Liechtenstein Lexicon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historisches-lexikon.li</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liechtenstein Lexicon Hexenverfolgung – Historisches Lexikon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Liechtenstein LexiconHexenverfolgung – Historisches LexikonDecember 31, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 2011</span></span></span>
