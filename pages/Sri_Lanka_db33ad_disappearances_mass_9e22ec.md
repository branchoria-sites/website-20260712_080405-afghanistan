## Introduction

Why are so many Sri Lankans still missing? The short answer is that disappearances occurred across several different periods of political violence, often in circumstances where arrests were undocumented, witnesses were intimidated, records were destroyed or never created, and investigations remained incomplete. The result is not one unresolved mystery but a series of overlapping historical puzzles affecting Sinhalese, Tamil and Muslim families in different parts of the country. While many deaths are no longer seriously disputed, the identity of many victims, the location of burial sites, the chain of command behind disappearances, and the fate of thousands of individuals remain unknown. The continuing discovery of mass graves has kept these questions alive, making the search for truth one of the most important unresolved chapters in modern Sri Lankan history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/sri-lanka/report-accountability-enforced-disappearances-sri-lanka-may2024-en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ohchr.org">[OHCHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ohchr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report accountability enforced disappearances sri lanka may2024 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACCOUNTABILITYAugust 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2024</span></span></span>

## The major waves of disappearance since 1971

[Sri Lanka]({{ 'what-really-lies-behind-sri-lankas/' | relative_url }})'s disappearances did not occur during a single conflict. Instead, they emerged during successive periods of insurgency, civil war and political repression.

The first major wave followed the suppression of the 1971 Janatha Vimukthi Peramuna (JVP) uprising. Thousands of mostly young Sinhalese men were reportedly arrested and never seen again. Emergency regulations and suspended legal safeguards made it difficult for families to trace detainees or challenge their detention. Later investigations acknowledged widespread disappearances, although many individual cases were never resolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/sri-lanka/report-accountability-enforced-disappearances-sri-lanka-may2024-en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ohchr.org">[OHCHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ohchr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report accountability enforced disappearances sri lanka may2024 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACCOUNTABILITYAugust 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2024</span></span></span>

A second, much larger pattern developed after the outbreak of the civil war in 1983. In the north and east, many Tamils disappeared after arrest by security forces, during military operations or at checkpoints. Others were abducted by armed groups, including the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE), which was itself responsible for disappearances, forced recruitment and killings. Responsibility therefore varied by place and period rather than resting with a single actor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/sri-lanka/report-accountability-enforced-disappearances-sri-lanka-may2024-en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ohchr.org">[OHCHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ohchr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report accountability enforced disappearances sri lanka may2024 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACCOUNTABILITYAugust 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2024</span></span></span>

The late 1980s brought another major wave during the second JVP insurgency in southern Sri Lanka. Thousands of Sinhalese suspected of supporting the insurgency disappeared amid counter-insurgency operations. Presidential commissions established in the 1990s documented many of these cases while also recording abuses committed by insurgent groups themselves, although on a smaller scale.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/sri-lanka/report-accountability-enforced-disappearances-sri-lanka-may2024-en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ohchr.org">[OHCHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ohchr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report accountability enforced disappearances sri lanka may2024 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACCOUNTABILITYAugust 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2024</span></span></span>

Disappearances continued through the 1990s and intensified again during the final years of the civil war between 2006 and 2009. Families continue to seek answers about civilians, surrendered combatants, humanitarian workers and others last seen in military custody or conflict zones. The exact number of missing persons remains disputed because different commissions, organisations and databases use different definitions and periods, but all agree that the total runs into many thousands.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/sri-lanka/report-accountability-enforced-disappearances-sri-lanka-may2024-en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ohchr.org">[OHCHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ohchr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report accountability enforced disappearances sri lanka may2024 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACCOUNTABILITYAugust 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2024</span></span></span>

## What can mass graves and forensic investigations actually establish?

Mass graves have become some of the most tangible evidence in a history often marked by missing records and conflicting testimony. Yet even when graves are discovered, they rarely provide immediate answers.

Archaeologists and forensic specialists can determine whether remains were buried individually or together, estimate age and sex, identify injuries, recover clothing or personal possessions, and sometimes estimate when burial occurred. DNA testing may allow identification if suitable family reference samples exist. None of these steps automatically identifies who carried out the killings or under whose authority they occurred.

Several grave sites have become nationally significant. Among the best known are:

* **Chemmani near Jaffna**, first brought to public attention after testimony in the late 1990s and the subject of renewed excavations. Recent discoveries have uncovered large numbers of human remains, including children, renewing calls for comprehensive forensic analysis and identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/d570beb78890405c73bd0ee75c059e69" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Personal items such as a baby bottle, children’s clothing, and toys were also found, raising suspicions that the site contains bodies of...</span></span></span>
* **Mannar**, where hundreds of skeletal remains were uncovered during excavations beginning in 2018. Scientific dating suggested that many of the remains pre-dated the final phase of the civil war, illustrating why forensic evidence is essential before assigning responsibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/sri-lanka/report-accountability-enforced-disappearances-sri-lanka-may2024-en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ohchr.org">[OHCHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ohchr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report accountability enforced disappearances sri lanka may2024 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACCOUNTABILITYAugust 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2024</span></span></span>
* Other sites, including **Matale**, **Sooriyakanda** and several smaller burial locations, have been linked to different periods of political violence, especially the southern insurgencies of the late twentieth century. Their interpretation has often remained contested because of incomplete excavation, legal disputes or uncertainty about the date of burial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/sri-lanka/report-accountability-enforced-disappearances-sri-lanka-may2024-en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ohchr.org">[OHCHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ohchr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report accountability enforced disappearances sri lanka may2024 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACCOUNTABILITYAugust 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2024</span></span></span>

One important lesson from these excavations is that a mass grave is not, by itself, proof of a particular historical narrative. Establishing identity, date, cause of death and responsibility requires careful archaeological work, forensic science, documentary evidence and witness testimony.

## Why records and witnesses matter as much as graves

Many disappearances left no known burial place at all. In those cases, investigators rely on other forms of evidence.

Official detention registers, military and police records, hospital documents and court files may establish whether someone entered state custody. Witness testimony can reconstruct arrests, transfers or checkpoints where people were last seen. Human rights organisations have also preserved thousands of family statements that might otherwise have been lost.

These different forms of evidence become stronger when they reinforce one another. For example, if multiple witnesses describe the same arrest, surviving official records place security personnel in the area, and forensic evidence later confirms burials nearby, investigators gain a much clearer picture than any single source could provide alone.

International humanitarian organisations have long stressed the importance of maintaining detention records, identifying the dead and preserving burial information during armed conflict precisely because these records become crucial decades later when families seek answers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.icrc.org/en/document/protected-persons/missing-persons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icrc.org">[ICRC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icrc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Missing persons and international humanitarian law &#124; International Committee of the Red CrossOctober 29, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2010</span></span></span>

## Why identification remains so difficult

Even when remains are recovered, identifying individual victims is often a slow process.

Several obstacles repeatedly appear:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Many disappearances occurred decades ago, meaning DNA has deteriorated and close relatives may themselves have died.
* Contemporary records are frequently incomplete, inconsistent or missing altogether.
* Excavations require specialist forensic expertise, laboratories and sustained funding.
* Legal procedures can delay excavation, analysis and release of findings.
* Communities affected by violence sometimes distrust state institutions because earlier investigations produced few answers.

</div>

Dating also presents challenges. Scientific analysis can sometimes establish an approximate period of burial, but linking remains to a particular incident or missing person usually requires additional documentary and genetic evidence rather than archaeology alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/d570beb78890405c73bd0ee75c059e69" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Personal items such as a baby bottle, children’s clothing, and toys were also found, raising suspicions that the site contains bodies of...</span></span></span>

## The Office on Missing Persons and the search for answers

After decades of commissions of inquiry with limited long-term impact, Sri Lanka established the Office on Missing Persons (OMP) through legislation in 2016, becoming operational in 2018.

The OMP was created as a permanent independent institution responsible for clarifying the fate and whereabouts of missing people connected with conflict, political unrest and enforced disappearances. Its responsibilities include collecting existing records, building a central database, supporting searches, observing exhumations and helping protect the rights of relatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moj.gov.lk/index.php/en/departments-institutes/institutions/office-on-missing-persons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moj.gov.lk">[moj.gov.lk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moj.gov.lk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office on Missing Persons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office on Missing Persons</span></span></span>

The institution has reported receiving more than 21,000 complaints, although duplicate cases and reports relating to missing military personnel require further verification before active investigations proceed. The existence of such a database is significant because previous commissions often collected evidence without creating a lasting national system for future investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://moj.gov.lk/index.php/en/departments-institutes/institutions/office-on-missing-persons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moj.gov.lk">[moj.gov.lk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moj.gov.lk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office on Missing Persons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 8, 2026</span></span></span>

Supporters view the OMP as an essential step towards establishing facts for families regardless of political affiliation. Critics argue that progress has been too slow and that many families still lack concrete answers or accountability after decades of waiting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.moj.gov.lk/index.php/en/departments-institutes/institutions/office-on-missing-persons" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: moj.gov.lk">[moj.gov.lk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">moj.gov.lk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office on Missing Persons</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office on Missing Persons</span></span></span>

## Why accountability and public trust remain contested

The search for truth is about more than identifying bones or locating graves. Families often seek acknowledgement of what happened, official recognition of responsibility where evidence supports it, and confidence that investigations are impartial.

Political disagreement has complicated this process. Some groups emphasise crimes committed by state security forces; others stress abuses committed by the LTTE and other armed organisations. International human rights bodies have argued that all credible allegations should be investigated regardless of the identity of the suspected perpetrators, while Sri Lankan governments have varied in how they have approached accountability mechanisms over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/hrbodies/hrcouncil/sri-lanka/report-accountability-enforced-disappearances-sri-lanka-may2024-en.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ohchr.org">[OHCHR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ohchr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report accountability enforced disappearances sri lanka may2024 en</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACCOUNTABILITYAugust 22, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2024</span></span></span>

The discovery of new grave sites periodically reopens these debates because each excavation has the potential either to confirm long-standing allegations or to challenge assumptions through new forensic evidence. Careful scientific investigation therefore serves both historical accuracy and public confidence.

## Why this remains one of Sri Lanka's greatest unresolved historical questions

Unlike legendary mysteries, Sri Lanka's disappearances concern real people whose relatives are still searching for answers. The uncertainty lies less in whether disappearances occurred than in determining the fate of specific individuals, identifying those recovered from graves, reconstructing chains of responsibility and creating a historical record accepted across divided communities.

Every newly excavated grave, newly identified victim or newly recovered document reduces part of that uncertainty. Yet thousands of cases remain unresolved, ensuring that disappearances and mass graves continue to shape Sri Lanka's understanding of its recent past. The enduring mystery is therefore not whether violence occurred, but whether enough evidence can still be assembled to establish the truth for every family still waiting.
