## Introduction

The disappearances of Guðmundur Einarsson and Geirfinnur Einarsson in 1974 remain one of [Iceland]({{ 'what-are-icelands-greatest-unresolved/' | relative_url }})'s greatest unresolved historical mysteries because two separate missing-person cases eventually produced murder convictions without bodies, a confirmed crime scene, forensic evidence or reliable eyewitness testimony. Decades later, those convictions were largely overturned after investigators, psychologists and courts concluded that the confessions on which the case depended had been obtained under deeply flawed conditions. Yet the central question has never been answered: what actually happened to the two missing men? The case therefore stands as both a genuine disappearance mystery and one of the world's most studied examples of wrongful conviction based on false confession.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7937609/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## The Two Disappearances

The mystery begins with two unrelated disappearances separated by almost ten months.

In the early hours of 27 January 1974, 18-year-old Guðmundur Einarsson was last seen after leaving a dance in Hafnarfjörður. He faced a long walk home during severe winter weather. Despite extensive searches, neither he nor his remains have ever been found. Whether he died accidentally from exposure, became the victim of violence, or disappeared for another reason remains unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%B0mundur_and_Geirfinnur_case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guðmundur and Geirfinnur case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guðmundur and Geirfinnur case</span></span></span>

On 19 November 1974, 32-year-old Geirfinnur Einarsson disappeared under very different circumstances. After receiving a telephone call at home, he drove to the harbour area in Keflavík for what appeared to be a planned meeting. His vehicle was later discovered parked near the harbour café where he had intended to meet someone, but Geirfinnur himself vanished without trace. His unexplained phone call and abandoned car have fuelled decades of speculation, yet no definitive evidence has established what happened afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%B0mundur_and_Geirfinnur_case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guðmundur and Geirfinnur case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guðmundur and Geirfinnur case</span></span></span>

Although the disappearances occurred in different locations and under different circumstances, investigators eventually came to believe they were connected. That assumption became the foundation for a criminal investigation that would later collapse under scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7937609/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why Were People Convicted Without Bodies or Evidence?

The investigation changed dramatically after police focused on a group of young people connected through Reykjavík's social scene. Rumours, investigative theories and growing public pressure gradually replaced direct evidence. By the late 1970s six individuals had confessed, in varying ways, to involvement in one or both alleged murders.

The remarkable feature of the case is what was missing:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no bodies;
* no confirmed murder weapon;
* no verified crime scene;
* no forensic evidence linking the suspects to homicide;
* no consistent eyewitness account of either killing.

</div>

Instead, prosecutors relied primarily on confessions that frequently contradicted one another. Suspects repeatedly altered important details such as where the killings supposedly occurred, who was present and how the bodies had been disposed of. Searches based on these accounts consistently failed to locate human remains or corroborating evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7937609/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

Nevertheless, Iceland's Supreme Court convicted six people in 1980. The sentences ranged from one year for alleged disposal of a body to seventeen years' imprisonment for the principal murder convictions. At the time, the courts accepted that the confessions outweighed the absence of physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7937609/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## How Confessions Became Unreliable

Subsequent investigations transformed understanding of the case by examining how the confessions had been obtained.

Several suspects spent extraordinarily long periods in solitary confinement while undergoing repeated interrogations. Interviews often continued for months, with investigators repeatedly confronting suspects with evolving theories and encouraging them to reconstruct events they increasingly struggled to remember independently. Later official reviews criticised prolonged isolation, intense psychological pressure and serious investigative failures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332415031_Out_of_the_Icelandic_cold_murder_they_wrote_The_Gudmundur_and_Geirfinnur_criminal_case_of_1974" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Out of the Icelandic cold murder they wrote: The Guðmundur and Geirfinnur criminal case of 1974...</span></span></span>

Psychologist Gísli H. Guðjónsson used the case to develop the concept of **memory distrust syndrome**. The idea describes a situation in which vulnerable individuals lose confidence in their own recollections after sustained questioning and begin relying instead on suggestions from interrogators. Rather than deliberately inventing a story, some suspects may gradually come to believe that uncertain or imagined events genuinely occurred. The Guðmundur and Geirfinnur investigation became one of the best-known real-world examples discussed in research on false confessions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781119315636.ch18" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[wiley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Online Library Albert Skaftason&#x27;s Memory Distrust Syndrome and Confession</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryAlbert Skaftason&#x27;s Memory Distrust Syndrome and Confession - The Psychology of False Confessions - Wiley Online Libra...</span></span></span>

Researchers have also noted that different suspects produced incompatible narratives despite apparently confessing to the same crimes. Instead of strengthening the prosecution's case, these contradictions later became evidence that the statements were unreliable products of the interrogation process rather than independent recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7937609/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why the Convictions Were Overturned

Pressure for a re-examination grew over many years through investigative journalism, academic criticism and official inquiries.

An independent review commissioned by the Icelandic authorities identified serious shortcomings in the original investigation. The findings contributed to decisions by the Case Review Commission and prosecutors to seek new hearings before the Supreme Court.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332415031_Out_of_the_Icelandic_cold_murder_they_wrote_The_Gudmundur_and_Geirfinnur_criminal_case_of_1974" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Out of the Icelandic cold murder they wrote: The Guðmundur and Geirfinnur criminal case of 1974...</span></span></span>

In September 2018, Iceland's Supreme Court acquitted the five men whose convictions related directly to the alleged murders of Guðmundur and Geirfinnur. The court concluded that the evidence supporting the convictions was fundamentally unreliable. One related conviction for perjury involving Erla Bolladóttir was not quashed, leaving that aspect of the case legally distinct and still controversial among legal scholars and campaigners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7937609/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

The Icelandic government later apologised to the acquitted men, and compensation was awarded to those wrongfully convicted or their families. The case is now widely regarded as Iceland's most significant miscarriage of justice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7937609/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 22, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2021</span></span></span>

## What Remains Unsolved?

The legal collapse of the convictions did not solve either disappearance.

For Guðmundur Einarsson, accidental death remains one plausible explanation given the winter conditions and his long journey on foot. However, no physical evidence has ever confirmed this, and his body has never been recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%B0mundur_and_Geirfinnur_case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guðmundur and Geirfinnur case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guðmundur and Geirfinnur case</span></span></span>

Geirfinnur Einarsson's disappearance presents a different puzzle. His mysterious telephone call, his apparent appointment at the harbour and the abandonment of his car suggest that he expected to meet someone. Whether that meeting involved criminal activity, personal business or something entirely unrelated remains unknown. Numerous tips have emerged over the decades, but none has produced verifiable evidence explaining his fate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%B0mundur_and_Geirfinnur_case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Guðmundur and Geirfinnur case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Guðmundur and Geirfinnur case</span></span></span>

An equally important unanswered question is whether the two disappearances were connected at all. The original investigation assumed they were, but later reviews found little objective evidence supporting that conclusion. ## Why the Case Still Matters

The 1974 disappearances occupy a unique place in Icelandic history because they combine two enduring mysteries in one story.

The first mystery concerns the missing men themselves. Nearly half a century later, neither Guðmundur nor Geirfinnur has been found, and no definitive account of their fate has emerged.

The second concerns the justice system. The investigation has become an international case study in how prolonged isolation, suggestive interviewing and overreliance on confession evidence can produce wrongful convictions even in the absence of supporting physical proof. It is frequently discussed in legal and psychological research alongside broader debates about interrogation methods, memory and safeguards against miscarriages of justice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781119315636.ch18" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[wiley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Online Library Albert Skaftason&#x27;s Memory Distrust Syndrome and Confession</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryAlbert Skaftason&#x27;s Memory Distrust Syndrome and Confession - The Psychology of False Confessions - Wiley Online Libra...</span></span></span>
