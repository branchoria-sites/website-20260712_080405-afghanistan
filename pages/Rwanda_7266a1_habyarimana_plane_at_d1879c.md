## Introduction

The question of who shot down President Juvénal Habyarimana's aircraft on 6 April 1994 remains one of the most consequential unresolved events in modern African history. The Falcon 50 carrying Habyarimana, Burundian President Cyprien Ntaryamira and others was struck by surface-to-air missiles as it approached Kigali airport. Within hours, organised mass killings began across [Rwanda]({{ 'which-mysteries-still-haunt-rwandas/' | relative_url }}). While there is no serious dispute that the aircraft was deliberately destroyed, there is still no judicially proven answer to the central question: who fired the missiles, and who ordered the attack? More than three decades later, competing investigations have narrowed some possibilities but have not established a verified firing team, command chain or surviving written order. The mystery therefore lies not in whether the attack occurred, but in the identity of those responsible and why definitive proof has remained elusive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span></span></span>

## What happened on 6 April 1994

On the evening of 6 April 1994, the presidential Falcon 50 was returning from regional peace talks in Tanzania. As it made its final approach to Kigali International Airport, at least one surface-to-air missile struck the aircraft. It crashed inside the grounds of the presidential residence, killing everyone aboard.

The attack instantly transformed Rwanda's political crisis. Roadblocks appeared within hours, senior political opponents were targeted, and the genocide against the Tutsi began almost immediately. Although the chronology linking the missile attack and the outbreak of mass killing is firmly established, that sequence does not by itself identify those responsible for launching the missiles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span></span></span>

The aircraft itself offered only limited forensic evidence. Much of the crash scene was quickly overtaken by military operations and then by the violence engulfing the country. As a result, investigators have had to rely heavily on witness testimony, ballistic modelling, terrain analysis and later judicial inquiries rather than on an intact crime scene.

## Kanombe, Masaka and the launch-site dispute

One of the most important questions has been where the missiles were fired from, because the answer affects which groups could realistically have carried out the attack.

### Why Kanombe became the leading hypothesis

The most influential modern investigations have concluded that the most probable launch area lay around the Kanombe military camp and the nearby presidential compound. These conclusions emerged independently from the 2010 Rwandan Mutsinzi Commission, which relied in part on British ballistic specialists, and from French judicial experts appointed during Judge Marc Trévidic's investigation. Both studies used witness testimony together with topographical and ballistic reconstruction to argue that the geometry of the attack better matched positions near Kanombe than more distant alternatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span></span></span>

Kanombe was not simply another location on the map. In April 1994 it was a heavily protected military zone occupied by elite units of the Rwandan Armed Forces, close to both the presidential residence and Kigali airport. If the missiles originated there, investigators argue that launching them without the knowledge of at least some government security personnel would have been extremely difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.kas.de/en/web/rspssa/single-title/-/content/blickpunkt-afrika25" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kas.de">[Konrad Adenauer Foundation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kas.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Konrad Adenauer Foundation Focus on Africa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Konrad Adenauer FoundationFocus on Africa - Rule of Law Programme for Sub-Saharan Africa - Konrad-Adenauer-StiftungJanuary 24, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2012</span></span></span>

Numerous witnesses interviewed years later described seeing missile launches or hearing shots from the Kanombe area, although individual accounts differ in detail. The combination of these testimonies with ballistic analysis forms the principal basis for the Kanombe hypothesis rather than any single decisive witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.minubumwe.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/MINUBUMWE/Publications/BOOKS_ON_GENOCIDE_AGAINST_TUTSI/ENQUETE_SUR_LE_CRASH_DU_6_AVRIL_1994_DE_L_AVION_DE_L_ANCIEN_PRESIDENT_JUVENAL_HABYARIMANA.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: minubumwe.gov.rw">[National Unity Ministry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">minubumwe.gov.rw</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Unity MinistryCONFIDENTIELApril 15, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 15, 2026</span></span></span>

### Why Masaka was originally favoured

Earlier investigations placed greater emphasis on Masaka Hill, east of Kigali. This location became central to French Judge Jean-Louis Bruguière's inquiry, which argued that members of the Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF) had infiltrated government territory, fired the missiles from Masaka and escaped.

Over time, however, that reconstruction faced growing criticism. Later ballistic work questioned whether missile trajectories from Masaka matched the damage and flight path. Several witnesses whose testimony supported the Masaka theory later altered or qualified their statements, weakening the evidential basis for the original conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span></span></span>

Importantly, the shift towards Kanombe does not itself identify the attackers. A launch site and a firing team are separate questions.

## Why the competing accusations remain unproved

The debate has largely centred on two broad explanations, neither of which has been proved in court.

The first accuses the RPF, then the rebel movement led by Paul Kagame. Supporters of this theory argue that eliminating Habyarimana would have allowed the RPF to resume military operations and ultimately seize power. This interpretation influenced early French judicial proceedings and some subsequent investigations. However, critics argue that the evidential chain depends heavily on disputed witness testimony and assumptions about infiltration into a heavily guarded military area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span></span></span>

The second theory argues that Hutu extremists within the existing government or military ordered the attack because they opposed implementation of the Arusha Peace Agreement, which required power-sharing with the RPF. Under this interpretation, removing Habyarimana eliminated the last obstacle to a long-prepared campaign of mass violence while also allowing extremists to blame their opponents. The Kanombe launch-site findings are frequently cited as strengthening this possibility because the area was under government military control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justiceinfo.net/en/24149-190112-francerwanda-expert-report-casts-some-new-light-on-crash-that-sparked-genocide.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justiceinfo.net">[JusticeInfo.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justiceinfo.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Justice Info.net19.01.12</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">19.01.12 - FRANCE/RWANDA - EXPERT REPORT CASTS SOME NEW LIGHT ON CRASH THAT SPARKED GENOCIDE - JusticeInfo.net...</span></span></span>

Yet even this interpretation stops short of definitive proof. No authenticated operational order has been found. No verified firing team has been conclusively identified. No surviving participant has provided an account that has achieved broad acceptance across judicial investigations and historical scholarship.

## Why the evidence remains incomplete

Several factors have prevented the mystery from being conclusively resolved.

The first is the rapid collapse into genocide. The attack was immediately followed by nationwide violence that destroyed opportunities for a conventional criminal investigation.

Second, many potential witnesses were killed, fled Rwanda or later gave accounts influenced by trauma, political allegiance or the passage of time. Contradictory testimony has repeatedly complicated judicial inquiries.

Third, the physical evidence was limited. Although wreckage could be examined, investigators had no preserved missile launch position, no recovered firing system linked to identified operators and no complete forensic crime scene.

Finally, the case became deeply politicised. Because responsibility for the attack affects interpretations of both the genocide and the civil war, nearly every major investigation has been scrutinised for possible political bias. This has made agreement on the meaning of evidence almost as contentious as the evidence itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span></span></span>

## What historians regard as established

Although disagreement persists over responsibility, several conclusions command broad acceptance.


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The attack occurred during the aircraft's final approach to Kigali on 6 April 1994.
* The assassination immediately preceded organised mass killings on a national scale.
* Later ballistic investigations have generally favoured a launch area around Kanombe rather than Masaka, although this remains part of an ongoing historical debate.
* No court has conclusively established who fired the missiles or identified a verified chain of command behind the attack.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana and Cyprien Ntaryamira</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery still matters

The destruction of Habyarimana's aircraft remains central to understanding Rwanda's recent history because it sits at the intersection of military evidence, political responsibility and collective memory.

The identity of the attackers has implications far beyond one assassination. It shapes competing narratives about the final collapse of the Arusha peace process, the planning of the genocide, the conduct of the civil war and the legitimacy of rival political claims that continue to influence regional and international debate.

After decades of investigations by judges, commissions and technical experts, the available evidence has narrowed some possibilities—particularly regarding the probable launch area—but it has not produced the decisive proof needed to identify the firing team or those who gave the order. For that reason, who brought down Habyarimana's plane remains one of Rwanda's most significant unresolved historical mysteries.
