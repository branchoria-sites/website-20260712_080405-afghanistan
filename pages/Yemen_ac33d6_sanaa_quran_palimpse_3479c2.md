## Introduction

The Sana'a Qur'an palimpsest is one of the most important manuscript discoveries ever made in [Yemen]({{ 'which-mysteries-still-shape-yemens-past/' | relative_url }}) because it preserves two different layers of Qur'anic writing on the same parchment. The visible text was copied after an earlier version had been carefully erased, leaving traces of the original beneath. Rather than revealing a "hidden Qur'an" or overturning Islamic history, the manuscript offers something rarer: direct physical evidence of how some of the earliest Qur'anic manuscripts were copied, corrected, taught and preserved during the seventh century. It has become central to scholarly debates because it shows that the process of producing written Qur'ans involved human scribes working with manuscripts that sometimes contained small textual differences before later standardisation. At the same time, the manuscript demonstrates that the overall structure and content of the Qur'an were already remarkably stable at a very early date.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iis.ac.uk/publications-listing/the-sanaa-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iis.ac.uk">[The Institute of Ismaili Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iis.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Institute of Ismaili StudiesThe Sanaa Palimpsest &#124; The Institute of Ismaili StudiesJanuary 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>

## How the manuscript fragments were discovered

The story began in 1972 during restoration work at the Great Mosque of Sana'a, one of the oldest mosques in the Islamic world. Workers uncovered a concealed space above the mosque's ceiling containing thousands of damaged parchment and paper fragments. Many had become too worn for normal use and had apparently been stored rather than discarded.

Among this extraordinary cache was a manuscript now catalogued as DAM 01-27.1. Unlike ordinary manuscripts, it proved to be a palimpsest—a parchment whose original writing had been washed or scraped away so that it could be reused. Advances in ultraviolet photography and digital imaging made it possible to read much of the erased lower writing decades after the discovery, transforming what initially appeared to be a damaged manuscript into one of the earliest surviving witnesses to the history of the Qur'anic text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iis.ac.uk/publications-listing/the-sanaa-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iis.ac.uk">[The Institute of Ismaili Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iis.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Institute of Ismaili StudiesThe Sanaa Palimpsest &#124; The Institute of Ismaili StudiesJanuary 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>

The fragments remain incomplete. Scholars have identified around three dozen folios belonging to this particular manuscript, but many portions are missing. That incompleteness is one reason why interpretations continue to evolve as new imaging techniques recover additional readings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanaa_manuscript" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sanaa manuscript</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sanaa manuscript</span></span></span>

## What differs between the upper and lower texts?

The manuscript contains two separate layers.

* **The upper text** is the later writing that remains visible to the naked eye. It broadly conforms to the standard consonantal text associated with the Qur'anic tradition established under the Caliph Uthman.
* **The lower text** is the erased earlier version, preserved only faintly beneath the surface and recoverable through specialised imaging. It appears to belong to a very early stage in Qur'anic manuscript transmission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iis.ac.uk/publications-listing/the-sanaa-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iis.ac.uk">[The Institute of Ismaili Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iis.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Institute of Ismaili StudiesThe Sanaa Palimpsest &#124; The Institute of Ismaili StudiesJanuary 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>

The differences between the two texts are usually much smaller than popular accounts suggest. Scholars have identified variations including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* alternative spellings;
* different word choices with closely related meanings;
* omissions or additions of short words;
* occasional changes in word order;
* corrections made by the original scribe.

</div>

Importantly, the lower text contains the same surahs and the same general sequence found in the standard Qur'an. The variations occur within verses rather than representing an entirely different scripture. This distinction is crucial: the manuscript documents textual variation during early transmission, not the existence of a lost or radically different Qur'an.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://iqsaweb.org/2016/08/08/new-edition-of-the-%E1%B9%A3an%CA%BFa%CA%BE-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iqsaweb.org">[IQSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iqsaweb.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on iqsaweb.org.</span></span></span>

One particularly valuable feature is evidence of reading and teaching practices. The lower text includes what may be the oldest surviving reading instruction preserved in any Qur'anic manuscript, suggesting that these manuscripts were working copies used within circles of instruction rather than simply ceremonial books.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iis.ac.uk/publications-listing/the-sanaa-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iis.ac.uk">[The Institute of Ismaili Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iis.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Institute of Ismaili StudiesThe Sanaa Palimpsest &#124; The Institute of Ismaili StudiesJanuary 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>

## Why was the parchment erased and reused?

Modern readers often assume that erasing a sacred manuscript must indicate suppression or censorship. The evidence points to a far more ordinary explanation.

Parchment was expensive. Before paper became widespread, animal skins represented a valuable writing material, and manuscripts were sometimes erased and reused once they had become damaged, outdated or no longer suited their original purpose. Palimpsests are therefore known from many ancient and medieval cultures.

The Sana'a manuscript appears to fit this practical pattern. Scholars have suggested several possibilities:

* the earlier copy contained copying mistakes that were corrected by producing a new version;
* it represented a local working manuscript that was replaced once a more standard copy became available;
* it served as a teaching manuscript whose useful life had ended;
* the upper manuscript itself also shows signs of being unfinished or revised, suggesting that both layers may have been working documents rather than luxury copies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iis.ac.uk/publications-listing/the-sanaa-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iis.ac.uk">[The Institute of Ismaili Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iis.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Institute of Ismaili StudiesThe Sanaa Palimpsest &#124; The Institute of Ismaili StudiesJanuary 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>

No surviving evidence demonstrates a deliberate attempt to hide the lower text from history. The manuscript's preservation inside the mosque may instead reflect a long-standing tradition of respectfully storing worn religious manuscripts rather than destroying them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://independent.academia.edu/AsmaHilali" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: independent.academia.edu">[Academia.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">independent.academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Asma Hilali</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hilali - Independent Researcher...</span></span></span>

## Why scholars still debate what the palimpsest means

The manuscript has generated considerable scholarly discussion because its implications depend partly on how the lower text is interpreted.

One influential interpretation, associated with researchers such as Behnam Sadeghi and Mohsen Goudarzi, argues that the lower text preserves an early textual tradition that predates full standardisation under Uthman. From this perspective, the manuscript provides direct evidence that several closely related textual traditions circulated during Islam's earliest decades before one became dominant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://iqsaweb.org/2016/08/08/new-edition-of-the-%E1%B9%A3an%CA%BFa%CA%BE-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iqsaweb.org">[IQSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iqsaweb.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on iqsaweb.org.</span></span></span>

Other scholars, including Asma Hilali, place greater emphasis on the manuscript as evidence of scribal practice, teaching and manuscript production rather than treating it primarily as a witness to a separate textual tradition. They argue that both layers reveal manuscripts in active use, complete with corrections and evidence of ongoing work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iis.ac.uk/publications-listing/the-sanaa-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iis.ac.uk">[The Institute of Ismaili Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iis.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Institute of Ismaili StudiesThe Sanaa Palimpsest &#124; The Institute of Ismaili StudiesJanuary 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>

These interpretations are not entirely incompatible. Both agree that the manuscript preserves exceptionally early evidence for how the Qur'an was copied and transmitted. Their disagreement concerns what the surviving variations reveal about the earliest stages of textual history.

## What the manuscript ultimately reveals

The Sana'a Qur'an palimpsest remains mysterious not because its contents are unknowable, but because it offers a rare glimpse into a period from which very few manuscripts survive.

Its greatest contribution is that it replaces speculation with physical evidence. The manuscript demonstrates that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* early Qur'anic manuscripts could contain minor textual variations;
* scribes corrected, revised and reused manuscripts;
* written transmission and oral teaching operated together;
* the overall structure of the Qur'an was already firmly recognisable within the first century of Islam;
* standardisation was a historical process that can now be studied through surviving manuscripts rather than later literary accounts alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iis.ac.uk/publications-listing/the-sanaa-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iis.ac.uk">[iis.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iis.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Institute of Ismaili StudiesThe Sanaa Palimpsest &#124; The Institute of Ismaili StudiesJanuary 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>

</div>

Within Yemen's historical heritage, the palimpsest is therefore significant not because it solves every question about the Qur'an's earliest history, but because it preserves one of the few surviving artefacts that allows those questions to be investigated using contemporary manuscript evidence. It stands as one of the country's most important archaeological discoveries, illustrating how a damaged collection of forgotten parchment fragments became central to understanding the earliest history of one of the world's most influential texts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iis.ac.uk/publications-listing/the-sanaa-palimpsest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iis.ac.uk">[The Institute of Ismaili Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iis.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Institute of Ismaili StudiesThe Sanaa Palimpsest &#124; The Institute of Ismaili StudiesJanuary 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>
