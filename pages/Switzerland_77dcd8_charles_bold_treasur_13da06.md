## Introduction

The mystery surrounding Charles the Bold's treasure is not that it vanished overnight, but that it disappeared gradually into hundreds of different hands. After the Swiss Confederates defeated the Burgundian army at the Battle of Grandson on 2 March 1476, they captured one of the richest military camps ever seen in late medieval Europe. The booty included artillery, luxurious tents, jewellery, silver plate, ceremonial regalia, religious objects, textiles and personal possessions belonging to the Duke of Burgundy and his court. Yet instead of remaining intact as a royal treasury, the collection was rapidly divided, sold, melted down, altered or gifted away. Today, only scattered survivors can be identified with confidence, while the fate of countless famous objects remains uncertain. That mixture of well-documented capture and poorly documented dispersal is what makes the Burgundian treasure one of [Switzerland]({{ 'switzerland/' | relative_url }})'s enduring historical puzzles.

## What the Swiss captured at Grandson

Charles the Bold travelled with an exceptionally lavish military household. His field camp functioned as both headquarters and a travelling royal court, displaying the wealth and prestige of the Burgundian state. When panic spread through the Burgundian ranks at Grandson, much of the baggage train was abandoned almost intact, leaving the Confederates with an astonishing prize. Contemporary accounts and later chronicles describe the capture of:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Hundreds of cannon and firearms.
* Precious silver tableware and ceremonial vessels.
* Richly embroidered tents and banners.
* Jewels, pearls and gold ornaments.
* Fine clothing made from velvet, silk and cloth of gold.
* Religious relics and liturgical objects.
* Personal possessions associated with Charles himself.

</div>

The artillery proved militarily valuable in later campaigns, but luxury goods attracted even greater attention. Chroniclers described soldiers overwhelmed by the scale of the riches, and disputes quickly emerged over how the booty should be shared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/the-battle-of-grandson/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.nationalmuseum.ch">[Swiss History Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.nationalmuseum.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Swiss History Blog History of Switzerland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swiss History BlogHistory of Switzerland - The battle of GrandsonJune 19, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2020</span></span></span>

The victory also acquired enormous symbolic importance. An old Swiss saying summed up Charles's downfall: at Grandson he lost his possessions, at Murten his courage and at Nancy his life. Although simplified, the proverb reflects how contemporaries saw the loss of the Burgundian treasure as the beginning of the collapse of one of Europe's greatest princely powers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2024/06/the-battle-of-murten/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.nationalmuseum.ch">[Swiss History Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.nationalmuseum.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Swiss History Blog The Battle of Murten – Swiss National Museum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swiss History BlogThe Battle of Murten – Swiss National Museum - Swiss history blog...</span></span></span>

## How the booty was divided and transformed

The greatest obstacle to tracing the treasure is that there was never a single official treasury after the battle. Instead, the captured goods entered many different channels almost immediately.

Individual soldiers claimed portable valuables as personal rewards. Cantons divided larger military equipment and prestige objects among themselves. Churches received gifts. Wealthy families purchased luxury goods. Merchants bought jewellery and precious metals for resale, while goldsmiths dismantled elaborate pieces to recover valuable materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/the-battle-of-grandson/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.nationalmuseum.ch">[Swiss History Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.nationalmuseum.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Swiss History Blog History of Switzerland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swiss History BlogHistory of Switzerland - The battle of GrandsonJune 19, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2020</span></span></span>

For historians, this rapid dispersal created several lasting problems.


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Objects were melted down.** Silver vessels and gold ornaments often disappeared into furnaces because their metal was worth more than preserving their original form.
* **Luxury textiles were repurposed.** Court garments became church vestments or were cut apart for valuable fabric.
* **Jewels were reset.** Precious stones could easily be removed from their original mountings and incorporated into new jewellery, erasing clear links with Burgundy.
* **Documentation was uneven.** Some cantons kept inventories, while many individual transactions left no surviving records.

</div>

As a result, historians know that the treasure was immense but cannot reconstruct it as a complete collection. The mystery lies less in whether it existed than in how thoroughly it dissolved into the economy and material culture of late medieval Europe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/the-battle-of-grandson/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.nationalmuseum.ch">[nationalmuseum.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.nationalmuseum.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Swiss History Blog History of Switzerland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swiss History BlogHistory of Switzerland - The battle of GrandsonJune 19, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2020</span></span></span>

## Which objects can still be identified?

Despite the enormous losses, a small number of artefacts can still be linked convincingly to the Burgundian booty.

The Swiss National Museum preserves several celebrated examples, including an English-style archer's shield bearing Burgundian insignia, Charles's tent banner and a richly decorated liturgical vestment fashioned from aristocratic clothing captured after Grandson. These pieces illustrate the extraordinary quality of the duke's military entourage while also demonstrating how objects were transformed after capture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2020/06/the-battle-of-grandson/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.nationalmuseum.ch">[Swiss History Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.nationalmuseum.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Swiss History Blog History of Switzerland</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Swiss History BlogHistory of Switzerland - The battle of GrandsonJune 19, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 19, 2020</span></span></span>

Other museums in Switzerland also hold items traditionally associated with the Burgundian booty, although the strength of individual identifications varies. Modern provenance research increasingly studies not only whether an object belonged to Charles's army, but also how it acquired new meanings after entering Swiss civic collections and eventually museum displays.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ikg.unibe.ch/research/research_projects/the_inheritance_of_looting/index_eng.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ikg.unibe.ch">[Institut für Kunstgeschichte]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ikg.unibe.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Institut für KunstgeschichteResearch: The Inheritance of Looting: Medieval Trophies to Modern Museums - Institute of Art History...</span></span></span>

## Why provenance remains disputed

Not every object traditionally described as part of Charles the Bold's treasure can be proven beyond doubt.

Several factors complicate identification:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Medieval inventories often used vague descriptions rather than detailed measurements.
* Decorative objects were routinely altered, repaired or dismantled.
* Similar luxury goods circulated widely among European courts.
* Later collectors sometimes attached prestigious Burgundian stories to objects with incomplete documentation.

</div>

Some famous regalia, jewels and ceremonial pieces appear in chronicles but cannot be matched securely to surviving artefacts. Others survive only in illustrations or written descriptions. Even when historians can identify a likely object, establishing an uninterrupted chain of ownership over more than five centuries is rarely possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ikg.unibe.ch/research/research_projects/the_inheritance_of_looting/index_eng.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ikg.unibe.ch">[Institut für Kunstgeschichte]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ikg.unibe.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Institut für KunstgeschichteResearch: The Inheritance of Looting: Medieval Trophies to Modern Museums - Institute of Art History...</span></span></span>

Recent museum scholarship has shifted attention from asking simply, "Is this Burgundian?" to broader questions about how war trophies became civic treasures, museum masterpieces and symbols of Swiss identity. That change reflects a wider interest in the histories of collecting, ownership and cultural heritage rather than only military victory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ikg.unibe.ch/research/research_projects/the_inheritance_of_looting/index_eng.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ikg.unibe.ch">[unibe.ch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ikg.unibe.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Institut für KunstgeschichteResearch: The Inheritance of Looting: Medieval Trophies to Modern Museums - Institute of Art History...</span></span></span>

## Why the treasure still fascinates historians

Unlike many legends of lost treasure, the Burgundian booty is unquestionably real. Contemporary witnesses, surviving inventories and museum collections all confirm that the Swiss captured one of the richest military camps of the fifteenth century. The unresolved question is what became of most of it.

The answer is probably not a hidden vault waiting to be discovered. Instead, the evidence points to a much more complex process of fragmentation. Thousands of individual objects entered churches, workshops, private households, princely collections and international trade networks. Some survive under altered forms; many were destroyed for their raw materials; countless others became impossible to recognise after centuries of changing ownership.

That makes Charles the Bold's treasure an unusual historical mystery. Rather than disappearing in a single dramatic event, it faded piece by piece into European history, leaving behind a handful of remarkable survivors and an enduring challenge for provenance researchers trying to reconnect scattered fragments of one of the medieval world's greatest captured treasures.
