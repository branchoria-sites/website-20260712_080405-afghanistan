## Introduction

The Somerton Man case is one of [Australia]({{ 'australia/' | relative_url }})'s most famous historical mysteries because it combines three separate puzzles: who the dead man was, how he died, and what the strange clues found with him actually meant. For decades, these questions encouraged speculation about espionage, secret codes and Cold War intrigue. In recent years, however, advances in genetic genealogy have dramatically changed the first part of the story. Strong DNA evidence now points to the man's identity as Carl (Charles) Webb, an electrical engineer from Melbourne. Yet that breakthrough has not solved the circumstances of his death, nor has it explained the mysterious scrap of paper reading "Tamam Shud", the handwritten code, or why he travelled to Adelaide in late 1948. The result is a case that is partly solved and partly as enigmatic as ever.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-08-02/somerton-man-who-was-carl-charles-webb/101288890" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[abc.net.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe Somerton Man has been named. ## How the body, the paper scrap and the coded book created a legend

On the morning of 1 December 1948, a well-dressed man was found dead against the seawall at Somerton Beach near Adelaide. Witnesses had seen him the previous evening apparently resting in the same position, so nobody initially realised he had died. He carried no identification, and labels had been removed from his clothing, making immediate identification impossible. The post-mortem revealed no fatal injuries and no obvious natural cause of death. Although pathologists suspected poisoning, laboratory testing available at the time could not identify any toxin. The coroner ultimately concluded that the death was not natural but could not determine exactly how it had occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/item/mystery-somerton-man" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nfsa.gov.au">[nfsa.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nfsa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NFSAThe mystery of the &#x27;Somerton Man&#x27; &#124; NFSA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NFSAThe mystery of the &#x27;Somerton Man&#x27; &#124; NFSA</span></span></span>

The investigation became extraordinary several months later when police discovered a tiny hidden pocket sewn into the man's trousers. Inside was a tightly rolled scrap of paper bearing the words "Tamam Shud", a Persian phrase commonly translated as "ended" or "finished". Investigators traced the fragment to the final page of a copy of *The Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam*. After police appealed publicly, a local man produced a matching copy of the book, saying he had found it in his car near the time and place of the death. Microscopic examination showed that the torn page matched the scrap found on the body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-12-01/will-the-mystery-of-the-somerton-man-ever-be-solved/10420794" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Unlocking the secrets of the Somerton Man</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsUnlocking the secrets of the Somerton Man - ABC NewsDecember 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2018</span></span></span>

The recovered book added further mysteries. Its back pages contained:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a short sequence of handwritten letters that resembled a cipher;
* two telephone numbers, one linked to a local nurse commonly referred to publicly as "Jestyn";
* faint writing impressions suggesting additional notes once existed.

</div>

Police questioned those connected to the telephone numbers but established no conclusive relationship between them and the dead man. The surviving code has resisted repeated attempts at decipherment by military cryptographers, amateur code-breakers and modern computer analysts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-12-01/will-the-mystery-of-the-somerton-man-ever-be-solved/10420794" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[abc.net.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Unlocking the secrets of the Somerton Man</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsUnlocking the secrets of the Somerton Man - ABC NewsDecember 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2018</span></span></span>

## How genetic genealogy pointed to Carl Webb

For decades investigators relied on fingerprints, dental records and missing-person reports without success. The breakthrough came through modern genetic genealogy rather than traditional detective work.

In 2021 South Australian authorities exhumed the Somerton Man's remains to obtain better DNA samples. Separately, researchers led by Professor Derek Abbott had already recovered usable DNA from hairs preserved inside the plaster death mask made shortly after the man's death. By comparing that DNA with large public genealogy databases and constructing extensive family trees, researchers concluded that the unidentified man was Carl "Charles" Webb, usually known simply as Carl Webb.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-08-02/somerton-man-who-was-carl-charles-webb/101288890" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe Somerton Man has been named. According to the reconstructed family history:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Webb was born in Melbourne in 1905.
* He worked as an electrical engineer or electrical instrument maker.
* He married Dorothy Robertson but the couple later separated.
* No confirmed records place him permanently in Adelaide before his death.
* No evidence has emerged that he was a professional spy or intelligence officer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-08-02/somerton-man-who-was-carl-charles-webb/101288890" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe Somerton Man has been named. ## Why the death itself remains unexplained

The DNA evidence addresses identity far more convincingly than cause of death.

The original autopsy found congestion in several internal organs and changes that some medical experts believed were consistent with poisoning. During the inquest, specialists suggested that certain cardiac glycosides—plant-derived poisons affecting the heart—could produce death while leaving little trace using the forensic methods available in 1948. Nevertheless, no poison was ever identified in laboratory testing, and no evidence established whether any toxic substance had been taken deliberately, accidentally or administered by another person.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.sa.gov.au/finding-information/discover-our-collection/stories-from-the-archive/but-what-poison-mystery-of-the-somerton-man" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.sa.gov.au">[sa.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.sa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sa.gov.au.</span></span></span>

Today, several broad explanations remain plausible:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Undetected poisoning.** Still considered possible because some poisons were difficult to detect with mid-twentieth-century forensic science.
* **Natural medical event.** Less widely supported, as the pathology did not clearly indicate heart disease or another obvious fatal illness.
* **Suicide.** Sometimes proposed because of the "Tamam Shud" message and the absence of signs of struggle, but no direct evidence confirms suicidal intent.
* **Homicide.** Also possible, though there is no established suspect, no proven motive and no confirmed murder weapon.

</div>

Modern researchers generally agree that none of these explanations can currently be demonstrated to the standard required to resolve the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.sa.gov.au/finding-information/discover-our-collection/stories-from-the-archive/but-what-poison-mystery-of-the-somerton-man" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.sa.gov.au">[sa.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.sa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sa.gov.au.</span></span></span>

## Was there really a secret code?

The handwritten letter sequence found inside the *Rubaiyat* remains one of the most discussed features of the case, yet it is also one of the easiest to exaggerate.

The text consists of only a few short lines of apparently random letters. Because it is so brief, cryptographers face a basic statistical problem: there is too little material to determine confidently whether it represents an actual encrypted message, initials, a mnemonic, personal shorthand or simply meaningless scribbling. Numerous decoding attempts have produced incompatible results, none independently verified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-12-01/will-the-mystery-of-the-somerton-man-ever-be-solved/10420794" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Unlocking the secrets of the Somerton Man</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsUnlocking the secrets of the Somerton Man - ABC NewsDecember 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2018</span></span></span>

Several popular theories have emerged over the decades:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Espionage cipher.** Cold War timing and the apparent code encouraged speculation about Soviet or Western intelligence operations.
* **Book cipher.** Some suggested the *Rubaiyat* itself functioned as a codebook.
* **Personal notes.** Others argue the letters could simply be reminders or initials meaningful only to their writer.
* **Coincidental markings.** It is also possible the writing had nothing to do with the dead man at all.

</div>

No documentary evidence has connected the code to any intelligence agency, and no verified decryption has ever been accepted by specialists. The existence of an undeciphered text is therefore a genuine mystery, but not evidence of espionage in itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2018-12-01/will-the-mystery-of-the-somerton-man-ever-be-solved/10420794" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Unlocking the secrets of the Somerton Man</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsUnlocking the secrets of the Somerton Man - ABC NewsDecember 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2018</span></span></span>

## How the identity discovery changes the larger mystery

The identification of Carl Webb significantly narrows the range of plausible explanations. It removes many earlier theories that depended upon the man being an unknown foreign agent, a missing sailor or someone whose origins lay outside Australia. Instead, the central questions become more personal and historically grounded:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Why did Webb travel from Victoria to Adelaide?
* What relationship, if any, did he have with the nurse connected to the telephone number?
* Why were identifying labels removed from his clothing?
* Was the *Rubaiyat* genuinely his book?
* Was the coded writing connected to him or to someone else?
* What actually caused his death?

</div>

## Is the Somerton Man mystery really solved?

The answer depends on which mystery is being discussed.

The identity question appears substantially resolved. Genetic genealogy provides strong evidence that the Somerton Man was Carl Webb, replacing decades of speculation with a coherent family history supported by DNA analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-08-02/somerton-man-who-was-carl-charles-webb/101288890" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsThe Somerton Man has been named. What do we know about Carl &#x27;Charles&#x27; Webb? - ABC NewsAugust 2, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 2, 2022</span></span></span>

The death, however, remains officially unexplained. No poison has been conclusively identified, no homicide has been established, no suicide note has been found, and no convincing natural cause has emerged. Likewise, the "Tamam Shud" paper, the coded writing and the purpose of Webb's journey to Adelaide continue to resist definitive explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.sa.gov.au/finding-information/discover-our-collection/stories-from-the-archive/but-what-poison-mystery-of-the-somerton-man" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.sa.gov.au">[sa.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.sa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sa.gov.au.</span></span></span>

For that reason, the Somerton Man has become a rare historical case in which modern science appears to have solved the victim's identity while leaving the most compelling human questions—the circumstances of his final days and the meaning of the clues surrounding him—still unresolved.
