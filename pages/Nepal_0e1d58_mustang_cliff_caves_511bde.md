## Introduction

The cliff caves of Upper Mustang in northern [Nepal]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-nepals-greatest/' | relative_url }}) are mysterious not because they belonged to a single lost civilisation, but because the same dramatic landscape was reused for very different purposes over thousands of years. Archaeology now shows that these hand-cut chambers served first as burial places, later as homes and refuges, and eventually as religious retreats, storage spaces and repositories for manuscripts. What remains uncertain is exactly who first excavated many of the caves, how the largest and highest complexes were engineered, and why some communities chose to create chambers hundreds of metres above valley floors rather than at ground level. Modern discoveries have steadily replaced romantic stories about a vanished "sky city" with a more complex picture of changing societies adapting the same cliffs to new needs across successive historical periods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/110301-himalayas-caves-defleshed-skeletons-science-nepal-mustang" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicNew Death Ritual Found in Himalaya—27 De-fleshed Humans &#124; National GeographicMarch 1, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1, 2011</span></span></span>

## Why Were Mustang's Cliff Caves Built?

Upper Mustang contains thousands of artificial caves carved into soft sedimentary cliffs above the Kali Gandaki valley. Many are isolated chambers, while others form elaborate complexes linked by tunnels, stairways and multiple rooms. Their locations, often appearing almost inaccessible today, long encouraged speculation about a forgotten civilisation with mysterious engineering skills.

Archaeological work over the past three decades paints a different picture. Rather than representing a single building programme, the caves belong to different periods stretching from prehistoric times through the medieval era. The same cliffs were repeatedly reused as political conditions, religious beliefs and settlement patterns changed. Carbon dating, artefacts, human remains and architectural evidence indicate that the caves' functions evolved rather than remaining fixed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222524403_Climbing_into_the_past_-_First_Himalayan_mummies_discovered_in_Nepal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Climbing into the past</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Climbing into the past - First Himalayan mummies discovered in Nepal...</span></span></span>

This changing use is one of the site's greatest historical puzzles. Many caves that appear similar from the outside contain entirely different archaeological evidence inside, making it impossible to assign one explanation to the entire landscape.

## Burials, settlements and sacred spaces

Researchers generally divide the caves' history into several broad phases, although exact dates vary between sites.

### Early burial chambers

Some of the oldest caves were used as communal burial chambers more than 2,000 years ago. Excavations at places such as Mebrak uncovered naturally preserved bodies placed in wooden coffins together with ornaments, textiles and imported objects. These finds revealed that Upper Mustang participated in long-distance trade across the Himalayas far earlier than previously believed. Glass beads, copper ornaments and other grave goods indicate connections extending beyond the immediate mountain valleys.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222524403_Climbing_into_the_past_-_First_Himalayan_mummies_discovered_in_Nepal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Climbing into the past</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Climbing into the past - First Himalayan mummies discovered in Nepal...</span></span></span>

Later discoveries near Samdzong identified another mortuary tradition dating roughly 1,500 years ago. Archaeologists recovered the remains of twenty-seven individuals, many showing deliberate cut marks made after death. The evidence suggests respectful defleshing before burial rather than violence or cannibalism. Researchers have proposed links with ancient funerary customs spreading along Himalayan trade routes, but no consensus exists about the precise cultural or religious tradition involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/110301-himalayas-caves-defleshed-skeletons-science-nepal-mustang" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicNew Death Ritual Found in Himalaya—27 De-fleshed Humans &#124; National GeographicMarch 1, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1, 2011</span></span></span>

### Defensive settlements and homes

By around the tenth century, many caves appear to have been adapted into living quarters. This period coincided with political instability, conflict and shifting control of Himalayan trade routes.

Excavated caves contain fireplaces, storage niches, sleeping platforms and evidence of everyday domestic life. Entire families probably occupied some of the larger complexes. Living high on cliff faces offered protection from raiders while allowing residents to monitor movement through strategically important valleys. Some cave systems resemble compact apartment blocks more than isolated shelters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.fr/histoire/nepal-assaut-un-des-plus-grands-mysteres-archeologiques-au-monde-recherche-archeologie-fouilles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.fr">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicNépal: À l&#x27;assaut de l&#x27;un des plus grands mystères archéologiques au monde &#124; National Geographic &#124; National Geographi...</span></span></span>

Not every settlement phase was permanent. Some caves may have served as seasonal refuges or temporary defensive positions during periods of unrest before people later returned to villages on the valley floor.

### Monastic and sacred use

As Buddhism became firmly established in the region, many caves acquired religious functions. Meditation chambers, shrines and repositories for sacred manuscripts replaced domestic occupation in some complexes.

One spectacular discovery was a cache of thousands of manuscript pages together with paintings and religious objects dating mainly to the fifteenth century. These documents demonstrated that some caves functioned as protected religious archives rather than ordinary dwellings. The manuscripts also revealed interactions between Buddhist traditions and older religious beliefs that survived alongside them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/relics-recovered-a-pair-of-worldclass-climbers-goes-where-archaeologists-cant" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicRelics Recovered: A Pair of World-Class Climbers Goes Where Archaeologists Can’t &#124; National GeographicNovember 16, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 2009</span></span></span>

The religious phase did not necessarily erase earlier meanings. Communities often reused places already regarded as ancient or spiritually significant, creating layers of cultural memory within the same caves.

## Why the caves stand so high

The height of many Mustang caves remains one of their most striking features. Today some entrances lie hundreds of metres above valley floors, reachable only with advanced climbing equipment.

Several factors probably explain this remarkable positioning.

* **Landscape change.** Thousands of years of erosion have altered cliff faces, causing pathways and ledges that once existed to collapse. Caves that were difficult but manageable to reach in antiquity can now appear almost impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/110301-himalayas-caves-defleshed-skeletons-science-nepal-mustang" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicNew Death Ritual Found in Himalaya—27 De-fleshed Humans &#124; National GeographicMarch 1, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1, 2011</span></span></span>

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Security.** Elevated locations protected burial chambers from scavenging animals and discouraged looting. During unsettled periods they also made settlements easier to defend.
* **Symbolic importance.** High places held religious significance in Himalayan traditions, making elevated caves appropriate locations for retreat, burial or ritual.
* **Soft geology.** Mustang's relatively soft sedimentary rock could be excavated with ancient tools more easily than harder mountain stone, allowing large chambers to be carved despite the challenging locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/nepal-mustang-kingdom-lo-road-china-feature" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationalgeographic.com.</span></span></span>

The original construction methods remain uncertain. Archaeologists suspect builders used wooden scaffolding, ladders and natural ledges that have since disappeared, but little direct evidence survives because organic materials decay rapidly.

## What archaeology still cannot explain

Although the broad sequence of cave use is increasingly well understood, several important mysteries remain unresolved.

The first concerns construction itself. Researchers still cannot determine whether all of the oldest caves were created by one cultural tradition or by different communities over many centuries. The sheer number of chambers—running into the thousands across Upper Mustang—suggests long-term activity rather than a single coordinated project.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/nepal-mustang-kingdom-lo-road-china-feature" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationalgeographic.com.</span></span></span>

Another uncertainty involves the identities of the earliest cave builders. Human remains, grave goods and DNA research have improved understanding of ancient populations, yet the cultural affiliations and languages of the oldest communities remain only partly understood. Written records from these early periods are absent.

The purpose of many individual caves also remains ambiguous. Some contain clear domestic evidence, while others have almost no surviving artefacts because erosion, collapse and looting removed much of the archaeological record. Similar-looking caves may have served entirely different purposes.

Even the famous manuscript cache raises unanswered questions. Scholars have learned much about religion and politics in medieval Mustang, yet debate continues over why valuable texts were hidden inside remote cliff chambers rather than preserved in monasteries, and whether they were intentionally concealed during periods of instability or simply stored in places considered especially sacred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/adventure/article/relics-recovered-a-pair-of-worldclass-climbers-goes-where-archaeologists-cant" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicRelics Recovered: A Pair of World-Class Climbers Goes Where Archaeologists Can’t &#124; National GeographicNovember 16, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 16, 2009</span></span></span>

## From lost city legend to layered history

Popular accounts sometimes describe Mustang's caves as the remains of a mysterious lost civilisation. Archaeological evidence instead supports a more nuanced and arguably more remarkable explanation. The caves document more than two millennia of human adaptation in one of the Himalayas' harshest environments.

Each generation inherited an existing landscape and reshaped it. Burial chambers became homes; homes became monasteries or storehouses; sacred spaces accumulated manuscripts and artworks. Rather than solving a single mystery, archaeological research has revealed a sequence of changing societies whose priorities evolved with trade, religion, conflict and environmental change.

That layered history explains why the Mustang cliff caves remain one of Nepal's most compelling archaeological enigmas.
