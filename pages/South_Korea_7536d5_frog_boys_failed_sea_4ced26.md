## Introduction

The Frog Boys case is one of [South Korea]({{ 'what-still-haunts-south-koreas-history/' | relative_url }})'s most haunting unsolved murder investigations, not only because five schoolboys disappeared in 1991 and were found dead eleven years later, but because their remains were discovered on Mount Waryong, close to where they vanished, in an area that had reportedly been searched many times. The enduring mystery is therefore twofold: who killed the boys, and how did investigators fail to find them for more than a decade? The case became a national symbol of investigative shortcomings, misplaced early assumptions and the irreversible damage caused when potential crime scenes are not secured properly. Although the deaths are now widely treated as homicides, no one has been identified or prosecuted.

## The disappearance and the nationwide search

On 26 March 1991, a temporary public holiday for South Korea's first local elections in decades, five boys from Daegu left home intending to collect salamander eggs on nearby Mount Waryong. Media reports mistakenly described them as searching for frog eggs, giving rise to the enduring name "Frog Boys".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/news/2025-06-23/national/socialAffairs/Frog-Boys-case-Five-students-vanished-in-1991-found-dead-years-later--still-no-answers/2308415" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[koreajoongangdaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Frog Boys case: Five students vanished in 1991, found dead years later — still no answersJune 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 23, 2025</span></span></span>

When the boys failed to return, what began as a local search rapidly became a national campaign. Police, soldiers, volunteers and local residents combed the mountain and surrounding countryside. Hundreds of thousands of personnel are reported to have participated over the following months, making it one of the largest missing-child searches in South Korean history. The families themselves became central figures in the search, distributing leaflets across the country and refusing to let public attention fade.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/20110222/calls-growing-for-removal-of-statute-of-limitations-for-crimes-against-children" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreatimes.co.kr">[The Korea Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreatimes.co.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Korea TimesCalls growing for removal of statute of limitations for crimes against children - The Korea TimesFebruary 22, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2011</span></span></span>

Despite this unprecedented effort, investigators found no convincing trace of the children. The lack of evidence encouraged speculation ranging from kidnapping to organised crime, military involvement and other rumours, but none produced verifiable evidence. At the same time, police were criticised for initially giving weight to the possibility that the boys had simply run away, potentially delaying a more focused criminal investigation during the crucial early period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.sedaily.com/society/2026/03/25/dad-ill-go-find-salamander-eggs-those-were-the-last-words" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.sedaily.com">[Seoul Economic Daily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.sedaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seoul Economic Daily...</span></span></span>

The greatest puzzle of the search remains geographical. In September 2002, two men gathering acorns discovered the boys' skeletal remains on Mount Waryong itself, only a short distance from where they had disappeared. The location had reportedly been searched repeatedly during the original operation, leading many observers to question whether the bodies had been overlooked, whether search methods had been inadequate, or whether the remains had been moved after the initial searches. None of these possibilities has been conclusively proved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2003/01/06/socialAffairs/Bodies-of-5-frog-boys-missing-since-1991-found-on-mountain/1909041.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[joins.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bodies of 5 &#x27;frog boys,&#x27; missing since 1991, found on mountainJanuary 6, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 6, 2003</span></span></span>

## What the remains revealed

The discovery transformed the case from a missing-person investigation into a suspected multiple murder.

Initially, some investigators suggested the boys may have become lost and died from exposure. That explanation was soon challenged by forensic specialists and by the families, who pointed to the condition of the remains and the surrounding evidence. Later forensic examinations identified injuries to several skulls that were considered consistent with blunt-force trauma, while other damage prompted discussion of possible weapon use. Because the remains had been exposed to weather and environmental damage for more than a decade, however, forensic experts could not reconstruct every detail of how the boys died.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2003/01/06/socialAffairs/Bodies-of-5-frog-boys-missing-since-1991-found-on-mountain/1909041.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[joins.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bodies of 5 &#x27;frog boys,&#x27; missing since 1991, found on mountainJanuary 6, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 6, 2003</span></span></span>

The discovery also exposed weaknesses in crime-scene management. Critics argued that the recovery area was not preserved as rigorously as it should have been once the remains were found. Any disturbance of a scene that had already suffered eleven years of natural deterioration risked destroying trace evidence that might have identified the killer or clarified how the bodies came to be there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.sedaily.com/society/2026/03/25/dad-ill-go-find-salamander-eggs-those-were-the-last-words" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.sedaily.com">[Seoul Economic Daily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.sedaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seoul Economic Daily...</span></span></span>

Another unresolved question concerns whether the boys died where they were found. Some investigators have argued that the bodies appeared to have remained at the site since 1991, while others have wondered whether they were concealed or relocated after the original searches. Public attention has also focused on reports of nearby military training areas and ammunition found in the vicinity. However, no publicly available evidence has established a direct connection between military personnel and the killings, and official investigations have never confirmed a military cover-up.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2006/03/20/socialAffairs/Statute-may-end-investigation-of-unsolved-cases/2699767.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[koreajoongangdaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">koreajoongangdaily Statute may end investigation of unsolved cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Statute may end investigation of unsolved casesMarch 20, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 20, 2006</span></span></span>

## Why the investigation still has no answer

The Frog Boys case illustrates how an investigation can become progressively harder to solve as time passes.

Several factors combined to reduce the likelihood of identifying the killer:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The first hours after the disappearance may not have been treated with the urgency appropriate for a suspected violent crime.
* Eleven years of exposure severely degraded physical evidence.
* The discovery site itself became the subject of criticism over evidence preservation.
* Numerous rumours generated public attention but produced little verifiable investigative value.
* No known eyewitness has provided evidence capable of identifying a perpetrator.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.sedaily.com/society/2026/03/25/dad-ill-go-find-salamander-eggs-those-were-the-last-words" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.sedaily.com">[Seoul Economic Daily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.sedaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Seoul Economic Daily...</span></span></span>

</div>

For many years another obstacle was legal. Under the law in force at the time, the statute of limitations for murder expired before anyone was charged. South Korea later abolished the statute of limitations for murder, meaning that if compelling new evidence or a credible suspect were to emerge today, prosecution would once again be legally possible. The legal change did not solve the case, but it removed one barrier that had frustrated the victims' families.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/southkorea/20110222/calls-growing-for-removal-of-statute-of-limitations-for-crimes-against-children" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreatimes.co.kr">[The Korea Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreatimes.co.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Korea TimesCalls growing for removal of statute of limitations for crimes against children - The Korea TimesFebruary 22, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2011</span></span></span>

Recent reviews of the case have also highlighted the growing contrast between modern forensic capabilities and the limited evidence preserved from 1991. While advances in DNA analysis and digital investigative methods have solved some historic crimes, they cannot recover evidence that was never collected or has been irretrievably lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/news/2025-11-26/national/socialAffairs/Frog-Boys-case-shows-how-staffing-shortfalls-hinder-cold-cases-despite-forensic-advances/2463871" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[koreajoongangdaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on joins.com.</span></span></span>

## Why the case remains significant

The Frog Boys murders occupy a unique place in South Korean public memory because they represent more than an unsolved crime. They have become a cautionary example of how investigative assumptions, search strategy and evidence handling can shape the outcome of a case for decades.

The image of five children being found on a mountain that had already been searched continues to symbolise the limits of even the largest search operations when evidence is missed or misunderstood. For the families, the discovery of the remains answered only one question—that the boys had not simply disappeared—but left the central mystery unresolved.

More than three decades after the disappearance, the identity of the killer, the precise sequence of events on Mount Waryong and the reason the bodies remained undiscovered for so long are still unknown. Those unanswered questions ensure that the Frog Boys case remains one of South Korea's most enduring historical mysteries.
