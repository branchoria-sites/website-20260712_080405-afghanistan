## Introduction

Who killed Andriy Yushchinskyi? More than a century after the murder of the Kyiv schoolboy in 1911, that question remains unanswered. What is known with much greater confidence is that the investigation was diverted into one of the most notorious antisemitic prosecutions in European history. Mendel Beilis, a Jewish factory clerk with no credible evidence linking him to the crime, spent more than two years in prison before a jury acquitted him in 1913. The failure to identify Yushchinskyi's killer and the state's determination to pursue a discredited accusation of ritual murder have made the case both an unsolved crime and a cautionary example of how prejudice can overwhelm criminal justice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

Although the Beilis affair is often remembered as a landmark trial over the medieval "blood libel" myth, the underlying mystery is the murder itself. Historians broadly agree that the original investigation uncovered evidence pointing away from Beilis and towards criminal figures operating in Kyiv. Because that line of inquiry was disrupted, the identity of the murderer has never been established beyond reasonable doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

## The 1911 murder and the early inquiry

On 12 March 1911, thirteen-year-old Andriy Yushchinskyi disappeared while travelling to school in Kyiv. Eight days later, his body was discovered in a cave near the Zaitsev brickworks. The killing was shocking because of the number of stab wounds and the apparent effort to conceal the body. Almost immediately, sensational rumours spread that the murder had been committed for a Jewish religious ritual, despite the absence of evidence supporting such a claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beilis_Affair" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Beilis Affair</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beilis Affair</span></span></span>

The first forensic examinations did not establish that the killing had any ritual character. Instead, investigators increasingly examined the possibility that the boy had been murdered by members of a local criminal gang associated with Vera Cheberiak, whose group was involved in theft and other offences. According to this line of inquiry, Yushchinskyi may have known information that endangered the gang or become an inconvenient witness. Several investigators regarded this as the strongest explanation available at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

That investigation, however, soon became entangled in politics. Far-right organisations, especially the antisemitic Black Hundreds, distributed leaflets alleging a ritual murder even before the evidence had been assessed. Their campaign transformed a local homicide into a national political controversy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

## How Mendel Beilis became the accused

Mendel Beilis worked as a supervisor at the nearby brick factory. He had no known connection to Yushchinskyi, yet his workplace's proximity to where the body was found made him a convenient suspect once officials embraced the ritual-murder narrative. He was arrested in July 1911 and remained imprisoned for more than two years awaiting trial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

Modern historians generally conclude that the prosecution was driven less by evidence than by political pressure. Senior figures in the Ministry of Justice encouraged the case to proceed despite serious weaknesses in the investigation. Police officers who had pursued the criminal-gang theory were removed, while prosecutors focused on proving a ritual murder instead. Archival research has also revealed official interference with witnesses and jury selection, reinforcing the view that the trial was manipulated for ideological purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

The prosecution relied heavily on the ancient blood libel—the false accusation that Jews murdered Christian children for religious purposes. By the early twentieth century, this allegation had long been rejected by mainstream scholarship and religious authorities, yet it continued to circulate in antisemitic political movements across Europe. The Beilis case became one of its last major courtroom appearances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Blood_Libels_and_Host_Desecration_Accusations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Blood Libels and Host Desecration Accusations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBlood Libels and Host Desecration Accusations - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

## Why the ritual murder claim collapsed

The prosecution struggled to connect Beilis personally to the crime. He had no convincing motive, no demonstrated involvement in any supposed conspiracy and evidence suggested he was occupied with his work at the relevant time. During the trial, defence lawyers dismantled claims that Jewish religious law contained any requirement for ritual killing, exposing supposed "experts" as lacking genuine knowledge of Judaism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

Medical evidence also failed to support the dramatic claims circulating in the press. While specialists disagreed over some forensic details, the autopsies did not establish that the injuries were consistent with any uniquely ritual method of killing. The prosecution therefore relied increasingly on speculation rather than demonstrable forensic proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beilis_Affair" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Beilis Affair</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beilis Affair</span></span></span>

The trial attracted enormous international attention. Intellectuals, lawyers, clergy and politicians in Russia, Europe and North America criticised the proceedings as an attempt to legitimise antisemitic mythology through the courts. At the same time, conservative newspapers and nationalist organisations continued to portray the prosecution as a defence of traditional Christianity, illustrating how deeply divided public opinion had become.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

## What the acquittal did—and did not—prove

On 28 October 1913, the jury acquitted Beilis, and he was immediately released. His acquittal established that the prosecution had failed to prove he committed the murder. It did not, however, identify who had killed Andriy Yushchinskyi.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/display.asp?linkpath=pages%5CB%5CE%5CBeilisaffair.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopediaofukraine.com">[Encyclopedia of Ukraine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopediaofukraine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia of Ukraine Beilis affair</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Encyclopedia of Ukraine Beilis affair</span></span></span>

The verdict itself reflected the contradictions of the trial. The jury rejected the case against Beilis but, through the way the questions were framed by the court, left room for the prosecution's broader narrative that the killing might nevertheless have been ritual in nature. This ambiguous outcome allowed antisemitic campaigners to claim partial vindication even though their chosen defendant had been found not guilty. Historians regard this as one of the trial's most damaging legacies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

Following his release, Beilis left the Russian Empire, eventually settling in the United States after a period in Palestine. He later published memoirs describing his imprisonment and trial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

## Who probably killed Andriy Yushchinskyi?

The central historical mystery survives because the original investigation was diverted before it could be completed. Most modern scholarship considers the criminal-gang hypothesis substantially more credible than the ritual-murder accusation. Contemporary investigators had already gathered evidence suggesting that associates of Vera Cheberiak possessed both motive and opportunity, and some later accounts describe statements from underworld figures implicating members of that circle. Yet no prosecution ever definitively established responsibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

Several factors prevent historians from treating any individual as conclusively identified:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The original inquiry was repeatedly interrupted by political intervention.
* Witnesses changed their testimony, and some later claimed they had been pressured or misled.
* Important evidence became entangled with the political battle surrounding the trial.
* No court ever tried or convicted anyone for Yushchinskyi's murder.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[YIVO Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>

For these reasons, the murder itself remains officially unsolved, even though the theory that Beilis committed it has been overwhelmingly rejected by historians.

## Why the case still matters in Ukraine's history

The Beilis affair occupies a distinctive place among [Ukraine]({{ 'what-still-remains-unsolved-in-ukraines/' | relative_url }})'s historical mysteries because the unresolved question is inseparable from the failure of the investigation. Unlike many famous unsolved murders, uncertainty here was not simply the result of missing clues. It arose because political ideology redirected the search for truth.

The case also illustrates how a genuine crime can become overshadowed by propaganda. Public memory often centres on the spectacular trial rather than on Andriy Yushchinskyi himself, whose death became the vehicle for a wider campaign of antisemitic mobilisation within the final years of the Russian Empire. The failure to solve his murder therefore stands as both a criminal mystery and a reminder that prejudice can permanently distort the historical record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Beilis_Mendel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.yivo.org">[yivo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.yivo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Encyclopedia Beilis, Mendel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">YIVO EncyclopediaBeilis, Mendel - YIVO EncyclopediaFebruary 12, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 12, 2010</span></span></span>
