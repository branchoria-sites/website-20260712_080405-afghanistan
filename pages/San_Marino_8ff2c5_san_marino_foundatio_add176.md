## Introduction

Was [San Marino]({{ 'which-san-marino-mysteries-still-defy/' | relative_url }}) really founded on 3 September AD 301? The short answer is that no surviving contemporary evidence proves it. The famous date comes from a deeply rooted national tradition centred on Saint Marinus, while the earliest secure written records of an organised community on Mount Titano appear centuries later. That gap between legend and documentation has made San Marino's foundation one of Europe's most enduring historical debates.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/San_Marino_8ff2c5_san_marino_foundatio_add176-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Foundation illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/San_Marino_8ff2c5_san_marino_foundatio_add176-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/San_Marino_8ff2c5_san_marino_foundatio_add176-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The mystery is not whether San Marino has ancient roots. Archaeology confirms that Mount Titano was occupied long before the Middle Ages, and medieval documents leave no doubt that a settled religious community existed there by the ninth century. The real question is whether that community can be traced continuously back to the lifetime of Saint Marinus, or whether later medieval institutions retrospectively created a foundation story that expressed political identity more than verifiable history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/contenuto/About-San-Marino/STORIA.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale History of San Marino &#124; Visit San Marino</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale History of San Marino &#124; Visit San Marino</span></span></span>

## The legend of Saint Marinus

According to tradition, Marinus was a Christian stonecutter from the Adriatic island of Rab who travelled to the Italian coast during the late Roman Empire. After working in Rimini, he is said to have withdrawn to Mount Titano to escape persecution under Emperor Diocletian, gathering a small Christian community around him. The traditional date of 3 September AD 301 marks the beginning of that community and has become San Marino's national foundation day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/contenuto/About-San-Marino/STORIA.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale History of San Marino &#124; Visit San Marino</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale History of San Marino &#124; Visit San Marino</span></span></span>

The story carries constitutional as well as religious meaning. Medieval and later accounts attribute to Marinus the famous declaration that he left his followers "free". Although historians debate the authenticity and age of this saying, it became a powerful symbol that the community's liberty rested on divine and moral authority rather than on grants from kings or popes. By the modern period, the saint had become both patron and founding figure of the republic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.castellidisanmarino.com/en/301-foundation-republic-san-marino/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: castellidisanmarino.com">[Castelli di San Marino]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">castellidisanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Castelli di San Marino301 AD the foundation of the Republic of San Marino</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1, 2025</span></span></span>

The difficulty is chronological rather than theological. The earliest surviving biography of Saint Marinus was written centuries after his supposed lifetime, making it impossible to verify many of its details independently. Like many early medieval saints' lives, it combines devotional tradition, miracle stories and historical memory, leaving historians cautious about treating it as straightforward evidence for events in AD 301.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1245.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre REPUBLIC OF SAN MARINO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreREPUBLIC OF SAN MARINO...</span></span></span>

## What the Placito Feretrano actually proves

The strongest documentary evidence for early San Marino is the *Placito Feretrano*, a legal record concerning a property dispute dated 20 February 885.

The case involved the Bishop of Rimini and Stephen, abbot of the monastery on Mount Titano. The judgement recognised the monastery's rights over several estates after witnesses and legal arguments were heard. This demonstrates that by the late ninth century there was an organised religious institution with recognised property and legal standing on the mountain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sanmarinosite.com/en/history/placito-feretrano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sanmarinosite.com">[sanmarinosite.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sanmarinosite.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Placito Feretrano San Marino, ancient parchment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 7, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 7, 2023</span></span></span>

The document is extremely important, but it is often misunderstood. It does **not** prove that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* San Marino was founded in AD 301.
* Political independence had continued uninterrupted since late Roman times.
* The modern republic already existed in anything like its present form.

</div>

Instead, it proves something narrower but historically secure: an established community, centred on a monastery, existed on Mount Titano by 885 and could successfully defend its property rights before recognised authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sanmarinosite.com/en/history/placito-feretrano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sanmarinosite.com">[sanmarinosite.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sanmarinosite.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Placito Feretrano San Marino, ancient parchment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 7, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 7, 2023</span></span></span>

Even this evidence requires careful handling. The surviving parchment is not the original ninth-century document but an eleventh-century copy, discovered in the state archives in 1749. Medieval copies of legal texts are entirely normal, but copying introduces opportunities for errors, omissions or later additions. Scholars therefore debate individual readings within the text without rejecting its overall historical importance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sanmarinosite.com/en/history/placito-feretrano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sanmarinosite.com">[sanmarinosite.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sanmarinosite.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Placito Feretrano San Marino, ancient parchment</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 7, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 7, 2023</span></span></span>

## How medieval institutions created a republic

The documentary trail becomes much clearer after the ninth century. As throughout much of medieval Italy, the community on Mount Titano gradually developed civic institutions alongside its religious foundations.

An assembly of household heads, known as the Arengo, exercised authority before increasingly formal communal government emerged. By the thirteenth century, officials identifiable as the predecessors of today's Captains Regent were being elected, and written statutes progressively defined the republic's legal order. Rather than appearing fully formed in AD 301, San Marino's republican institutions developed over several centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/contenuto/About-San-Marino/STORIA.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale History of San Marino &#124; Visit San Marino</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale History of San Marino &#124; Visit San Marino</span></span></span>

This gradual evolution helps explain why UNESCO describes San Marino as demonstrating exceptional continuity **since the Middle Ages**. Its significance lies not in proving every detail of the foundation legend but in preserving an unusually long and well-documented tradition of self-government once medieval records become abundant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/1497" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre UNESCO World Heritage Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreUNESCO World Heritage Centre - Decision - 32 COM 8B.36...</span></span></span>

## Why historians remain divided

The debate is less about whether Saint Marinus existed than about what can legitimately be claimed from the available evidence.

Most historians broadly agree on several points:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Well established:** Mount Titano was inhabited before the Middle Ages, and an organised religious community certainly existed by the ninth century.
* **Reasonably plausible:** A historical Christian hermit or religious founder may lie behind the tradition of Saint Marinus.
* **Not demonstrated:** The exact foundation date of AD 301 and an uninterrupted line of independent republican government stretching continuously from that year.
* **Firmly documented:** Medieval communal institutions gradually evolved into the republic recognised in later centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub1/VisitSM/en/contenuto/About-San-Marino/STORIA.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[visitsanmarino.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale History of San Marino &#124; Visit San Marino</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale History of San Marino &#124; Visit San Marino</span></span></span>

</div>

Some recent historical discussions have also questioned when the specific date of AD 301 became officially embedded in state tradition, noting that the precise year gained formal governmental status comparatively late despite much older devotion to Saint Marinus himself. That observation does not mean the tradition was invented in modern times, but it illustrates how national commemorations can become more precisely defined as historical identity evolves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.castellidisanmarino.com/en/301-foundation-republic-san-marino/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: castellidisanmarino.com">[Castelli di San Marino]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">castellidisanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Castelli di San Marino301 AD the foundation of the Republic of San Marino</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">March 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## Questions left unresolved by Foundation
The disputed foundation of San Marino remains significant because it sits at the intersection of legend, documentary history and national identity.

Many European states possess foundation myths, but few have preserved one so closely linked to living constitutional traditions. San Marino still celebrates 3 September as both the feast of its patron saint and the anniversary of the republic's foundation, reflecting the continuing importance of the traditional narrative regardless of ongoing scholarly debate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitsanmarino.com/pub2/VisitSM/en/evento/20260903_Festa_di_San_Marino.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsanmarino.com">[Visita San Marino, portale ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsanmarino.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visita San Marino, portale ufficialeFeast of Saint Marinus and Foundation of the Republic Day &#124; Visit San MarinoSeptember 3, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 3, 2026</span></span></span>

For historians, the mystery is not whether the legend should simply be accepted or dismissed. The more interesting question is how a small mountain community transformed a revered local saint into the symbolic founder of one of the world's oldest surviving republics. The documentary gap between AD 301 and the ninth century may never be filled completely, but the medieval evidence clearly shows how the community that emerged on Mount Titano developed institutions capable of preserving its autonomy for centuries. That combination of uncertain origins and remarkable institutional continuity is precisely what makes San Marino's foundation story such a lasting historical puzzle.
