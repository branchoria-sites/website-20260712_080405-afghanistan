## Introduction

The legend of La Buse's treasure is one of the most famous historical mysteries associated with [Seychelles]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-seychelles/' | relative_url }}), but the greatest puzzle is not where the treasure might be buried. It is whether the famous cryptogram said to reveal its location ever existed in the eighteenth century at all. The pirate Olivier Levasseur, known as La Buse, unquestionably took part in one of history's richest pirate captures in 1721. The coded message that supposedly identifies his hidden treasure, however, rests on far shakier foundations. Modern historical research increasingly argues that the cryptogram has no authenticated eighteenth-century provenance and first appears in a twentieth-century publication rather than in any surviving contemporary archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385137034_Demystifying_La_Buse%27s_Cryptogram_and_the_Fiery_Cross_of_Goa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because the treasure legend has inspired more than a century of excavations around Bel Ombre on Mahé. Although the searches are real and the pirate certainly existed, no authenticated pirate hoard, original cipher manuscript or documented chain of custody has ever emerged. The mystery therefore centres less on an undeciphered code than on the uncertain origins of the code itself.

## The 1721 robbery and the birth of the treasure legend

The historical foundation of the story is unusually strong. In April 1721, a coalition of pirates that included Olivier Levasseur captured the Portuguese flagship *Nossa Senhora do Cabo* near Réunion. Contemporary reports confirm that the ship carried the outgoing Viceroy of Goa together with an exceptionally valuable cargo of precious metals, jewels, luxury goods and other wealth accumulated across the Portuguese empire. The prize became one of the richest known captures of the late Golden Age of Piracy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385137034_Demystifying_La_Buse%27s_Cryptogram_and_the_Fiery_Cross_of_Goa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span></span></span>

After the loot was divided, the historical record becomes much thinner. Levasseur continued operating for several years before his capture and execution on Bourbon (now Réunion) in 1730. The familiar story claims that, moments before being hanged, he threw a coded document into the crowd and declared that whoever solved it would find his treasure.

That dramatic final gesture is the point where documented history gives way to legend. Researchers examining surviving colonial correspondence and official records relating to Levasseur's execution have found no contemporary account describing such an event. Official reports discuss his capture, trial and execution but not a cryptogram or theatrical last challenge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385137034_Demystifying_La_Buse%27s_Cryptogram_and_the_Fiery_Cross_of_Goa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span></span></span>

## Why the cryptogram's provenance is disputed

The strongest reason for doubting the cryptogram is not that nobody has solved it. Rather, it is that no authenticated eighteenth-century manuscript has ever been located.

Modern studies have traced the widely circulated seventeen-line cipher to French maritime historian Charles de La Roncière's 1934 publication *Le Flibustier mystérieux*. Although La Roncière was a respected historian, this particular work was issued as an inexpensive popular adventure story rather than as a scholarly edition of archival documents. It contains the cryptogram, a decoding key and an interpreted French text, but it does not establish where the original manuscript came from or document an unbroken chain of custody linking it to Levasseur himself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385137034_Demystifying_La_Buse%27s_Cryptogram_and_the_Fiery_Cross_of_Goa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span></span></span>

Subsequent treasure books often repeated the story while adding further details about supposed previous owners of the cipher, inherited pirate papers or hidden family archives. Yet historians have been unable to verify these claims from independent documentary evidence. Modern investigations into these traditions have likewise failed to identify an original manuscript in a recognised archive or private collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385137034_Demystifying_La_Buse%27s_Cryptogram_and_the_Fiery_Cross_of_Goa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span></span></span>

An important consequence follows from this. The debate is no longer primarily about breaking an unsolved code. If the only securely documented version originates in a twentieth-century literary work, the authenticity of the entire cryptogram becomes the central historical question.

### The code itself is not the main mystery

Popular accounts sometimes describe the cipher as an unbreakable eighteenth-century secret. In reality, researchers have long recognised that the symbols resemble a relatively simple substitution or pigpen-style cipher rather than an exceptionally sophisticated encryption system. La Roncière himself published both the cipher and his proposed solution in 1934.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385137034_Demystifying_La_Buse%27s_Cryptogram_and_the_Fiery_Cross_of_Goa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span></span></span>

The real difficulty lies elsewhere:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* there is no authenticated original manuscript from Levasseur's lifetime;
* there is no documented contemporary witness describing the alleged final throw of the cipher;
* there is no continuous documentary trail connecting the published cryptogram to the pirate;
* later writers frequently repeat one another rather than introducing independent evidence.

</div>

## What decades of digging at Bel Ombre found

Bel Ombre on the north-west coast of Mahé became the centre of treasure hunting because later versions of the legend associated the area with Levasseur's hidden wealth.

Excavations began in the early twentieth century, with members of the Savy family among the first to conduct systematic searches. The most famous excavator, Reginald Cruise-Wilkins, arrived in Seychelles in the late 1940s and spent decades investigating the site. He believed carvings, tunnels, rock formations and engineered structures formed part of an elaborate pirate concealment system. After his death, his son John Cruise-Wilkins continued the work using modern drilling and excavation equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptogram_of_Olivier_Levasseur" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cryptogram of Olivier Levasseur</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cryptogram of Olivier Levasseur</span></span></span>

Despite the remarkable persistence of these searches, no discovery has conclusively demonstrated the existence of Levasseur's treasure. Archaeological work has uncovered tunnels, chambers, worked stone and features that have inspired competing interpretations, but none has produced an authenticated cache of pirate gold or jewels that can be securely connected with the 1721 robbery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptogram_of_Olivier_Levasseur" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cryptogram of Olivier Levasseur</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cryptogram of Olivier Levasseur</span></span></span>

This does not prove that Levasseur never concealed part of his wealth. Pirates occasionally buried valuables, and documentary gaps leave room for possibilities. What decades of excavation have failed to produce, however, is the decisive physical evidence that would transform the legend into established history.

## Separating established history from later myth

One reason the La Buse story remains so compelling is that it combines a firmly documented historical event with later storytelling whose popularity eventually overshadowed the evidence.

The strongest historical points are well supported:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Levasseur was a real pirate active in the western Indian Ocean.
* He participated in the immensely profitable seizure of *Nossa Senhora do Cabo* in 1721.

## Why the mystery still matters in Seychelles

The La Buse legend remains part of Seychelles' cultural landscape because it illustrates how history, folklore and tourism can reinforce one another. Bel Ombre has become associated with generations of excavators, local stories and international treasure hunters, even though the historical evidence has grown more sceptical rather than more supportive.

Ironically, the greatest unresolved question may not concern buried gold at all. It is whether the famous cryptogram—the object that inspired so much excavation and speculation—was ever connected to Olivier Levasseur in the first place. Until an authenticated eighteenth-century manuscript or an indisputable pirate hoard is discovered, the doubtful provenance of the cryptogram will remain the central mystery behind the legend of La Buse's treasure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385137034_Demystifying_La_Buse%27s_Cryptogram_and_the_Fiery_Cross_of_Goa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Demystifying La Buse&#x27;s Cryptogram and the Fiery Cross of Goa</span></span></span>
