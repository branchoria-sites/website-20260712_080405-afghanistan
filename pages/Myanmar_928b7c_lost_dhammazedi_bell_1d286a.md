## Introduction

The Great Dhammazedi Bell is [Myanmar]({{ 'which-myanmar-mysteries-still-defy-a/' | relative_url }})’s most famous lost treasure and one of the world's most enduring historical mysteries. Most historians accept that an exceptionally large bronze bell once hung at the Shwedagon Pagoda in present-day Yangon. What remains uncertain is what happened after it was allegedly stolen in 1608 by the Portuguese adventurer Filipe de Brito e Nicote. According to the traditional account, the bell sank into the river while being transported away to be melted down for cannon. More than four centuries later, despite repeated searches using divers, sonar, dredging equipment and modern survey techniques, no verified trace of the bell has been recovered. The mystery survives because the evidence is unusually fragmented: an early eyewitness confirms the bell existed, later chronicles describe its loss, but the exact sinking site, the changing river landscape and conflicting historical records leave its fate unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/features/murky-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy A Murky Story</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IrrawaddyA Murky StoryMay 17, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 17, 2014</span></span></span>

## What early accounts say about the bell

The strongest evidence that the bell genuinely existed comes from the Venetian merchant Gasparo Balbi, who travelled through Lower Myanmar in the 1580s. During his visit he described seeing an enormous bell at the Shwedagon Pagoda, decorated with writing he could not understand. Because Balbi wrote decades before the supposed theft, historians regard his account as the most important independent confirmation that an unusually large bell stood at the pagoda.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/spirits-prayers-mark-hunt-burmas-lost-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span></span></span>

Traditional Burmese accounts attribute the bell to King Dhammazedi, the ruler of the Hanthawaddy Kingdom in the late fifteenth century. According to these traditions, the king commissioned the bell around 1490 as a religious donation. Later descriptions claim it weighed close to 300 tonnes, although the precise figure depends on historical units of measurement whose modern equivalents remain debated. The bell's reputed size would have made it larger than any surviving cast bell known today, adding to both its fame and the practical questions surrounding its transport.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/features/murky-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy A Murky Story</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IrrawaddyA Murky StoryMay 17, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 17, 2014</span></span></span>

The documentary record, however, is not straightforward. Some historians point out that major Burmese chronicles from the period do not clearly record the donation of such a colossal bell, despite documenting many of Dhammazedi's other religious gifts. This absence does not prove the bell never existed—Balbi's eyewitness account remains significant—but it complicates efforts to reconstruct its exact history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/spirits-prayers-mark-hunt-burmas-lost-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span></span></span>

## How the sinking story developed

The traditional narrative centres on Filipe de Brito e Nicote, the Portuguese ruler of Thanlyin (then Syriam). Having already removed and melted other temple bells to cast artillery, he is said to have ordered the Dhammazedi Bell taken from Shwedagon Pagoda in 1608.

According to the story, elephants and large numbers of labourers hauled the bell down to the river, where it was loaded onto a raft or barge to be transported across the water. Before reaching Thanlyin, the overloaded vessel failed near the junction of the Yangon and Bago rivers, causing both the raft and the bell to sink into deep mud. De Brito was unable to recover it, and several years later he himself was defeated and executed after the Burmese reconquest of Thanlyin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/features/murky-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy A Murky Story</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IrrawaddyA Murky StoryMay 17, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 17, 2014</span></span></span>

Whether every detail of this account is historically accurate remains uncertain. Contemporary evidence for the theft is much thinner than later retellings, and historians cannot confidently identify the exact route used or the precise place where the accident occurred. The familiar story may therefore combine authentic events with details that accumulated through centuries of oral tradition and historical retelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/spirits-prayers-mark-hunt-burmas-lost-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span></span></span>

## Why modern searches have failed

The repeated failure to recover the bell is not simply a matter of poor luck. Several practical obstacles make the search extraordinarily difficult.

First, the rivers around Yangon have changed dramatically since the early seventeenth century. Sediment has accumulated continuously, channels have shifted, shorelines have altered and the original riverbed may now lie beneath many metres of mud. Even if the bell remains intact, it could be deeply buried rather than resting on the riverbed surface.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/divers-probe-rivers-depths-latest-search-dhammazedi-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Divers Probe River’s Depths in Latest Search for Dhammazedi Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Irrawaddy Divers Probe River’s Depths in Latest Search for Dhammazedi Bell</span></span></span>

Second, the search area itself is uncertain. Different interpretations of historical descriptions place the sinking at slightly different locations around the confluence of the Yangon River, the Bago River and nearby waterways. Small errors in reconstructing a seventeenth-century route translate into large search areas today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/divers-probe-rivers-depths-latest-search-dhammazedi-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Divers Probe River’s Depths in Latest Search for Dhammazedi Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Irrawaddy Divers Probe River’s Depths in Latest Search for Dhammazedi Bell</span></span></span>

## The major search expeditions

Interest in recovering the bell increased during the late twentieth century. From the late 1980s onward, government-backed projects, private entrepreneurs and foreign specialists all proposed recovery operations.

Several expeditions combined sonar mapping, dredging and diver inspections. Some were supported by wealthy business figures, while others attracted international engineering companies interested in applying marine survey technology. Despite optimistic announcements, none produced independently verified evidence that the bell had been located.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/tycoon-plans-salvage-attempt-sunken-dhammazedi-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Tycoon Plans Salvage Attempt of Sunken Dhammazedi Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Irrawaddy Tycoon Plans Salvage Attempt of Sunken Dhammazedi Bell</span></span></span>

The best-known recent effort took place in 2014. A salvage team announced that it had identified the bell beneath the river and expected to recover it. The expedition also attracted widespread attention because its organisers publicly referred to supernatural guardians protecting the bell alongside their technical search methods. Although the announcement generated excitement across Myanmar, no recovery followed and no independently confirmed archaeological evidence established that the object had actually been found.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/dragons-add-mythical-twist-search-dhammazedi-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Dragons Add Mythical Twist to Search for Dhammazedi Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IrrawaddyDragons Add Mythical Twist to Search for Dhammazedi BellAugust 26, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 26, 2014</span></span></span>

## Could the bell still be underwater?

Several possibilities remain consistent with the available evidence.

The first is that the traditional account is broadly correct and the bell still lies beneath deep sediment somewhere within the lower Yangon river system. Many historians regard this as plausible because Balbi confirms the bell existed and later traditions consistently describe its loss in the river.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/spirits-prayers-mark-hunt-burmas-lost-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery still matters

The Dhammazedi Bell is important not only because of its possible monetary value but because it represents a powerful historical narrative. In Myanmar's popular memory, the story transforms an attempted act of conquest into one of failure: a sacred object is stolen, yet the plunderer never profits from it. That symbolism helps explain why each new search attracts national attention far beyond the archaeological question itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/features/murky-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy A Murky Story</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The IrrawaddyA Murky StoryMay 17, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 17, 2014</span></span></span>

The case also illustrates how historical mysteries persist when evidence is incomplete rather than absent. An eyewitness account strongly supports the bell's existence, while centuries of tradition preserve the story of its disappearance. Between those points lie missing records, changing landscapes and unsuccessful expeditions that prevent historians from reaching a definitive conclusion. Until an authenticated recovery—or compelling contrary evidence—emerges, the Great Dhammazedi Bell will remain one of Myanmar's most enduring unresolved historical puzzles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/spirits-prayers-mark-hunt-burmas-lost-bell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irrawaddy.com">[The Irrawaddy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irrawaddy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Irrawaddy Spirits, Prayers Mark Hunt for Myanmar’s Lost Bell</span></span></span>
