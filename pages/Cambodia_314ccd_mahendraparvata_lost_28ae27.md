## Introduction

Mahendraparvata is often described as [Cambodia]({{ 'what-remains-unsolved-in-cambodias-past/' | relative_url }})'s "lost city", but that label is only partly true. The ninth-century royal centre on Phnom Kulen was never completely unknown. Local communities had long lived among its temples, French scholars had linked the plateau to the early Khmer capital more than a century ago, and archaeologists had mapped scattered monuments before modern technology arrived. What changed was not the discovery of an unknown city, but the ability to see the entire urban landscape at once. Airborne lidar (Light Detection and Ranging), which uses laser pulses to measure the ground beneath forest cover, revealed roads, neighbourhoods, waterworks and planned layouts that had been almost impossible to recognise from the ground. The real mystery, therefore, is not whether Mahendraparvata existed, but why its true scale remained hidden for so long and what that newly revealed landscape tells us about the beginnings of the Khmer Empire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

## Was Cambodia's "lost city" ever truly lost?

The phrase "lost city" suggests a place forgotten by everyone until a dramatic modern discovery. Mahendraparvata does not fit that story.

Historical inscriptions had already associated Phnom Kulen with the reign of Jayavarman II, the ruler traditionally credited with establishing the Khmer Empire in AD 802. French researchers in the early twentieth century proposed that the plateau was the location of Mahendraparvata, although they lacked enough archaeological evidence to map an urban centre. Explorers identified temples, inscriptions and other remains, but these appeared as isolated monuments scattered across dense forest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

The plateau itself also remained familiar to local people. Phnom Kulen has long been an important religious landscape, with active pilgrimage sites, villages and sacred places. During the late twentieth century, however, archaeological work became especially difficult because of the region's rugged terrain, dense vegetation and its occupation by Khmer Rouge forces until the 1990s. These conditions slowed systematic survey rather than erasing knowledge of the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

Calling Mahendraparvata a "lost city" therefore reflects a modern media narrative more than the historical record. The city was not forgotten; its overall form simply could not be recognised from ground level.

## What was known before lidar?

Before airborne laser surveys, archaeologists already understood several important facts.

They knew that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Phnom Kulen contained numerous ninth-century brick temples linked to Jayavarman II.
* Inscriptions referred to Mahendraparvata as an early royal centre.
* Earlier mapping projects had documented many monuments across the plateau.
* The region appeared important in the political transition that led to later Angkorian civilisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

</div>

The unanswered question concerned scale. Were these temples isolated ceremonial sites? A loose collection of settlements? Or parts of a planned capital?

Dense tropical forest made the answer extremely difficult to obtain. Small earthworks, roads, ponds and embankments disappear beneath vegetation, while modest changes in ground level are almost invisible to archaeologists walking through woodland. Individual structures could be recorded, but their relationships to one another remained uncertain.

## What the laser surveys actually revealed

Lidar transformed understanding by removing the visual barrier created by forest. Instead of showing only surviving stone monuments, the surveys exposed subtle earthworks across the entire plateau.

Researchers identified a planned urban landscape covering roughly 40–50 square kilometres, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* major roads arranged on a deliberate grid;
* neighbourhood blocks divided into smaller land parcels;
* hundreds of ponds;
* dams and water-management features;
* an unfinished large reservoir;
* palace precincts and ceremonial architecture integrated into a single planned city.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[cambridge.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

</div>

This was a major shift in interpretation. The city was no longer understood as scattered temples connected by speculation, but as a coherent capital planned according to a recognisable urban design.

Equally important, lidar allowed archaeologists to verify patterns across an entire landscape instead of relying on isolated excavations. Many features remain almost invisible on the ground even today despite being clearly identifiable in the laser data.

## Population, waterworks and unanswered questions

The lidar discoveries showed that Mahendraparvata supported a substantial community rather than serving purely as a ceremonial centre. The presence of neighbourhoods, palace areas, local shrines and extensive infrastructure indicates a functioning royal capital with administrators, craftspeople, religious specialists and other residents. Exactly how many people lived there, however, remains uncertain. Unlike later Angkor, where larger archaeological datasets exist, Mahendraparvata has not produced enough excavated evidence to support confident population estimates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

Water management presents another unresolved question.

Researchers identified dams and what appears to be an ambitious but unfinished reservoir. Earlier interpretations sometimes assumed that Khmer hydraulic engineering primarily supported irrigated rice agriculture, but Mahendraparvata complicates that picture. Some major waterworks appear experimental or incomplete, suggesting that the plateau may have served as an early stage in developing engineering ideas later refined at Angkor. Scholars continue to debate exactly how these systems functioned and whether all were ever fully operational.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

Environmental evidence also suggests centuries of occupation rather than a brief political experiment. Sediment cores indicate sustained land use and periods of soil erosion, implying that human activity continued long after the city's foundation. Changes in water management during later centuries hint at evolving relationships between Phnom Kulen and the expanding Angkorian heartland.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3885555/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

## Why the "lost city" story persists

Media reports naturally focused on the dramatic phrase "ancient lost city discovered beneath the jungle." That framing captured public attention, but it oversimplified what archaeologists had actually achieved.

The real breakthrough lay in combining earlier historical knowledge with new technology. Inscriptions had already pointed researchers towards Mahendraparvata. Earlier surveys had documented temples. Local communities had never abandoned Phnom Kulen. Lidar did not reveal an unknown civilisation; it revealed the urban structure connecting evidence that had previously appeared isolated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because it shows how archaeology often advances. Revolutionary discoveries frequently arise not from finding entirely new places, but from seeing familiar places in fundamentally new ways.

## Why Mahendraparvata still matters

Mahendraparvata occupies a crucial position in understanding the origins of the Khmer Empire. The lidar surveys demonstrated that sophisticated urban planning existed on Phnom Kulen before the monumental cityscape of Angkor reached its greatest extent. The evidence suggests deliberate city planning, integrated infrastructure and ambitious engineering at a formative stage of Khmer state development.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/mahendraparvata-an-early-angkorperiod-capital-defined-through-airborne-laser-scanning-at-phnom-kulen/CAC3E93D6046CC27D862C1E333FD0713" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentMahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom K...</span></span></span>

At the same time, important questions remain unresolved. Archaeologists continue to investigate the city's population, the precise functions of its waterworks, the duration of its occupation and the reasons why political focus shifted away from Phnom Kulen towards the Angkor plain. Those are genuine historical mysteries.

The enduring myth is therefore not that Mahendraparvata vanished without trace. It is the popular belief that modern science suddenly found a city nobody knew existed.
