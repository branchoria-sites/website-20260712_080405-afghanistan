## Introduction

The mystery of Port Royal is not whether a city disappeared beneath the sea. That is one of the best-documented natural disasters in Caribbean history. The enduring puzzle is how much of the drowned town still survives, what it can reveal about seventeenth-century colonial life, and how much may never be recovered. When a powerful earthquake struck on 7 June 1692, large sections of Port Royal collapsed through waterlogged sand into Kingston Harbour, creating one of the world's most remarkable underwater archaeological sites. More than three centuries later, archaeologists have uncovered streets, houses, taverns, warehouses and thousands of everyday objects, yet much of the submerged settlement remains unexplored beneath layers of sediment. Recent recognition as a UNESCO World Heritage Site has highlighted not only the site's exceptional preservation but also the many unanswered archaeological questions that continue to shape research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1595/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre The Archaeological Ensemble of 17th Century Port Royal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreThe Archaeological Ensemble of 17th Century Port Royal - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

## The 1692 earthquake and collapse

Port Royal occupied a narrow sand spit guarding the entrance to Kingston Harbour. By the late seventeenth century it had become one of the English Empire's busiest Caribbean ports, famous for trade, privateering and its strategic naval position. The catastrophe that struck in June 1692 was caused not simply by violent shaking but by soil liquefaction. Water-saturated sand temporarily behaved like liquid, causing buildings to tilt, sink or slide into the harbour almost intact.

This unusual process explains why Port Royal remains archaeologically exceptional. Instead of being levelled and rebuilt over centuries, parts of the town were rapidly sealed beneath sand and mud. In many places walls, streets and room layouts survived in their original positions, creating an underwater landscape unlike most colonial settlements, where later construction destroyed earlier evidence. UNESCO describes the surviving terrestrial and submerged remains as an outstanding illustration of a seventeenth-century English urban settlement whose archaeological integrity is unusually high.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/?id_decision=8966" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre UNESCO World Heritage Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreUNESCO World Heritage Centre - Decision - 47 COM 8B.32...</span></span></span>

The collapse, however, was not uniform. Some districts disappeared beneath several metres of water, others subsided only partially, and many areas were disturbed by later storms, harbour activity and centuries of sediment movement. This uneven preservation is one reason why the archaeological story remains incomplete.

## What archaeology has uncovered

Scientific underwater archaeology began transforming understanding of Port Royal during the twentieth century. Rather than discovering a legendary pirate city frozen in time, researchers found evidence of an ordinary but prosperous colonial town whose everyday life had been unexpectedly preserved.

Excavations have revealed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Stone and brick houses with recognisable rooms and doorways.
* Streets and property boundaries still traceable beneath harbour sediment.
* Taverns, warehouses and commercial buildings reflecting the town's role in Atlantic trade.
* Pottery, glassware, pipes, coins, tools and household goods abandoned where they fell.
* Organic materials preserved unusually well because oxygen-poor sediments slowed decay.

</div>

These discoveries have changed historians' understanding of Port Royal. Popular culture often reduces it to a pirate haven, yet the archaeological record shows a sophisticated commercial settlement serving merchants, sailors, officials, artisans and enslaved labourers alongside privateers. The surviving buildings provide evidence for urban planning, domestic life and trade networks that written records alone could never reconstruct.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1595/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre The Archaeological Ensemble of 17th Century Port Royal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreThe Archaeological Ensemble of 17th Century Port Royal - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

Equally important is what archaeologists have not done. Modern investigations generally favour careful recording, mapping and conservation over large-scale excavation. Once artefacts are removed from their stable underwater environment they require expensive conservation, meaning many buried deposits are intentionally left untouched for future generations equipped with better technology.

### Why Port Royal is often compared with Pompeii

Port Royal is frequently described as the "Pompeii of the Caribbean", but the comparison has limits.

Both sites preserve moments of sudden catastrophe, yet Port Royal differs in important ways. Pompeii was buried beneath volcanic ash on land. Port Royal entered a marine environment where tides, biological growth, harbour traffic and changing sediment constantly reshape the archaeological landscape. Preservation can therefore be extraordinary in one location and poor only metres away.

The underwater setting also makes investigation slower, more expensive and technically demanding. Visibility is often limited, excavation seasons are shorter, and many structures remain inaccessible beneath later deposits.

## Missing graves, treasure and unanswered questions

The greatest mysteries surrounding Port Royal are archaeological rather than legendary. Researchers know substantial parts of the town remain buried, but many key questions remain unresolved.

### How much of the town still survives?

Only portions of the submerged settlement have been systematically investigated. Large areas remain hidden beneath sediment, while other sections lie beneath modern harbour infrastructure or are difficult to access without disturbing fragile deposits.

As a result, archaeologists still cannot produce a complete picture of the drowned town's street plan, neighbourhoods or building distribution. UNESCO's evaluation of the site noted the continuing need for comprehensive inventories of both submerged and terrestrial archaeological resources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/7402/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre UNESCO World Heritage Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreUNESCO World Heritage Centre - Decision - 43 COM 8B.39...</span></span></span>

### Where are the missing burials?

One of the best-known unresolved questions concerns graves lost during the earthquake.

The cemetery that contained the burial of the privateer and later Lieutenant-Governor Sir Henry Morgan is believed to have disappeared beneath the harbour after the earthquake. Despite extensive archaeological work, neither Morgan's grave nor many other burials from the submerged cemetery have been securely identified. Whether these graves remain preserved beneath sediment, were destroyed during collapse or have been altered by later geological processes remains uncertain.

The mystery is significant because securely identified burials could provide valuable information about health, diet, ancestry and social life in colonial [Jamaica]({{ 'jamaica/' | relative_url }}), extending well beyond famous historical figures.

### Is there really pirate treasure below the harbour?

Treasure legends remain among Port Royal's most persistent myths, but the archaeological evidence is much less dramatic.

Historical records confirm that enormous wealth passed through the port during its commercial peak. Privateers captured valuable cargoes, merchants handled international trade, and ships carried precious metals and luxury goods. However, no credible archaeological evidence supports claims of vast undiscovered pirate hoards hidden beneath the submerged city.

Instead, excavations have consistently recovered the kinds of objects archaeologists most value: household possessions, commercial goods, building materials and personal belongings. These artefacts reveal how people actually lived rather than confirming romantic stories of buried chests filled with gold.

## Why so much remains unexplored

It might seem surprising that a famous site has not been fully excavated after more than 300 years. In reality, several factors make complete investigation both impractical and undesirable.

First, underwater archaeology is slow and expensive. Every object must be mapped, recovered carefully and conserved, often over many years.

Second, excavation is destructive. Once archaeologists remove sediment, the original context can never be recreated. Modern practice therefore favours preserving large sections in place until improved recording techniques become available.

Third, the site remains vulnerable to natural and human pressures, including hurricanes, coastal erosion, harbour development, pollution and biological activity. Managing these threats has become as important as uncovering new discoveries. UNESCO's inscription and associated management plans emphasise long-term conservation of both the underwater and terrestrial remains rather than rapid excavation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1595/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre The Archaeological Ensemble of 17th Century Port Royal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreThe Archaeological Ensemble of 17th Century Port Royal - UNESCO World Heritage Centre...</span></span></span>

## Why the archaeology still matters

Port Royal is important not because it confirms colourful pirate legends, but because it preserves a rare archaeological snapshot of the early English Caribbean. Few colonial towns have survived with such a combination of standing remains, written records and sealed archaeological deposits.

The unresolved questions continue to drive research. Future surveys using improved sonar, underwater photogrammetry, remotely operated vehicles and non-invasive mapping may reveal neighbourhoods that remain hidden beneath harbour sediment without disturbing them. Equally significant is the possibility that untouched deposits could illuminate aspects of everyday colonial life—including commerce, architecture, disease, foodways and the experiences of enslaved Africans—that written records recorded only imperfectly.

The mystery of Port Royal therefore lies less in spectacular lost treasure than in the extraordinary amount of ordinary history still concealed beneath Kingston Harbour. Each carefully excavated building has shown that the submerged city contains evidence capable of reshaping understanding of Jamaica's colonial past, while vast sections remain silent beneath the seabed, waiting for future archaeological investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/?id_decision=8966" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre UNESCO World Heritage Centre</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreUNESCO World Heritage Centre - Decision - 47 COM 8B.32...</span></span></span>
