## Introduction

The mystery surrounding the hidden *Hunminjeongeum* manuscript is not about whether the book exists. Most scholars accept that a genuine second copy of the rare explanatory edition survives. The unresolved question is where it is, what condition it is in, and whether the South Korean state will ever recover it. For nearly two decades, the manuscript has been at the centre of one of the country's most unusual cultural heritage disputes, pitting private possession against public ownership and raising difficult questions about how a nation protects treasures that are legally claimed but physically out of reach.

Unlike many historical mysteries, this case is documented through court records, government investigations and public statements. Yet the manuscript itself remains hidden, reportedly in the hands of the man who first revealed it in 2008, despite repeated court rulings that ownership belongs to the state. Its uncertain whereabouts, reported fire damage and continuing secrecy mean that one of Korea's most important historical documents may still be deteriorating beyond the reach of conservators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20190722001700315" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.yna.co.kr">[Yonhap News Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.yna.co.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2019</span></span></span>

## Why the manuscript matters

The disputed manuscript is a copy of the *Hunminjeongeum Haerye*, the detailed explanation published in 1446 describing the principles behind the Korean alphabet created under King Sejong. While the original proclamation introducing the alphabet is historically significant, the explanatory edition is especially valuable because it explains why the letters were designed as they were and how they should be used.

For decades, only one complete historical copy—the famous Kansong copy—was known to exist. That manuscript became one of [South Korea]({{ 'what-still-haunts-south-koreas-history/' | relative_url }})'s most celebrated cultural treasures and was later included in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register. When another authentic copy emerged in 2008, scholars immediately recognised that it could provide fresh evidence about the history of the Korean writing system. According to experts, the newly revealed manuscript contains marginal notes and textual features not found in the Kansong version, making it valuable even though the underlying text is largely the same.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2019/07/17/koreanHeritage/Struggle-to-retrieve-treasured-manuscript-continues-Supreme-Courts-ruling-that-Huminjeongeum-belongs-to-CHA-creates-new-options/3065643.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[koreajoongangdaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Struggle to retrieve treasured manuscript continues: Supreme Court’s ruling that ‘Huminjeongeum’ belongs to CHA creates...</span></span></span>

The importance of the second copy therefore lies not simply in its rarity but in the possibility that it preserves details lost from the better-known manuscript. Every surviving fifteenth-century copy has its own physical history, annotations and condition, all of which matter to historians of printing, language and book culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2021/07/25/culture/koreanHeritage/nft-Hunminjeongeum-haerye-hangul/20210725153900570.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[koreajoongangdaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">koreajoongangdaily Is a national treasure still valuable in digital form?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">koreajoongangdaily Is a national treasure still valuable in digital form?</span></span></span>

## The ownership dispute and court battles

The modern dispute began in 2008 when collector Bae Ik-gi publicly announced that he possessed a second *Hunminjeongeum Haerye* manuscript in Sangju. Authentication by the Cultural Heritage Administration confirmed that the book itself appeared genuine, but almost immediately another antique dealer claimed that the manuscript had been stolen from his collection years earlier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20190722001700315" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.yna.co.kr">[Yonhap News Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.yna.co.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2019</span></span></span>

What followed was a long series of legal battles that shifted the question from authenticity to ownership.

The broad sequence is well established:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The antique dealer successfully argued in court that he was the lawful owner.
* Before his death in 2012, he donated his legal ownership rights to the South Korean government.
* The Cultural Heritage Administration inherited those rights and sought recovery of the manuscript.
* In 2019, South Korea's Supreme Court confirmed that legal ownership belonged to the government rather than to Bae.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20190722001700315" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.yna.co.kr">[Yonhap News Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.yna.co.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2019</span></span></span>

</div>

Despite those rulings, the manuscript has never been recovered.

Bae has continued to refuse to surrender it, arguing that he deserves substantial compensation because of the manuscript's enormous cultural value. Public reports have repeatedly stated that he demanded around 100 billion won in exchange for handing it over, while government officials maintained that court judgments—not private negotiations—determine ownership.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2021/07/25/culture/koreanHeritage/nft-Hunminjeongeum-haerye-hangul/20210725153900570.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[koreajoongangdaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">koreajoongangdaily Is a national treasure still valuable in digital form?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">koreajoongangdaily Is a national treasure still valuable in digital form?</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to the mystery. Legally, the state claims ownership. Physically, it does not possess the manuscript.

## The search, fire damage and conservation risk

The greatest concern today is no longer simply who owns the manuscript but whether it can still be preserved.

In 2015, a fire at Bae's residence reportedly damaged the hidden manuscript. Only limited photographs of scorched pages have been released publicly, making it difficult for independent specialists to assess the extent of the damage. Heritage officials have repeatedly warned that continued concealment places the book at serious risk from humidity, mould, insects and further deterioration because medieval paper manuscripts require carefully controlled conservation conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20190722001700315" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.yna.co.kr">[yna.co.kr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.yna.co.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2019</span></span></span>

Since the fire, the manuscript's exact location has remained undisclosed.

Although government officials have discussed legal measures to recover it, actually locating the book has proved far more difficult than establishing ownership in court. Officials have acknowledged that compulsory recovery is legally possible, but a hidden object cannot easily be seized if investigators do not know where it is being stored.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20190722001700315" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.yna.co.kr">[Yonhap News Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.yna.co.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2019</span></span></span>

This has transformed the dispute into an unusual heritage dilemma:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the state possesses recognised legal title;
* the physical manuscript remains outside government control;
* conservation experts cannot examine or stabilise it;
* every additional year increases the possibility of irreversible damage.

</div>

The mystery is therefore partly archival and partly practical. The question is not whether the manuscript once existed, but whether it can be recovered before its physical condition declines further.

## What remains uncertain

Several important aspects of the case are settled.

The manuscript's authenticity has been accepted by the Cultural Heritage Administration after expert examination. Multiple courts have ruled on ownership, culminating in a Supreme Court judgment recognising the government's legal rights. The existence of fire damage has also been publicly acknowledged through photographs and official statements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20190722001700315" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.yna.co.kr">[Yonhap News Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.yna.co.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 2019</span></span></span>

Other questions remain unresolved.

The current condition of the manuscript is unknown because independent conservation experts have not been allowed to examine it since the reported fire. Likewise, the precise location where it is stored has not been publicly confirmed. Estimates of its monetary value vary enormously—from hundreds of billions of won to figures exceeding one trillion won—reflecting both its rarity and the difficulty of placing a market price on an object regarded as a national cultural treasure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2021/07/25/culture/koreanHeritage/nft-Hunminjeongeum-haerye-hangul/20210725153900570.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[koreajoongangdaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">koreajoongangdaily Is a national treasure still valuable in digital form?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">koreajoongangdaily Is a national treasure still valuable in digital form?</span></span></span>

There is also uncertainty over whether any additional scholarly information could still be obtained from the manuscript. Earlier reports noted that it contains annotations absent from the Kansong copy, but if fire or poor storage has caused substantial damage, some of that research value may already have been lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/2021/07/25/culture/koreanHeritage/nft-Hunminjeongeum-haerye-hangul/20210725153900570.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: koreajoongangdaily.joins.com">[koreajoongangdaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">koreajoongangdaily.joins.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">koreajoongangdaily Is a national treasure still valuable in digital form?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">koreajoongangdaily Is a national treasure still valuable in digital form?</span></span></span>

## Why the dispute still matters

The hidden *Hunminjeongeum* manuscript has become more than a disagreement over ownership. It illustrates the tension between private property rights, cultural heritage law and the public interest in preserving nationally important historical objects.

For South Korea, the case poses a difficult governance question: when a privately held artefact is recognised as part of the nation's shared cultural heritage, how should the law balance individual possession against public preservation?

Unlike stories of lost treasure that depend largely on legend, this mystery revolves around a documented object whose legal status is largely settled but whose physical recovery remains incomplete. Until the manuscript is recovered—or its fate is definitively established—it will remain one of South Korea's most important unresolved archival mysteries, with the greatest risk being not theft or disappearance, but the slow destruction of an irreplaceable historical record while it remains hidden.
