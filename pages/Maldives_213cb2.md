## Who first settled the Maldives?

The origins of the Maldivian population remain uncertain because the archipelago left little early written history of its own. The islands are small, low-lying and exposed to monsoon weather, while old buildings were frequently dismantled and their coral stone reused. Later religious change also altered or destroyed some traces of pre-Islamic life. As a result, historians have had to combine archaeology, inscriptions, language, oral traditions and evidence from travellers who viewed the islands from outside.

What is well established is that the Maldives had a substantial pre-Islamic civilisation. Archaeological remains include Buddhist stupas, monasteries, sculptures and ritual objects. A major study of Maldivian archaeology noted that definite physical evidence of Buddhism was first recognised in the 1920s, although centuries of weathering, rebuilding and deliberate destruction had left the record fragmented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_1987_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">befeo 0336 1519 1987 num</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PerséeThe Pre-Islamic Archaeology of the Maldive Islandsby A Forbes · 1987 · Cited by 25 — It was not until 1922 that definite archaeolog...</span></span></span> More recent work at Kaashidhoo has identified several phases of a Buddhist monastic complex, with the earliest occupation possibly beginning around the third century AD and later construction continuing for centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.kon-tiki.no/en/news/the-archaeology-of-buddhism-in-the-maldives" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kon-tiki.no">[Kon-Tiki museet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kon-tiki.no</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kon-Tiki museetThe Archaeology of Buddhism in the MaldivesThe project documented three phases of the Buddhist monastery at Kaashidhoo. This evidence does not reveal one simple moment of colonisation. Genetic research based on samples from several Maldivian communities found a strong South Asian ancestry but also signs of multiple, separate migrations. The researchers described the islands as an Indian Ocean crossroads rather than the product of a single founding voyage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://repub.eur.nl/pub/39699" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: repub.eur.nl">[RePub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">repub.eur.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Indian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin and...1 May 2013 — Indian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin. Maldivian populatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2013</span></span></span> That conclusion fits the Maldives’ position between Sri Lanka, southern India, Arabia, East Africa and the maritime routes of Southeast Asia.

Older national traditions sometimes present particular exiled princes, royal dynasties or named peoples as the original settlers. Such stories may preserve memories of genuine migration, but they were written down long after the supposed events and cannot presently be verified as literal accounts. The safest conclusion is that settlement occurred in stages and involved several populations, probably with the strongest early links to South Asia.

### Why Thor Heyerdahl’s theory remains controversial

The explorer Thor Heyerdahl popularised a more dramatic theory after investigating Maldivian ruins in the 1980s. He interpreted some ancient mounds and structures as evidence of a pre-Buddhist, sun-worshipping civilisation connected with distant maritime cultures. His book *The Maldive Mystery* presented the archipelago as part of a very old network of oceanic migration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Maldive_Mystery.html?id=LcmFAAAAIAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books The Maldive Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksThe Maldive Mystery - Thor HeyerdahlThe enduring author of Kon-Tiki now unravels one of archaeology&#x27;s most beguiling island m...</span></span></span>

Heyerdahl helped draw international attention to Maldivian archaeology, but specialists have generally been more cautious about his conclusions. Critics argue that the supposed pyramids and sun temples were largely collapsed Buddhist stupas whose original shapes had been obscured by erosion and stone removal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maldivesroyalfamily.com/maldives_romero_heyerdahl.shtml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maldivesroyalfamily.com">[maldivesroyalfamily.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maldivesroyalfamily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Since islanders took away stones from those Stupas for building their homes...Read more...</span></span></span> Archaeological evidence supports extensive Indian Ocean contact, but it does not establish the sweeping migration narrative that Heyerdahl proposed.

The real mystery is therefore less sensational and more historically useful: the Maldives was never as isolated as its geography suggests, yet the precise sequence of early arrivals remains beyond recovery. Future excavations may clarify regional connections, but identifying one founding civilisation is unlikely.

## What happened when the Maldives converted to Islam?

The Maldives’ conversion to Islam in the twelfth century is a historical event surrounded by one of the country’s most famous legends. The basic facts are comparatively secure: the Buddhist monarchy became Islamic, the ruler adopted a Muslim name and title, and an Islamic sultanate emerged. A scholarly study of the conversion places it within the wider commercial and religious world of the Indian Ocean, where Muslim merchants had been active long before the formal change at court.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.jstor.org/stable/23731038" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jstor.org">[JSTOR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jstor.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CONVERSION OF MALDIVES TO ISLAMby HA Maniku · 1986 · Cited by 23 — Islam in the Maldives is a subject, the firs approach to which mu...</span></span></span>

The mystery concerns the identity of the religious teacher credited with the conversion and the meaning of the supernatural story attached to him.

The best-known version comes from the fourteenth-century traveller Ibn Battuta. He was told that the islands had once been terrorised by a sea being demanding the regular sacrifice of a young woman. A Muslim visitor took the intended victim’s place, spent the night reciting the Quran and drove the creature away. The king then accepted Islam. In Ibn Battuta’s account, the visitor was Abu al-Barakat Yusuf al-Barbari, apparently a man from the western Islamic world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.yabiladi.com/articles/details/66201/when-moroccan-merchant-al-barakat-yusuf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.yabiladi.com">[Yabiladi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.yabiladi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When Moroccan merchant Abu al-Barakat Yusuf al-Barbari</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Moroccan merchant Abu al-Barakat Yusuf al-Barbari</span></span></span>

Other Maldivian historical traditions identify the missionary differently, associating him with Tabriz in Persia rather than North Africa. The disagreement may reflect the unreliability of names transmitted orally, competition between later religious traditions or the blending of several visitors into one founding figure. Arabic inscriptions and surviving chronicles establish a medieval Islamic presence, but they do not remove every contradiction in the conversion narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_2005_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Persée Inscriptions islamiques en arabe de l&#x27;archipel des Maldives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Persée Inscriptions islamiques en arabe de l&#x27;archipel des Maldives</span></span></span>

The sea-monster episode belongs to folklore rather than verifiable history. It may symbolise the defeat of an older ritual, a local spirit belief or Buddhism itself. It may also be a familiar type of conversion tale in which a new religious specialist proves his power by overcoming a danger that the old order cannot control. There is no dependable evidence that young women were actually sacrificed to a monster.

The legend nevertheless matters because it gives the national conversion a clear moral drama: fear is replaced by faith, a vulnerable victim is spared and the ruler recognises a new religious truth. In this form, the story has been repeated in education, public memory and popular retellings. Its cultural importance is real even where its supernatural details are not historical.

## Why the Buddhist past is so difficult to reconstruct

The uncertainty surrounding early Maldives is not merely the result of age. Parts of the physical record have been actively lost.

Buddhist sites were frequently quarried after the conversion to Islam. Coral blocks and other materials could be reused in mosques, graves and houses, leaving once recognisable monuments as shapeless mounds. Climate, vegetation and coastal erosion caused further damage. Written records from the pre-Islamic period are extremely scarce, so archaeology carries an unusually heavy burden.

A major additional loss occurred in February 2012, when attackers entered the National Museum in Malé and smashed pre-Islamic objects, including Buddhist sculptures. Museum personnel described the damage as devastating to the surviving record of the country’s ancient heritage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maldivesindependent.com/society/mob-storms-national-museum-destroys-buddhist-statues-a-significant-part-of-our-heritage-is-lost-now-31813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maldivesindependent.com">[Maldives Independent]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maldivesindependent.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Maldives Independent Mob storms National Museum, destroys Buddhist statues</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maldives Independent Mob storms National Museum, destroys Buddhist statues</span></span></span> These were not merely valuable works of art. In a country with few early texts, each statue or carved fragment could contain evidence about artistic influences, religious practice and overseas connections.

The destruction illustrates why the Maldivian past can become politically sensitive. Archaeological evidence of Buddhism does not challenge the well-established place of Islam in modern Maldivian identity, but some extremists have treated pre-Islamic objects as religiously unacceptable rather than historically informative. When artefacts disappear, later theories become harder to test and myths become easier to repeat.

This is also why confident claims about secret temples, lost races or unknown ancient religions should be approached carefully. The archaeological record is incomplete, but an incomplete record is not evidence for any theory that can be imagined. The surviving material strongly supports a long Buddhist period linked to South Asian and Indian Ocean networks. What remains uncertain is its detailed chronology, regional variation and relationship with earlier beliefs.

## What happened at Havaru Thinadhoo in 1962?

The destruction and depopulation of Havaru Thinadhoo is one of the most painful and disputed episodes in modern Maldivian history. It occurred during the collapse of the United Suvadive Republic, a breakaway state established in the southern atolls amid tensions over taxation, political authority, trade and the British military presence at Addu.

Thinadhoo became a centre of southern resistance. In January 1962, Prime Minister Ibrahim Nasir travelled south aboard the *Silver Crest* with an armed government force. The island was brought back under central control, its inhabitants were expelled and much of the settlement was destroyed. Residents were dispersed to other islands and were not permitted to resettle Thinadhoo until several years later.

These broad facts are not seriously in doubt. The controversy concerns the scale and nature of the violence. Survivor accounts describe gunfire, intimidation, looting, imprisonment, abuse and deaths during or after displacement. Some modern retellings use terms such as “massacre”, “ethnic cleansing” or “genocide” and give very high estimates for deaths caused by shooting, hunger, disease and forced removal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depopulation_of_Havaru_Thinadhoo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Depopulation of Havaru Thinadhoo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Depopulation of Havaru Thinadhoo</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that figures repeated in public debate are not supported by a complete, independently verified list of victims. Contemporary state documentation is limited, testimony was collected late, and the political reputations of both the central government and the southern separatists have shaped later narratives. Sources also differ over the degree of local support for the breakaway republic and over violence committed by its supporters against opponents.

Calling the episode a mystery should not obscure what is known. A civilian population was collectively punished, forcibly displaced and deprived of homes and property. Even where precise casualty figures remain disputed, the operation inflicted severe human suffering. The historical uncertainty concerns the number of deaths, the details of individual abuses and the extent to which decisions were planned at the highest level.

Thinadhoo remains important because it exposes a division between the political history told from Malé and memories preserved in the southern atolls. The event is not a legend waiting for an ingenious solution. It is an incompletely documented episode whose surviving witnesses and families have sought recognition, records and a fuller public accounting.

## Why Ahmed Rilwan’s disappearance still matters

Journalist and blogger Ahmed Rilwan Abdulla disappeared on 8 August 2014. He had written about religious extremism, political affairs and Maldivians travelling to foreign conflicts. Before his disappearance, he had received threats linked to his public commentary.

Evidence later assembled by investigators indicated that Rilwan was followed, abducted near his home and killed by members of a local extremist group. A presidential commission announced findings connecting the crime to an affiliate sympathetic to al-Qaeda and criticised failures in the original police investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://maldivesindependent.com/crime-2/translation-summary-of-report-on-rilwans-abduction-147805" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maldivesindependent.com">[Maldives Independent]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maldivesindependent.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Maldives Independent Translation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Maldives Independent Translation</span></span></span> The Committee to Protect Journalists documented the failed prosecution of two men accused of involvement in the abduction, while human-rights organisations have continued to describe the case as an enforced disappearance without justice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cpj.org/tags/ahmedrilwanabdulla/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cpj.org">[Committee to Protect Journalists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cpj.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cpj.org.</span></span></span>

The central uncertainty is no longer simply whether Rilwan left voluntarily, an early suggestion contradicted by later evidence. The unresolved questions concern the exact sequence of his final hours, the location of his remains, the full membership of the group involved and whether officials deliberately obstructed or merely mishandled the investigation.

The case entered Maldivian public memory through the persistence of Rilwan’s family, colleagues and supporters. Their campaign challenged official inaction and kept attention on threats to journalists and critics of extremism. It also demonstrated how delay damages an investigation: witnesses’ memories fade, physical evidence disappears and suspected networks have time to coordinate their accounts.

Rilwan’s fate therefore represents a different type of national mystery from the origins of the islands or the [conversion legend]({{ 'conversion-legend/' | relative_url }}). The evidence points towards a crime whose broad outline is understood. What remains missing is the complete truth required for legal responsibility and a body that his family can bury.

## What the strongest evidence allows us to say

The Maldives’ historical enigmas are largely products of fragmentation rather than the supernatural. Its first inhabitants cannot be reduced to one legendary migration; archaeology and genetics instead indicate repeated movement through a busy Indian Ocean world. The country’s conversion to Islam is historical, but the famous defeat of a sea demon is best understood as a powerful conversion legend, not a record of an actual creature or confirmed sacrifice.

The Buddhist past is real but difficult to recover because monuments were dismantled, records disappeared and museum objects were destroyed. Heyerdahl’s theories brought attention to this lost world, though his proposed sun-worshipping civilisation goes beyond the available evidence.

In modern history, Havaru Thinadhoo and Ahmed Rilwan show that uncertainty can also be political. Thinadhoo’s forced depopulation is established, while its total death toll and the full extent of abuses remain contested. Rilwan was almost certainly the victim of a targeted extremist abduction and killing, yet the absence of his remains and incomplete accountability keep the case open in the public mind.

What connects these mysteries is not evidence of a hidden master narrative. It is the vulnerability of history in a scattered island country where documents are limited, physical remains are fragile and powerful institutions have sometimes controlled which memories are preserved.

    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
