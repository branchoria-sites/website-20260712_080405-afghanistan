## Introduction

Tchitundu-Hulu is [Angola]({{ 'what-remains-unsolved-in-angolas-past/' | relative_url }})'s most celebrated prehistoric rock art complex, yet its greatest mystery remains unresolved: nobody knows with certainty who created its thousands of engravings and paintings, when every phase of decoration took place, or exactly what many of the symbols meant. Located on the edge of the Namib Desert in present-day Namibe Province, the site preserves one of the richest collections of ancient imagery in the country. Archaeologists agree that it represents a long-lived ceremonial and artistic landscape rather than a single moment in history, but the identities of its creators remain debated because the surviving art spans different styles, techniques and probably different periods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Angola_a42522_tchitundu_hulu_rock_99e2b0-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Rock Art illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Angola_a42522_tchitundu_hulu_rock_99e2b0-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Angola_a42522_tchitundu_hulu_rock_99e2b0-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Unlike speculative claims about "lost civilisations", the real mystery is archaeological. The evidence suggests repeated use of the site over centuries, perhaps even longer, by communities whose names, beliefs and traditions are only partly recoverable from the archaeological record.

## What survives at Tchitundu-Hulu

Tchitundu-Hulu is not one rock shelter but a complex of four related sites: Tchitundu-Hulu Mulume, Tchitundu-Hulu Mucai, Pedra das Zebras and Pedra da Lagoa. Together they occupy isolated rocky hills, or inselbergs, rising from the semi-arid plain roughly 120 kilometres inland from the Atlantic coast. Seasonal watercourses nearby would have made the area an attractive stopping place despite its harsh environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>

The surviving art is remarkably varied. Visitors encounter:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Thousands of pecked engravings covering exposed rock surfaces.
* Painted shelters containing red, white and occasionally black pigments.
* Concentric circles, spirals, grids and other geometric motifs.
* Human figures and animals.
* Possible hunting scenes and abstract symbolic compositions.
* Layers where newer paintings overlap older ones, showing the site was revisited repeatedly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>

</div>

The geometric designs are especially striking. Rather than realistic pictures, many consist of repeated circles, dots, parallel lines and enclosed shapes. Because no written explanation survives, archaeologists cannot confidently identify their purpose. They may have represented ritual symbols, territorial markers, cosmological ideas or something entirely different.

One important clue is that the engravings and paintings are not all alike. Differences in weathering, colour, carving technique and superimposed images strongly suggest that several artistic episodes occurred rather than one single act of decoration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>

## Dating the paintings and engravings

Establishing the age of rock art is notoriously difficult, and Tchitundu-Hulu illustrates why.

Unlike a buried settlement, paintings on exposed rock rarely contain organic material that can easily be dated. Archaeologists therefore combine several forms of evidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Excavated archaeological layers beneath or beside decorated shelters.
* Radiocarbon dating of rare pigment samples.
* Relative dating based on which images overlap others.
* Comparisons with similar artistic traditions elsewhere in Central and southern Africa.
* Depictions of recognisable technologies, such as metal weapons or firearms, which must belong to later periods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/introduction/chronologies/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Chronologies</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Chronologies</span></span></span>

</div>

The main chronological evidence points in different but compatible directions.

Excavations at Tchitundu-Hulu Mulume produced archaeological material dating to roughly the early first millennium BC. However, researchers cannot prove that these deposits were created by the same people who painted every image on the shelter walls. A rare radiocarbon date obtained from pigment instead suggests at least one painting belongs to the first centuries AD. These dates imply that parts of the complex are around two thousand years old, but they do not establish a single age for every engraving and painting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>

Indeed, the rock art almost certainly accumulated over an extended period. Some engravings appear considerably older than others because of differences in surface weathering, while elsewhere in Angola depictions of firearms demonstrate that rock art traditions continued into the period of European contact after the sixteenth century. Tchitundu-Hulu itself therefore appears to preserve multiple chapters of artistic activity rather than one frozen moment in prehistory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Angola</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Angola</span></span></span>

## Who created the rock art?

This is the central mystery.

No continuous oral tradition identifies the artists, and the archaeological evidence does not link the images securely to a single named community. Instead, scholars consider several possibilities.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Angola_a42522_tchitundu_hulu_rock_99e2b0-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Rock Art illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Angola_a42522_tchitundu_hulu_rock_99e2b0-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Angola_a42522_tchitundu_hulu_rock_99e2b0-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

### Local hunter-gatherers

One possibility is that some of the earliest paintings were created by Later Stone Age hunter-gatherer groups who occupied or seasonally visited the region before later pastoral societies arrived.

This interpretation fits the great antiquity suggested by some archaeological evidence and the widespread association between hunter-gatherers and prehistoric rock art across southern Africa. However, there is no direct proof connecting a particular excavated population with specific paintings at Tchitundu-Hulu.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>

### Ancestors of later pastoral communities

Researchers have also examined whether communities historically associated with the region, including the Kwisi and Kuvale, may have produced at least some of the art.

The problem is chronological. These groups occupied or used the landscape much later than some of the oldest archaeological evidence, and there is no uninterrupted historical tradition explaining that they created every panel. Even if they contributed to later phases, they may not have been responsible for the earliest engravings and paintings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>

### Multiple generations of artists

Many archaeologists now regard this as the strongest explanation.

The combination of differing artistic styles, overlapping images, varying weathering and evidence for repeated occupation suggests that successive communities probably added new artwork over many centuries. Instead of asking who created Tchitundu-Hulu, the better question may be who contributed to its long history at different times.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>

## What might the images have meant?

The meanings of many motifs remain just as uncertain as their makers.

Some images are comparatively straightforward. Human figures, animals and possible hunting scenes probably relate to everyday experience, ritual or storytelling. The geometric signs are much harder to interpret.

Several explanations have been proposed:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Ceremonial symbolism.** The shelters may have served as sacred places used during rituals over many generations.
* **Landscape markers.** The prominent hills could have marked important stopping places near seasonal water and travel routes.
* **Shared symbolic traditions.** Similar geometric styles occur elsewhere in Central Africa, suggesting participation in broader artistic traditions rather than complete isolation.
* **Multiple meanings through time.** Different communities may have reused older images while assigning them new significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/tchitundu-hulu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Tchitundu Hulu</span></span></span>

</div>

No interpretation has gained universal acceptance because the images cannot be linked securely with written records or continuous cultural traditions. Archaeologists therefore distinguish carefully between evidence-supported interpretation and speculation.

## Why claims about lost civilisations are not supported

Because the engravings are visually unusual, Tchitundu-Hulu has occasionally attracted imaginative explanations involving forgotten advanced societies or astronomical knowledge.

Current archaeological evidence does not support these ideas.

Instead, researchers note that similar geometric rock art traditions appear across parts of Central and southern Africa. The motifs fit within recognised regional artistic traditions rather than requiring an unknown civilisation or external origin. The site's true importance lies not in extraordinary claims but in the insight it offers into prehistoric symbolic behaviour and long-term human occupation of Angola's south-west.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://africanrockart.britishmuseum.org/country/angola/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africanrockart.britishmuseum.org">[African Rock Art]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africanrockart.britishmuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">African Rock Art Angola</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">African Rock Art Angola</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery still matters

Tchitundu-Hulu remains one of Angola's most important archaeological sites precisely because so much has survived while so many questions remain unanswered.

Recent heritage projects have focused on detailed recording through high-resolution photography, three-dimensional scanning and digital documentation before erosion damages additional engravings and paintings. These records not only preserve the site but may eventually allow researchers to detect superimpositions, pigment composition and weathering patterns that were previously impossible to study in detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ifas.org.za/research/uncategorized/dig-arq-2023-2026/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ifas.org.za">[French Institute of South Africa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ifas.org.za</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dig arq</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">French Institute of South AfricaDIG-ARQ – Digitalisation, Accessibility, and Heritage Preservation of Archaeology in Angola (2024-2027) –...</span></span></span>

The complex has also been placed on Angola's UNESCO Tentative List, reflecting its outstanding cultural significance and the hope that continued conservation and research will eventually clarify its chronology and history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/statesparties/ao/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Angola</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreAngola - UNESCO World Heritage Convention...</span></span></span>

For now, the enduring mystery is not whether Tchitundu-Hulu is genuine—it unquestionably is—but how many different communities contributed to it, over what span of time, and what stories its intricate circles, figures and symbols once told to the people who created them.
