## Did the island of Kuwae really explode?

The destruction of [Kuwae]({{ 'kuwae/' | relative_url }}) is Vanuatu’s largest historical and geological enigma. Oral traditions from the central islands describe a populated landmass between Epi and Tongoa that was shaken by earthquakes, overwhelmed by volcanic violence and partly swallowed by the sea. Survivors are said to have escaped to neighbouring islands before returning or founding new communities elsewhere.

There is no serious doubt that a major eruption occurred in the Kuwae area during the fifteenth century. Geological surveys have found thick volcanic deposits, while the submarine depression between Epi and Tongoa is associated with a caldera—an enormous collapse feature formed when a volcano’s magma chamber is emptied or disrupted. Recent reviews combining archaeology, volcanology and oral history conclude that the eruption had severe regional consequences and transformed the local landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13617-023-00138-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[springer.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">His specific goal was to investigate the...Read more...</span></span></span>

The unresolved question is not simply whether Kuwae erupted. It is **how large the eruption was, exactly when it happened and whether it caused climatic disruption around the world**.

For decades, researchers connected Kuwae with a large concentration of volcanic sulphate found in polar ice dating from the middle of the fifteenth century. Historical records from distant regions also describe unusual cold, atmospheric haze and poor growing conditions during roughly the same period. This encouraged claims that Kuwae had produced one of the greatest eruptions of the past 2,000 years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gfzpublic.gfz.de/pubman/faces/ViewItemOverviewPage.jsp?itemId=item_5016725_2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gfzpublic.gfz.de">[GFZ Public]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gfzpublic.gfz.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">View Item Overview Page.jsp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GFZ PublicThe eruption of Kuwae, Vanuatu, and its effects on...by S Stern · 2023 — Its source remains highly debated to this day, but th...</span></span></span>

That neat solution gradually became less secure. Radiocarbon measurements from Vanuatu do not provide a single uncontested eruption year, and much of the volcano lies underwater, where its deposits are difficult to map. Researchers also questioned whether the calculated volume of erupted material had been exaggerated and whether every climatic signal attributed to Kuwae came from the same eruption.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/2012/05/kuwae-eruption-of-the-1450s-missing-or-mythical-caldera" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">kuwae eruption of the 1450s missing or mythical caldera</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">kuwae eruption of the 1450s missing or mythical caldera</span></span></span>

A 2025 study complicated the picture further. Microscopic volcanic glass recovered from Antarctic ice associated with the 1458–59 sulphate event showed two chemical compositions. The researchers linked one group of particles to Kuwae and another to Reclus volcano in southern Patagonia. Their findings suggest that the atmospheric record may preserve material from more than one eruption rather than a single Vanuatu event responsible for the entire climatic disturbance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02797-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nature.com.</span></span></span>

### What is firmly established?

Several conclusions are now reasonably secure:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A destructive fifteenth-century eruption occurred at or near Kuwae.
* It caused major environmental and social disruption in central Vanuatu.
* Oral traditions preserve recognisable elements of volcanic catastrophe, evacuation and resettlement.
* Kuwae probably contributed volcanic material to the atmosphere on a very large scale.
* Its exact share of the global sulphate spike and climate disturbance remains disputed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13617-023-00138-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[springer.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">His specific goal was to investigate the...Read more...</span></span></span>

</div>

The idea that a single explosion instantly destroyed a vast island and changed weather across the entire planet may be too simple. Activity could have involved several eruptive stages, while some atmospheric material may have originated elsewhere. Yet attempts to dismiss the whole event as legend are equally difficult to defend. Geology confirms that something extraordinary happened.

The real mystery is therefore not “myth or fact”. It is how multiple genuine events—eruption, caldera collapse, migration and possibly eruptions on another continent—became compressed into one dramatic historical narrative.

## Who was the last Roi Mata?

Chief Roi Mata occupies an unusual position between remembered ancestor and archaeologically documented historical figure. Oral traditions on Efate, Lelepa and Artok describe a powerful chief who brought an end to warfare, reorganised social relationships and was buried with numerous followers after dying at around the beginning of the seventeenth century.

The traditions identify three principal places: Mangaas, where he lived; Fels Cave on Lelepa, where he died; and Artok Island, where he was buried. These locations form Chief Roi Mata’s Domain, Vanuatu’s first UNESCO World Heritage property. UNESCO regards the landscape as an exceptional example of oral tradition, archaeology and customary restrictions reinforcing one another over roughly four centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1280/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata&#x27;s DomainThe property includes Roi Mata&#x27;s residence, the site of his death and Roi Mata&#x27;s mass...</span></span></span>

The case became internationally famous after the archaeologist José Garanger investigated the stories during the 1960s. On Artok, excavators uncovered a large communal grave broadly matching the location described by local custodians. The burial contained the remains of a high-status man and numerous other individuals, some arranged with ornaments and grave goods in ways consistent with remembered accounts of the chief’s funeral.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on unesco.org.</span></span></span>

This was not proof that every episode in the traditions was literally true. It was something more historically important: evidence that oral knowledge had preserved the location and broad character of a centuries-old burial that had not been identified through written records.

### What remains uncertain?

“Roi Mata” was a chiefly title rather than necessarily a unique personal name. More than one title-holder may have contributed to later stories, and researchers continue to examine whether traditions about a single heroic ruler combined the actions of several people. Precise dates for the last Roi Mata’s life are unknown, although the archaeological evidence supports a date around 1600.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on unesco.org.</span></span></span>

The cause of his death is also unresolved. Oral accounts describe illness following a feast, sometimes accompanied by suggestions of poisoning or supernatural attack. Archaeology cannot determine whether he died naturally, was deliberately killed or became the subject of political storytelling after his death.

The burial raises still more difficult questions. UNESCO’s assessment notes that it was remarkable for both the number of people interred and the apparent willingness with which some were said to have accompanied the chief in death. Physical remains can reveal age, sex, health and burial position, but they cannot directly establish consent. The suggestion of voluntary death belongs principally to oral tradition and interpretation, not demonstrable forensic fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on unesco.org.</span></span></span>

Likewise, the claim that Roi Mata personally ended a great period of warfare may preserve a real political settlement while simplifying a longer process. His death, the abandonment of Mangaas and the dispersal of its population appear to be connected, but the precise sequence remains unclear.

The most plausible interpretation is that the traditions retain a genuine historical core: an influential title-holder died, received an exceptional burial and became associated with a major political reorganisation. Over generations, that core acquired moral lessons, supernatural details and explanations for later social customs.

## Why were the Teouma dead missing their heads?

In 2003, quarrying near [Teouma]({{ 'teouma/' | relative_url }}) Bay on Efate exposed human bones and decorated pottery. Subsequent excavation revealed a cemetery about 3,000 years old, associated with the Lapita people—the seafaring settlers whose rapid expansion carried communities through Remote Oceania.

Teouma is one of the most important archaeological discoveries in the Pacific because it contains the earliest known large cemetery from the Lapita period. It offered rare direct evidence about the bodies, diets, origins and funerary customs of some of Vanuatu’s first settlers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://os.pennds.org/archaeobib_filestore/pdf_articles/Reports/2007_Spriggs.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: os.pennds.org">[os.pennds.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">os.pennds.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2007 Spriggs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2007 Spriggs</span></span></span>

What made the discovery initially appear macabre was the treatment of the dead. Many adult skeletons had no skull in its expected position. Some skulls had been removed after decomposition; others were placed in pottery vessels or associated with different burials. At least one funerary arrangement involved several skulls positioned on the chest of another individual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281063209_Three-thousand-year-old_jar_burials_at_the_Teouma_Cemetery_Vanuatu_A_Southeast_Asian_-_lapita_connection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Three-thousand-year-old jar burials at the Teouma</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) Three-thousand-year-old jar burials at the Teouma</span></span></span>

The evidence does not support the idea of a massacre or routine beheading at death. Archaeologists instead interpret the cemetery as evidence of **multi-stage funerals**. A body might first be buried intact, then reopened after the soft tissue had decomposed. The skull or other bones could be removed, retained, displayed, placed in a vessel or reburied later.

This practice made the cemetery a place of repeated activity rather than a collection of sealed graves. The dead remained involved in the social world of the living, perhaps as ancestors whose bones carried identity, authority or spiritual significance. Researchers have compared aspects of the Teouma burials with traditions found in Island Southeast Asia, although the precise relationship—shared ancestry, transmitted custom or independent development—is debated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/three-thousand-year-old-jar-burials-at-the-teouma-cemetery-vanuat/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchportalplus.anu.edu.au">[The Australian National University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchportalplus.anu.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">three thousand year old jar burials at the teouma cemetery vanuat</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">three thousand year old jar burials at the teouma cemetery vanuat</span></span></span>

### Were the people foreigners?

Chemical analysis of teeth has provided another layer to the mystery. Strontium isotopes reflect the geological environment in which a person obtained food and water while growing up. Studies of the Teouma individuals found that some had spent their childhood away from the immediate burial area, supporting the view that the cemetery belonged to an early migrant population rather than a community established on Efate for many generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-prehistoric-society/article/west-mouth-neolithic-cemetery-niah-cave-sarawak/F45B5C4B5E0B0A5F71AF6341A73B9174" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cambridge.org.</span></span></span>

The evidence does not identify a single island from which each person came. Nor does it settle the larger question of exactly how Lapita communities were organised. Teouma may have served one settlement, several related groups or people moving through an expanding network of islands.

The removed skulls are therefore not an unsolved crime. They are a partially understood ritual system. Archaeology explains the physical process reasonably well but cannot yet recover its full meaning: who was selected for special treatment, how long bones were kept, whether skulls represented named ancestors, and why the cemetery ceased to be used.

Teouma matters because it replaces a sensational image—“headless bodies”—with a more challenging mystery about migration, memory and relationships between the living and dead.

## What happened during the Malakula punitive expeditions?

Some of Vanuatu’s deepest historical uncertainties were created not by the absence of documents but by the survival of documents written by those using violence.

During the period when the New Hebrides were administered jointly by Britain and France, warships and armed parties repeatedly carried out punitive expeditions against island communities. These operations were commonly presented in colonial records as responses to killings, resistance or alleged offences. The surviving paperwork tends to describe official objectives and actions from the viewpoint of naval officers, administrators and settlers.

A major expedition in north-eastern Malakula in 1916 involved British, French and Australian personnel, together with local auxiliaries. Archaeologist Stuart Bedford’s study compares official archives with oral traditions and material evidence, revealing substantial tension between the colonial record and community memories of the operation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3489006_2/component/file_3489007/content" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pure.mpg.de">[MPG.PuRe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pure.mpg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pu Re11</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives, oral traditions and archaeologyby S Bedford · 2022 · Cited by 1 — Here the focus is on the 1916 expedition undertaken on the no...</span></span></span>

Official language could make such actions sound controlled and lawful. Local accounts instead preserve memories of indiscriminate shooting, village destruction, deaths and fear extending well beyond any clearly identified suspects. The difference is not merely one of emotional emphasis. It affects basic historical questions: how many people died, which settlements were attacked, whether those targeted had any connection to the original incident and whether the expedition’s true purpose was punishment, intimidation or the assertion of colonial authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3489006_2/component/file_3489007/content" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pure.mpg.de">[MPG.PuRe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pure.mpg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pu Re11</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives, oral traditions and archaeologyby S Bedford · 2022 · Cited by 1 — Here the focus is on the 1916 expedition undertaken on the no...</span></span></span>

### Why the records do not agree

Colonial reports were administrative instruments. Officers had reasons to present expeditions as necessary, disciplined and successful. Casualties among island communities could be underestimated, described vaguely or omitted, especially when bodies were not counted or when deaths occurred later from wounds, displacement or destroyed food supplies.

Oral traditions have different limitations. Accounts can merge separate attacks, move events between places or emphasise the experiences of particular descent groups. Names and sequences may change as stories pass between generations. Yet oral evidence can also preserve information that official writers had no interest in recording, including abandoned settlements, remembered escape routes and the identities of victims.

Archaeology can test parts of both narratives by locating former villages, ammunition, burnt structures or patterns of sudden abandonment. It rarely produces a complete casualty list, but it can expose places where the official story is physically or geographically implausible.

The mystery of Malakula is therefore not an invitation to choose blindly between “archive” and “tradition”. The strongest reconstruction comes from comparing both while recognising the unequal power under which the written record was produced.

The broad conclusion is disturbing but not especially mysterious: colonial forces used severe and sometimes indiscriminate violence. What remains unresolved is the full scale of particular expeditions and the identities of many people affected by them. Those gaps matter because bureaucratic descriptions such as “punitive action” can conceal individual deaths, destroyed communities and long-lasting displacement.

## How oral tradition changes the investigation

The cases of Kuwae, Roi Mata and Malakula demonstrate that oral tradition in Vanuatu is neither a flawless recording nor material that can be dismissed as folklore. It is historical evidence created and maintained for purposes that may include ancestry, land rights, chiefly authority, mourning and moral teaching.

At Kuwae, community narratives preserve the shape of a volcanic disaster confirmed in broad terms by geology. In Roi Mata’s Domain, remembered locations guided archaeologists to an exceptional burial. On Malakula, oral accounts challenge the completeness and neutrality of colonial paperwork.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13617-023-00138-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[springer.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">His specific goal was to investigate the...Read more...</span></span></span>

Yet correspondence between tradition and physical evidence does not make every detail literal. A sea parting before a funeral procession, supernatural explanations for illness or exact speeches attributed to historical leaders belong to a different level of claim from the existence of a grave, eruption or abandoned settlement.

A useful way to assess these mysteries is to separate four categories:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Materially supported history:** an eruption at Kuwae, the Artok burial and complex bone treatment at Teouma.
* **Reasonable historical inference:** large-scale migration after volcanic destruction or political change after Roi Mata’s death.
* **Disputed interpretation:** Kuwae’s precise climatic impact, the voluntary nature of accompanying deaths and the casualty figures from punitive expeditions.
* **Sacred or legendary detail:** elements carrying cultural and moral meaning that cannot be tested by archaeology alone.

</div>

This approach avoids two common mistakes. The first is treating local traditions as primitive stories waiting for outsiders to explain them. The second is accepting every remembered detail as a literal eyewitness account. Vanuatu’s historical record is strongest where communities, archaeologists, historians and scientists examine the evidence together.

## Which explanations are most plausible?

The best-supported reading of Vanuatu’s principal enigmas is less sensational than many popular retellings, but more interesting.

**Kuwae was a real catastrophe**, although its size, date and responsibility for global cooling have often been stated too confidently. The latest scientific evidence allows Kuwae to remain an important source of fifteenth-century atmospheric material while indicating that another volcano may also have contributed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02797-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nature.com.</span></span></span>

**Roi Mata was probably a real chiefly figure or title-holder whose life became the centre of a powerful historical tradition.** Archaeology supports the remembered landscape and extraordinary burial, but cannot verify every deed, motive or supernatural episode.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1280/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata&#x27;s DomainThe property includes Roi Mata&#x27;s residence, the site of his death and Roi Mata&#x27;s mass...</span></span></span>

**The Teouma dead were not unexplained beheading victims.** Their remains show deliberate, complex funerary treatment carried out in several stages. The ritual’s physical procedures are increasingly clear; its beliefs and social rules remain only partly recoverable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/lapita-burial-practices-evidence-for-complex-body-and-bone-treatm/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchportalplus.anu.edu.au">[The Australian National University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchportalplus.anu.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">lapita burial practices evidence for complex body and bone treatm</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Australian National UniversityLapita burial practices: Evidence for complex body and bone...by F Valentin · 2010 · Cited by 67 — Div...</span></span></span>

**The gaps surrounding colonial violence on Malakula are partly archival creations.** Official reports recorded what colonial authorities wished to justify, while local memories preserved consequences and victims that paperwork obscured. The uncertainty concerns scale and detail, not whether coercive violence occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3489006_2/component/file_3489007/content" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pure.mpg.de">[MPG.PuRe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pure.mpg.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pu Re11</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives, oral traditions and archaeologyby S Bedford · 2022 · Cited by 1 — Here the focus is on the 1916 expedition undertaken on the no...</span></span></span>

## Why these mysteries still matter

Vanuatu’s unresolved past is closely connected to living questions of identity, authority, land and historical justice. Roi Mata’s story continues to shape customary relationships and cultural protection. Kuwae’s eruption remains part of community memory as well as modern research into volcanic risk. Teouma has changed scientific understanding of the first settlers of Remote Oceania. Reassessment of punitive expeditions restores agency and suffering to people who appeared in colonial documents chiefly as targets or obstacles.

These mysteries also reveal a wider lesson: uncertainty does not mean that every explanation deserves equal weight. Some conclusions are supported by excavated graves, dated deposits or chemical analysis. Others rest on indirect comparison. Some belong to sacred tradition rather than testable history, while modern claims of lost civilisations, secret treasure or grand conspiracies have little or no reliable evidence behind them.

Vanuatu’s most enduring enigmas survive not because nothing is known, but because several kinds of knowledge overlap without fitting perfectly. A vanished landscape lies beneath the sea. A chief’s grave confirms a story without explaining every part of it. Ancient bones reveal actions but not beliefs. Colonial records describe campaigns while concealing much of their human cost. The result is a national history in which the remaining gaps are not empty spaces: they are places where evidence, memory and power still meet.
