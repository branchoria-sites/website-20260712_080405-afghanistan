## Introduction

On the night of 22 November 1970, a Portuguese-led amphibious raid struck Conakry, the capital of [Guinea]({{ 'what-still-cannot-be-proved-about/' | relative_url }}), in one of the most audacious operations of the Portuguese Colonial War. Known as **Operation Green Sea** (Portuguese: *Mar Verde*), the attack was real, carefully planned and partly successful. Portuguese commandos, supported by Guinean exiles, freed captured Portuguese soldiers, damaged facilities used by the independence movement fighting Portugal in neighbouring Portuguese Guinea (now Guinea-Bissau), and briefly seized key locations in the city. Yet they failed in their larger political goals, including capturing independence leader Amílcar Cabral or toppling President Ahmed Sékou Touré.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Mar_Verde.html?id=ojojEQAAQBAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[google.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mar Verde</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksMar Verde: The Portuguese Amphibious Assault on Conakry, 1970 - José Augusto Matos - Google BooksSeptember 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 30, 2021</span></span></span>

The enduring mystery is not whether the raid happened, but how it became the justification for one of the largest waves of political repression in Guinea's history. The government portrayed the attack as proof of a vast internal conspiracy involving officials, soldiers and civilians. Hundreds were arrested, many disappeared into [Camp Boiro]({{ 'camp-boiro/' | relative_url }}) and other prisons, and numerous executions followed after proceedings that independent observers have regarded as lacking credible judicial safeguards. The genuine foreign attack and the domestic purge became inseparably linked, even though the evidence connecting many of those accused to the raid has never been convincingly demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/abs/history-is-stubborn-talk-about-truth-justice-and-national-reconciliation-in-the-republic-of-guinea/BC519EAFB35267A2289BC85BE01E3C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment“History is stubborn”: Talk about Truth, Justice, and National Reconciliation in the Republic of G...</span></span></span>

## What the raiders planned and achieved

Operation Green Sea emerged from overlapping but not identical objectives.

Portugal had been fighting the African Party for the Independence of Guinea and Cape Verde (PAIGC), whose leadership operated from Conakry with the support of Sékou Touré's government. Portuguese planners hoped to weaken this sanctuary while also rescuing Portuguese prisoners captured during the colonial war. Some also hoped that removing Touré would cripple support for the insurgency across the border.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Mar_Verde.html?id=ojojEQAAQBAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mar Verde</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksMar Verde: The Portuguese Amphibious Assault on Conakry, 1970 - José Augusto Matos - Google BooksSeptember 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 30, 2021</span></span></span>

The Guinean exile movement that joined the operation had different ambitions. Members of the Front for the National Liberation of Guinea (FLNG) believed that if they entered Conakry with Portuguese support, popular unrest and military defections would bring down Touré's government. Their expectation proved badly mistaken. Instead of triggering a mass uprising, the attack encountered organised resistance and rapidly lost momentum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/entangled-political-histories-of-twentiethcentury-west-africa-the-case-of-guinean-exile-networks/DB200192A43D16F6275C932C5717E6B3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentEntangled political histories of twentieth-century West Africa: The case of Guinean exile networks...</span></span></span>

Despite its ultimate failure, the raid achieved several concrete objectives:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Portuguese prisoners held by the PAIGC were rescued.
* Some PAIGC naval assets and facilities around Conakry were destroyed.
* Government buildings, including the national radio station, were temporarily occupied.
* The attackers withdrew before Guinean forces could completely encircle them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Mar_Verde.html?id=ojojEQAAQBAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mar Verde</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksMar Verde: The Portuguese Amphibious Assault on Conakry, 1970 - José Augusto Matos - Google BooksSeptember 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 30, 2021</span></span></span>

</div>

The principal goals, however, were missed. Touré remained in power, Amílcar Cabral escaped capture, and the PAIGC quickly resumed its operations from Guinea. Instead of weakening Touré politically, the failed coup attempt allowed him to consolidate power more aggressively than before.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Mar_Verde.html?id=ojojEQAAQBAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mar Verde</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksMar Verde: The Portuguese Amphibious Assault on Conakry, 1970 - José Augusto Matos - Google BooksSeptember 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 30, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why key military details remain disputed

Unlike many Cold War military operations, Operation Green Sea has never produced a single uncontested narrative.

One point once heavily disputed was Portugal's involvement. Lisbon initially denied responsibility, implying that Guinean dissidents alone had organised the assault. The United Nations Security Council's Special Mission reached a different conclusion after investigating events on the ground. It concluded that the invasion force had been assembled in Portuguese Guinea and consisted of Portuguese naval and military units acting together with Guinean dissidents. Security Council Resolution 290 subsequently condemned the attack.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.refworld.org/legal/resolution/unsc/1970/en/80004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: refworld.org">[Refworld]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">refworld.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Security Council resolution 290 (1970) [Complaint by Guinea] &#124; Refworld...</span></span></span>

Other operational questions remain less certain because participants pursued different objectives and later memoirs sometimes conflict.

Among the continuing areas of debate are:

* **The precise size of the invasion force.** Contemporary estimates generally place it between roughly 350 and 400 men, but exact figures vary between sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/859580/files/1122291-EN.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ited Nations Digital Library SystemYEARBOOK OF THE...</span></span></span>
* **Whether overthrowing Touré was Portugal's primary objective.** Some historians argue that destroying PAIGC infrastructure and freeing prisoners were the principal military goals, while others conclude that regime change formed an integral part of Portuguese planning alongside those objectives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/Mar_Verde.html?id=ojojEQAAQBAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mar Verde</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google BooksMar Verde: The Portuguese Amphibious Assault on Conakry, 1970 - José Augusto Matos - Google BooksSeptember 30, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 30, 2021</span></span></span>
* **How close the attackers came to success.** Some accounts portray the operation as narrowly failing through poor coordination and intelligence, whereas others argue that expectations of an internal uprising were unrealistic from the outset because Touré's security apparatus remained largely intact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/entangled-political-histories-of-twentiethcentury-west-africa-the-case-of-guinean-exile-networks/DB200192A43D16F6275C932C5717E6B3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; AssessmentEntangled political histories of twentieth-century West Africa: The case of Guinean exile networks...</span></span></span>

These uncertainties concern operational history rather than the central facts. The existence of Portuguese participation, the landing in Conakry and the mixed Portuguese-exile composition of the force are now well documented through UN investigations and subsequent historical research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.refworld.org/legal/resolution/unsc/1970/en/80004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: refworld.org">[refworld.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">refworld.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Security Council resolution 290 (1970) [Complaint by Guinea] &#124; Refworld...</span></span></span>

## How the attack justified mass repression

The greatest historical significance of Operation Green Sea lies less in the military raid than in what followed.

Touré's government declared that the invasion had exposed an extensive domestic network of collaborators. Senior ministers, military officers, civil servants, intellectuals and ordinary citizens were accused of belonging to hidden conspiracies connected with Portugal or foreign intelligence services. Arrests spread rapidly across Guinea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/abs/history-is-stubborn-talk-about-truth-justice-and-national-reconciliation-in-the-republic-of-guinea/BC519EAFB35267A2289BC85BE01E3C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment“History is stubborn”: Talk about Truth, Justice, and National Reconciliation in the Republic of G...</span></span></span>

Many detainees were imprisoned at Camp Boiro, already notorious as the regime's principal political prison. Numerous suspects disappeared after secret detention, while others died through execution, starvation or abuse. Families often received little reliable information about what had happened to relatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/abs/history-is-stubborn-talk-about-truth-justice-and-national-reconciliation-in-the-republic-of-guinea/BC519EAFB35267A2289BC85BE01E3C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment“History is stubborn”: Talk about Truth, Justice, and National Reconciliation in the Republic of G...</span></span></span>

The central historical puzzle concerns the relationship between the real invasion and the alleged conspiracy inside Guinea.

Few historians dispute that some Guinean exiles participated in the raid and hoped to replace Touré. What remains highly controversial is whether the government uncovered a vast internal network or instead used the genuine foreign attack to eliminate political opponents who had little or no connection to the invasion. ## The unresolved legacy of Green Sea
Operation Green Sea illustrates an important distinction in Guinea's modern history: the attack itself is no longer mysterious, but its political consequences remain only partly understood.

Researchers continue to investigate questions that have never been fully resolved:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* How many people were arrested, executed or died in detention because of the post-raid purge.
* Which accused individuals genuinely collaborated with the attackers.
* How many confessions were extracted under coercion.
* Which state records survive and whether others were deliberately destroyed.
* Where many victims of the repression were buried.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/abs/history-is-stubborn-talk-about-truth-justice-and-national-reconciliation-in-the-republic-of-guinea/BC519EAFB35267A2289BC85BE01E3C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment“History is stubborn”: Talk about Truth, Justice, and National Reconciliation in the Republic of G...</span></span></span>
