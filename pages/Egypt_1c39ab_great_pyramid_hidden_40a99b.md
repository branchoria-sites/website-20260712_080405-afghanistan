## Introduction

The Great Pyramid of Khufu has been explored for centuries, yet it still contains spaces whose purpose is unknown. Modern, non-invasive scanning has revealed previously hidden cavities inside the monument without drilling through its masonry, creating one of [Egypt]({{ 'which-mysteries-of-ancient-egypt-still/' | relative_url }})'s most important archaeological mysteries. The key question is no longer whether these spaces exist—they have been independently confirmed—but why the pyramid's builders created them and what role they played in the monument's design.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Egypt_1c39ab_great_pyramid_hidden_40a99b-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Pyramid Voids illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Egypt_1c39ab_great_pyramid_hidden_40a99b-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Egypt_1c39ab_great_pyramid_hidden_40a99b-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The discoveries have transformed the debate from speculation about secret chambers into a careful investigation of ancient engineering. Researchers have identified two major hidden features: a large void above the Grand Gallery and a smaller corridor behind the pyramid's original northern entrance. Neither has produced evidence of treasure, inscriptions or an undiscovered burial chamber, but both could reshape understanding of how the world's largest pyramid was planned and constructed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery of a big void in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; NatureNovember 2, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2017</span></span></span>

## How muon scans found the hidden spaces

The breakthrough came through **muography**, a technique borrowed from particle physics. High-energy cosmic rays striking Earth's atmosphere continually produce particles called **muons**, which pass through rock more easily than X-rays can penetrate large structures. Thick stone absorbs some muons, while empty spaces allow more of them to reach detectors placed inside or around a monument.

By measuring how many muons arrive from different directions, researchers can create a density map of a structure's interior. Areas where unexpectedly large numbers of muons pass through indicate less dense material, potentially revealing hidden cavities.

The international **ScanPyramids** project combined several independent detection systems, including nuclear emulsion films, scintillator detectors and gas detectors. Crucially, all three methods identified the same large anomaly, giving researchers high confidence that it represented a genuine internal space rather than an instrumental error. This independent confirmation was one of the strongest aspects of the discovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery of a big void in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; NatureNovember 2, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2017</span></span></span>

Unlike earlier generations of pyramid exploration, the technique required no excavation or damage to the monument. Instead, naturally occurring cosmic particles effectively turned the pyramid itself into a giant scientific imaging experiment.

## The two known hidden spaces

### The ScanPyramids Big Void

Announced in 2017, the most famous discovery is the **ScanPyramids Big Void**, located above the Grand Gallery.

Researchers estimate that it is at least **30 metres long**, with a cross-section broadly comparable to the Grand Gallery itself. Its precise shape remains uncertain because muon imaging measures density rather than producing a direct photographic image. It could be:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* one long chamber;
* a sloping corridor;
* several connected compartments; or
* another complex architectural form.

</div>

Current data show where the empty volume exists but not its detailed internal layout.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery of a big void in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; NatureNovember 2, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2017</span></span></span>

### The North Face Corridor

A second discovery became much clearer in 2023.

Behind the large chevron-shaped limestone blocks above the pyramid's original entrance, researchers identified a corridor around **9 metres long** with a cross-section of roughly **2 by 2 metres**. Muon measurements were combined with radar, ultrasound and an endoscopic camera inserted through a tiny existing joint to confirm both the corridor's dimensions and that it was genuinely empty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/S41467-023-36351-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Precise characterization of a corridor-shaped structure in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; Nature Communications...</span></span></span>

Unlike the Big Void, this corridor's position and geometry are now relatively well understood, although its original purpose remains debated.

## What might these spaces have been for?

The discoveries prompted widespread speculation about hidden tombs, sealed archives and undiscovered treasures. However, the evidence currently favours far more practical explanations.

The strongest hypotheses include:

* **Structural stress relief.** The corridor behind the north entrance may have helped redistribute enormous loads above one of the pyramid's weakest points, similar to relieving chambers found elsewhere in Egyptian pyramid architecture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/S41467-023-36351-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Precise characterization of a corridor-shaped structure in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; Nature Communications...</span></span></span>
* **Construction features.** Some researchers suggest the spaces formed part of the building process, perhaps allowing access during construction or accommodating temporary engineering systems later sealed off.
* **Weight reduction above the Grand Gallery.** The Big Void may have reduced pressure on the remarkable corbelled roof of the Grand Gallery beneath it, although its unusual size makes this explanation incomplete for many specialists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery of a big void in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; NatureNovember 2, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2017</span></span></span>

More speculative ideas remain under discussion but currently lack supporting evidence. These include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an undiscovered royal burial chamber;
* hidden ritual rooms;
* symbolic architectural spaces with no practical function; and
* features linked to broader theories about pyramid construction.

</div>

Some scholars have proposed detailed symbolic or engineering interpretations—for example relating the void to funerary traditions or construction mechanisms—but these remain hypotheses rather than accepted conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04617" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A possible explanation of the void discovered in the pyramid of Khufu on the basis of the pyramid textsNovember 13, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2017</span></span></span>

## Why direct exploration remains difficult

Many readers naturally ask why archaeologists do not simply drill into the hidden spaces.

The answer lies in the pyramid's exceptional historical importance. The Great Pyramid is one of the world's most significant archaeological monuments, and Egyptian authorities generally require overwhelming justification before permitting any invasive investigation.

Several practical problems also complicate exploration:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* the exact orientation and internal geometry of the Big Void are still uncertain;
* drilling risks damaging original Fourth Dynasty masonry;
* introducing robotic equipment requires an extremely small access route; and
* any intervention must balance scientific curiosity against long-term conservation.

</div>

The North Face Corridor has already been inspected with a miniature camera because researchers could exploit tiny existing gaps rather than create major new openings. No comparable access currently exists for the much larger Big Void.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1038/S41467-023-36351-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Precise characterization of a corridor-shaped structure in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; Nature Communications...</span></span></span>

## What remains uncertain

Although the discoveries attracted global attention, the list of established facts is surprisingly short.

Researchers know that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the hidden spaces are genuine;
* they were independently confirmed using multiple scientific techniques;
* the Big Void extends for at least 30 metres above the Grand Gallery; and
* the North Face Corridor is a real corridor-like cavity behind the entrance chevrons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery of a big void in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; NatureNovember 2, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2017</span></span></span>

</div>

Researchers do **not** yet know:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the precise shape of the Big Void;
* whether it connects to any known passages;
* whether either space was ever intended for human access;
* whether the voids served structural, constructional or ceremonial purposes; or
* whether additional undiscovered cavities remain elsewhere inside the pyramid.

</div>

This distinction between confirmed observation and interpretation is central to the mystery. Modern physics has demonstrated that hidden spaces exist, but archaeology has not yet determined why the builders created them.

## Separating evidence from popular myths

The discoveries quickly became linked online with claims about secret libraries, lost technologies and hidden civilisations. None of these ideas is supported by the published scientific evidence.

The ScanPyramids findings revealed **empty spaces**, not artefacts. No inscriptions, objects, human remains or hidden chambers containing historical treasures have been documented. Likewise, the discoveries provide no evidence for Atlantis theories or other claims that circulate widely on social media.

Instead, the real mystery is arguably more interesting. The Great Pyramid remains one of humanity's greatest engineering achievements, and these newly detected spaces show that even its internal architecture is not yet fully understood. Each refinement in non-invasive imaging brings researchers closer to understanding how Old Kingdom builders solved immense structural challenges more than 4,500 years ago, while reminding us that careful scientific investigation often raises better questions before it delivers final answers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/nature24647" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery of a big void in Khufu’s Pyramid by observation of cosmic-ray muons &#124; NatureNovember 2, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2017</span></span></span>
