## Introduction

Rongorongo, the undeciphered script of Rapa Nui (Easter Island), is one of [Chile]({{ 'chile/' | relative_url }})'s greatest historical mysteries. The surviving inscriptions are carved on a remarkably small number of wooden objects, and no one has yet demonstrated a translation that convinces the wider scholarly community. The mystery is not that researchers lack ideas, but that the available evidence is too limited. Too few inscriptions survive, there is no bilingual text equivalent to the Rosetta Stone, and the cultural knowledge needed to interpret the script was largely lost during the devastating social upheavals of the nineteenth century. Recent radiocarbon dating has revived the debate by suggesting that at least one inscribed object may pre-date European contact, but this does not by itself prove when the symbols were carved or how the writing system functioned. The enduring puzzle lies in balancing exciting new evidence against the severe limits of what the surviving material can actually reveal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1038%2Fs41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2, 2024</span></span></span>

## What the surviving tablets contain

Today, fewer than 30 authentic rongorongo objects are known. They include wooden tablets, staffs and other carved items now dispersed among museums and collections outside Rapa Nui. Most left the island during the nineteenth century, after missionaries and collectors acquired them while the traditional culture was undergoing catastrophic disruption. Some inscriptions were reportedly destroyed during this period, making the surviving corpus even smaller.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2...</span></span></span>

The inscriptions consist of hundreds of recurring pictorial signs depicting human figures, birds, fish, plants, tools and abstract motifs. Rather than being arranged randomly, the glyphs appear in ordered sequences with repeated patterns. The lines are usually written in reverse boustrophedon style: one line is read in one direction, then the tablet is rotated 180 degrees before continuing with the next line. This distinctive arrangement strongly suggests an organised writing tradition rather than decorative carving.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2...</span></span></span>

Researchers generally agree on several points:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The signs were deliberately standardised rather than improvised.
* Individual glyphs occur in predictable combinations.
* Some inscriptions are carefully corrected, implying trained scribes.
* The surviving corpus is far too small for secure decipherment.

</div>

These observations indicate that rongorongo represents a structured system of communication, but they do not reveal exactly what information it encoded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2...</span></span></span>

## Why decipherment remains so difficult

The central obstacle is not the complexity of the script but the lack of evidence.

Unlike Egyptian hieroglyphs, rongorongo has no bilingual inscription that links its symbols with a known language. There are no surviving documents explaining how the glyphs were read, and by the time sustained scholarly investigation began in the late nineteenth century, people who had mastered the script had apparently disappeared or no longer possessed a complete reading tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2...</span></span></span>

The corpus itself is unusually small. With only a few dozen inscribed objects, statistical analysis has severe limitations. Many tablets are damaged, some contain only fragments, and several may preserve overlapping rather than entirely different texts. Every missing section reduces the possibility of identifying consistent grammatical or phonetic patterns.

The problem is compounded by uncertainty over the underlying language. Although most researchers assume the inscriptions relate to the ancestral East Polynesian language of Rapa Nui, the absence of confirmed readings means even this cannot be demonstrated directly from the texts themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2...</span></span></span>

## Why proposed translations remain disputed

Many researchers have proposed solutions since the nineteenth century, but none has achieved broad acceptance.

Some scholars argue that rongorongo recorded spoken language in a genuine writing system. Others suggest it functioned partly as a mnemonic device, helping trained specialists recite genealogies, rituals or calendars rather than representing every spoken word. A third possibility places it somewhere between these two extremes: more systematic than simple symbolic art but less fully phonetic than familiar writing systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2...</span></span></span>

Most proposed decipherments fail for similar reasons:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* They successfully explain only one or two inscriptions.
* They require inconsistent values for identical glyphs.
* They cannot predict readings across independent texts.
* Other researchers cannot reproduce the claimed results.

</div>

In historical linguistics, successful decipherments normally become convincing because they explain many inscriptions using a single consistent system. Rongorongo has not reached that point. Every proposed reading leaves substantial portions of the corpus unexplained, which is why no translation has become scholarly consensus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/d43978-024-00037-y" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reading between the lines on an ancient island script</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reading between the lines on an ancient island script...</span></span></span>

## What radiocarbon dating can and cannot prove

A major development came in 2024 when researchers directly radiocarbon dated four rongorongo objects preserved in Rome.

Three were made from nineteenth-century wood, broadly matching previous expectations. However, one tablet was found to have been fashioned from wood dating to the mid-fifteenth century, well before the first documented European arrival on Rapa Nui in 1722. If the carving occurred soon after the tree was cut, this would support the possibility that rongorongo developed independently rather than being inspired by exposure to European writing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1038%2Fs41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2, 2024</span></span></span>

The discovery is important, but it has often been overstated.

Radiocarbon dating establishes the age of the wood, not the precise date when the inscription was carved. A tree might have been preserved for decades, reused, or recarved. Although the researchers argue that the characteristics of the wood make long-term storage less likely, the evidence cannot yet eliminate those possibilities. One early tablet also cannot establish the age of the entire writing tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1038%2Fs41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2, 2024</span></span></span>

For that reason, the strongest current conclusion is cautious: the new dates make an independent pre-contact origin more plausible than before, but they do not conclusively prove it.

## Does rongorongo represent true writing?

This question remains one of the most significant debates surrounding the script.

Several features favour the interpretation that rongorongo records language:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* Long sequences of ordered glyphs.
* Repeated sign combinations.
* Evidence that scribes corrected mistakes.
* Consistent structural organisation across multiple objects.

</div>

These characteristics resemble known writing systems more closely than random symbolic decoration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2...</span></span></span>

However, scholars remain cautious because structured symbols do not automatically represent full writing. Systems for recording calendars, ritual performances or genealogies can also develop regular patterns without encoding every element of spoken language. Until reliable phonetic values or grammatical relationships can be demonstrated, the exact nature of rongorongo remains unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/d43978-024-00037-y" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reading between the lines on an ancient island script</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Reading between the lines on an ancient island script...</span></span></span>

## The historical picture around Rongorongo
The importance of rongorongo extends well beyond Rapa Nui.

If it proves to be an independently invented writing system, it would become one of the very few known cases in world history where writing emerged without borrowing from earlier literate cultures. Such a conclusion would reshape understanding of cultural innovation in the Pacific and challenge long-standing assumptions about the island's intellectual history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1038%2Fs41598-024-53063-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doi.org">[DOI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doi.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The invention of writing on Rapa Nui (Easter Island). New radiocarbon dates on the Rongorongo script &#124; Scientific ReportsFebruary 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2, 2024</span></span></span>

A successful decipherment could also recover aspects of Rapa Nui society that are otherwise only partially preserved through oral traditions and archaeology. The inscriptions might contain genealogies, religious knowledge, political history, astronomical observations or ceremonial texts. At present, however, those possibilities remain hypotheses rather than established facts.

For Chile, rongorongo represents a uniquely significant historical puzzle because it combines archaeology, linguistics and Indigenous heritage.
