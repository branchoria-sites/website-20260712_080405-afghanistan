## Introduction

What really happened in [Equatorial Guinea]({{ 'what-remains-hidden-in-equatorial/' | relative_url }}) in March 1969 remains one of the country's most important historical disputes. Five months after independence, President Francisco Macías Nguema announced that he had crushed a coup led by Foreign Minister Atanasio Ndongo Miyone. Within days, several of the new state's most prominent political figures were dead or had disappeared into detention. The official version portrayed Macías as the defender of constitutional government. Many later historians, however, argue that the alleged coup was either exaggerated, poorly organised, or manipulated into a pretext for eliminating rivals and concentrating power. The uncertainty surrounding how leading politicians died, and how genuine the threat to Macías actually was, has made the March 1969 crisis a defining mystery in Equatorial Guinea's modern history.

## The palace confrontation and the alleged coup

The crisis unfolded during the night of 4–5 March 1969 in Bata, then the political centre of the young republic. President Macías accused Foreign Minister Atanasio Ndongo Miyone and several associates of attempting to overthrow his government. Official telegrams sent immediately afterwards to the United Nations stated that the coup had failed, that Ndongo had led the conspiracy, and that Macías had assumed additional ministerial powers in response. These documents provide the earliest official account of the events but naturally reflect the government's own narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/604846/files/S_SUPP_1969_1--%5EOR_SC_1969_1%5E-EN.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System EC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ITY COUNCIL OffiCIAL RECORDS TWENTY.FOURTH YEARNovember 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

There is broad agreement on only a few core facts:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Ndongo was arrested during the crisis.
* Several leading politicians associated with him were detained.
* Macías emerged with dramatically expanded personal authority.
* The political system established at independence effectively ceased to function as intended.

</div>

Almost everything beyond those points is contested.

According to the official version promoted by Macías, Ndongo entered the presidential palace intending to seize power but was surprised by the president, who personally resisted the coup. Some later retellings even claimed Macías physically fought Ndongo before guards intervened. These dramatic details became part of the dictatorship's founding myth.

Many historians question this reconstruction. Research using Spanish diplomatic archives and newly examined documentation suggests there was genuine political plotting against Macías, but that the operation was poorly prepared and far less extensive than official propaganda later claimed. The available evidence also indicates that tensions between Macías and Ndongo had been escalating for months because of disagreements over relations with Spain, control of the armed forces and the direction of the new state. Rather than a large military coup, the evidence points towards a confused political confrontation involving only a limited number of participants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historia-actual.org/Publicaciones/index.php/hao/article/view/1427" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historia-actual.org">[Historia Actual]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historia-actual.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Historia ActualEL GOLPE DE ESTADO CONTRA MACÍAS EN MARZO DE 1969. ¿INTERVENCIÓN ESPAÑOLA O CONFLICTO INTERNO GUINEANO? &#124; Historia Actual...</span></span></span>

Another long-running controversy concerns Spain's role. At the time, relations between Madrid and Malabo had deteriorated sharply despite independence having occurred only months earlier. Some writers have alleged Spanish officials quietly encouraged Ndongo, while others argue Spain merely expected Macías to be replaced without directly organising events. Archival research has uncovered evidence of Spanish concern about Macías but has not produced conclusive proof that Madrid directed the attempted overthrow. Most recent scholarship treats alleged Spanish involvement as plausible but unproven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historia-actual.org/Publicaciones/index.php/hao/article/view/1427" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historia-actual.org">[Historia Actual]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historia-actual.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Historia ActualEL GOLPE DE ESTADO CONTRA MACÍAS EN MARZO DE 1969. ¿INTERVENCIÓN ESPAÑOLA O CONFLICTO INTERNO GUINEANO? &#124; Historia Actual...</span></span></span>

## How Ndongo and Ondo Edu died

The greatest historical mystery is not simply whether a coup occurred but how its principal victims actually died.

### Atanasio Ndongo Miyone

Ndongo's death has never been described consistently.

The official account maintained that he was injured while attempting the coup and later died after being detained. Other versions circulated during Macías's rule claimed he had jumped or been thrown from a palace window during a struggle.

Numerous later testimonies contradict those claims. Some witnesses reported that Ndongo had been summoned to the presidential palace rather than storming it. Others alleged he was shot or beaten by presidential guards before imprisonment. Several accounts state that, after suffering severe injuries, he was killed inside prison rather than during the confrontation itself. Because no independent judicial investigation took place and the regime tightly controlled information, these conflicting versions have never been reconciled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au/issues/pnpa47/pnpa47_05.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au">[mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.au.</span></span></span>

The result is an unusual historical situation. Historians generally agree that Ndongo died because of the political crisis, but the precise sequence of events, the identities of those directly responsible and the location of his fatal injuries remain disputed.

### Bonifacio Ondó Edu

The fate of Bonifacio Ondó Edu, Equatorial Guinea's first prime minister under the autonomous colonial administration and Macías's principal electoral rival in 1968, is similarly uncertain.

Ondó Edu sought refuge during the crisis but was eventually arrested. Official explanations of his subsequent death varied. Some accounts suggested suicide while imprisoned; many historians instead regard his death as an extrajudicial killing carried out on Macías's orders or with the regime's approval. Unlike Ndongo, whose death occurred immediately after the confrontation, Ondó Edu disappeared into state custody before dying under circumstances that have never been independently clarified.

The absence of transparent records, forensic evidence or impartial investigations means neither version can be demonstrated conclusively. Most modern historical works nevertheless regard the official explanation with considerable scepticism because similar official accounts were repeatedly used during the Macías dictatorship to conceal political killings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au/issues/pnpa47/pnpa47_05.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au">[mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.au.</span></span></span>

### A wider purge

Ndongo and Ondó Edu were not the only casualties.

Other ministers, diplomats and senior officials linked to rival political movements disappeared, were imprisoned or were later executed. Among those frequently identified in historical accounts are Saturnino Ibongo Iyanga and several senior civil servants who had played leading roles during the transition to independence. The exact number of victims from the immediate March purge remains uncertain because official records are incomplete and many later testimonies were collected only after the dictatorship had fallen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au/issues/pnpa47/pnpa47_05.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au">[mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mongobeti.arts.uwa.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.au.</span></span></span>

## How Macías turned crisis into dictatorship

Whatever the precise nature of the March confrontation, its political consequences are much clearer than its origins.

Within days Macías assumed direct control over additional ministries, including defence and foreign affairs, presenting the concentration of power as an emergency response to the failed coup. Official communications to the United Nations announced these changes almost immediately, signalling that the constitutional balance established at independence had effectively ended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/604846/files/S_SUPP_1969_1--%5EOR_SC_1969_1%5E-EN.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digitallibrary.un.org">[United Nations Digital Library System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digitallibrary.un.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ited Nations Digital Library System EC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ITY COUNCIL OffiCIAL RECORDS TWENTY.FOURTH YEARNovember 7, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 7, 2025</span></span></span>

The crisis also accelerated the collapse of relations with Spain. Thousands of Spanish residents left Equatorial Guinea during the ensuing diplomatic confrontation, while Spanish military personnel withdrew. The departure of administrators, teachers, doctors and technical staff further weakened state institutions just as Macías was centralising authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://historia-actual.org/Publicaciones/index.php/hao/article/view/1427" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historia-actual.org">[Historia Actual]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historia-actual.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Historia ActualEL GOLPE DE ESTADO CONTRA MACÍAS EN MARZO DE 1969. ¿INTERVENCIÓN ESPAÑOLA O CONFLICTO INTERNO GUINEANO? &#124; Historia Actual...</span></span></span>

Over the following years the president dismantled organised political opposition, eliminated independent institutions and built an increasingly personalised dictatorship. The March 1969 events became the regime's foundational justification for extraordinary security measures. Anyone portrayed as connected with the alleged conspiracy could be labelled an enemy of the state.

This use of an unresolved political crisis as a legitimising narrative helps explain why historians continue to examine the events so closely. If the coup was smaller than officially claimed, or if some alleged conspirators were eliminated after they had ceased to pose any threat, then March 1969 was not merely the defeat of an insurrection but the decisive moment in the creation of one of Africa's most repressive post-colonial regimes.

## Why the mystery endures

The March 1969 crisis remains difficult to reconstruct because nearly every category of evidence has limitations.

Official government statements were issued by the victorious side and served immediate political purposes. Diplomatic reports from Spain, while valuable, reflected Spanish interests during a period of rapidly deteriorating relations. Survivor testimony is indispensable but was often recorded years later after exile, imprisonment or the collapse of the dictatorship. Many official records from Macías's government are incomplete, inaccessible or were never preserved.

As a result, historians generally distinguish between what is firmly established and what remains uncertain.

Well established are the failure of some form of challenge to Macías's authority, the deaths of Ndongo and Ondó Edu soon afterwards, the rapid concentration of presidential power, and the beginning of widespread political repression.

Still disputed are the scale of the conspiracy, the extent of Spanish involvement, the precise circumstances of Ndongo's fatal injuries, the manner of Ondó Edu's death, and whether the crisis represented a genuine coup attempt, a bungled palace plot, or an opportunity that Macías exploited to eliminate rivals.

That combination of documented violence and unresolved evidence is what makes March 1969 one of Equatorial Guinea's defining historical mysteries. The uncertainty lies not in whether lives were lost or dictatorship followed, but in precisely how the crisis unfolded and how much of the official narrative was shaped by the regime that emerged victorious.
