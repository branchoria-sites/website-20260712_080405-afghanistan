## Introduction

The Sarajevo Haggadah is one of the world's most celebrated medieval Hebrew manuscripts, yet one of the most intriguing parts of its history is not how it was created but how it reached Bosnia. Historians are confident that the richly illuminated book was produced in northern Spain around the middle of the fourteenth century. They also know that it was purchased by the National Museum of [Bosnia and Herzegovina]({{ 'bosnia-and-herzegovina/' | relative_url }}) in Sarajevo in 1894 from the Sephardic Koen (Kohen) family. Between those two firmly documented points lies a gap of roughly five centuries in which the manuscript's owners, movements and circumstances remain only partly visible. Rather than being a mystery of hidden codes or secret societies, the Haggadah's "lost journey" is an archival mystery created by missing records, fragmentary notes and the upheavals that reshaped Jewish communities after their expulsion from Spain. The surviving evidence allows historians to reconstruct parts of the route while leaving important questions unanswered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/secret-story-sarajevo-haggadah" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">secret story sarajevo haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Story of Sarajevo Haggadah &#124; UNESCOFebruary 3, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 3, 2020</span></span></span>

## What the manuscript itself reveals

The strongest clues about the Haggadah's travels come from the manuscript itself. Art historians identify its style as belonging to the Crown of Aragon, probably Barcelona, around 1350. Its Gothic-inspired illustrations, Hebrew script and heraldic devices suggest it may have been commissioned as a wedding gift for members of two prominent Jewish families, commonly identified as the Shoshan and Elazar families. Although this interpretation is widely accepted, it cannot identify the first owners with complete certainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[Zemaljski muzej BiH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

Unlike many medieval books, the Sarajevo Haggadah also preserves later handwritten notes in its margins. These annotations are among the most valuable pieces of evidence for tracing its movements.

Several details stand out:

* The manuscript appears to have left Spain after the expulsion of Jews under the Alhambra Decree of 1492, although no document records the journey itself.
* Marginal inscriptions place the book in northern Italy during the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.
* A note dated 1609 records that an official examination found nothing offensive to the Catholic Church, probably reflecting review by the Roman Inquisition before the book could legally circulate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[Zemaljski muzej BiH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

These annotations are unusually important because they provide physical evidence written directly into the manuscript. They establish that the Haggadah travelled beyond Spain and survived a period when many Hebrew books were confiscated or destroyed, but they reveal almost nothing about who owned it or how it changed hands.

## The missing centuries between Spain and Sarajevo

The greatest historical puzzle concerns the period between the early seventeenth century and the nineteenth century. There is no continuous chain of ownership, no surviving bills of sale and no family archive documenting the manuscript's movements.

The most widely accepted reconstruction is cautious rather than dramatic. Following the expulsion from Spain, many Sephardic Jewish families settled in Italian cities before moving throughout the Ottoman Empire. Sarajevo became an important centre of Sephardic life after Jewish refugees were welcomed into Ottoman territories during the sixteenth century. It is therefore entirely plausible that the manuscript reached Bosnia through ordinary migration, inheritance, marriage or commercial exchange rather than through a single dramatic escape. What historians cannot demonstrate is exactly which family carried it or when it entered Sarajevo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/secret-story-sarajevo-haggadah" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">secret story sarajevo haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Story of Sarajevo Haggadah &#124; UNESCOFebruary 3, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 3, 2020</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because popular retellings often simplify the story into a single uninterrupted family journey from Spain to Bosnia. The documentary evidence does not support such certainty. The manuscript may have passed through several owners over generations before eventually belonging to the Koen family in Sarajevo.

The historical record becomes reliable again only in 1894. That year the newly established National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina purchased the Haggadah from Joseph Koen for 150 Austro-Hungarian crowns. After acquisition it was briefly sent to Vienna for scholarly examination before returning to Sarajevo, where it has remained ever since.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[Zemaljski muzej BiH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

## Why the route remains uncertain

The absence of evidence is not unusual for a medieval manuscript that crossed several political and religious frontiers.

Several factors explain why the trail disappears:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Private ownership:** Family prayer books rarely generated official records unless they were sold or confiscated.
* **Forced migration:** The displacement of Sephardic Jews after 1492 scattered people and possessions across the Mediterranean, often without formal documentation.
* **Loss of archives:** Wars, fires and changing governments destroyed many commercial and communal records that might once have recorded ownership.
* **Changing jurisdictions:** The manuscript moved through regions governed by different kingdoms, republics and empires, each with different record-keeping practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/secret-story-sarajevo-haggadah" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">secret story sarajevo haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Story of Sarajevo Haggadah &#124; UNESCOFebruary 3, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 3, 2020</span></span></span>

</div>

For historians, the mystery is therefore one of missing paperwork rather than missing evidence altogether. The surviving clues strongly suggest a Mediterranean route from Spain through Italy before arrival in Bosnia, but they do not allow a complete itinerary to be reconstructed.

## Wartime rescues, disputed details and modern myth

Although the medieval journey remains obscure, the Haggadah's twentieth-century survival is much better documented, even if some details are still debated.

During the Second World War, German occupation authorities sought valuable Jewish cultural property. Museum officials, particularly librarian Derviš Korkut, prevented the manuscript from being seized. The broad outline is well supported: Korkut removed the Haggadah from danger and it survived the occupation. However, accounts differ over precisely where it was hidden. Some place it in a mosque in a village on Mount Bjelašnica among Islamic manuscripts, while other versions identify different villages or slightly different hiding places. The disagreement concerns the exact location rather than the fact that local Muslims helped safeguard the manuscript.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/item/sarajevo-haggadah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: zemaljskimuzej.ba">[zemaljskimuzej.ba]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">zemaljskimuzej.ba</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zemaljski muzej Bi HSarajevo Haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zemaljski muzej BiHSarajevo Haggadah - Zemaljski muzej BiH...</span></span></span>

The manuscript faced danger again during the 1992–1995 siege of Sarajevo. Museum staff removed it from the threatened building and secured it in a bank vault. During the war rumours circulated that the Bosnian government had secretly sold the Haggadah abroad to finance military operations. Those claims proved false, and the manuscript was publicly displayed in 1995 to demonstrate that it remained in Bosnia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rferl.org/a/28701521.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rferl.org">[RadioFreeEurope/RadioLiberty]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rferl.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rferl.org.</span></span></span>

These later episodes sometimes overshadow the earlier mystery because they are supported by living memory and surviving witnesses. Yet they also show how easily extraordinary cultural objects become surrounded by rumours whenever they disappear from public view.

## What remains unresolved today

The central historical questions are surprisingly specific.

Researchers still cannot answer with confidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Who first commissioned and owned the manuscript.
* Which individuals carried it out of Spain after 1492.
* Whether it reached Bosnia directly from Italy or through other Ottoman cities.
* How many owners possessed it before the Koen family acquired it.
* Exactly when it first arrived in Sarajevo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/secret-story-sarajevo-haggadah" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">secret story sarajevo haggadah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Secret Story of Sarajevo Haggadah &#124; UNESCOFebruary 3, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 3, 2020</span></span></span>

</div>

These uncertainties are genuine but limited. They do not cast doubt on the manuscript's authenticity, age or significance. Instead, they illustrate how even an internationally famous object can retain gaps in its documentary history despite centuries of scholarly attention.

## Why the lost journey still matters

The Sarajevo Haggadah's missing centuries have become part of its significance. The unanswered questions encourage continued research into Sephardic migration, medieval book culture and the movement of Jewish communities across Europe and the Ottoman world. At the same time, the manuscript's later documented rescues demonstrate that its survival depended less on chance than on the repeated decisions of individuals who recognised its cultural value despite war and persecution.

Today the Haggadah is recognised on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register and remains one of Bosnia and Herzegovina's most important cultural treasures. Its incomplete journey reminds historians that silence in the archive is not evidence of conspiracy.
