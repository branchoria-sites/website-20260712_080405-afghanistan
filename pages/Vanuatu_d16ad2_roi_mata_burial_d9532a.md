## Introduction

Chief Roi Mata is one of the most remarkable figures in [Vanuatu]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-vanuatus/' | relative_url }})'s history because archaeology has confirmed key parts of a story that survived for centuries through oral tradition. According to those traditions, Roi Mata was a powerful chief who ended long-running conflicts, reorganised society across central Vanuatu and died around the beginning of the seventeenth century. His story connects three places: his residence at Mangaas on Efate, the cave on Lelepa Island where he is said to have died, and his burial on nearby Artok Island. Excavations have shown that these sites were occupied at the same period and are linked by an extraordinary mass burial, making Roi Mata's story one of the strongest known examples of archaeology supporting Indigenous oral history in the Pacific. Yet important questions remain unanswered, especially about the circumstances of his death, the identities of those buried with him and how much of the tradition can be treated as literal history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1280/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJuly 8, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2008</span></span></span>

## The chief remembered in oral tradition

Across central Vanuatu, oral traditions describe Roi Mata as the last great paramount chief to hold that title. Rather than being remembered chiefly as a conqueror, he is celebrated as a leader who brought together previously hostile communities, established new political relationships and promoted forms of conflict resolution that remained influential long after his death. These traditions continue to shape customary authority today and were a major reason why UNESCO recognised the landscape associated with him as a living cultural heritage site rather than simply an archaeological monument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1280/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJuly 8, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2008</span></span></span>

The traditions also provide an unusually detailed sequence of events. They place Roi Mata's residence at Mangaas on Efate, describe his attendance at a ceremonial feast on Lelepa Island, recount that he became fatally ill there, and state that he died inside Fels Cave before being taken for burial on Artok Island. For generations these accounts were preserved without written records, leading many early outsiders to question whether Roi Mata had been a real historical individual or a legendary composite figure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu)April 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

What makes the case distinctive is that the oral tradition identified specific places before modern archaeological investigation. Researchers were therefore able to test the traditions against physical evidence rather than using archaeology to invent a story after the fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centrecrmd_nomination.indd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage Centrecrmd_nomination.indd...</span></span></span>

## The Artok burial and archaeological discoveries

French archaeologist José Garanger began excavations in the 1960s after working closely with local knowledge holders who identified the traditional sites associated with Roi Mata. His work revealed that the locations named in oral tradition contained archaeological remains dating to the correct period, lending strong support to the historical core of the narrative. Later investigations refined the chronology but broadly confirmed that the three principal sites belonged to the same era around AD 1600.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centrecrmd_nomination.indd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage Centrecrmd_nomination.indd...</span></span></span>

The most dramatic discovery came on Artok Island, traditionally regarded as sacred and largely left undisturbed for roughly four centuries. Excavation uncovered a mass burial centred on a single high-status individual identified by tradition as Roi Mata. Around him lay the remains of dozens of other people arranged in deliberate positions, with grave markers and ceremonial objects including large conch shells. The burial is widely regarded as the most significant prehistoric mass burial yet discovered in the Pacific.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu)April 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

The broader cultural landscape also supports the traditional account:

* **Mangaas (Efate):** the site identified as Roi Mata's residence includes settlement remains, ceremonial areas and stone structures associated with chiefly authority.
* **Fels Cave (Lelepa):** oral tradition identifies this as the place where Roi Mata died after attending a feast. The cave also contains extensive rock art, some of which has been radiocarbon dated to approximately Roi Mata's era.
* **Artok Island:** the burial site remained protected by customary taboos that discouraged settlement, farming and disturbance, helping preserve both the archaeological remains and the surrounding landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu)April 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

The close correspondence between oral testimony and archaeological evidence is the principal reason the area became Vanuatu's first UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008. UNESCO considers the landscape exceptional because it demonstrates the convergence of archaeology, living tradition and continuing customary authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1280/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJuly 8, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2008</span></span></span>

## What remains uncertain about death and sacrifice

Although the archaeological discoveries strongly support Roi Mata's existence, they do not answer every question.

The cause of his death remains unknown. Oral traditions describe him becoming ill after attending a feast on Lelepa before dying in Fels Cave, but they do not provide an explanation that can be verified scientifically. Some versions suggest poisoning, while others simply describe sudden illness. No surviving physical evidence allows researchers to determine whether disease, poisoning or another cause was responsible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu)April 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

An even more debated issue concerns those buried alongside him. Local traditions commonly state that many of Roi Mata's followers or wives voluntarily accompanied him in death so they could continue serving him in the next world. Archaeology confirms that many people were buried together with extraordinary care and deliberate arrangement, but it cannot determine whether they died willingly, were ritually killed, died naturally over a short period, or represent a more complex sequence of funerary practices. Human remains alone cannot reveal motivation or consent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu)April 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

Numbers also vary considerably. Some traditions speak of hundreds of companions, whereas the excavated burial contained far fewer individuals. This difference may reflect symbolic storytelling, incomplete excavation, or the gradual reshaping of memory over centuries. The evidence is insufficient to resolve the discrepancy with certainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu)April 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

## How much of Roi Mata's story is true?

The evidence suggests that neither complete scepticism nor literal acceptance provides the best explanation.

Several elements are now well supported:

* A powerful chief named Roi Mata almost certainly lived in central Vanuatu around AD 1600.
* The places identified in oral tradition correspond closely with genuine archaeological sites.
* A remarkable mass burial occurred on Artok Island during the same period.
* The sites have remained protected through customary taboos for centuries, preserving both archaeological evidence and living cultural practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1280/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJuly 8, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2008</span></span></span>

Other aspects remain uncertain:

* The precise cause of Roi Mata's death.
* Whether those buried with him died voluntarily, through ritual killing or by another process.
* The exact historical details behind stories that have been transmitted orally for around 400 years.
* Whether every episode in the traditional narrative reflects historical events or incorporates later symbolic additions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1280.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain (Vanuatu)April 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2026</span></span></span>

Rather than weakening the story, these uncertainties make it a valuable historical case. Archaeology has verified enough of the tradition to demonstrate that long-preserved oral history can preserve accurate information about real people and places. At the same time, it shows the limits of archaeology: even when graves, settlements and ceremonial sites survive, the beliefs, intentions and exact events surrounding a person's death may remain beyond recovery. That combination of confirmed history and enduring unanswered questions is what makes Chief Roi Mata's life, death and burial one of Vanuatu's most compelling historical mysteries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1280/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whc.unesco.org">[UNESCO World Heritage Centre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whc.unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">World Heritage Centre Chief Roi Mata’s Domain</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNESCO World Heritage CentreChief Roi Mata’s Domain - UNESCO World Heritage CentreJuly 8, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 2008</span></span></span>
