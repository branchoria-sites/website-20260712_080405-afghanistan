## Introduction

Who first settled the [Maldives]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-the-maldives/' | relative_url }}) is one of the country's oldest historical puzzles. The short answer is that nobody knows with certainty. Archaeology, linguistics and genetics all point towards the same broad conclusion: the islands were settled over time by several groups rather than by a single founding people. The strongest evidence links the earliest permanent inhabitants with southern India and Sri Lanka, but later arrivals from other parts of the Indian Ocean also helped shape the population and culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajpa.22256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[wiley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryIndian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin and population structure in the maldives - Pijpe - 2013 - American Jour...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Maldives_213cb2_first_settlers_7c3a16-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="First Settlers illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Maldives_213cb2_first_settlers_7c3a16-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Maldives_213cb2_first_settlers_7c3a16-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The mystery persists because the Maldives has very few surviving records from its earliest history. Coral-stone buildings were repeatedly dismantled and rebuilt, tropical conditions damaged archaeological sites, and later political and religious changes erased or altered parts of the pre-Islamic past. Instead of one clear founding story, historians must combine fragments of archaeological evidence, inscriptions, language, oral tradition and modern scientific research.

## What archaeology reveals about early island life

The archaeological record shows that the Maldives had established communities long before the country's conversion to Islam in the twelfth century. Excavations have uncovered Buddhist monasteries, stupas, ceramics and inscriptions demonstrating a sophisticated pre-Islamic civilisation spread across several atolls. These discoveries confirm that organised communities already existed, but they do not identify who the first settlers actually were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajpa.22256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[Wiley Online Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryIndian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin and population structure in the maldives - Pijpe - 2013 - American Jour...</span></span></span>

Dating the very earliest settlement is more difficult. Archaeologists generally agree that people were living on the islands at least 2,000 years ago, while some official Maldivian historical summaries place initial settlement around 1500 BC. Even these earlier estimates remain provisional because few securely dated sites survive from the earliest occupation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajpa.22256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[Wiley Online Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryIndian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin and population structure in the maldives - Pijpe - 2013 - American Jour...</span></span></span>

Several factors make the evidence unusually fragmentary:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The Maldives is built from low coral islands where organic materials decay rapidly.
* Coral blocks from older monuments were frequently reused in later buildings.
* Coastal erosion and rising seas have damaged ancient settlements.
* Few inscriptions survive from the earliest centuries of occupation.

</div>

As a result, archaeologists can reconstruct the existence of early communities more easily than they can identify their precise origins.

## Genetic and linguistic clues to migration

Modern genetic research provides some of the strongest evidence that the Maldives developed through repeated migration rather than a single founding event.

A landmark population genetics study found that present-day Maldivians derive most of their ancestry from South Asia while also showing contributions associated with populations from the Middle East and elsewhere around the Indian Ocean. The researchers described the Maldives as an "Indian Ocean crossroads", with multiple independent migration events shaping the population over centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajpa.22256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[wiley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryIndian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin and population structure in the maldives - Pijpe - 2013 - American Jour...</span></span></span>

Language tells a similar story.

Dhivehi belongs to the Indo-Aryan branch of languages and is most closely related to Sinhala, the principal language of Sri Lanka. At the same time, it contains substantial layers of older Dravidian vocabulary and grammatical influence, reflecting prolonged contact with southern India. Rather than pointing to one migration, the language preserves traces of several waves of interaction across the region.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajpa.22256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[wiley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryIndian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin and population structure in the maldives - Pijpe - 2013 - American Jour...</span></span></span>

Taken together, the evidence suggests a sequence broadly like this:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Earliest permanent settlers** most likely arrived from southern India and nearby Sri Lanka.
* **Subsequent migrations** added people from different parts of the Indian Ocean trading world.
* **Centuries of intermarriage and trade** blended these communities into the ancestors of today's Maldivians.

</div>

This pattern fits the Maldives' geographical position astride major sea routes linking India, Sri Lanka, Arabia, East Africa and Southeast Asia.

## What do traditional origin stories claim?

Maldivian historical traditions preserve several accounts of the islands' first inhabitants, but these stories were written down long after the events they describe.

Some traditions speak of an early people known as the Dheyvis, while others describe royal founders or exiled princes arriving from the Indian subcontinent. Local folklore also identifies the people of Giraavaru as descendants of some of the earliest settlers and associates them with the founding traditions of Malé.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

These accounts remain historically valuable because they may preserve memories of genuine migrations. However, historians treat them cautiously because:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* they were recorded centuries after the supposed events;
* different versions contradict one another;
* none can currently be confirmed by archaeology alone.

</div>

Rather than reading them as literal history, many scholars regard them as cultural memories that may contain fragments of real population movements mixed with later political and religious traditions.

## Why single-founder stories remain unproven

One reason the question remains unresolved is that people naturally seek simple origin stories. Many nations celebrate a founding ancestor or dramatic migration, but the evidence from the Maldives resists such neat conclusions.

Several theories have proposed that the islands were first settled by a particular ethnic group—whether ancient Tamils, Sinhalese, Gujaratis or another community. Each draws on some combination of linguistic, cultural or traditional evidence. Yet none explains the full archaeological, linguistic and genetic picture.

Modern research increasingly favours a more complex model in which:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* settlement occurred over many centuries rather than during one expedition;
* different islands may have been populated at different times;
* later migration continually reshaped the population;
* trade routes encouraged repeated arrivals from neighbouring regions.

</div>

This interpretation also explains why Maldivian culture contains influences from several parts of the Indian Ocean instead of reflecting a single ancestral tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajpa.22256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[wiley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryIndian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin and population structure in the maldives - Pijpe - 2013 - American Jour...</span></span></span>

## Why the mystery still matters

The question of the Maldives' first settlers is important because it shapes how the country's earliest history is understood. Instead of revealing an isolated island kingdom founded by one people, the available evidence points to a society that developed through centuries of movement across the Indian Ocean.

The mystery is therefore not simply "Who arrived first?" but "How did repeated migrations create a distinct Maldivian identity?" Archaeology confirms an ancient pre-Islamic civilisation, genetics demonstrates multiple ancestral sources, and language preserves traces of diverse cultural contacts. Together they suggest that the Maldives emerged as a maritime crossroads whose history cannot be reduced to a single founding event.

While future excavations or new ancient DNA discoveries may clarify aspects of early settlement, the prospect of identifying one definitive founding people remains unlikely. The strongest evidence supports a gradual peopling of the islands, led by migrants from South Asia and continually enriched by later arrivals from across the wider Indian Ocean.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajpa.22256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: onlinelibrary.wiley.com">[wiley.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">onlinelibrary.wiley.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiley Online LibraryIndian ocean crossroads: Human genetic origin and population structure in the maldives - Pijpe - 2013 - American Jour...</span></span></span>
