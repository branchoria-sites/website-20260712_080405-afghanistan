## Introduction

Project Riese is one of the largest unfinished construction projects of the Second World War and one of [Poland]({{ 'poland/' | relative_url }})'s most enduring historical mysteries. Between 1943 and 1945, Nazi Germany drove thousands of forced labourers to excavate an extensive network of underground tunnels and chambers beneath the Owl Mountains and around Książ Castle in what is now south-western Poland. The tunnels are real, many survive, and their scale is beyond dispute. What remains uncertain is exactly what the completed complex was meant to become.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Poland_5ff03b_project_riese_tunnel_7991cb-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Project Riese illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Poland_5ff03b_project_riese_tunnel_7991cb-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Poland_5ff03b_project_riese_tunnel_7991cb-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The mystery exists because the project was abandoned before completion, many wartime records disappeared, and no surviving master plan has been found. Historians broadly agree that the tunnels had an important military purpose, but they continue to debate whether they were primarily intended as a new Führer headquarters, underground arms factories, or a combination of both. The lack of documentation has also encouraged decades of treasure legends and claims about secret weapons that go far beyond the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/11/1802" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forgotten Nazi Forced Labour Camps: Arbeitslager Riese (Lower Silesia, SE Poland) and the Use of Archival Aerial Photography and Cont...</span></span></span>

## Where were the tunnels built?

Project Riese—"Giant" in German—was not a single underground base but a construction programme spread across several locations. Seven major tunnel systems are usually counted as part of the project, including complexes beneath or near Osówka, Włodarz, Rzeczka, Sokolec, Soboń and other sites in the Owl Mountains, together with major excavation beneath Książ Castle.

Rather than forming one continuous underground labyrinth, these were separate excavation sites connected by newly built roads, rail access, workers' camps and construction infrastructure. Many tunnels remain unfinished, with raw rock faces ending abruptly where work stopped during the final months of the war. Only a small proportion of the excavations received reinforced concrete, indicating that construction was still far from complete when Germany's military situation collapsed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Riese" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Riese</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Riese</span></span></span>

### Książ Castle and forced labour

Książ Castle became one of the most unusual elements of the project. Existing rooms inside the medieval castle were heavily altered while deep shafts, lift wells and underground passages were excavated beneath it. These modifications suggest preparations for a major administrative or command installation rather than simply defensive bunkers.

The human cost was immense. Construction relied on prisoners from the Gross-Rosen concentration camp system, alongside prisoners of war and other forced labourers under the control of Organisation Todt and the SS. Thousands died from starvation, disease, exhaustion and abuse before the project was abandoned. Modern archaeological research has identified numerous forgotten labour camp sites around the construction zones using wartime aerial photography, LiDAR surveys and field investigation, reinforcing that forced labour was central to Project Riese rather than a secondary aspect of it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/11/1802" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forgotten Nazi Forced Labour Camps: Arbeitslager Riese (Lower Silesia, SE Poland) and the Use of Archival Aerial Photography and Cont...</span></span></span>

## Was it meant to be Hitler's headquarters?

One of the oldest and most persistent explanations is that Project Riese was intended to become a new Führer headquarters.

Several pieces of evidence support this idea:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* Książ Castle underwent extensive rebuilding suitable for high-ranking occupation.
* Underground chambers beneath the castle appear designed for protected access and services.
* Hitler increasingly favoured heavily fortified headquarters as Allied bombing intensified.
* The project received exceptionally high priority despite Germany's worsening military position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Riese" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Riese</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Riese</span></span></span>

</div>

However, this explanation does not comfortably account for every tunnel complex. Many excavations in the Owl Mountains resemble industrial underground workings more than command bunkers. They lack obvious features expected in completed leadership headquarters, such as finished communications rooms, accommodation and command facilities. Because construction stopped early, some planned features may simply never have been built, making interpretation difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Riese" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Riese</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Riese</span></span></span>

## Were the tunnels intended for underground weapons production?

Many historians now favour a mixed interpretation rather than a single purpose.

By late 1943 Germany was moving critical war industries underground to protect them from Allied bombing. Across the Reich, factories producing aircraft, rockets and other military equipment were relocated into mountains, mines and specially excavated tunnels. Project Riese fits this broader strategic pattern.

Several characteristics point towards industrial use:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* exceptionally large chambers capable of housing machinery;
* transport routes planned into excavation areas;
* substantial electrical and engineering works;
* multiple dispersed sites rather than one central bunker.

</div>

In this interpretation, Książ Castle may have served as a command or administrative centre while the surrounding tunnel complexes became protected industrial facilities producing military equipment. This hybrid explanation also matches the practical needs of Germany in 1944 better than the idea of an enormous headquarters alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Riese" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Riese</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Riese</span></span></span>

## Why historians still disagree

The central mystery survives because the evidence is incomplete rather than contradictory.

Several factors limit certainty:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no complete master construction plan has been recovered;
* many German records were destroyed before the end of the war;
* construction halted while many tunnels remained unfinished;
* surviving physical evidence shows engineering works but not necessarily their final intended function.

</div>

As a result, historians must reconstruct intentions from engineering layouts, surviving correspondence, procurement records, wartime priorities and comparisons with similar underground projects elsewhere in Nazi Germany. None of these sources provides a definitive answer on its own.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/11/1802" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mdpi.com">[MDPI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mdpi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forgotten Nazi Forced Labour Camps: Arbeitslager Riese (Lower Silesia, SE Poland) and the Use of Archival Aerial Photography and Cont...</span></span></span>

## How missing records created treasure and superweapon legends

The absence of documentation created ideal conditions for speculation after the war.

Stories have claimed that the tunnels conceal:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* vast Nazi gold reserves;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.history.co.uk/articles/nazi-gold-train-mystery" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.co.uk">[history.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* the missing Amber Room;
* hidden trains loaded with artwork;
* secret aircraft factories;
* revolutionary "wonder weapons";
* laboratories for advanced technology.

</div>

Although occasional discoveries of wartime equipment or sealed passages continue to attract attention, none has produced convincing evidence for these larger claims. Likewise, there is no credible evidence that Project Riese housed a nuclear programme or fantastical experimental technologies. Such stories usually combine genuine historical uncertainty with post-war folklore and modern internet mythmaking rather than archival documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Riese" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Riese</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Riese</span></span></span>

The famous "Nazi gold train" legend is closely associated with Lower Silesia because of the region's wartime rail infrastructure and numerous underground works. Despite repeated searches, no verified train has been found, and the legend should not be treated as evidence for Project Riese's original purpose.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Riese" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Riese</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Riese</span></span></span>

## What is the strongest explanation today?

Most specialists reject simple either-or answers.

The best-supported interpretation is that Project Riese evolved as Germany's strategic situation deteriorated. Initial planning may have included a protected headquarters associated with Książ Castle, while the wider tunnel systems increasingly reflected the urgent wartime need to move sensitive military production underground beyond the reach of Allied bombers. These roles were not mutually exclusive, and different parts of the project may have been intended for different functions.

What remains unresolved is not whether the tunnels existed—they unquestionably did—nor whether they were strategically important. The uncertainty lies in the exact balance between headquarters, industrial production and military infrastructure that planners ultimately intended before defeat halted construction forever.
