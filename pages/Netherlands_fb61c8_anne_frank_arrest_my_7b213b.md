## Introduction

The arrest of Anne Frank and the seven other people hiding in the Secret Annex at 263 Prinsengracht in Amsterdam on 4 August 1944 remains one of the [Netherlands]({{ 'netherlands/' | relative_url }})' most emotionally significant unresolved wartime questions. For decades, the common assumption was that someone betrayed their hiding place to the German security police. Yet despite repeated investigations, no individual has ever been proved to have informed on them. Modern historical research has broadened the debate, suggesting that the raid may instead have resulted from an investigation into wartime economic offences, with the hidden group discovered during the search. The mystery today is therefore not simply "Who betrayed Anne Frank?" but whether there was a deliberate betrayal at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/anne-frank/go-in-depth/was-anne-frank-betrayed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

## What happened at 263 Prinsengracht?

On the morning of 4 August 1944, members of the German Sicherheitsdienst (Security Service), accompanied by Dutch police officers, entered Otto Frank's business premises at 263 Prinsengracht. They discovered the concealed rooms behind the movable bookcase where Anne Frank, her family, the Van Pels family and Fritz Pfeffer had been hiding for more than two years. The eight people in hiding were arrested, along with two of their Dutch helpers.

Although the broad outline of the raid is well established, remarkably little official documentation survives. No original report explaining why the premises were searched has been found. Historians therefore rely on post-war testimony, surviving correspondence, police records from related investigations and later archival research. This absence of a definitive contemporary record is one of the main reasons the circumstances of the raid remain unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/anne-frank/go-in-depth/was-anne-frank-betrayed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

Several officers have been securely identified, including Austrian SS sergeant Karl Silberbauer and Dutch officers Gezinus Gringhuis and Willem Grootendorst. Their identities are known, but their presence alone does not reveal what intelligence, if any, prompted the search.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/anne-frank/go-in-depth/was-anne-frank-betrayed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

## Why was betrayal assumed for so long?

For many years, betrayal appeared to be the obvious explanation. During the German occupation, thousands of Jewish people in hiding across the Netherlands were discovered after being denounced by neighbours, informers or collaborators. Otto Frank himself believed after the war that someone had betrayed the group and spent years trying to identify the person responsible.

A widely repeated story claimed that an anonymous telephone call alerted the Sicherheitsdienst shortly before the raid. However, historians have found no surviving evidence that such a call took place. The story became accepted largely through repetition rather than documentary proof, illustrating how assumptions can harden into historical "facts" when records are incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/anne-frank/go-in-depth/was-anne-frank-betrayed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

The Anne Frank House now distinguishes carefully between what is documented and what remains speculative. While betrayal remains possible, it has never been established as a historical fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/anne-frank/go-in-depth/was-anne-frank-betrayed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

## The suspects and the limits of the evidence

Over the decades, numerous individuals have been accused of betraying the Secret Annex. Warehouse workers, business associates, neighbours, collaborators and other figures connected with the building have all been proposed at various times.

What these accusations generally have in common is the absence of conclusive evidence. Some suspects were linked through hearsay, others through coincidence, personal disputes or circumstantial opportunities. Repeated investigations have found that the surviving evidence is too weak to identify any individual with confidence.

The Anne Frank House has emphasised that many proposed solutions rest on assumptions rather than verifiable documentation. Because the official records of the raid have largely disappeared, historians cannot reconstruct the decision-making process that led officers to the building with certainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/anne-frank/go-in-depth/was-anne-frank-betrayed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

The most publicised recent accusation came in 2022, when an international cold-case team argued that the Jewish notary Arnold van den Bergh was the most likely informer. Their conclusion relied heavily on an anonymous post-war note received by Otto Frank and a chain of inferences about wartime Jewish Council records.

The claim attracted worldwide attention but was quickly challenged by Dutch Holocaust specialists and the Anne Frank House. Critics argued that key assumptions were unsupported, including whether lists of hiding places even existed, whether Van den Bergh had access to such lists, and whether the anonymous note could be treated as reliable evidence. Following a critical historical review, the Dutch publisher withdrew the Dutch edition of the book presenting the theory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/about-us/news-and-press/news/2022/1/17/statement-cold-case-investigation/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank WebsiteStatement: Anne Frank House and cold case investigation &#124; Anne Frank HouseJanuary 17, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2022</span></span></span>

## Could the raid have begun as an economic investigation?

One of the most important developments in recent scholarship came from the Anne Frank House's 2016 investigation, which deliberately asked a different question. Instead of asking who betrayed the people in hiding, researchers examined why the authorities entered the building in the first place.

Their investigation identified evidence that illegal employment, black-market activity and ration-coupon fraud were taking place within Otto Frank's business. Such offences were actively investigated by German occupation authorities, and at least one of the officers involved in the raid belonged to a unit concerned with these kinds of economic crimes.

The researchers concluded that it is plausible the premises were searched because of suspected economic offences and that the Secret Annex was discovered during that search. This does not prove that no betrayal occurred, but it demonstrates that another explanation fits the surviving evidence without requiring an unidentified informer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/about-us/news-and-press/news/2016/12/16/new-perspective-anne-franks-arrest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website New Perspective on Anne Frank&#x27;s Arrest &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website New Perspective on Anne Frank&#x27;s Arrest &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

This interpretation also explains several features of the case that have long puzzled historians, including why decades of searching failed to uncover convincing evidence for a specific betrayal despite the many names proposed.

## Why the betrayal story remains disputed

The mystery persists because crucial evidence has been lost. Historians face several enduring obstacles:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* No surviving German report explains the purpose of the raid.
* No authenticated record confirms an anonymous telephone tip.
* Most evidence comes from recollections recorded after the war rather than contemporary documents.
* Every proposed informer has significant evidential weaknesses.
* Alternative explanations remain consistent with the surviving archival record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/anne-frank/go-in-depth/was-anne-frank-betrayed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[annefrank.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

</div>

Rather than converging on a single answer, modern scholarship has become more cautious. The central historical question has shifted from identifying a culprit to understanding the limits of what the surviving evidence can genuinely support.

## Why the uncertainty still matters

The unresolved nature of Anne Frank's arrest has broader historical significance than identifying one possible informer. The focus on a single act of betrayal can unintentionally obscure the wider system of persecution that made such arrests possible. German occupation authorities relied on extensive bureaucracy, surveillance, collaboration, economic investigations and systematic anti-Jewish policies, not solely on individual denunciations.

For that reason, many historians argue that the mystery should be approached with restraint. Naming an individual without conclusive proof risks creating a false certainty while diverting attention from the broader machinery of the Holocaust in the occupied Netherlands.

The most responsible historical conclusion today is therefore a measured one: Anne Frank and the other people in hiding were certainly discovered and arrested on 4 August 1944, but no informer has ever been proved responsible. Deliberate betrayal remains one possibility, yet discovery during an investigation into wartime economic offences is also supported by credible evidence. Until new archival material emerges, the reason the raid occurred must remain one of the Netherlands' most important unresolved historical questions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.annefrank.org/en/anne-frank/go-in-depth/was-anne-frank-betrayed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: annefrank.org">[Anne Frank Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">annefrank.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anne Frank Website Was Anne Frank betrayed? &#124; Anne Frank House</span></span></span>

        break;

      if (!languageMarket && localeMarketMap[languageOnly]) languageMarket = localeMarketMap[languageOnly];
    }

    var matches = localeMatches();

      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};
      try {
        if (new RegExp(rule.pattern).test(tz)) return rule.market;
      } catch (err) {}
    }
    return '';
  }

    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
